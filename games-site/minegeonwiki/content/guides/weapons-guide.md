## Weapon System Overview

Weapons are one of the three pillars of builds in MineGeon: Renegades, alongside **gadgets** and **ultimate abilities**. Kickstone Studio describes players discovering and equipping a variety of weapons across runs, with legendary weapons and the powerful weapons of the "Ancient Ones" among the most sought-after finds.

> **Accuracy note:** as of early August 2026, the developer has not published a full weapon list, weapon names, or stats. Any site claiming a complete catalog with damage numbers is speculating. This guide covers what is confirmed about the system and how to build effective loadouts while the community documents the actual arsenal.

## What We Know About Weapons (Confirmed)

- **Weapons are equippable and found during runs.** The Steam page describes leveling that unlocks gadgets and equippable weapons — your loadout evolves as you descend and level.
- **Weapons combine with gadgets and ultimates into builds.** Synergy between the three systems is the game's core build philosophy.
- **Legendary weapons exist.** The highest tiers of weaponry are explicitly mentioned in the game's marketing.
- **Weapons of the "Ancient Ones" exist.** A special weapon class tied to PAIMO's ancient civilization lore — described as powerful, and likely tied to the game's deeper biomes and lore fragments.
- **Classes pair with different weapons.** Marshall (Tank), Trevor (Gunslinger), and Ari (Engineer) each have distinct roles, and loadouts should match the role.

## What We Don't Know Yet (Unconfirmed)

- The full weapon catalog and tier list — not published
- Whether weapons have rarity tiers beyond "legendary" (e.g., common/rare/epic)
- How weapon upgrades work between runs vs. within a run
- Whether weapons are class-locked or equippable by anyone
- Ammo or durability systems — not mentioned anywhere officially

We will update this guide with a real weapon catalog once the community documents the arsenal post-launch.

## Building a Loadout

Until the catalog is documented, build loadouts around the confirmed role system:

| Role | Loadout Thinking | Example Approach |
|------|------------------|------------------|
| Marshall (Tank) | Close-range durability; weapons that let you hold a position | Pair defensive gadgets (energy shields) with whatever weapon the run offers |
| Trevor (Gunslinger) | Ranged damage; weapons with damage per shot | Keep distance, look for high single-target damage |
| Ari (Engineer) | Flexible; match the weapon to the run's gadget luck | Balanced stats let Ari adapt to whichever weapon appears |

### Loadout Checklist
1. **One weapon for the role, one for the situation.** E.g., a boss-killer for arenas and something for swarm clear.
2. **Gadgets cover weapon gaps.** Ranged weapon + defensive gadget beats ranged weapon + nothing.
3. **Ultimate complements the weapon.** Save your ultimate for the moments your weapon is weakest (crowds for single-target weapons, bosses for slow ones).
4. **Adapt to the route.** The monolith path choice changes what you will face — factor it into loadout decisions.

## Weapon Progression Strategy

Because the roguelite structure hands you weapons during runs, think of progression in layers:

1. **Per-run:** take better weapons as they appear; commit to one early so upgrades stack.
2. **Between runs:** permanent upgrades to your ship and Renegade attributes raise your baseline, making every weapon you find hit harder in practice.
3. **Long-term:** legendary and Ancient Ones weapons are the chase items — expect them to appear in deeper biomes and boss rewards.

## Common Loadout Mistakes

1. **Hoarding items.** In a roguelite, an un-equipped weapon is dead weight — switch when something better appears.
2. **Ignoring gadgets.** A great weapon with no gadgets is fragile in a bullet-hell game; gadgets are half the build.
3. **Saving the ultimate forever.** The biome boss is the right place to spend it; hoarding it past the boss wastes it.
4. **Judging weapons by marketing hype.** "Legendary" doesn't mean best for your class — role fit matters more than rarity.
