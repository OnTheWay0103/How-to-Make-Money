## Why Navigation Is the Hidden Skill

MineGeon: Renegades gives you no maps. Its **minegeons** — procedurally generated dungeons on planet PAIMO — are different every run, so you cannot learn "the route" the way you learn a hand-crafted dungeon. The players who extract consistently are not the ones who know the caves; they are the ones with a **navigation system**: knowing where they are, how deep they have gone, and when the extraction door is closer than the next vein.

## The Three Navigation Truths

1. **Depth is your only compass.** The game's core rule — deeper = richer, deeper = riskier — means depth is the single most useful reference point. Track it mentally at every transition.
2. **Every run is a new cave.** Never trust memory of a previous seed; trust your current run's landmarks.
3. **Extraction is a direction, not a place.** The ship door is where you started. The classic death is "one more room" — the navigation rule is "the door is behind you."

## The Depth Dial System

Think of depth as a dial you turn up and down deliberately, not a ladder you climb until you die:

| Depth State | Mindset | Extraction Trigger |
|-------------|---------|--------------------|
| Safe depth | Farm freely, mine everything | Any meaningful damage taken |
| Comfort depth | Mine smart, skip risky rooms | Significant damage, or any rare find |
| Push depth | Mine only high-value veins | Set a hard trigger *before* descending |
| Overextended | Retreat — the dial goes down | Any elite spawn, any near-death |

**The rule:** never descend past the depth you planned before the run. The dial goes down as easily as it goes up — retreating is a navigation decision, not a failure.

## Reference Points That Work

Procedural generation does not mean *random chaos* — room templates repeat between seeds. Use what repeats:

| Reference | How to Use |
|-----------|------------|
| Room templates | Learn the recurring layouts (spawn rooms, hazard rooms, loot rooms) and their tells |
| Hazard signatures | Vents, pools, and trap patterns mark room types — a vent room leads somewhere specific |
| Biome transitions | Biome changes are loud visual signals — a biome boundary is a depth marker |
| Your own trail | Drop a marker when you leave a rich vein or a hazard chamber — return paths matter |

**The landmark rule:** every time you find something notable (a rich vein, a boss door, a hazard chamber), pause for one second and note what the room looked like. That one-second habit is the difference between "I can retrace" and "where am I."

## Hazard Awareness While Navigating

Environmental hazards are navigation constraints — they decide which paths are viable:

- **Biome hazards** get worse with depth (each biome brings its own environmental hazards; specifics of named biomes are still being documented).
- **Hazard rooms are optional rooms** — if a chamber's hazard costs more than its loot, the correct navigation is *around it*.
- **Escapes run through hazards** — the extraction path can cross hazard chambers; budget health and gadgets for the return, not just the descent.

## Gadgets as Navigation Tools

Your gadget loadout is also your navigation kit:

| Gadget Type | Navigation Use |
|-------------|----------------|
| Combat drones | Scout forward while you stay safe — the closest thing to a teammate solo |
| Energy shields | Cover extraction channels under fire — the return trip's insurance |
| Grenades | Break a pursuing pack at a choke — turns a fatal corridor into a clean escape |

**Loadout rule:** on deep runs, carry at least one defensive gadget. The [Gadgets Guide](/guides/gadgets-guide) covers the full catalog thinking.

## Team Navigation (Co-op)

- **Split roles, not players** — one mines, one guards, one navigates; swap.
- **Call out landmarks** — "vent room to the west, rich vein north of it" — a team that shares navigation knowledge extracts together.
- **The escape buddy rule** — never split for extraction; the team that extracts together banks together.
- **Split the map** only when both wings can describe their route back to the meeting point.

## The Lost Protocol

When you are turned around (and it will happen):

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| 1 | Stop moving — panicked movement is how lost runs die |
| 2 | Identify the last landmark you passed and head back to it |
| 3 | If no landmark: pick a wall and follow it consistently — procedural caves eventually loop |
| 4 | If extraction matters more than the haul: follow the depth gradient up — shallower is always toward the exit |
| 5 | When the gear is safe: the break point rule — if you cannot reconstruct your path in 30 seconds, extract at the first exit you reach |

**The rule that saves more runs than any other:** a lost miner with a small haul that extracts beats a lost miner with a full bag who dies — the whole game is banked minerals, and the bank is only at the ship.

## Navigation Checklist

- [ ] Depth budget set before the descent
- [ ] Landmark habit active (note notable rooms)
- [ ] Hazard rooms mapped as optional
- [ ] Defensive gadget carried for the return
- [ ] Lost protocol memorized — retreat is a plan, not a panic
