# Gadgets Guide — Grenades, Shields & Drones in MineGeon: Renegades

Gadgets are one of the three pillars of every build in MineGeon: Renegades — alongside weapons and ultimate abilities. The full game features **over 150 unique gadgets**, from grenades and energy shields to combat drones and beyond. If you want to survive the depths of PAIMO, mastering gadgets matters as much as mastering your aim.

This guide covers what is confirmed about the gadget system, how to think about gadget builds for each Renegade, and what is still unconfirmed at launch.

## What We Know About Gadgets (Confirmed)

The developer's materials confirm the essentials of the gadget system:

- **150+ unique gadgets** exist in the full game
- **Gadgets unlock through leveling** — as you level up during runs, more gadgets become available
- **Gadget families include grenades, energy shields, and combat drones** — these are the named examples, and the catalog goes well beyond them
- **Gadgets combine with weapons and ultimates into synergistic builds** — the core build philosophy of the game is the combination of all three
- **Some gadgets are offensive, some defensive** — "each can mean the difference between life and death" per the studio's own description
- **Gadgets are a per-run resource** — like weapons, you equip and use what a run gives you

> **Accuracy note:** specific gadget names, stats, cooldowns, and unlock levels were not published by early August 2026. Guides listing named gadgets with damage numbers are inventing them. This guide gives you the confirmed system and the strategy layer that will hold no matter what the final catalog looks like.

## The Three Confirmed Gadget Families

### Grenades (Offensive)
The named explosive family. Grenades are area-denial tools: they break up swarms, create pressure, and open escape windows.

**Where they shine:**
- Crowded rooms — break the swarm's grouping before it surrounds you
- The escape — the most valuable grenade is the one that breaks a pursuing pack at the extraction door
- Boss fights — clearing summoned adds so the team can focus the boss

**Loadout thinking:** every run should have at least one offensive gadget. Grenades are the baseline answer.

### Energy Shields (Defensive)
The named defensive family. Shields absorb incoming damage — in a bullet-hell game where most classes die in a few hits, that's a life-saver.

**Where they shine:**
- Boss phases — survive the burst attack that ends runs
- Extraction under fire — shield the channel moment
- Deep-biome corridors — when the hazards and Scavengers mass up

**Loadout thinking:** shields are panic buttons. Carry one on deep runs; use it deliberately, not prematurely.

### Combat Drones (Autonomous)
The named autonomous family. Drones deal damage or act while you do something else — the closest thing to a teammate in solo play.

**Where they shine:**
- Mining — a drone covers you while you dig in a contested room
- Solo play — the solo player's force multiplier
- Co-op — a drone holding a flank while the team focuses a room

**Loadout thinking:** drones reward planning. Place them with intent — guarding a corridor, covering a vein, holding the rear during extraction.

## How Gadgets Fit the Build System

The developer describes builds as the combination of **gadgets + weapons + ultimates**. The way to think about it:

| Build Component | Its Job | Gadget's Role |
|-----------------|---------|---------------|
| Weapon | Consistent damage in safe windows | Cover the unsafe windows |
| Gadgets | Answer every situation | The flexible layer — offense, defense, autonomy |
| Ultimate | The big moment | Set it up with gadgets, don't waste it |

A loadout isn't "one good weapon" — it's a weapon for damage, an ultimate for the boss, and gadgets for everything between. The best builds are the ones where the three layers cover each other's gaps.

## Gadget Strategy by Class

### Marshall (Tank) — Gadget Usage
- **Core need:** survivability and crowd breaking
- **Priority gadgets:** energy shields (you're the frontline — you get hit), grenades (break swarms that threaten your team)
- **Drones:** good for holding a position while you anchor
- **Mindset:** your gadgets buy your team time. The shield you save for the boss phase is the one that wins the fight

### Trevor (Gunslinger) — Gadget Usage
- **Core need:** keeping distance and surviving mistakes
- **Priority gadgets:** energy shields (your low survivability is the cost of damage), grenades for pressure relief
- **Drones:** excellent — autonomous damage while you reposition
- **Mindset:** every gadget is a second life. Trevor dies in seconds without them; spend them before the situation is lost

### Ari (Engineer) — Gadget Usage
- **Core need:** flexibility — match the run
- **Priority gadgets:** all three families; the balanced class gets the most from a mixed bag
- **Drones:** the natural Ari pick — control and autonomy in one
- **Mindset:** Ari rewards game knowledge. The better you know what each gadget does, the more the balanced class outperforms expectations

## Gadget Build Archetypes

Until the full catalog is documented, build around these confirmed archetypes:

### 1. The Self-Sufficient Miner (Solo)
- Grenade + drone + shield
- The miner's kit: drone mines with you, grenade breaks pressure, shield saves the mistake
- Best for: farming runs, solo play, first biome progression

### 2. The Breach Team (Co-op)
- Roles split across the team: one grenadier, one shield-bearer, one drone operator
- The team kit covers everything: offense, defense, autonomy
- Best for: deep runs, boss pushes

### 3. The Glass Cannon (Damage-Focused)
- Grenades and offensive gadgets, minimal defense
- Maximum pressure, minimum safety
- Best for: skilled Trevor players, speed farming

### 4. The Unbreakable (Defense-Focused)
- Shields and defensive gadgets
- Survive everything, kill slowly
- Best for: Marshall mains, learning new biomes

## Gadget Progression

Gadgets unlock through **leveling during runs** — the deeper and longer your run, the more of the catalog you see. Progression advice:

1. **Level with intent.** Longer runs expose more of the catalog — extraction discipline is also gadget progression
2. **Experiment early.** The demo's gadget subset is a preview — try every family before committing to a style
3. **Learn the families, not just the items.** With 150+ gadgets, the catalog will rotate through runs — understanding what a family does beats memorizing one item
4. **Watch the community catalog.** Players are documenting the real gadget list post-launch; the official Discord is the source to watch

## Common Gadget Mistakes

1. **Hoarding.** "Saving the grenade for later" is how you die with a full inventory of unused gadgets — they're per-run resources, spend them
2. **No defense.** An all-offense loadout in a bullet-hell game is a dead loadout
3. **No offense.** All-defense loadouts can't clear rooms — balance the families
4. **Ignoring drones.** The most underrated family early on; they literally fight for you
5. **Saving the ultimate forever.** Ultimates are the third pillar — boss arenas are their home, not storage

## Gadget FAQ

### Are gadgets permanent unlocks?
Unlocks via leveling persist in the sense that leveled characters can access more of the catalog, but the game's run structure means you equip gadgets per-run. Permanent power comes from ship and Renegade attribute upgrades.

### Does every class use the same gadgets?
The catalog is shared, but role fit differs — Marshall gets more from shields, Trevor from survival tools, Ari from the mixed bag.

### Are there "trap" gadgets?
The game has no trap system. Area-denial is done with grenades and similar gadgets — see our [Area Control Guide](/guides/trap-guide) for the full picture.

### What are ultimates?
Each Renegade has its own ultimate ability — a signature power that changes combat approach. Ultimates are the third pillar of builds alongside weapons and gadgets.

## The Gadget Mindset

Think of gadgets as your answers to the question the game keeps asking: **"what happens when the situation goes wrong?"** A weapon is the plan; gadgets are the contingency. The players who extract with full hauls are the ones with a grenade left for the door, a shield left for the boss, and a drone covering their backs.
