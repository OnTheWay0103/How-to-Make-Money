## Enemy Overview

PAIMO is not empty. Every descent throws hostile life at you — swarms of creatures, hostile factions, and towering bosses. The confirmed cast is thin but clear:

- **The Scavengers** — a hostile faction determined to stop your descent
- **Swarms of creatures** — described as everything "from minor creatures to towering bosses"
- **Biome bosses** — an ancient entity at the end of every biome

> **Accuracy note:** individual enemy names, stats, and attack patterns had not been published by early August 2026. Invented bestiary entries (Crawlers, Spitters, Golems, etc.) appear in some fan guides but have no official basis. This guide sticks to what is confirmed and gives combat fundamentals that apply to any enemy you meet.

## The Scavengers (Confirmed)

The Scavengers are the game's named hostile faction. From the official story: after your crew's failed heist, you crash-land near PAIMO and descend into its depths seeking help — and the Scavengers are determined to stop you. Think of them as the game's rival mercenary force:

- They actively oppose your descent, biome by biome
- They are intelligent opposition rather than simple wildlife — expect coordinated pressure
- They are part of PAIMO's conflict, alongside the planet's own creatures

Community documentation of Scavenger enemy types (riflemen, heavies, etc.) was still in progress as of early August 2026.

## The Creatures of PAIMO

The confirmed enemy roster beyond the Scavengers is described generically: "swarms of enemies" and creatures ranging "from minor creatures to towering bosses." What that tells us strategically:

- **Minor creatures** — the bulk of encounters; swarms that test your movement and positioning
- **Mid-tier creatures** — likely elite-style enemies guarding richer mineral veins
- **Towering bosses** — the biome-end ancient entities covered in our [Boss Guide](/guides/boss-guide)

Each biome's enemies are part of that biome's identity, alongside its hazards and lore — but specific biomes' creature rosters are unconfirmed.

## Bullet-Hell Combat Fundamentals

MineGeon: Renegades is a **bullet-hell twin-stick shooter** (per the game's Steam tags). That one fact drives all combat advice:

### 1. Movement is your main defense
In a bullet-hell game, damage avoidance comes from positioning, not stats. Keep moving, keep a direction in mind, and never corner yourself.

### 2. Learn patterns, then act
Enemies telegraph their attacks. The first time you meet an enemy type, watch it before engaging. Once you know the pattern, you know the damage windows.

### 3. Prioritize threats
In mixed fights, the order matters more than the kill count:
- Anything attacking your mining spot (you're vulnerable while mining)
- Ranged/summoning enemies that force you out of position
- Then the swarm

### 4. Use the geometry
Walls block projectiles (depending on the weapon), chokepoints funnel swarms, and mined terrain creates cover. The minegeon is a weapon.

### 5. Kite when outnumbered
Retreating while shooting (kiting) beats fighting in a surrounded position every time. Run past the swarm, turn, shoot, repeat.

### 6. Know when to run
Nothing in the game requires you to fight everything. Elites guarding modest ore are not worth a death — and death costs your whole haul.

## Co-op Combat Coordination

- **Designate roles mid-fight:** one draws attention (Marshall), one deals damage (Trevor), one covers (Ari).
- **Call out new enemy types.** The first sighting of a new pattern is when someone dies — announce it.
- **Protect the miner.** One Renegade mining with a teammate covering is the most efficient way to play.
- **Don't all fight one enemy.** Focus fire on priority targets, but keep someone watching the flanks.

## The Scavenger Mindset

Because the Scavengers are a thinking faction rather than simple wildlife, expect them to exploit your habits: ambushes near mineral deposits, pressure during extraction, and stronger resistance as you descend. Treat deep biomes as contested territory — the Scavengers own the depths, and you're taking them.
