# Big Walk Puzzles Guide — Every Puzzle Type Explained

> Launch Version / Updated: August 4, 2026

Puzzles are the core of Big Walk. They come in many shapes, but they all share one design principle: **a puzzle is a conversation**. The game separates players, hides information, and makes you describe what you see — then rewards you with gourds, unlocks, and new areas.

This guide explains the puzzle types confirmed in previews and the launch build, plus the communication techniques that solve them.

---

## The Puzzle Philosophy

Big Walk gives you almost no instructions. There are no tutorials holding your hand, no waypoints, and no checklist. Every puzzle is a small investigation:

1. **What is this thing asking for?**
2. **Who can see what I cannot?**
3. **How do I tell them?**

Previews described the game as a "trust fall" — every puzzle requires you to trust that your friend actually sees what they say they see. That is the mechanic.

## Confirmed Puzzle Types

### 1. Symbol Relay Puzzles

One player sees symbols (on a wall, a panel, a structure) that the rest of the group cannot. The group must reproduce or match them — often from across a barrier, a wall, or a locked door.

**How to solve:**
- Name the symbols consistently. "Sun, fish, three stripes" beats "the one at the top."
- Use a **laser pointer** to trace the symbol in the air or on the target.
- Use the **whiteboard** for longer sequences — write the order down.
- Match order, not just appearance: confirm "first, second, third" before pressing anything.

### 2. Player Tower Puzzles

Buttons are too high, switches are out of reach, and the solution is **you**. Players climb on each other to reach high switches, chain platforms, or press multiple buttons at once.

**How to solve:**
- The heaviest/steadiest player is the base — the bottom of the tower does not change while the tower is up.
- Designate one player as the "climber" who presses the switch; climbing order matters.
- Coordinate "press it now" timing with the walkie-talkie when the switch is in a different room.

### 3. Ball Hauling / Relay Puzzles

Heavy balls need to be moved — across terrain, into hoops, onto pressure plates, or through gaps. Previews specifically mention relaying balls and basketball-hoop-style targets.

**How to solve:**
- Balls are heavy: line up players between source and target instead of one player carrying it the whole way.
- The last leg is the hardest — have the "accurate" player finish the throw.
- When a puzzle has multiple balls, do one at a time end-to-end instead of splitting.

### 4. Matching Tile Puzzles

Tile or panel matching where players must coordinate which tiles are pressed, and in what order — often with players on opposite sides seeing different halves.

**How to solve:**
- Call out positions with a shared reference frame: "top-left, bottom-right" — and agree on whose "left" it is.
- One player is the **caller**, everyone else presses. Never have two callers.
- Use the laser pointer for tiles that look identical.

### 5. Communication-Restricted Challenges

The purest Big Walk puzzles: one player is **separated** — behind glass, in a tunnel, on a distant ledge — and must complete an objective guided only by what the group can communicate through walls, distance, or walkie-talkie.

**How to solve:**
- Set up a single relay channel first: separated player + walkie-talkie carrier + spotter.
- Use simple yes/no calls: "up, up, left, STOP."
- Describe by sound and context when sight fails: "you should be hearing the river now."
- These puzzles are why **gestures ("Arms!!") exist** — waving through glass is a real communication channel.

### 6. Landmark & Exploration Puzzles

Some objectives are just **finding things** — a structure hidden behind a ridge, a sign that only reads from a certain angle. Big Walk's lack of a map turns every "find X" into a navigation puzzle.

**How to solve:**
- Post a **spotter with binoculars** on high ground and describe the route in stages: "toward the red tower, then right at the fork."
- Split the group and converge — the first to spot it announces via walkie-talkie.
- Follow the structures: the island's bright, colorful buildings are landmarks by design.

## Puzzle Rewards: Gourds & Unlocks

Solving puzzles earns **gourd-like red objects** (called "babies"/"nubbins" in previews) which function as keys:

- **Unlock towers** that open new regions.
- **Unlock fast travel** — ski lifts and the underground tunnel network.
- Feed certain **hidden questlines** and secrets (see the [Hidden Secrets Guide](/guides/hidden-secrets-guide)).

Gourds are carried and delivered to their destinations — the delivery itself can be a mini-puzzle of "how do we get this thing up there?"

## Co-op Techniques That Solve Everything

1. **One caller, one action.** For any multi-step puzzle, one player directs and everyone else acts. Two people giving orders is the #1 way to fail.
2. **Describe, then confirm, then act.** "I see three buttons — red, blue, green. Confirm green is on the right. Press green." The confirmation step is what makes Big Walk satisfying.
3. **Use the terrain.** High ground solves vision problems; tunnels solve distance problems (voices echo and carry).
4. **Split smart.** Two players on opposite ends of a relay puzzle solve it twice as fast — but only if both have walkie-talkies.
5. **Name things.** The group's shared vocabulary ("the sun tower," "the fish panel") is its memory. Naming landmarks and symbols makes every later puzzle easier.
6. **Take breaks.** Big Walk is pressure-free on purpose. If the group is frustrated, sit down and watch the sunset. The puzzle will still be there.

## When You Are Stuck

Big Walk never fails you for being stuck. There are no timers and no consequences — so when a puzzle stalls:

- **Change who is looking.** The person who has stared at a symbol panel for ten minutes is the last person who will see the answer. Swap spotters.
- **Walk away and come back.** Puzzles are placed in the world, not instanced — a fresh approach from a different angle often reveals the trick.
- **Split and scout.** Another puzzle elsewhere may reward you with the tool or gourd you actually need.
