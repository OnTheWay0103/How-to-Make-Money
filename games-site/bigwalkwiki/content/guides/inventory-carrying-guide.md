# Big Walk Carrying & Inventory Guide — Backpacks, Bags & Item Slots

> Updated: August 8, 2026

Big Walk has **no inventory menu**. There is no grid, no slots screen, no backpack UI — what you carry is what your character visibly holds. By default **each player can carry one item at a time**, and everything else about carrying is about finding the equipment and the spots that let you hold more.

**Honesty note:** the carrying system was confirmed in first-week coverage (The Gamer, TechWiser, GamesHorizon and others all documented it August 5–6). Item-slot totals are community-measured; exact counts of bag buildings are still being verified.

---

## The Core Rule: One Item Per Player

- Every player starts able to carry **one item at a time** — a tool, a gourd, a key, a ball, anything.
- Items are **physical**: they sit in your hands or on your character, and your friends can see exactly what you are carrying.
- Swapping what you hold requires **dropping, handing off, or throwing** — there is no quick-equip menu.
- This is the game's pressure valve: splitting up means allocating gear, because a 12-player group does not have 12 free hands (see the [Multiplayer & Co-op Guide](/guides/multiplayer-coop-guide)).

**Practical result:** the carrying limit is a coordination puzzle, not an inconvenience. The game hands you exactly the tools to fix it.

---

## The Two Carrying Upgrades

### Backpack — +1 Slot for Anything

- Adds a **full extra item slot** that accepts **any item of any size or shape**.
- Found inside the **nine giant blue basket-like buildings** scattered around the map (community-reported count, awaiting confirmation).
- Carries any item — tools, keys, gourds, balls — without size restrictions.

### Waist Bag / Hip Bag / Holster / Satchel — +1 Slot for Small Items

- Adds a **second slot, for smaller items only** — radios, walkie-talkies, and similarly small gear.
- Found in the **blue circular buildings** around the map.
- Cannot hold large items (gourds, balls, tower keys).

**Combined effect:** a fully equipped player carries **three items** — one in hand, one in the backpack, one in the waist bag. Most groups find that two equipped players per sub-team is plenty.

---

## How to Equip a Bag

The one catch: **equipping and unequipping bags requires a teammate's help.** You cannot strap a backpack onto yourself — the friend who finds the building has to help you put it on (and take it off).

**Why this matters:**

- Bags are **removable** — a friend can take the backpack off you and wear it themselves.
- Pass bags around based on the current task: the gourd-carrier needs the backpack this minute; the walkie-talkie relay needs it next.
- Unequipping is free and instant (with help) — there is no reason to hoard a bag you are not using.

---

## The Lost and Found Table

When a session ends, items you were holding appear on the **Lost and Found table in the gym** — the building where sessions start.

- **Check it before every session** — anything your group was carrying last time (tools, keys, progress items) is waiting there.
- It is the game's way of making item loss painless: throw the walkie-talkie in the ocean and it will be back on the table next session.
- No item is permanently lost in Big Walk — the Lost and Found is the safety net under the whole carrying system.

---

## The Item Scanner — Finding Things to Carry

The **item scanner** marks nearby interactable items with a **yellow X** overlay.

- Found in the **starter area** and inside **control panel towers** (community-reported).
- Great for locating tools and pickup items you have walked past — and for spotting items dropped by the ocean-throwing friend.
- One scanner per sub-team makes "where did we leave the key" problems disappear.

## Group Carrying Tips

1. **Equip bags early.** The blue buildings are worth a detour in the first session — two equipped players cover most group needs.
2. **One gourd-carrier per unlock.** When delivering gourds, put the backpack on the delivery player and keep everyone else light.
3. **Split small items, not big ones.** Waist bags handle the communication kit (walkie-talkies, radios); keep hand slots free for puzzle items.
4. **Use the Lost and Found as a staging table.** Drop items there between puzzles instead of carrying them across the island.
5. **Scanner beats memory.** When a puzzle needs a specific tool, scan — the yellow X beats "I think we left it near the tower."
