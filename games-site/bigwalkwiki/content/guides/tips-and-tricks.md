# Big Walk Tips & Tricks — Advanced Co-op Strategies

> Launch Version / Updated: August 4, 2026

Big Walk is easy to play and deep to coordinate. These are the advanced techniques the community is developing at launch — from communication discipline to gourd logistics. Master these and your group will cover the island twice as fast, with twice the laughs.

---

## Communication Systems That Scale

### 1. Build a group vocabulary
Naming things is the single highest-value habit in Big Walk. Your group's shared language ("the sun tower," "fish panel," "the big arch") is its memory. Names persist across sessions — a returning group with established names walks faster than any random group.

### 2. Role cards for every session
Assign roles at the start of every walk — they make 2-player duos and 12-player parties equally smooth:

| Role | Kit | Job |
|------|-----|-----|
| **Spotter** | Binoculars | Reads terrain, landmarks, signs; the group's eyes |
| **Relay** | Walkie-Talkie | Organizes and repeats instructions; the group's voice |
| **Runner** | Flare + walkie | Moves between sub-teams, delivers gourds, marks locations |
| **Actors** | Whatever's free | Execute puzzle actions on the caller's command |

For 2 players: Spotter and Relay, swapping as needed. For 5+: one full kit per sub-team.

### 3. One caller per puzzle
The fastest way to fail a puzzle is two people giving orders. Designate one caller per active puzzle; everyone else executes or reports. The caller is the only person allowed to say "press it."

### 4. Gestures are a language, not an emote
Pointing, waving, and flailing ("Arms!!") are real communication — previews confirm gestures matter for puzzles, especially through glass and across distances where voice fails. When you cannot talk, gesture with intent.

## Navigation & Time-Saving

### 5. Spot from high ground, move in a line
Before entering new terrain: spotter climbs, describes, and the group walks as a line toward the chosen landmark. Zigzagging wastes the most time in Big Walk — the game is a walk, so make every step count.

### 6. Unlock fast travel early, ride it often
Ski lifts and tunnels are the island's highway system. Prioritize towers that reveal transport; a group that unlocks two lifts early covers regions in minutes that take other groups half an hour.

### 7. The split-and-converge pattern (4+ players)
- Split into sub-teams of 2-3, each with a walkie-talkie.
- Fan out toward different landmarks.
- The first team to find something **flares the location** and announces it.
- Everyone converges via landmarks.
This triples exploration speed without losing anyone.

### 8. Flare like a lighthouse
Flares mark locations from anywhere on the island. Place one at a landmark before night falls — it becomes a beacon your whole group can navigate home to. Save one per night walk for the way back.

## Gourds: Logistics & Priority

### 9. Gourds first, puzzles second
Gourd delivery unlocks towers, regions, and fast travel — which reveals more puzzles. When choosing between a random puzzle and delivering a gourd you already hold, **deliver first**; the unlock snowballs.

### 10. One gourd, one trip
If a gourd needs to travel across the island, do not assign a single carrier to a round trip. Use the **runner chain**: pass the gourd between sub-teams like a relay baton. Everyone moves forward, nobody backtracks.

### 11. Feed every machine
At launch, the golden rule of gourd handling is: **if it has a mouth, feed it.** Towers, machines, and unusual structures all accept gourds (see the [Hidden Secrets Guide](/guides/hidden-secrets-guide) for the cutter machine reports). Unusual destinations are how secrets unlock.

## Night & Weather Survival

### 12. Do discovery in daylight, business at night
Big Walk has no dangers at night — but darkness hides landmarks. Plan it: explore and spot in daylight, then use the night for cozy indoor puzzles, tower unlocks, and landmark-watching (a confirmed pastime).

### 13. Flashlight discipline
One flashlight per sub-team minimum. When the group splits at night, the flashlight beam is a visual signal: players can see each other's light across a valley before they hear the voice.

### 14. Follow the voices, not the path
When lost (and you will be), stand still and listen. Proximity chat means your group's voices are directional — you can walk *toward* your friends' chatter. This is the most underrated navigation tool in the game.

## Social Tech (Yes, It's Strategy)

### 15. The walk is the content
The biggest "trick" in Big Walk is not rushing. Groups that walk together, comment on the scenery, and stop for sunsets report enjoying the game substantially more — and solving puzzles better, because they have practiced describing the world to each other.

### 16. Let the golden head find its purpose
The golden head is unconfirmed mystery item territory. Experiment on every unusual structure — and coordinate the experiment with the group so you know what you tested. When someone figures it out, the find is bigger than the item.

### 17. Two-session strategy for big groups
With 6-12 players, split into two session groups on alternate nights if scheduling is hard — each group maps different regions and reports back. Big Walk rewards shared intelligence even across sessions.

## Common Advanced Mistakes

1. **All four players carry binoculars and nobody carries a walkie-talkie.** Tools should diversify, not duplicate.
2. **Splitting up without a signal plan.** Split is fine; split without flares and walkies is how groups lose each other for twenty minutes.
3. **Talking over the caller.** Discipline: the caller calls, everyone else confirms.
4. **Hoarding gourds at a tower "in case."** Unlock it. The island only opens when gourds move.
5. **Ignoring the night cycle.** Getting caught in the dark without a flashlight is the closest Big Walk gets to a penalty — it is just slow, but it is still slow.
