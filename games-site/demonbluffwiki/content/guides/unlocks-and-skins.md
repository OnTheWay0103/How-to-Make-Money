# Demon Bluff Unlocks and Skins: How Progression Works

Demon Bluff unlocks two things: **character cards**, which change what you can draw, and **cosmetics** — skins, card backs and decorations — which do not. Cards are collected by saving villages in Standard mode. Cosmetics come from play. There is no shop, no currency and no redeem-code system.

That is the whole economy, and its simplicity is the point. Here is what the official material confirms and what it leaves open.

## Card unlocks: 100+ collectibles, 44 roles

The Steam page advertises "100+ unique character cards to collect". The community wiki documents **44 distinct roles**: 25 Villagers, 7 Outcasts, 9 Minions and 3 Demons.

Both numbers are right, and the gap between them is the thing to understand before you start hunting a card that does not exist. The 44 roles are the characters you meet in a run. The 100+ is the collectible set built on top of them — alternate versions, skins and variants of the same underlying abilities.

So when you see a list of "all Demon Bluff characters" with 100+ entries, check whether it is counting roles or card art. Those are different questions and the answers have different uses: role lists help you deduce, card lists help you collect.

## How unlocking works

Per the community wiki, **cards can only be unlocked in Standard mode**. That single rule shapes everything about how you should play early:

- **Standard is the only progression mode.** If you are playing Endless, you are not unlocking anything, even though every character is available to you there.
- **Endless makes the whole cast playable** without unlocking, but it does not unlock it for Standard.
- **Village-by-village progress is the unit.** The store page's wording — "keep saving villages and unlock dozens of new character cards" — matches the Standard loop: clear villages, raise Ascension, expand the pool.

That creates a deliberate early-game tightness. Your first villages draw from a restricted pool, which makes each Evil read close to decisive; the pool widens as you clear more, which is exactly when the deduction gets harder.

## Cosmetics: skins, card backs, decorations

The store page names three cosmetic types: **alternate skins**, **card backs** and **decorations**, all "collected as you play".

The community wiki documents seasonal skin variants as separate pages — Halloween sets for Architect, Bard, Empress, Fortune Teller, Knight, Lilis, Lover, Medium and Baa, and Christmas sets for Alchemist, Confessor, Empress, Jester and Wretch. That is the shape of the cosmetic system: per-character themes, collected through play, releasing alongside seasonal updates.

There is no announced premium currency, no store tab and no battle pass in the store listing. The Steam categories list Achievements, Cloud, Stats and Family Sharing, and there is **no Steam Workshop** entry, so community skins are not a thing at launch either.

What the official material does not say is the drop rate, whether cosmetics are tied to specific villages, or whether any are missable. Those details usually surface in the first week of a launch, and we will not guess at them here.

## Is there a shop or premium currency?

Nothing on the store page describes one. `is_free: false` means the game itself is paid; there is no listed DLC, no in-game purchase category, and no currency mentioned in the feature list.

That is not a promise about post-launch monetisation. It is what the store page says today, and it is the only claim we will make.

## Relics are not cosmetics

Easy to confuse with collectibles: a **Relic** is a passive item that modifies a village's starting conditions — the developer's own definition — and nine of them ship in the demo with more planned for release. Grave Flame makes you lose health if you reveal the last four cards; Severed Horn makes the character in slot #1 always tell the truth.

Relics are gameplay, not decoration. They are also not something you equip permanently: in Endless a different random Relic activates each round, and in Deckbuilding a relic with a negative effect arrives every few villages. If you were hoping the collection layer was purely cosmetic, it is not — relics change the puzzle.

## Thirteen roles that exist in code but not in play

The community wiki also lists **13 "Unused Roles"** — Acrobat, Arbiter, Architect (Old), Bounty Hunter, Delusion, Marionette, Mathematician, Mutant, Saint, Sapper, Skinwalker, Spy and Useless Villager. These are names found in the build that are not part of the 44 playable roles.

Treat them as development history, not content to chase. If a future patch promotes one of them into the pool, it will appear as a new role and we will add it to the [characters list](/guides/characters-list).

## No codes

Demon Bluff has no redeem-code system. This matters because code pages are the default page type for a game wiki, and every competitor template will produce one. There is nothing to redeem: unlocks come from clearing villages and cosmetics drop from play.

If a code system ever appears, it will be announced on the game's Steam news feed, which is where the studio posts. Anything advertising "Demon Bluff codes" today is generating a page for a search term, not reporting a feature.

## Achievements and Stats

Both are listed as Steam features, which means the game ships with an achievement set and exposes global unlock percentages through Valve's public API. That gives us a verifiable progression signal from day one: once the game launches, achievement percentages tell us which unlocks are common and which are effectively abandoned by most players.

Until launch, there is no achievement data to read — the API returns nothing for an unreleased app.

## What to focus on before launch

You cannot pre-farm unlocks, so the useful preparation is understanding what you will be unlocking from. Three things are worth your time now:

1. **Learn the four role groups.** Villagers, Outcasts, Minions and Demons behave differently enough that knowing the group is half of any read. The [characters list](/guides/characters-list) has all 44.
2. **Learn the HP economy.** Ten HP per village, five per innocent execution. Unlocks change which cards you see, not how much a mistake costs.
3. **Decide whether you will play Standard or Endless first.** If you want the collection, Standard is mandatory. If you want to learn the full cast fast, Endless opens at Ascension 4, and it will not unlock anything for you.

## What we cannot verify yet

Four things about unlocks are genuinely unknown before launch, and we would rather list them than fill them in with plausible-sounding numbers:

- **Whether any unlock is missable.** Nothing on the store page or the wiki mentions time-limited or route-locked cards.
- **Whether cosmetics are random or fixed.** "Collected as you play" describes the trigger, not the distribution.
- **Whether unlock progress is account-wide across modes.** Cards unlock in Standard; whether a card unlocked in Standard then becomes available in a Deckbuilding draft is not documented.
- **Whether a day-one patch changes any of this.** Pre-launch builds and release builds differ more often than not.

Each of these turns into a real answer within days of launch, because achievement percentages and player reports arrive together. We will update this page then rather than speculate now.

## Checking your own collection

The Steam page lists **Stats**, which means the game exposes counters to your profile. Combined with the achievement set, that gives you two independent ways to see what you have collected without keeping a hand-written list.

The practical habit for the first few weeks: after each Standard village, note whether a new card appeared in your pool, and which role it belongs to. Forty-four roles is a small enough set that you will have met most of them well before the 100+ collectible count fills in — which is the clearest sign that the collection is cosmetic variants layered on a modest cast, not a hundred distinct puzzles.

## Frequently asked

**How many cards are in Demon Bluff?**
The store page says 100+ collectibles; the community wiki documents 44 distinct roles. The rest are variants and alternate skins of those roles.

**How do you unlock characters in Demon Bluff?**
By saving villages in Standard mode. Cards cannot be unlocked in Endless or Deckbuilding.

**Are there Demon Bluff codes?**
No. There is no code redemption system.

**Is there a shop?**
No shop or premium currency is described on the store page. There is no listed DLC.

**Do skins affect gameplay?**
No. Skins, card backs and decorations are cosmetic; the ability belongs to the underlying role.

**Can I mod in new characters?**
The store page lists no Steam Workshop support, and the game is pre-launch. `demon bluff modded characters` is a search people run, not a feature that exists.
