# Demon Bluff Characters: All 44 Roles Explained

Demon Bluff's cast is split into four groups: 25 Villagers, 7 Outcasts, 9 Minions and 3 Demons. Villagers and Outcasts are Good and tell you true things — unless something has corrupted or disguised them. Minions and Demons are Evil and lie every time. Here is what each card's ability actually reports.

The counts and ability wording come from the community wiki's role pages, read on 29 October 2026. The game is still pre-launch, so treat any specific number as version-dependent.

## How the four groups differ

**Alignments and groups are not the same thing.** A card's group decides what it is; its alignment decides which side it is on. Villagers are always Good, Demons and Minions are always Evil, and Outcasts are the wrinkle — they are Good cards that do not look Good.

That matters because most information abilities ask about **Evil**, not about **group**. A Druid finds Outcasts, a Gemcrafter finds Good characters, and a Hunter measures distance to Evil. Knowing which one a claim came from tells you what the claim can and cannot mean.

Four keywords do most of the work on this page, so define them once:

- **Lie** — the character's information is false. If it is also Disguised, its disguised ability will not work.
- **Disguise** — the card pretends to be a different role. Its real role only shows when it is Executed.
- **Corruption** — the character lies and its ability fails, but it keeps its alignment. A Corrupted Villager is still Good.
- **Silenced** — the character says "..." instead of its information.

## Villagers (25)

Every Villager is Good and every Villager tells the truth. Their abilities are all information-gathering, and each one points at a different slice of the board.

| Role | Ability (wiki wording) | What it is really good for |
|---|---|---|
| Alchemist | Villagers within Range 2 are cured from Corruption; learn how many Corruptions were around me; cannot be Corrupted | The only cure. Also a passive Corruption counter |
| Architect | Learn which side of the circle is more Evil (or "Equal") | Splits the board in half; strong opener |
| Baker | On Reveal: 1 random unrevealed Good Villager becomes a Baker; learn which Villager I was | Turns a good card into a detective, but the new Baker may not be your next reveal |
| Bard | Learn how far I am from the closest Corrupted character | Distance probe aimed at Corruption, not Evil |
| Bishop | Learn up to 3 characters: 1 Villager, 1 Outcast and 1 Evil if possible | Guarantees a mixed read when all three exist |
| Confessor | If I am Evil or Corrupted: "I am dizzy" | A self-check card; a clean answer is weak evidence |
| Dreamer | Pick 2 characters: learn a role among them and one that is not | One true, one false by construction — the classic half-lie |
| Druid | Pick 3 characters: learn 1 random Outcast among them, if any | Narrow; only speaks to Outcasts |
| Empress | Learn 3 characters, only 1 is Evil | Three-card slot with a hard guarantee |
| Enlightened | Learn whether the closest Evil is clockwise or counter-clockwise (or "Equidistant") | Direction, not distance |
| Fortune Teller | Pick 2 characters: learn if any of them is Evil | A yes/no that collapses two cards into one bit |
| Gemcrafter | Learn 1 Good character | A safe name, no Evil read |
| Hunter | Learn how far the nearest Evil is from me | Pure distance |
| Investigator | Pick 2 characters: learn the most Suspicious type among them | Returns a group, not a card |
| Jester | Pick 3 characters: learn how many of them are Evil | Count only, not identity |
| Judge | Pick 1 character: learn if they are Lying | Direct lie detector — the strongest single-card read |
| Knight | I cannot die | Executing a corrupted Knight costs you extra HP |
| Knitter | Learn how many Evils are adjacent to each other | Board-shape read |
| Lover | Learn how many Evil characters I am adjacent to | Local count |
| Medium | Learn a Good character and its role | One fully identified Good card |
| Oracle | Learn that 1 out of 2 characters is a specific Minion role | Pins a Minion type onto a pair |
| Poet | Learn random info | Unreliable by design |
| Scout | Learn how far a specific Evil is to another closest Evil | Evil-to-Evil distance |
| Slayer | Pick 1 character: if Evil, Execute it | A kill, not a read. Wrong picks cost HP |
| Witness | Learn a character that was affected by an Evil ability | Catches Puppets, Corruption and Chancellor conversions |

Two of these deserve a warning. The **Baker** does not hand its ability to whoever you reveal next: the game picks a random unrevealed Good Villager at the moment the Baker is revealed, so a good card can appear several reveals later. And the **Confessor** only produces information when something has gone wrong with it, which makes it a card you learn to distrust rather than trust.

## Outcasts (7)

Outcasts are Good cards that other abilities can mistake for Evil. That is the whole point of the group — they are noise generators that make Evil reads ambiguous.

| Role | Ability | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Bombardier | Lose if you Execute me | A trap. Executing it is a loss |
| Doppelganger | Game start: I Disguise as a Good Villager currently in play | Duplicates a real card, so two identical claims can both be honest-looking |
| Drunk | I Disguise as a random not-in-play Villager, I am Corrupted and I Lie, and I cannot be cured | The most misleading card in the game. Executing it costs only 2 HP instead of 5 |
| Lycanthrope | At night, if unrevealed: become an Evil Werewolf, lie, and disguise as a not-in-play Villager | A Good card that turns Evil mid-run |
| Plague Doctor | Game start: 1 Good Villager is Corrupted; pick 1 character: if it is corrupted, learn an Evil character | Corruption plus a conditional read |
| Rambler | Tells you something interesting; adjacent truthful characters tell it to shut up instead of sharing their info | Silences its neighbours — which is itself information |
| Wretch | I Register as a random Evil Minion to other characters | Every Evil-detection ability can flag it |

The **Wretch** is the single most important card to understand before you trust any Evil read. It registers as a Minion to every other card, so an Investigator, Hunter or Fortune Teller pointing at it is telling the truth — and the truth is useless.

## Minions (9)

Every Minion lies and disguises. Their abilities are mostly about corrupting or converting Villagers before you get to them.

| Role | Ability | Effect on your board |
|---|---|---|
| Chancellor | Game start: 1 Villager becomes an Outcast role, and I sit next to it | Creates a fake Outcast next to itself |
| Minion | I Lie and Disguise | No special effect — pure noise |
| Poisoner | Game start: one adjacent Villager is Corrupted | Your honest neighbour now lies |
| Puppet | I Disguise as a Villager, I do not Lie | **The one Minion whose claims are true** |
| Puppeteer | Game start: a random adjacent Villager becomes a Puppet (Evil, cannot lie) | Turns a Villager against you without corrupting it |
| Shaman | Game start: there are 2 of the same Villager role in the village | Duplicate roles in the deck |
| Twin Minion | I Lie and Disguise | No special effect |
| Werewolf | At night: I deal 1 damage to you; I lie and disguise as a not-in-play Villager | Night damage |
| Witch | You cannot reveal the last card | Cuts your information short |

The **Puppet** breaks the rule everyone learns first. A truthful claim is not evidence of a Good card, because a Puppet is Evil and does not lie. The wiki is explicit that Confessor and Puppet cannot lie even when disguised.

## Demons (3)

Three Demons, each with a start-of-game or night effect. Killing all of them is the win condition.

| Role | Ability | What it does to the run |
|---|---|---|
| Baa | Hide 1 Outcast in the deck; lie and disguise | Adds a fake Outcast for you to trip over |
| Lilis | At night: kill a random unrevealed character and deal 2 damage to you | The night-cycle demon. Ending a night with it alive costs 2 HP |
| Pooka | Game start: villages adjacent to me are Corrupted | Corruption spread, plus lie and disguise |

**Suspicion order** is Demon > Minion > Outcast > Villager. Any ability that reports "most Suspicious" is walking down that list, which is why an Investigator answer of "Demon" on a pair is so much stronger than "Villager".

## Which cards lie

Ten of the 44 are guaranteed liars whenever they speak — every Demon, every Minion except the Puppet, and the Drunk. The full list of cards that produce false information:

- **All 3 Demons** (Baa, Lilis, Pooka) — lie and disguise.
- **7 of the 9 Minions** — Minion, Twin Minion, Chancellor, Poisoner, Puppeteer, Shaman, Werewolf and Witch all lie. Only the **Puppet** tells the truth.
- **Drunk** — Good alignment, but Corrupted, so it lies and its disguised ability fails.
- **Lycanthrope** — honest by day, lies as a Werewolf at night.
- **Any Corrupted Villager** — Poisoner, Pooka and Plague Doctor can cause this.

Everything else tells the truth. That is the whole deduction: count the liars the board allows, then work out which claims fit.

## How to use this list in a run

Read claims by what they can prove, not by how confident they sound. A Judge answer ("is this card lying?") is worth more than any distance read, because it points at a single card. A Hunter answer gives you a number that only becomes decisive once you have a second distance claim to intersect it with.

Two habits pay off immediately:

1. **Bank every claim, including the useless ones.** Distance and direction reads are worthless alone and decisive in pairs.
2. **Track Corruption separately from Evil.** A corrupted Villager will lie to you while being on your side, and executing it still costs the full 5 HP.

## Frequently asked

**How many characters does Demon Bluff have?**
44 are playable: 25 Villagers, 7 Outcasts, 9 Minions and 3 Demons. The Steam page advertises "100+ unique character cards to collect", which counts card variants and skins rather than distinct roles. The wiki also lists 13 "unused roles" that exist in the build but are not playable — roughly a third of what looks like a cast listing.

**Which Demon Bluff character lies the most?**
All Minions except the Puppet, and all three Demons, lie every time. The Drunk is the most misleading Good card because it is Corrupted and cannot be cured.

**Can a Villager lie?**
Only if it has been Corrupted by a Poisoner, Pooka or Plague Doctor. Corruption keeps the alignment but makes the ability fail and the information false.

**What is the Drunk?**
An Outcast that disguises as a random Villager not in play, is permanently Corrupted and always lies. Executing it costs 2 HP instead of the usual 5, which is the cheapest way to confirm one.
