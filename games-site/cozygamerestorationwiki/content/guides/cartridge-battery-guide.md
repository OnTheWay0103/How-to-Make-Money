# Cozy Game Restoration Cartridge Battery Guide — Saves, Contacts & Read Failures

This is the guide where honesty matters most. Cozy Game Restoration is a game about **fictional** retro cartridges, and the version shown in the demo is a **cosmetic cleaning and restoration** game. It does not, as far as official material and hands-on coverage show, simulate save batteries, soldering, multimeter diagnostics, or board-level repair.

So this page is split into two clearly labeled parts:

1. **In the game** — what Cozy Game Restoration actually asks you to do.
2. **Real-world context** — genuine retro cartridge knowledge, included because players often search for it alongside the game.

Do not mix the two. A real CR2032 battery swap is not a confirmed game mechanic.

## Part 1: In the Game

### What the game confirms

The cartridges in Cozy Game Restoration are fictional. The game's restoration loop is about condition and appearance: removing dust, grime, tape, stickers, ink, scratches and adhesive residue, then deciding whether to keep or sell the cartridge. It has no timers and no fail states.

The full version is described as having 108 fictional titles across six fictional platforms, each in PAL and NTSC regional variants, plus rarity and shell variation. The appeal is the tactile cleaning loop and the collection, not hardware troubleshooting.

### What the game does not confirm

Based on the demo and official material, the following are **not** confirmed systems:

- Save-battery replacement
- Battery corrosion treatment
- Soldering or board-level repair
- Contact-pin bending or replacement
- Multimeter diagnostics
- A "cartridge won't read" failure state
- Save-file loss or corruption

If you see a guide claiming that Cozy Game Restoration has a battery-replacement minigame, treat it as unverified until Parasol Corp shows it or the full game ships with it.

### What you can do in the game

The closest thing to "contact care" is careful cleaning around the cartridge's lower edge and seams:

- Use a dry cotton swab or a lightly damp one.
- Do not flood the cartridge with solvent.
- Do not dig into the contact area with tweezers.
- Use the UV torch to check for residue you missed.
- Clean your tools before the final pass so you do not smear grime back onto the edge.

That is cosmetic care, not electrical repair. It will not change whether a fictional cartridge "reads" because reading is not a modeled failure state in the demo.

### Why the distinction matters

Cozy games often attract guides that import real-world hobby knowledge and present it as game mechanics. That is how players end up looking for a battery slot that does not exist. The safe rule is: if the game does not show the tool or the interface, it is not a confirmed mechanic.

## Part 2: Real-World Retro Cartridge Context

The rest of this page is about **real** retro cartridges. It is general hardware knowledge, not a description of Cozy Game Restoration.

### How save batteries work

Many cartridge-based games from the 1980s through the early 2000s used a small coin-cell battery — commonly a CR2032 on Game Boy-style cartridges — to keep SRAM save data alive when the console was off. The battery powers the save chip, not the game itself. When the battery dies, the cartridge still boots; it simply cannot hold a save.

Common symptoms of a dead or dying save battery:

- The game starts with no save file.
- Saves disappear after powering off.
- The game reports that the save file is corrupted.
- A save works for a short time and then vanishes.

Battery replacement is a real-world repair. It usually involves opening the shell, desoldering the old cell or removing it from a holder, and installing a new cell. It requires soldering equipment and some practice. It is not something the game simulates.

### Cleaning cartridge contacts

Real cartridge contacts are metal edge connectors. They oxidize, collect dust, and sometimes pick up residue from the console slot. A common safe approach among hobbyists is:

- Isolate the cartridge and remove it from the console.
- Use a cotton swab lightly dampened with high-percentage isopropyl alcohol.
- Wipe along the contacts, not across them.
- Let the cartridge dry completely before inserting it.
- Avoid abrasive erasers, metal tools, and household cleaners that can remove the plating.

This is real-world maintenance. It is not a confirmed in-game action beyond ordinary cleaning around the cartridge edge.

### "The cartridge won't read"

In real hardware, a cartridge that will not boot can have many causes: dirty contacts, a dirty console slot, a cracked solder joint, a damaged board, or a failed chip. The standard first step is cleaning the contacts and testing another cartridge to isolate whether the problem is the game or the console.

In Cozy Game Restoration, no equivalent diagnostic tree has been confirmed. The game's cartridges are fictional and the demo's challenge is cleaning, not troubleshooting.

### What not to do with real cartridges

- Do not blow into the cartridge or console slot. Moisture accelerates corrosion.
- Do not lick contacts.
- Do not use metal tools to scrape pins.
- Do not store cartridges loose in damp or hot places.
- Do not assume a dead save battery means the game is broken.
