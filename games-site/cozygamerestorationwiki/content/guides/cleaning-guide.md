# Cozy Game Restoration Cleaning Guide — Tools & Cleaning Order

Cleaning is the heart of Cozy Game Restoration. The game gives you a dirty cartridge, a small workbench, and a set of tools, then asks you to decide what to use and in what order. There is no timer and no fail state, which means the only thing that separates a satisfying restoration from a frustrating one is method.

This guide covers the tools that have been confirmed in official material and hands-on demo coverage, then gives a cleaning order you can use on every cartridge.

## The Confirmed Tools

Hands-on demo coverage has identified the following tools. This list reflects what has been shown publicly before launch — check the workbench after release for anything added.

| Tool | Used for | Notes |
|------|----------|-------|
| Tweezers | Peeling tape and stickers | Presented as a physical pull; slow, small movements work better than yanking |
| Cotton swab | Loose dust, glue residue, detail work | The most useful all-round tool; gets visibly dirty quickly |
| Toothbrush | Stubborn grime in textured areas | Bristles can hide dirt, so check and clean it often |
| Solvent | Ink, adhesive residue, stubborn marks | Use sparingly; test a small area first |
| Polish | Light scratches and scuffs | Cosmetic improvement, not a full repair |
| UV torch | Revealing missed dirt and untreated defects | A final-check tool, not a cleaning tool |
| Hand tool | Moving and rotating the cartridge | Helps you reach angles without fighting the camera |

The full item list may expand at release; the table above is what has been publicly shown.

### Tools get dirty

This is the most important confirmed detail on the page. In the demo, tools accumulate dirt as you use them, and a dirty tool can smudge grime back onto the cartridge. A dirty cotton swab is easy to see; a dirty toothbrush is not, because the bristles point down.

Practical routine:

1. Clean or swap the swab after adhesive, ink, or heavy grime.
2. Rinse or clean the toothbrush before moving from a dirty edge to a clean face.
3. Do not use a dirty tool for a final pass.
4. Check the UV torch after the final pass, then clean the tool you used to fix what it reveals.

## The Cleaning Order

Use this order on a typical cartridge. It moves from "things that trap dirt" to "things that are part of the surface."

### 1. Inspect and plan

Rotate the cartridge under normal light. Identify tape, stickers, ink, grime, scratches, cracks, and discoloration. Then hit it with the UV torch. The UV pass tells you where the real dirt is, which prevents you from polishing a clean area while missing a patch of grime.

### 2. Remove tape and parcel residue

Tape is the first thing to remove because it covers the surface and traps dust at its edges. Use the tweezers, pull slowly, and keep the angle low so you do not lift the plastic. Adhesive left behind becomes its own job.

### 3. Remove stickers and label glue

Stickers behave like tape but with more residue. Peel the sticker with tweezers, then treat the remaining glue with a cotton swab and, if needed, solvent. Do not soak the cartridge. The goal is to lift the adhesive, not to flood the shell.

Why stickers come before dust: adhesive holds dust. If you brush first, you push dust into the glue and create a muddy smear that takes longer to remove.

### 4. Clear loose dust and edge grime

Now use the cotton swab and toothbrush. Work from the outer edges and seams inward. The edges and seams are where grime collects and where a brush reaches better than a swab. Keep the pressure light. You are lifting dirt, not sanding the shell.

### 5. Treat ink and stubborn marks

Ink and pen marks are slower. Use solvent sparingly on a swab, work a small area, and check the result before continuing. If a mark is not lifting, stop and return later. Repeated heavy scrubbing is more likely to dull the finish than to remove the mark.

### 6. Polish light scratches

Polish is for light scratches and scuffs, not for deep gouges. Apply it to the affected area, work it in, and remove the residue. Check the result under normal light. If the scratch is still obvious, that is normal — polish improves appearance, it does not rebuild plastic. Our [repair guide](/guides/repair-guide) covers the damage-by-damage approach.

### 7. Final UV check

Run the UV torch over the whole cartridge. Fix any remaining specks with a clean swab. This is the step that separates a cartridge that looks clean from one that is clean, and it matters if you plan to sell.

## Surface-Specific Advice

### Flat plastic faces

Easy to clean, easy to over-clean. Use light circular motions with a clean swab, then a final pass with a clean, dry swab. Do not let solvent pool.

### Textured plastic and grip lines

The toothbrush is the right tool here. Work along the texture rather than across it. Because bristles hide dirt, clean the brush before the final pass.

### Labels and printed artwork

Treat labels as the most fragile part of the cartridge. Use a dry swab or a barely damp swab, minimal pressure, and no solvent unless you have tested it. A lifted or discolored label is much harder to improve than a scuffed shell.

### Seams and screw recesses

Grime collects here and is easy to miss. A brush and a pointed swab tip work well. Do not dig with metal tools.

### Cracks and chips

Clean around them gently. A crack is a structural feature of the cartridge, not a dirt trap you should force open. There is no confirmed shell-replacement or plastic-welding mechanic in the demo.

## Common Cleaning Mistakes

- **Starting with solvent.** It is the strongest tool and the easiest to overuse. Start with the swab.
- **Using a dirty tool for the final pass.** This is how a clean cartridge gets a gray smear.
- **Skipping the UV check.** You will miss dirt that normal light hides.
- **Chasing 100% on a cartridge you intend to sell immediately.** Reach a good standard, check the UV, and move on.
- **Assuming the order is mandatory.** The game has no fail state. The order above is efficient, not a rule the game enforces.

## A Quick Reference Routine

For a normal cartridge:

1. Inspect under normal light.
2. UV check.
3. Tweezers: tape.
4. Tweezers plus swab and solvent: stickers and glue.
5. Swab and toothbrush: dust and grime.
6. Solvent on a swab: ink.
7. Polish: light scratches.
8. Clean tools, then UV check again.

That is the whole job. The rest is patience, and the game is designed to let you take as much of it as you want.
