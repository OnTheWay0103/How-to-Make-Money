# Cozy Game Restoration Quests Guide — How Collection Quests Work

Cozy Game Restoration is not a quest-driven RPG. It is a restoration and collection game with a large set of **collection-based objectives** layered on top of the cleaning loop. The developer has described around **300 quests at launch**, rewarding decorations and cosmetics for your collection room.

That is the confirmed shape of the system. This guide explains what that means in practice, what is still unknown, and how to prepare your collection so quests complete naturally instead of forcing you to buy parcels blindly.

## The Confirmed Quest Structure

| Element | Confirmed detail |
|---------|------------------|
| Quest count | Around 300 at launch |
| Quest type | Collection-based objectives |
| Rewards | Decorations and cosmetics for the collection room |
| Content pool | 108 fictional titles, six fictional platforms, PAL/NTSC variants, multiple rarity levels |
| Failure state | None |
| Timer | None |

The important word is **collection-based**. These are not missions with a story branch, a boss, or a pass/fail check. They are objectives that ask you to own, display, or organize certain cartridges. You complete them by collecting, not by executing a sequence correctly.

## What "Collection-Based" Means

Press material and previews describe objectives along the lines of:

- Collect a set number of games from a genre (for example, puzzle games)
- Have a certain number of games from a theme on your shelf by a given in-game date
- Build a collection around a platform or a regional variant

These examples come from press coverage, not from a published quest list. Treat the exact wording as illustrative. The pattern is what matters: **the game asks you to broaden or shape your collection**, and then rewards you with something for the room.

### Why this is a good fit for the loop

The cleaning loop is relaxing but repetitive if you have no goal. Collection quests give the parcel-opening a purpose beyond "sell for more money." They also make duplicate management interesting: a duplicate you were about to sell might be the piece that completes a set.

### Why it is not a customer-order system

Some guides describe "customer orders" or "commissions" — a customer asks for a specific cartridge, you find and restore it, and you deliver it. That is a plausible cozy-game structure, but it is **not confirmed** for Cozy Game Restoration.

Hands-on coverage of the demo explicitly reports no quest or commission system in the demo build. Official material describes collection-based quests, not customer requests. Until Parasol Corp shows an order board or a named customer system, treat "customer orders" as speculation.

This matters because it changes your strategy. A commission system would reward targeted searching and deadline management. A collection system rewards breadth and patience. The confirmed design is the latter.

## How to Prepare Your Collection

You cannot see the full quest list before launch, and the demo does not expose the full progression. What you can do is build a collection that is resilient to many possible objectives.

### 1. Keep breadth

The single best preparation is to own at least one cartridge from as many titles, platforms, genres and regions as possible. A broad collection satisfies more objective types than a narrow, high-value one.

### 2. Keep regional pairs

PAL and NTSC variants are distinct cartridges. If an objective asks for a regional set, a pair is progress; if it asks for a platform set, a pair is still two entries. Keep the first copy of each region.

### 3. Do not sell your last copy

Sell duplicates. Keep the only copy of a title, the only copy of a region, and any crossover cartridge. This is the simplest rule that prevents self-inflicted collection stalls.

### 4. Hold high-rarity cartridges

High-rarity shells are display pieces and likely quest-relevant collectibles. They are also the best candidates for waiting on a good market day. Do not dump them for quick cash unless you have a duplicate.

### 5. Clean before you display or sell

A quest that checks ownership may not care about cleanliness, but the collection room is a visual reward. Cleaning every keeper is the point of the game. It also protects resale value if you later decide to sell.

### 6. Leave hard restorations for later

No timer means no reason to force a cartridge. If a sticker or ink mark is fighting you, set it aside, work on another parcel, and come back. The quest is not going anywhere.

## A Sensible Quest Rhythm

A sustainable session looks like this:

1. Open a small batch of parcels.
2. Clean each cartridge to a good standard, using the UV torch before you call it done.
3. Sort into keep, display, and sell.
4. Sell duplicates and low-rarity extras when the market price is favorable.
5. Check whether the new keeps have moved any collection objective forward.
6. Spend rewards on the collection room when you have something worth displaying.

This rhythm keeps money moving, keeps the collection growing, and avoids the two failure modes of collection games: hoarding everything and selling everything.

## What We Do Not Know Yet

As of October 24, 2026, the following are unconfirmed:

- The exact quest list and objective wording
- Whether quests are sequential, tiered, or all available at once
- Whether there are daily, weekly, or date-specific objectives in the full game
- The full reward table and which decorations come from which quests
- Whether quests can expire or be missed
- Whether there are achievements separate from quests

We will update this page after launch when the full quest structure is visible. For now, the safe assumption is the one supported by official material: collection-based objectives, no timers, no fail states, and cosmetic rewards.

## The Bottom Line

Quests in Cozy Game Restoration are a reason to collect widely and display proudly, not a checklist of customers to satisfy. Build breadth, keep regional pairs, protect your last copy of each title, and let the objectives complete as your shelves fill. If you see a guide describing named customers and delivery deadlines, check whether it cites an official source — as of this writing, that system has not been confirmed.
