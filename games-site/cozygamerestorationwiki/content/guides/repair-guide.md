# Cozy Game Restoration Repair Guide — Scratches, Shells & Discoloration

"Repair" in Cozy Game Restoration is not electronics repair. The game is about cosmetic restoration: making a worn cartridge look cared for again. The demo gives you cleaning tools and a polish, not a soldering iron or a replacement-shell system.

That distinction matters because it tells you what to expect. You can improve how a cartridge looks, and you can decide when a mark is part of its history. You cannot, as far as the demo shows, replace a shell, fix a cracked board, or rebuild a damaged label. This guide covers the damage types the game actually presents and a sensible approach to each.

## What the Game Confirms

The store page and press material describe cartridges as procedurally dirtied, with randomized condition and appearance. The listed damage and wear types include:

- Cracked shells
- Scuffed plastic
- Pen marks and ink
- Discoloration
- Stubborn residue
- Dust, grime, tape and stickers

That list mixes two different categories. Dust, grime, tape, stickers and residue are **dirt**: they can be removed. Cracks, scuffs, ink stains and discoloration are **condition**: they can be cleaned around, reduced, or accepted, but they are not the same kind of problem.

## The Repair Mindset: Clean First, Judge Second

Every cartridge should be cleaned before you decide what to do about its damage. A scuff under a layer of grime looks worse than it is. A "stain" is sometimes adhesive. A dull patch is sometimes dirt, not worn plastic.

The order:

1. Complete a normal cleaning pass — see the [cleaning guide](/guides/cleaning-guide).
2. Inspect under normal light.
3. Run the UV torch to catch anything you missed.
4. Only then decide which marks are permanent and which need polish.

If you skip this, you will polish dirt into the surface and wonder why the cartridge looks worse.

## Damage by Damage

### Light scratches and scuffs

**Tool:** polish.

**Approach:** apply polish to the affected area, work it in, and remove the residue. Check under normal light. Light scratches can become less visible; deep scratches usually remain visible at an angle.

**Expectation:** improvement, not disappearance. Polish is a cosmetic tool. It does not restore plastic that has been physically removed.

**When to stop:** if the scratch is still obvious after one or two passes, stop. More polish is not more repair.

### Deep scratches and gouges

**Tool:** none confirmed.

**Approach:** clean the area and accept the mark. A deep gouge catches light and cannot be polished flat with the tools shown in the demo. It is part of the cartridge's condition.

**Selling note:** a deep scratch lowers the visual appeal, but cleanliness still matters more than perfection. Do not ruin a good cleaning job trying to erase a mark that the game does not let you erase.

### Cracked shells

**Tool:** none confirmed for structural repair.

**Approach:** clean gently around the crack. Do not force the shell open, do not dig into the crack with a tool, and do not assume the crack can be closed. In the demo, a crack is a condition the cartridge arrives with, not a repair puzzle with a hidden solution.

**Important:** if a future build adds a shell-replacement or adhesive-repair tool, this section will be updated. Until then, treat cracks as cosmetic character and price the cartridge accordingly.

### Scuffed or dull plastic

**Tool:** polish for light scuffing; cleaning for dullness caused by grime.

**Approach:** first determine whether the dullness is dirt or wear. Clean it. If it remains dull, a light polish pass can improve the sheen. If the plastic is genuinely worn, the scuff is permanent.

### Pen marks and ink

**Tool:** solvent on a cotton swab.

**Approach:** use solvent sparingly, work a small area, and check the result. Ink that has soaked into textured plastic may leave a faint shadow even after the surface mark is gone. That shadow is discoloration, not dirt.

### Discoloration and yellowing

**Tool:** none confirmed.

**Approach:** clean the surface, then accept the color as part of the cartridge's age. The demo does not show a retrobright-style treatment, a dye, or a paint system. Do not expect a yellowed shell to become white again.

**Editorial note:** discoloration is one of the most characterful things about a vintage cartridge. A shelf of uniformly pristine shells can look less interesting than a shelf with visible history.

### Stubborn residue

**Tool:** solvent, cotton swab, toothbrush, and patience.

**Approach:** soften the residue, lift it in small amounts, and clean the tool between passes. Residue that has hardened in a seam may need several short sessions rather than one long scrub.

### Labels and printed artwork

**Tool:** dry or barely damp cotton swab.

**Approach:** minimal pressure, no solvent unless tested, and no scrubbing. Labels are the least forgiving surface on the cartridge. A lifted corner or a dissolved print cannot be restored with the confirmed tools.

## A Damage Decision Table

| Damage | Confirmed tool | Realistic outcome | Keep or sell? |
|--------|----------------|-------------------|---------------|
| Dust and grime | Swab, brush | Fully removable | Either |
| Tape and stickers | Tweezers, swab, solvent | Fully removable, residue may need patience | Either |
| Ink | Solvent | Reduced; faint shadow may remain | Either |
| Light scratches | Polish | Visibly improved | Either |
| Deep scratches | None | Permanent | Sell if you only want pristine carts |
| Cracked shell | None | Permanent | Keep if you like the history; sell if condition matters |
| Discoloration | None | Permanent | Same as above |
| Damaged label | None | Permanent | Sell if label condition is your priority |

## Should You Repair or Sell?

Once the cleaning is done, the decision is about what kind of collection you want.

**Keep the damaged cartridge if:**

- It is the only copy of that title you own.
- It is a regional variant you need.
- It is a high-rarity shell or a crossover cartridge.
- You like the visible history.

**Sell the damaged cartridge if:**

- You already have a cleaner copy.
- You are collecting pristine examples and the damage is permanent.
- You need the money for a parcel that might contain a better copy.

Because the market fluctuates daily, a damaged cartridge is not an emergency. You can hold it, clean it properly, and sell on a better day.

## What Not to Do

- **Do not polish before cleaning.** You will grind dirt into the plastic.
- **Do not use solvent on a label without testing.** It can lift print.
- **Do not force a cracked shell.** There is no confirmed structural repair.
- **Do not over-polish.** More passes do not equal more repair.
- **Do not assume damage is a fail state.** There are no fail states. A damaged cartridge is still a valid collectible, and you can return to it later.

## The Honest Summary

Cozy Game Restoration lets you make cartridges look better. It does not let you make them new. The satisfying part is the judgment call: knowing when a mark is dirt, when it is wear, and when it is simply the story of the object. Clean first, polish lightly, and keep the cartridges whose imperfections you enjoy looking at.
