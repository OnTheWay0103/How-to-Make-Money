# Cozy Game Restoration Platforms Guide — Six Fictional Platforms & PAL/NTSC

Cozy Game Restoration is built on a collection of **fictional** retro platforms. That is the first thing to understand, because it changes how you should read every "platform guide" for the game. These are not NES, SNES, Mega Drive, Game Boy, PlayStation or N64 cartridges, even if they evoke that era. They are invented platforms with invented titles, and the game's official material describes six of them.

Here is what is confirmed, what it implies for collecting, and where the limits of our knowledge are.

## The Confirmed Structure

| Element | Confirmed detail |
|---------|------------------|
| Titles | 108 distinct fictional games |
| Platforms | Six fictional platforms |
| Era inspiration | Games from the 1980s through the early 2000s |
| Regional variants | PAL and NTSC versions of each title |
| Total variants | 216 (108 titles × 2 regions) |
| Rarity | Multiple rarity levels, including rare shells |
| Shells | Different shell types and artwork variants |
| Quest structure | Collection-based quests, around 300 at launch |
| Display | A customizable collection room with shelves and decorations |

The six platform names have not been consistently published in the material we reviewed. We are not going to invent them. If a future official update names them, this page will be updated with the real list.

## What PAL and NTSC Mean in This Game

In the real world, PAL and NTSC are analog television standards. They are associated with different regions, different refresh rates, and — in the cartridge era — different packaging, languages and sometimes different game speeds. Collectors use the terms as shorthand for regional releases.

In Cozy Game Restoration, PAL and NTSC are **collectible variants** of the same fictional title. The game treats them as distinct cartridges worth finding and displaying. That is the confirmed function.

What is **not** confirmed:

- That PAL and NTSC cartridges have different in-game performance
- That one region is rarer than the other across the board
- That a cartridge is "incompatible" with a platform or region in-game
- That region affects cleaning difficulty or value in a fixed way

If you see a guide claiming "PAL carts always sell for more" or "NTSC carts clean faster," treat it as unverified. The market fluctuates daily, and rarity is attached to individual cartridges rather than a blanket regional rule as far as official material shows.

## Why Regional Pairs Matter for Collecting

Even without confirmed mechanical differences, regional pairs are useful for three reasons:

1. **Breadth.** A collection quest that asks for a platform or a genre is easier to satisfy when you own more distinct cartridges. A PAL/NTSC pair is two distinct cartridges.
2. **Display.** A shelf with both regional versions of a favorite title looks intentional. It tells a collecting story.
3. **Trade value.** Duplicates are sellable. A regional variant you already own is a natural duplicate; a regional variant you lack is a natural target.

The practical rule: **keep the first copy of each region, sell the third.**

## Building a Platform Collection

There are two reasonable approaches, and they suit different players.

### Approach A: Depth on one platform

Pick a platform you like and collect it heavily. Advantages:

- Collection quests often reward coherent sets.
- You learn the platform's visual identity and shell types.
- Your shelf looks like a curated platform collection rather than a random pile.

Disadvantages:

- Parcels are random, so you cannot reliably target a platform.
- You may sit on duplicates from other platforms that could fund missing slots.

### Approach B: Breadth across all six

Keep one good copy of every title you find, regardless of platform. Advantages:

- Maximizes the chance of satisfying a collection objective.
- Gives you something to display on every shelf.
- Makes duplicate-selling decisions simple: keep the first, sell the rest.

Disadvantages:

- The collection room can look scattered without deliberate arrangement.
- You may keep low-rarity cartridges you do not particularly like.

For most players, a hybrid works best: **breadth first, depth later.** Keep the first copy of every title and region you find. Once the collection is broad, choose a platform to complete and sell duplicates to fund it.

## Rarity and Shell Types

Rarity is a confirmed layer on top of title and region. The exact rarity ladder has been described in press coverage as running from common upward, with rare and special shells at the top. We have not seen an official published table of every tier, so we are not going to assign percentages or drop rates.

What rarity means for you:

- **High-rarity cartridges are worth displaying.** They are the centerpieces of a shelf.
- **High-rarity cartridges are worth holding for a good market day.** If prices fluctuate daily, a rare cartridge is the one you can afford to wait on.
- **Rarity does not replace cleanliness.** A rare cartridge in poor condition still sells for less than a clean one. Clean it properly before selling.

Shell types are a cosmetic variation. They change how a cartridge looks, and therefore how it fits a display. They are not a confirmed gameplay modifier.

## Parcels and Genre Mix

Parcels are mystery purchases. Hands-on coverage reports that the genre mix inside parcels can vary, and that the market price of a cartridge fluctuates daily. That means you cannot fully plan a collection around what you will open next.

The practical response:

- Buy parcels regularly rather than saving for one perfect purchase.
- Keep a wishlist of missing titles and regions in your head, but do not refuse to open a parcel because it is not the platform you wanted.
- Sell duplicates on a day when the price is good, not the moment you finish cleaning.

## A Platform Collection Checklist

Use this as a lightweight tracking method:

- [ ] One clean copy of every title you encounter
- [ ] Both PAL and NTSC for titles you love
- [ ] At least one high-rarity shell displayed
- [ ] At least one crossover cartridge kept
- [ ] No duplicate commons left unsold
- [ ] Every shelf has a deliberate order (platform, region, or color)

## What We Still Do Not Know

As of October 24, 2026, the following are unconfirmed:

- The names and visual identities of the six fictional platforms
- The full rarity ladder and any official drop rates
- Whether regional variants differ in value beyond normal market fluctuation
- Whether the full game adds a platform-completion reward
- Whether cross-platform sets are part of quests

We will update this guide when official material or the full release answers these questions. Until then, the safe strategy is the same: collect broadly, clean carefully, keep regional pairs, and sell duplicates rather than your only copy.
