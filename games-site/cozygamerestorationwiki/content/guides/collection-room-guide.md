# Cozy Game Restoration Collection Room Guide — Display & Decorating

The collection room is the payoff. Cleaning a cartridge is a small, tactile pleasure; filling a room with cartridges you rescued is the long-term reward. Cozy Game Restoration gives you a customizable collection room with display shelves, decorations and cosmetics, many of them unlocked through collection quests.

There is no confirmed room score, no official decoration tier list, and no fail state for a messy shelf. That means the collection room is genuinely yours to design. This guide offers a practical approach: what to display, how to arrange it, and how to keep the room growing without turning it into a storage unit.

## What the Game Confirms

- A customizable collection room
- Display shelves for finished cartridges
- Decorations and cosmetics
- Collection quests that reward room items
- The ability to rearrange trophies and add items such as lamps and televisions, according to hands-on coverage
- Around 300 quests at launch feeding the room's decoration pool

What is **not** confirmed:

- A room score or rating
- A fixed list of decoration categories
- A completion percentage for the room
- Whether decorations have gameplay effects
- Whether shelves have a hard capacity limit

Do not assume a decoration system that the game has not shown. The room is a creative space, not a stat sheet.

## Start With the Shelf, Not the Sofa

New players often spend their first rewards on furniture before they have anything to put on it. Reverse that.

### 1. Build a shelf worth looking at

A shelf with five well-chosen cartridges is more satisfying than a room with five decorations and no collection. Your first priority is a clean, coherent shelf:

- One cartridge from each platform you own
- Both PAL and NTSC versions of a favorite title
- One high-rarity or special-shell cartridge as a centerpiece
- One crossover cartridge if you find one

This gives the room a focal point before you add lamps, posters or furniture.

### 2. Group by a rule

A display needs a logic, even if the logic is only visible to you. Useful grouping rules:

| Rule | Best for | Example |
|------|----------|---------|
| By platform | Showing breadth | One shelf per fictional platform |
| By region | Showing a regional collection | PAL shelf, NTSC shelf |
| By genre | Showing taste | Puzzle shelf, action shelf |
| By color or shell | Showing visual coherence | Gold shells together, dark shells together |
| By story | Showing personal favorites | A "rescued from the worst condition" shelf |

The game does not enforce any of these. Pick one and commit. A room with a rule reads as a collection; a room without one reads as clutter.

### 3. Leave negative space

The most common decorating mistake is filling every slot. Leave gaps. A shelf with breathing room makes the individual cartridges legible, and it gives you space for future finds. You will thank yourself when a rare variant arrives.

## Lighting and Focal Points

Hands-on coverage mentions lamps as a decoration example. Lighting does real work in a display room:

- Light the shelf you are most proud of.
- Keep the light source slightly to one side so cartridge labels and shell textures catch it.
- Avoid washing out the whole room; contrast is what makes a display look curated.

A single focal point — a high-rarity shell, a crossover cartridge, or a complete regional pair — is more memorable than evenly distributing attention across every shelf.

## How Quests Feed the Room

Collection quests reward decorations and cosmetics. That creates a loop:

1. Collect broadly to satisfy quest objectives.
2. Complete quests.
3. Earn decorations.
4. Use decorations to make the room a better home for the collection.
5. Keep collecting to fill the improved room.

The important consequence is that **the room is a reason to keep collecting, not a reason to stop**. If you decorate around your current collection and then stop buying parcels, the room stops growing. A good rhythm is to alternate: collect for a while, then decorate with what you earned, then collect again.

## Keep, Display, Sell: A Room-Friendly Sorting System

Use three mental buckets after every cleaning session:

### Keep and display

- The first copy of a title
- A regional variant you lack
- High-rarity shells
- Crossover cartridges
- Anything you personally like

### Keep in storage (or a less prominent shelf)

- Duplicates you are holding for a market day
- Cartridges you are not sure about
- Low-rarity extras that might complete a future set

### Sell

- Duplicates of titles you already display
- Commons that do not serve a set
- Anything you have cleaned and decided you do not want

The goal is that every displayed cartridge has a reason to be there. If you cannot explain why a cartridge is on the shelf, it probably belongs in the sell pile or the storage pile.

## Decorating Without Clutter

A few rules that keep a cozy room cozy:

- **One focal point per shelf.** Do not compete with yourself.
- **Repeat a material or color.** Wood with wood, warm light with warm light.
- **Keep the floor clear.** Furniture that blocks the shelf defeats the purpose.
- **Do not decorate around a mess.** Clean and sort first, then place items.
- **Change one thing at a time.** It is easier to judge whether a decoration improves the room when you have not changed five things at once.

## A Sample Room Plan

This is an editorial plan, not an official layout:

1. **Main shelf:** one cartridge from each of the six fictional platforms, arranged left to right.
2. **Feature shelf:** your best regional pair, centered, with a high-rarity shell beside it.
3. **Crossover corner:** any crossover cartridges, grouped together.
4. **Work area:** keep the workbench visually distinct from the display. The room should read as "workshop plus museum," not one undifferentiated surface.
5. **Lighting:** one warm lamp aimed at the feature shelf; a second, softer light for the room.
6. **Decorations:** add furniture and cosmetics only after the shelves have a logic.

## What We Still Do Not Know

As of October 24, 2026, the following are unconfirmed:

- Whether the collection room has a maximum shelf capacity
- Whether decorations are purely cosmetic or have any effect
- The full decoration catalog
- Whether there is a room-completion reward
- Whether room layout can be saved as presets

We will update this guide after launch. Until then, treat the room as a creative project with no wrong answers and no timer.

## The Bottom Line

The collection room rewards intention. Start with a shelf, give it a rule, leave space, and let quest rewards add character over time. The game will not grade your room. The only test is whether you enjoy standing in it — and whether the cartridges on the shelf are ones you are glad you rescued.
