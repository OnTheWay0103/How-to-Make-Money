GONE Fishing supports one to eight players in online co-op, and the game does not assign roles for you. A lobby where everyone fishes will hit the quota and then lose someone at dusk, because nobody cooked, nobody scouted and nobody called the blue flame. This guide sets out the four jobs that actually need doing, what the game shares between players, and how to run a first group night without a wipe.

## What Co-op Actually Changes

The mechanics are the same solo and in a group. The difference is throughput and attention.

| System | Solo | Co-op |
|---|---|---|
| Fishing | Your only job | One job among several |
| Altar delivery | You carry and deposit | Can be delegated |
| Cooking | Competes with fishing time | Runs in parallel with fishing |
| Totems | Team-wide buff for one player | Team-wide buff for everyone |
| Trinkets (1.0) | Yours | Shared with the lobby |
| Night threats | You are alone | Coordination helps, noise hurts |

Two details matter more than they look. First, **totems are team-wide**: one player buying Leviathan or Fish Frenzy improves every rod in the lobby. Second, **the 1.0 trinkets are shared with friends**, which means a purchase is a group purchase. Upgrades, by contrast, are personal. That split should decide who buys what.

## The Four Jobs

You do not need four players to run four jobs, but you do need someone to own each one.

| Job | What it owns | Best equipment focus | Fails when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Angler | Casting, hook sets, landing fish | Depth and line access | Chasing size while the quota is unmet |
| Runner | Carrying fish, altar deposits, calling the flame | Movement and light | Delivers late or loses fish at dusk |
| Cook | Stove queue, cooked buffer, selling surplus | Big Grill and Fish Value | Cooks fish the quota needed raw |
| Scout | Bait respawns, trader window, night watch | Totems and light | Wanders alone after dark |

In a two-player lobby, the angler and runner are the natural split, and cooking happens between casts. In a three- or four-player lobby, give cooking to the player with the worst aim and the best map sense; they will contribute more at the stove than at the water.

### The runner is the most underrated job

The runner decides whether a day succeeds. They know where the altar is, how long the walk takes, and when the flame turns blue. A good runner delivers in two trips and never carries the whole quota at dusk. A bad runner treats carrying as a taxi service and arrives with five fish after the lake has already started hunting.

If you only formalise one role, formalise this one.

## Shared Versus Personal: Who Buys What

| Purchase | Shared? | Who should buy it |
|---|---|---|
| Totems | Team-wide | Anyone with spare money; coordinate so you do not duplicate |
| Trinkets (1.0) | Shared with friends | The player who fishes most, since the buff applies to their rod too |
| Rod / line / sinker upgrades | Personal | Each angler buys their own |
| Big Grill / Fish Value | Personal (shop upgrade) | The cook first, then the anglers |
| Bait | Personal | Whoever is fishing that day |

The practical rule: **buy shared items when the group is behind, personal items when you are ahead.** A totem bought on day two lifts four players; a rod upgrade lifts one. A trinket bought by the best angler lifts the whole lobby, because that angler is the one landing the fish.

## Lobby Sizes: What to Expect

| Players | What works | What to watch |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Slow, quiet, all roles on one player | Cooking competes with fishing; raw delivery is often correct |
| 2 | Angler + runner, cook between casts | One death removes two jobs |
| 3–4 | Full role split, one trader run per day | Someone must own the flame call |
| 5–8 | Pairs, spread fishing spots, group regroup | Noise, friendly explosions, and the illusion that someone else did the quota |

The official store page says 1–8 players, and the Alpha 4.0 update raised the lobby cap to eight. Whether the quota scales with lobby size is `[Unconfirmed]`; we found no official statement either way. Test it on your first eight-player run before you plan a route around it.

## How to Join and What to Expect

GONE Fishing is online co-op on Windows. The store lists Online Co-op and Single-player but no split-screen or LAN category, so plan on each player owning the game and connecting through Steam. There is no console version, which makes crossplay a non-question: everyone is on PC.

Practical setup advice:

1. **Use voice chat outside the game if you can.** Voice chat is not listed among the Steam features, and community reports are inconsistent. A separate call removes the uncertainty.
2. **Agree on a difficulty before the first cast.** Jeremy Wade gives a +20% weight bonus but forces Hardcore, which means a wipe is permanent. Do not surprise a new player with that.
3. **Decide the first-day route on land.** The worst group runs start with four players running in four directions.
4. **Set a regroup point.** Pick a landmark near the altar and make it the dusk meeting spot.

## A First Group Night, Minute by Minute

This is a plan, not a script. Adjust it to the map and the quota.

| Phase | Group action | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Morning | Anglers fan out; runner stays near the altar | Early deliveries keep the quota visible |
| Midday | Scout visits the balloon trader | The trader window is easy to miss |
| Afternoon | Cook processes the buffer; anglers push depth | Value multiplies before dusk |
| Dusk | Runner calls the flame; everyone returns | No one should be carrying at dark |
| Night | Group up, lights on, no fishing | Isolation is what gets players killed |

The single biggest group improvement is a **dusk rule**: when the runner calls the flame, fishing stops. Not "one more cast". Not "I'm almost landed". The group returns. Players who ignore the rule are the ones who die with the quota in their hands.

## Solo Is a Real Option

Single-player is a listed feature, and the loop does not change. Solo is slower because you cannot cook and fish at the same time, and it is less forgiving because a single mistake has no backup. The compensation is that every fish, every coin and every upgrade is yours.

If you are learning the game, solo is the better teacher for the first two days. If you are learning the night threats, co-op is the better teacher because someone else can tell you what grabbed you.

## Group Etiquette That Saves Runs

- **Do not detonate near the altar.** The store page advertises the ability to explode monsters and people. Fun at the lake, disastrous at the delivery point.
- **Do not take a shared trinket purchase personally.** It is a lobby buff, not a gift.
- **Call your departures.** "Going deep" and "coming back" are the two most useful sentences in the game.
- **Do not reset the day in Hardcore.** Bait is destroyed on reload, and the group pays for it.
- **Leave a bone on the altar if you have one.** Community reports say the dog arrives the next morning, returns fish to the altar, and provides light if given a lantern. Treat the exact behaviour as `[Unconfirmed]`, but the trick is cheap to test.

## Common Questions

**Can I play with friends who do not own the game?** No. There is no split-screen or shared-copy mode listed on the store page.

**Is there matchmaking?** The store lists Online Co-op but does not describe a matchmaking system. Community lobbies are usually organised through Steam friends and Discord.

**Do we share money?** Community reports say gold is shared, which is why the Midas Touch perk benefits the lobby. The exact economy is `[Unconfirmed]`.

**Does progress carry between lobbies?** Trinkets and totems are described as shared with friends, but we could not verify whether that means the same save file or the same session. Test before relying on it.

**How many players should a first run have?** Two or three. Enough hands to cook and deliver, few enough that everyone knows who is doing what.

Co-op in GONE Fishing is not harder than solo; it is louder. The quota is the same, the lake is the same, and the night is the same. What changes is how quickly a small mistake becomes a group problem, which is why the [quota plan](/guides/gone-fishing-sacrifice-quota) and the [night threat list](/guides/gone-fishing-enemies-guide) matter more in a full lobby than they do alone.
