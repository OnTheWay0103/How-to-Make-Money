In GONE Fishing the **sacrifice quota** is the day's win condition. Each morning the lake wants a certain amount of fish; you catch them, carry them to the altar, and drop them in. When the altar flame turns **blue**, the lake is satisfied and you can sleep safely. If night arrives first, it hunts you instead.

## What the Quota Actually Is

The quota is a floating target, not a fixed number of fish. It grows with each day you survive, and the current value is visible in the Esc menu. That design has two consequences that shape every decision:

- **A big fish can be worth several small ones.** The altar cares about value, not count, so a single high-value catch can close a gap that three small fish could not.
- **Yesterday's route stops working.** A route that cleared day three with time to spare may be a panic on day ten. The quota moves; your plan has to move with it.

Community documentation describes the altar as feeding the Lake Demon, with indicator lights showing progress. The important UI detail is the flame colour: blue means satisfied. Treat the flame, not the clock, as your primary readout.

## The Altar, Step by Step

| Step | What to do | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Open the Esc menu and note the quota | The only number that defines success today |
| 2 | Fish until you have a safe buffer | Buffer absorbs a bad hook-set or a lost fish |
| 3 | Cook what you can | A cooked fish is worth 33% more at the altar or the trader |
| 4 | Carry fish to the altar | Deposit with `G` or left mouse |
| 5 | Watch the flame | Blue flame means the day's hunger is satisfied |
| 6 | Sleep before dark | Night is for surviving, not for finishing the quota |

The order is not arbitrary. Cooking before delivery means the same number of fish produces more quota progress. Delivering before dark means you are not the player who gets grabbed while carrying the last fish.

## Why Cooking Is a Quota Multiplier

A fully cooked fish is worth 33% more whether you sacrifice it or sell it. The stove takes about 60 seconds per fish and processes one at a time. That is the entire system, and it changes the arithmetic of a day.

| Fish state | Quota progress | Time cost | Best use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Raw | Base | None | Emergency delivery at dusk |
| Cooked | +33% | 60 seconds per fish | Normal play, especially in a group |

In a solo run, the stove competes directly with your rod: every minute cooking is a minute not fishing. In a group, it does not. One player can cook while two fish, and the lobby gets the multiplier for free. If you are playing with three or more people and still sacrificing raw fish, you are leaving a third of your quota on the table.

The official FAQ notes that cooking and gambling bonuses multiply. That matters later, when a cooked fish can be pushed through the gambling pit for a much larger return. It does not matter on day two, when the quota is small and the risk of losing a fish is real.

## Planning the Day Around the Quota

The most common failure is not a lack of fish. It is a lack of time to deliver them. A day plan that works at every scale:

1. **First trip: quota only.** Fish the closest productive water and deliver as soon as you have enough for the current quota. Do not cook this trip unless someone else is already at the stove.
2. **Second trip: buffer and money.** With the quota covered, fish for value. Cook, sell, or gamble the surplus. This is the trip that funds upgrades.
3. **Trader window at midday.** The balloon trader appears around midday. If your route puts you far from the trader at noon, you lose the day's buying opportunity.
4. **Final check before dusk.** Look at the flame, not the fish count. If it is blue, stop. If it is not, deliver raw fish immediately.
5. **Night.** Sleep if the flame is blue. If it is not, the night becomes a survival problem, and the correct response is to group up and move, not to fish.

Two trips are better than one long one because the walk back is the risk. A player carrying five fish who dies at dusk loses all five; a player who delivered three and died carrying two loses two.

## What Happens When You Miss

Missing the quota is not an instant loss, but it escalates.

| Misses | Community-reported consequence |
|---|---|
| One night | The lake hunts the lobby; you must survive until dawn |
| Repeated misses | The whole team is hunted and the run can end |
| In Hardcore | Death is permanent, and reloading destroys your bait |

The exact escalation thresholds are not published by the developer, so treat the table as `[Unconfirmed]` community behaviour rather than a rule. What is confirmed is the direction: the game gets less forgiving each time the altar goes hungry, and Hardcore removes the safety net entirely.

## The Deliberate-Miss Question

Some experienced players deliberately miss a day to make the next one easier. The logic is that a failed night costs less than a wasted day, and a smaller quota tomorrow is worth more than a risky delivery today.

Our assessment: this is a high-skill play, not a beginner strategy. It requires knowing the night threats well enough to survive a hunt on purpose, and it requires a group that agrees. In a public lobby it is a good way to get someone killed. If you want to try it, do it on a low difficulty with a full group, and decide before dusk rather than after.

A safer version of the same idea is to stop fishing early. If the flame is blue and you still have daylight, bank the day. Do not convert a safe day into a risky one for a fish you do not need.

## Solo, Duo, and Full Lobby

The quota loop is identical at every lobby size; what changes is how much time you have to service it.

| Lobby | Strength | Weakness | Advice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solo | No coordination cost, all money is yours | No one cooks while you fish, no one watches your back | Build the stove early and cook in batches |
| Duo | One fishes, one cooks and delivers | Both roles collapse if one dies | Agree who owns the altar before the first cast |
| 3–4 | Enough hands for fishing, cooking and a trader run | Someone always thinks the quota is covered when it is not | Nominate one player to call the blue flame |
| 5–8 | Enormous throughput | Noise, spread, and friendly explosions | Split into pairs and set a regroup time |

Whether the quota scales with lobby size is `[Unconfirmed]`. We could not find an official statement, and community reports are mixed. Do not assume eight players means eight times the quota; test it on your first group run.

## A Day Checklist You Can Reuse

| Time of day | Check | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| Morning | Quota read | You know the number |
| Midday | Trader visited | Upgrades or totems bought |
| Afternoon | Buffer built | You can afford one lost fish |
| Dusk | Flame checked | Blue, or raw fish on hand |
| Night | Group position | Everyone is together and not fishing |

The checklist is deliberately short. The game's own systems provide the rest of the information, and the flame is more reliable than any plan you write down.

## Common Questions

**Does the quota reset if I reload?** In Hardcore it cannot be reset, and reloading destroys bait. Outside Hardcore, community reports describe day resetting as possible, but the exact behaviour is `[Unconfirmed]`.

**Should I sacrifice big fish or sell them?** Sacrifice what the quota needs and sell the surplus. A fish sold above quota still advances your upgrades, which makes tomorrow easier.

**Can I finish the quota at night?** Technically the altar is still there, but night is when the lake and other predators are active. Treat a night delivery as an emergency, not a plan.

**What is the blue flame?** The visual signal that the current day's hunger is satisfied. It does not carry over; tomorrow brings a larger quota.

**Is cooking worth it in solo?** Only when you are ahead of the quota. Behind schedule, raw delivery beats a cooked fish that arrives after dark.

Meeting the quota is not the hard part of GONE Fishing. Meeting it with daylight left, a cooked buffer and money in hand is. That is the habit that carries a lobby from day three to day thirty, and it is the same habit that makes the [night threats](/guides/gone-fishing-enemies-guide) survivable when the altar does go hungry.
