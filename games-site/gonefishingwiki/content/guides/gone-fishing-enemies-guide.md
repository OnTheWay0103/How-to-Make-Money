Night in GONE Fishing is a different game. During the day the threats are time and distance; after dark they are creatures that hear you, see you, and in one case cannot be killed at all. The store page divides them into territorial beasts, stalking nightmares and ambushing predators, then adds the Lake Entity as a fightable endgame threat. This guide covers what the community has documented about each, how to tell them apart, and the rules that keep a group alive.

## First, the Official Framing

Three phrases from the store page matter:

| Official phrase | What it implies in play |
|---|---|
| Territorial beasts | Some creatures defend an area rather than hunt you across the map |
| Stalking nightmares | Some follow and wait; spotting them early changes the outcome |
| Ambushing predators | Some attack from concealment with little warning |
| The Lake Entity | A boss-level threat you can fight, not just avoid |

The practical takeaway is that there is no single "night enemy" to learn. The correct response changes with the creature, and the wrong response can make things worse: running from a territorial beast may work, running from a stalker may not.

## The Threats We Could Verify

The table below combines official wording with community field reports. Names and behaviours are community-reported unless marked otherwise, so treat every entry as `[Unconfirmed]` until the developer documents it.

| Threat | Warning sign | Counter | Killable? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Giant | Low roar at night, heavy steps | Stare at it; it freezes while watched | Yes, explosives; corpse can be used as bait |
| Shy One | Stands or crouches near grass and buildings | Spot it first; it flees when seen | `[Unconfirmed]` |
| Crocodile | Low growl, then stomping as it closes | Mash Space if grabbed | Yes, explosives; corpse and young are bait |
| Anaconda | Hissing nearby | Break line of sight, avoid water edges | `[Unconfirmed]` |
| Shark | Circles anglers standing in water | Get out of the water | `[Unconfirmed]` |
| Big Fish Man | Heavy footsteps from above, all maps at night | Sleep; it cannot be fought | No — immune to explosives |
| Giant Spider | Roar at night, footsteps outside the jungle cabin | Leave the jungle at dusk | `[Unconfirmed]`; often clips terrain |

Two entries deserve a warning. The **Big Fish Man** is the one threat the community consistently describes as unkillable: it drops in, moves faster than a player, and kills on contact. The only documented answer is to be asleep before it arrives. The **Giant Spider** is described as so large that it often clips into terrain and cannot grab players, which makes it frightening but inconsistent.

## The Giant: The One You Can Stare Down

The Giant is the best example of a threat with a mechanic rather than a stat check.

- It announces itself with a low roar at night.
- It is fast, but it freezes while a player looks at it.
- Community reports say it can be killed with explosives, and its corpse can be used as bait.

The counter is therefore not "run" but "look and back away". In a group, that means one player holds its attention while the others move. Do not assume the freeze is permanent; treat it as a window, not a solution.

## The Shy One: Punish It Before It Commits

The Shy One is described as hiding in grass or beside buildings, standing or crouching while it watches. Community reports say spotting it first makes it flee.

This is a threat that rewards the scout role. A player who sweeps the perimeter with a light before dusk will see it; a player who walks into the fishing spot at night will not. If you see it and it does not flee, do not close distance to test the behaviour. Leave.

## Crocodiles: The Grab Is the Fight

The crocodile is the most mechanically legible night threat:

1. A low growl is the warning.
2. Stomping means it is closing in.
3. If it grabs you, mash Space to escape.
4. Explosives can kill it, and its corpse can be used as bait.

The official hotfix notes add an important detail: a lake-map crocodile bug could drag players into cliffs and throw them under the map, and that has been fixed. That bug confirms the behaviour the community describes, which is why the grab counter matters.

Young crocodiles are also reported as bait. If you kill an adult, check for the young before you leave.

## Anaconda, Shark, Spider: Avoidance Beats Fighting

These three share a pattern: the warning is close, and the fight is not worth taking.

| Threat | Best response | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Anaconda | Stay out of dense water edges; move as a pair | Reported to coil around a whole player |
| Shark | Fish from shore or a dock, not from waist-deep water | Reported to lunge at anglers in water |
| Giant Spider | Do not stay in the jungle after dusk | Reported to be enormous and inconsistent |

None of these have a confirmed kill method in our sources. That does not mean they are unkillable; it means the reliable answer is positioning. A shark cannot lunge at a player who is on the dock, and a spider cannot corner a group that left the biome.

## The Big Fish Man: The Sleep Check

If the community reports are right, the Big Fish Man is the reason the quota is a survival mechanic rather than a score.

- It appears on all maps at night.
- It descends from above with heavy footsteps.
- It moves faster than a player and kills on contact.
- Explosives do not work.
- The only reported counter is to be asleep.

That last point is why the blue flame matters so much. A lobby that hits the quota can sleep and skip the encounter. A lobby that misses the quota is awake when the Big Fish Man arrives. The threat is not a combat check; it is a consequence for being late.

## Night Rules That Actually Help

| Rule | Reason |
|---|---|
| Stop fishing when the flame is blue | The night encounter is optional if you sleep |
| Regroup before dusk | Isolation is the common factor in most deaths |
| Carry light | Community reports say the dog can provide light if given a lantern |
| Do not investigate a roar alone | Territorial beasts punish solo scouting |
| Do not use explosives near the altar or the group | The store page advertises exploding people for a reason |
| If grabbed, mash the escape input | Confirmed for the crocodile; likely for other grabs |

The first rule is the one that saves runs. Everything else is damage control.

## What the 1.0 Hotfixes Tell Us

Two official patches from 2026-10-22 are useful because they confirm what the developers consider a threat:

- The **yeti** was failing to spawn on the arctic map, which confirms a yeti encounter exists there.
- The **final boss whale nuke** could be invisible while still dealing damage, which confirms an area attack in the boss fight.
- Player health regen delay went from 7.5 to 6 seconds, and regen speed rose 10%.
- The rolling turtle's damage went from 75 to 60, its hitbox shrank, and the gaps between them widened.

Those changes are balance, not new mechanics, but they tell you what the endgame expects you to survive: area attacks, rolling hazards, and a health pool that regenerates slowly.

## Fighting Back Versus Avoiding

The store page promises you can fight back against the Lake Entity, and the 1.0 update added a final boss. That does not mean every night threat is a fight.

| Threat type | Right response | Wrong response |
|---|---|---|
| Territorial beast (Giant, crocodile) | Fight if you have explosives; otherwise leave the area | Brawling without a plan |
| Stalker (Shy One, Big Fish Man) | Break contact or sleep | Standing still to see what happens |
| Ambusher (shark, anaconda, spider) | Avoid the biome or the water | Testing whether it can reach you |

The pattern is simple: fight what announces itself, avoid what hides, and sleep through what cannot be killed.

## Common Questions

**Can I kill the Big Fish Man?** Community reports say no, including with explosives. We found no official statement, so treat the immunity as `[Unconfirmed]`, but do not plan a fight around it.

**Do enemies attack the altar?** We found no verified report of altar damage. The danger is to players carrying fish, not to the altar itself.

**Is night threat behaviour different on each map?** The arctic map has a yeti, the jungle has a giant spider, and the lake has crocodiles, per community and hotfix notes. Treat map-specific spawns as real but not exhaustive.

**What should a solo player do at night?** Sleep if the flame is blue. If it is not, stay near the altar, keep light on, and do not fish.

**Do lights attract enemies?** We could not verify this either way. The community uses light for visibility and for the dog, not as a repellent.

The night system is designed to make the quota feel like a survival decision rather than a chore. Once you internalise that, the threats stop being surprises and start being schedules: the Giant is a stare check, the crocodile is a grab check, and the Big Fish Man is a bedtime check.
