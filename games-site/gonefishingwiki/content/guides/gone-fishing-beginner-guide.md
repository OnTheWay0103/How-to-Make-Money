GONE Fishing is a 1–8 player survival horror game where the day job is fishing and the night job is not being eaten. You catch fish, carry them to the altar, and feed a lake entity its daily quota. Miss the quota and the lake feeds on you instead. This guide covers the first week: what to do each day, in what order, and which habits cost runs.

## The Loop You Are Actually Learning

Every in-game day has the same four phases, and each has a different failure mode.

| Phase | What you do | What usually goes wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Prep | Check the quota in the Esc menu, pick bait, repair or restock at the trader | Leaving camp with three bait and no plan |
| Fishing | Cast, set the hook, fight the tension bar, land the fish | Holding the mouse too early and snapping the line |
| Delivery | Cook what you can, carry the rest to the altar, deposit with `G` or left mouse | Hoarding fish until the light is already red |
| Night | Sleep if the flame is blue; otherwise survive until dawn | Trying to fish after dark instead of banking the day |

Phases two and three compete for the same resource: time. A fish in your hands is worth nothing until it is on the altar or sold, so the first skill this game teaches is deciding when to stop fishing.

## Day One: Follow the Quota, Not the Map

Your first day should be boring on purpose.

1. **Open the Esc menu and read the quota.** It is the only number that matters on day one. The quota grows every day, so the number you see is a ceiling, not a target to beat.
2. **Fish close to camp.** Official FAQ advice points to docks around 500+ depth as the consistent option; deep water is for size, not for reliability. On day one, consistency beats a trophy.
3. **Keep every fish, including small ones.** Small fish still count toward the altar. The altar accepts what you give it, and the flame tells you when the lake is satisfied.
4. **Cook before you deliver if you have time.** A fully cooked fish is worth 33% more, whether you sacrifice it or sell it. The stove takes about 60 seconds per fish and handles one at a time, so cooking is a queue, not a button.
5. **Watch the flame, not the clock.** When the altar flame turns blue, the day's hunger is satisfied and you are safe to sleep. That blue flame is your real "day complete" signal.

A common day-one mistake is treating the altar as a bank: players fish until the sun is low, then discover the walk back is longer than the remaining daylight. Deliver in two or three trips instead.

## The Fishing Minigame, Step by Step

The controls are simple and the timing is not. Equip your rod and bait with `TAB`, cast with left mouse, then wait.

| Step | Input | Timing note |
|---|---|---|
| Cast | Left mouse | Aim at open water; depth matters later, not now |
| Hook set | Left mouse when the bite comes | One clean click; late is worse than early |
| Tension | Hold and release left mouse | Keep the marker in the green zone |
| Land | Keep tension until the fish reaches you | Do not reel through a red zone to save a second |

The single most useful timing tip from the community guide: after a bite, the tension bar does not start dropping for roughly 2–5 seconds. Players who hold the mouse down immediately lose fish they should have landed. Wait for the drop, then hold, then release before the marker leaves the green.

Bait score decides what bites, and several things feed it. The official FAQ lists the levers: sinker level, beer score, the Leviathan totem, and fishing depth of at least 2,500. Weather adds another multiplier, and thunderstorms are the friendly version of bad weather.

## Bait: What to Pick Before You Understand Bait Score

Bait is a live animal, and the game is not subtle about it. The store page promises dozens of live animals as bait; the practical rule is simpler than the full table:

| Situation | Bait approach | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Meeting a normal daily quota | Common, fast-respawning bait | Volume matters more than rarity |
| Chasing size | Bait value 60+ | The official FAQ points at 60+ for big fish and legendaries |
| Chasing the single highest score | Troll (86) | Named by the official FAQ as the highest point-value bait |
| Learning a new map | Whatever spawns near your fishing spot | Walking time is the hidden cost |

Do not copy a bait tier list and assume it applies to you. The same bait behaves differently with a low sinker, shallow water and an early day count. Bait value sets a ceiling; your gear and depth decide whether you reach it. A "critical" catch multiplies value, but you cannot schedule one.

## Why Cooking Is Not Optional

Cooking is the cheapest value multiplier in the game and the one beginners skip.

| Action | Effect | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Sell raw fish | Base value | None |
| Sacrifice raw fish | Base quota progress | None |
| Cook, then sell | +33% value | About 60 seconds per fish |
| Cook, then sacrifice | +33% quota progress | About 60 seconds per fish |

The trade-off is time and attention. Cook while someone else fishes, or cook the fish you know you will not need for the quota. A four-player lobby should almost never sacrifice raw fish.

The official FAQ adds a second multiplier: cooking and gambling bonuses multiply rather than add. A 50-point fish becomes 75 after cooking, and a 10x gamble on that is 750, not 550.

## The First Three Upgrades, in Order

The official FAQ is unusually direct here: prioritise **Big Grill** and **Fish Value**, each of which gives +50% when maxed, then improve your line so you can fish deeper without snapping. The 1.0 patch complicates the old advice by replacing the line/rod/sinker upgrade path with eight trinkets sold by a new trader, Clinket. Trinkets are shared with the whole lobby, which changes the maths for groups.

| Priority | Upgrade | Why it comes first |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Big Grill | More cooked fish per session, and cooked fish are worth 33% more |
| 2 | Fish Value | A flat multiplier on every fish you sell or sacrifice |
| 3 | Depth access / line | Deep water is where size and legendary odds live |
| 4 | Totems (team-wide) | Leviathan and Fish Frenzy extend what a day can produce |
| 5 | Trinkets | Strong, but they are multipliers on a loop that must already work |

If you play solo, the order barely changes. If you play with four or more, buy the team-wide items earlier than a solo player would, because one totem buffs every rod in the lobby.

## Difficulty: Jeremy Wade Is the Recommended Start

The official FAQ recommends Jeremy Wade difficulty for new players because it gives a +20% weight bonus on the leaderboard. That recommendation comes with a warning the FAQ states plainly: in Jeremy Wade, hardcore cannot be disabled. Hardcore means permanent death, bait is destroyed when you reload a save, and you cannot reset the day.

The community description of the mode is less polite: least bait, shortest days, and a 20% boost to legendary and mythical catch rates. You are accepting a harsher clock in exchange for better fish.

Our advice: start on Jeremy Wade only if you are playing with someone who has already survived a week. For a first solo run, take the standard difficulty and move up once you can hit the quota without a panic delivery.

## The Five Mistakes That End Runs Early

1. **Fishing past the safe window.** The blue flame is permission to sleep. Ignoring it to chase one more fish is how a good day becomes a night fight.
2. **Sacrificing raw fish in a group.** Cooking is a 33% multiplier and a four-player lobby has the spare hands to use it.
3. **Buying bait you cannot use.** High-value bait on a shallow rod is wasted. Bait value is a ceiling, not a guarantee.
4. **Treating the balloon trader as a shop you visit later.** The trader appears at midday; if you are deep in a route at noon, you miss the buying window.
5. **Splitting the group at night.** Night threats punish isolated players. The fishing spots can be spread out during the day; the night should not be.

## What to Do on Days Two Through Seven

The first week is a ramp, not a grind.

| Day | Focus | Success looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Add depth | You consistently fish past the dock and still make quota |
| 3 | Add cooking | No raw fish goes to the altar in a multiplayer lobby |
| 4 | Add the trader | You buy your first meaningful upgrade before noon |
| 5 | Add a totem | The team has one shared buff running |
| 6 | Learn one night threat | You can name what killed you and how to avoid it next time |
| 7 | Survive | Perks unlock after seven consecutive days survived |

That last row is the real week-one goal. Nine perks exist, and they unlock by surviving seven consecutive days; each player can then bring up to two into a run. Details are in our [perks guide](/guides/gone-fishing-best-perks).

## When to Move From Quota to Size

You are ready to fish for size when you hit the quota before the light turns red, Big Grill and Fish Value are at a useful level, and you can fish at 2,500+ depth without snapping.

The official FAQ's own benchmark is day 40 for a fully stacked bait score: max sinker, beer score, and the Leviathan totem. Week one exists to make week six possible, not to produce a trophy fish.

## Quick Answers

**Can I play solo?** Yes. Single-player is a listed Steam feature; solo is slower, not impossible.

**What kills new players most?** The walk back after dark, and the decision to take one more cast.
