# How to Use the Basement Dojo & Vault of Memories in Echoes of Mystralia

The **All You Can Cast** update (October 8, 2026) gave Echoes of Mystralia its first real practice space: the **Basement Dojo** under the Azimuth Temple, plus the **Vault of Memories**, a searchable codex of every Memory you have discovered. For a game built on a freeform spellcrafting canvas, this is the difference between guessing at combinations and actually testing them.

This guide covers how to unlock the basement, what Echo Fragments and Ancient Lessons buy, how the Vault and Dojo work together, and a repeatable method for testing a spell idea before you commit a run to it. It also lists the seven Memories added in the same update.

## What the Update Added

The official Devlog #3 describes three connected additions:

| Addition | What it does | Where it lives |
|----------|--------------|----------------|
| **Basement Dojo** | A training area with configurable dummies where you test spells | Under the Azimuth Temple, entered through the basement doors at the Altar |
| **Vault of Memories** | A searchable codex of Discovered Memories; place them on the spell canvas and practice | Also in the basement |
| **New endgame tiers** | Additional tiers for existing Watcher's Duties | Existing Duties board |

The update also reworked Mazarim's animations, improved visual effects, added an option to turn puzzle hints on or off, changed Guardian Seals so a challenge only spawns the first time you fail a timed puzzle, and fixed a wide range of gameplay and spell bugs.

## How to Unlock the Basement Dojo

The unlock is specific: **open the basement doors at the Altar**. The Altar is part of the Azimuth Temple hub, the place you return to between runs to level up, spend the Lotus, and bond with fellow Watchers.

A practical routine:

1. **Finish or return from a run** so you are back at the Azimuth Temple.
2. **Go to the Altar** and look for the basement doors. If they are not interactable, progress further in the Early Access content — the update does not publish an explicit prerequisite beyond the Altar, so any additional gate is **[Unconfirmed]**.
3. **Enter the basement.** The Dojo and the Vault of Memories are both down here, so you only need to find the room once.
4. **Open the Vault first.** It is the lower-friction of the two features: you can browse what you own without spending anything.

Do not confuse the Basement Dojo with the fight challenges from the demo. The Dojo is a hub training space; the old challenges are repeatable encounters. Both are useful, but only the Dojo lets you place any discovered Memory on a canvas and hit a dummy without starting a run.

## What Echo Fragments and Ancient Lessons Buy

The Dojo does not hand you every dummy configuration at once. The official devlog says: **"Invest Echo Fragments and Ancient Lessons to unlock the different dojo configurations, and types of training dummies."**

That confirms two currencies and two categories of purchase:

- **Echo Fragments** — spent on Dojo configurations and dummy types. Echo Fragments are also tied to Watcher's Duties rewards; the same update adjusted at least one Duty's Echo Fragment reward, which confirms the currency is part of the endgame economy.
- **Ancient Lessons** — the second unlock currency for Dojo setups. The devlog does not say where Ancient Lessons drop **[Unconfirmed]**.

What is **not** published: exact prices, the full list of dummy types, or how many configurations exist. Any guide quoting "500 Echo Fragments for the fire dummy" is inventing a number. Treat the Dojo as a progression sink you feed as you play, not a menu you can fully price out on day one.

### Should You Spend Echo Fragments on the Dojo?

Yes, but not all at once. A sensible order:

1. **Unlock one additional dummy type** beyond the default. You want at least one target that behaves differently from a stationary post.
2. **Test whether the new dummy changes your results.** If it does not, stop spending and go back to the Vault.
3. **Buy configurations that answer a question you actually have.** A moving dummy is worth more than a cosmetic arena if your build keeps missing fast targets.
4. **Leave a buffer.** Echo Fragments also feed Watcher's Duties progression; emptying the wallet on Dojo furniture can stall the endgame track.

## How the Vault of Memories Works

The Vault is the feature that fixes the game's biggest usability problem: remembering what you have found. The official description is precise:

- It is a **codex of Discovered Memories**.
- You can **search** within it.
- You can **place a Memory on your spell canvas** and practice it against Dojo dummies.
- It shows **how many Memories you have yet to uncover**.

That last point turns the Vault into a completion tracker. If you are hunting the 100+ launch Memories, the Vault tells you whether a gap is a missing discovery or a Memory you simply have not tried in a build.

### Vault Workflow

1. **Open the Vault and search for the Memory family you care about.** If you are building around homing, search for the homing Memories rather than scrolling the whole list.
2. **Place the Memory on the canvas.** The Vault lets you move it into the spell canvas directly, so you do not need to reconstruct the Memory from memory.
3. **Walk into the Dojo and cast.** Test against a dummy before you spend a run slot on the idea.
4. **Check the undiscovered count before you leave.** If you are close to completing a family, note which enemy or region you have not farmed recently.

The Vault does not make Memories stronger. It makes your existing collection legible, which is a different kind of power: you stop rebuilding the same three spells because you forgot the fourth component existed.

## The Seven New Memories (Official Descriptions)

The update adds seven Memories. The official devlog gives one-line descriptions, reproduced here without embellishment:

| Memory | Official description | Practical reading |
|--------|----------------------|-------------------|
| **Alliance of Forces** | "combining elemental powers for devastating results" | An elemental-combination piece; test it next to your existing element components |
| **Full Circle** | "bringing a new type of movement" | A movement-behavior Memory, not a direct damage modifier |
| **Walled City** | "triggering on any critical hit damage" | A crit-triggered effect; pairs with crit-focused canvases |
| **Fully Focused** | "rewarding your spellcrafting canvas designs" | A design-dependent payoff; the arrangement matters more than the component count |
| **Way Back** | homing quality | One of three homing Memories |
| **Aim High** | homing quality | One of three homing Memories |
| **Easy Prey** | homing quality | One of three homing Memories |

The exact numbers behind "devastating," "rewarding," and "homing" are **not published**. Test them in the Dojo and write down what changes: damage, projectile path, cast time, or area. Do not assume the three homing Memories behave identically; the devlog groups them by quality, not by effect.

## A Repeatable Spell-Testing Method

The Dojo and Vault are only useful if you test like a scientist. A loop that works:

1. **Write the question first.** "Does moving the ice component next to fire still produce steam when Full Circle is on the canvas?" A vague test produces a vague result.
2. **Change one variable.** Swap one Memory or move one component. If you change three things, you cannot attribute the result.
3. **Test in the Dojo, not in a run.** The Dojo removes enemy pressure, which is exactly what you want when measuring a spell's behavior.
4. **Record the result immediately.** Note the canvas layout, the dummy type, and what changed. The combination space is too large to remember.
5. **Promote the build to a run only after it survives a dummy test.** A spell that looks strong against a stationary target can still fall apart against a Rift-spawned pack.

This method also solves the Early Access problem: balance changes. A build you tested and documented is easy to re-test after a patch; a build you "remember working" is not.

## Common Mistakes

- **Treating the Dojo as a damage contest.** It is a lab, not a leaderboard. A dummy parse tells you output; it does not tell you whether the spell is usable while dodging.
- **Ignoring the Vault's undiscovered count.** It is the closest thing the game has to a Memory checklist.
- **Spending all Echo Fragments on one dummy type.** Configurations are meant to answer different questions.
- **Assuming the new Memories are automatically better.** New does not mean stronger; the update adds options, not a new tier.
- **Testing with a full endgame canvas.** Start from a simple layout so you can feel what the new Memory actually does.

## What Is Still Unconfirmed

- Exact Echo Fragment and Ancient Lesson prices
- The full list of dummy types and Dojo configurations
- Where Ancient Lessons drop
- The numeric effects of all seven new Memories
- Whether Dojo testing consumes resources or is free once unlocked

The safest reading is the official one: the basement is a practice space, the Vault is a codex, and the currencies unlock configurations. Everything beyond that should be verified in your own build before it goes into a guide.
