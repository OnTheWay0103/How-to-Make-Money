# Echoes of Mystralia vs Mages of Mystralia — What's New in the Sequel

**Echoes of Mystralia is a sequel to *Mages of Mystralia* (2017)** — same developer, same world, same spellcrafting DNA — but it is not the same kind of game. The original was a story-driven action-adventure; the sequel is a **spellcrafting ARPG roguelite** built around runs, a new protagonist, and an evolved version of the award-winning magic system that made the first game famous. If you're a fan of the original wondering what carried over and what changed, this guide is for you.

> **Pre-release note:** Echoes of Mystralia enters Steam Early Access on **August 11, 2026**. Details marked **TBD** have not been confirmed by Borealys Games.

## The Connection: Borealys Games

Both games come from **Borealys Games**, the Montreal-based indie studio. *Mages of Mystralia* launched in **May 2017** after a successful Kickstarter, and its spellcrafting system earned real recognition — **Best of PAX South 2017** (Hardcore Gamer), Best Indie Game at PAX East 2016 (Gamer Nexus), and nominations including the SXSW Gamer's Voice Award. Borealys has consistently described Echoes of Mystralia as **evolving that award-winning spellcrafting system** — the sequel is, first and foremost, a magic-system game.

## What Carries Over: The Spellcrafting Philosophy

The original's magic was its identity: instead of learning spells from a list, you discovered **runes with magical properties** and combined them — in millions of possible arrangements — into custom spells that the game rendered dynamically. Players were the designers.

Echoes of Mystralia keeps that philosophy and rebuilds the mechanics around **Memories**:

- You **harvest Memories from defeated enemies** (the original's runes were found in the world; the sequel's memories come from combat)
- You **forge and customize spells** by reordering and combining Memories
- The developer promises **"millions of possible combinations"** — the same marketing language, and the same core promise
- Showcased results include **lightning bolts, freezing tornadoes, and fire vortices** — the dynamic, player-built spell variety the original pioneered

For fans of the original, the muscle memory transfers: experiment constantly, trust your own designs over presets, and treat the magic system as the game.

## What Changes: Story and Protagonist

### Zia → Mazarim

The original followed **Zia**, a young girl born with magic in a kingdom where magic was forbidden. She fled, found allies, and became a mage — a coming-of-age story written by **Ed Greenwood** (creator of the Forgotten Realms).

Echoes of Mystralia's protagonist is **Mazarim, a Watcher** — a sworn guardian of the **Cycle of Memories** and the **World's Memory** itself. Where Zia was an outsider discovering magic, Mazarim is an established protector facing a crisis: a mysterious **Rift** is opening and threatening to consume all of Mystralia.

### A Darker World

Borealys describes the sequel's world as the lands of Mystralia **haunted by the past, with peoples scarred by war and exploitation**. The original had its share of darkness, but Echoes of Mystralia's enemies reflect the new tone — **Rift-spawned foes** and "**Shadows of the Past**" — as do its bosses: massive, ancient **primordial Guardians**.

## What Changes: Genre and Structure

This is the biggest difference. *Mages of Mystralia* was a linear action-adventure: explore the world, solve puzzles, progress the story, fight scripted bosses.

Echoes of Mystralia is a **roguelite**:

- **Runs through regions** — each run plays through procedurally varied territory
- **The Azimuth Temple hub** — between runs you return to the hub to upgrade permanent skills, unlock new combat options, and build relationships with fellow Watchers
- **Death is a loop, not a failure** — failed runs still feed permanent progression
- **3 regions and 3 Guardians at Early Access launch** — the confirmed launch scope

If you loved the original's world and story but prefer structured campaigns, the shift to roguelite structure is worth weighing. If you loved the original's magic system and wished there was more of it, the sequel's design is aimed directly at you.

## What's Confirmed About the New Magic System

- **12 spell forms** at Early Access launch (4 in the free demo) — the base templates of spellcrafting
- **110 Memories** at launch (50 in the demo) — the components you harvest and combine
- **Fire, ice, and lightning** — the element trio across official material
- **Memories are reordered and combined** — arrangement changes what a spell does
- **The demo save transfers to Early Access** — your progress follows you (Echo Fragments, the demo currency, reset by design)

The full mechanics — how Memory placement maps to spell behavior, hybrid element effects, rarity systems — are **TBD** until launch.

## Should You Play the Sequel If You Loved the Original?

**Yes, almost certainly** — with honest caveats:

**Play it for:**
- The evolved spellcrafting system — the original's best feature, rebuilt
- The same developer's craft — Borealys' polish carried the first game
- The lore — the Cycle of Memories, the Rift, and the haunted lands of Mystralia are natural continuations of the world

**Understand before buying:**
- It's a roguelite, not a story campaign — expect runs, resets, and permanent progression instead of a linear narrative
- It's Early Access — content arrives in waves, and balance will change
- The story writer for the sequel has not been announced (TBD)

**The cheapest test:** play the free demo. It contains the Ash Forest, the first Guardian, and the crafting sandbox — enough to know within an hour whether the magic system speaks to you. Your demo save transfers to Early Access, so nothing is wasted.

## Fan Questions, Honest Answers

### Is Mages of Mystralia 2 the same as Echoes of Mystralia?

Yes — Echoes of Mystralia is the direct sequel to Mages of Mystralia, developed by the same studio. It was announced at gamescom 2024.

### Does Zia appear in Echoes of Mystralia?

**TBD** — no cast list has been announced. Mazarim is the confirmed protagonist; whether characters from the original appear is unknown.

### Do I need to play Mages of Mystralia first?

**No** — the game is designed as a standalone experience. But playing the original (available on Steam and consoles) enriches the context: the spellcrafting philosophy, the world, and the studio's lineage all make more sense having played it.

### Is the spellcrafting the same system?

**No, it's an evolution.** The philosophy is identical (player-designed spells, millions of combinations), but the mechanics are rebuilt: Memories harvested from enemies replace world-found runes, the game structure is roguelite, and the form/Memory layering is new.
