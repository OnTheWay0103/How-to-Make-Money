# Difficulty & Settings Guide — No Modes, a Real Curve

Does Echoes of Mystralia have difficulty options? **No difficulty selector has been announced** — no Easy/Normal/Hard menu exists in any official material. Instead, the difficulty is **designed into the structure**: an action-heavy combat model, three regions confirmed to progress in difficulty, and a roguelite loop where death resets a run but permanent upgrades at the hub make each subsequent run easier. This guide covers what is confirmed about difficulty and settings, the real difficulty knobs you control, and the settings status you can verify in the free demo before Early Access launches on **August 11, 2026**.

> **EA Launch note:** Anything marked **TBD** has not been confirmed by Borealys Games, and anything marked **[EA — subject to change]** depends on the launch build — including the difficulty feel, which Borealys has said will be balance-tuned during Early Access.

## Does It Have Difficulty Options? (Honest Answer)

As of this writing, **no difficulty levels have been announced.** There is no confirmed Easy mode, no difficulty slider, and no accessibility difficulty options. The game's difficulty is instead communicated through its design statements:

- The combat is officially described as **"action-heavy"** — rapid, fast-paced, and tactical, rewarding quick reflexes and precise movement
- **The three launch regions are confirmed to progress in difficulty** — the first (the Ash Forest) is the tutorial-shaped entry, and the later regions are harder by design
- **Death is a loop, not a failure** — runs end, you return to the Azimuth Temple hub, and permanent upgrades there make future runs easier

What this means practically: difficulty exists, but it is a **curve and a loop**, not a menu option. Whether Borealys adds difficulty settings during Early Access is **[EA — subject to change]** — nothing has been announced.

## The Real Difficulty Knobs (Confirmed)

Since there is no difficulty menu, these are the levers the game actually gives you:

1. **Permanent hub upgrades** — spend progress at the **Azimuth Temple** between runs; this is the confirmed answer to any wall
2. **Spell crafting quality** — your crafted loadout determines your effective power more than anything else; tuned Memory combinations outperform default spells
3. **Element coverage** — one spell per element (fire, ice, lightning) covers more situations while match-ups are unknown
4. **Practice in the fight challenges** — the 4 repeatable challenges are a zero-risk place to learn elite-style fights and test loadouts
5. **Route discipline** — entering a boss arena at full HP with a clean run is a huge advantage; treat earlier fights as an HP economy

In roguelite terms: **the game's difficulty is tuned around your progression**, so a player who invests hub upgrades and crafting knowledge experiences a much gentler curve than one who rushes.

## Performance: What You Can Verify Today

The demo is your benchmark before you spend anything:

- **Minimum specs** are published (quad-core CPU, 8 GB RAM — see the [System Requirements Guide](/guides/system-requirements) for the full table)
- **Particle-heavy spell effects** are the main GPU stressor — dense spell stacks are exactly where frame dips appear, so test the fire vortices and lightning-heavy fights in the demo
- If the demo runs comfortably on your machine at your target settings, the EA build's baseline should behave similarly — with the caveat that the launch build adds content and may change performance (**[EA — subject to change]**)
