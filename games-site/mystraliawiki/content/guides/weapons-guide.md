# Weapons Guide — Your Spells Are Your Arsenal in Mystralia

Does Echoes of Mystralia have weapons? **No weapon system has been announced** — no swords, staves, bows, or any equipment layer. The Steam description is explicit about what your arsenal is: **"Memories are your only weapon against the Shadows of the Past."** In this game, your weapon list is your **spell loadout**, and your weapon crafting is the **spell crafting system**. This guide explains what "weapons" actually means in Echoes of Mystralia, what the confirmed arsenal looks like, and how to plan your loadout in the **Early Access build (launched August 11, 2026)**.

> **Early Access note:** Echoes of Mystralia is in Steam Early Access (launched August 11, 2026). Anything marked **TBD** is not confirmed by Borealys Games, and anything marked **[EA — subject to change]** depends on the current build.

## The Confirmed Answer: No Weapon System

Borealys Games has confirmed exactly one combat toolkit:

1. **Starting spells** — the base templates of your spells (16 at Early Access, 4 in the demo)
2. **Memories** — the components you harvest from enemies and combine into spells
3. **Elements** — 7 at launch, with fire, ice, and lightning among those shown, woven through the Memories you craft
4. **Permanent hub upgrades** — long-term power at the Azimuth Temple between runs

There is no weapon loot, no stat-stick item, no vendor selling better swords. Earlier versions of this wiki described 15+ weapons with stats and drop locations — **that catalog was fabricated and removed.** Any pre-launch list of "weapon locations" circulating elsewhere is fiction; there is no official source for it.

## Your "Weapon Types": The Starting Spells

If starting spells are your weapon classes, the confirmed roster at launch is **16 spells** (4 in the demo). A starting spell is the base template you build on — the framework a crafted spell takes. Which of the 16 are available, and how each behaves, is being documented from the EA build; only the showcased examples are confirmed:

| Showcased spell | What it demonstrates |
|-----------------|----------------------|
| **Lightning bolts** | Fast, direct single-target damage |
| **Freezing tornadoes** | Area control, slowing and freezing behavior |
| **Fire vortices** | Sustained area damage around a zone |

The pattern is the same as a weapon lineup: some spells hit hard and single-target, some control space, some burn entire packs. The difference is that **you build each one** from Memories — the form is a starting point, not the finished weapon.

## Your "Damage Types": Elements

The game's **7 elements** work like a damage-type system; fire, ice, and lightning are the ones showcased:

- **Fire** — showcased as vortices and sustained area effects
- **Ice** — showcased as freezing tornadoes; control-focused
- **Lightning** — showcased as bolts; fast, direct damage

One warning that matters in Early Access: **elemental match-ups (fire beats X, etc.) are TBD.** Borealys has not confirmed any rock-paper-scissors system, and the earlier counter tables on this wiki were removed as fabricated. Until the EA build verifies status effects and match-ups, treat element choice as a playstyle decision, not a counter-picking minigame. See the [Spell Crafting Guide](/guides/spell-crafting-guide) for the honest state of the element system.

## Your "Gearing": Memory Crafting

In most ARPGs you upgrade by finding better weapons. In Echoes of Mystralia you upgrade by **crafting better spells**:

- **Harvest Memories from enemies** — every foe you defeat is a potential drop
- **Forge and customize spells from Memories** — you build, not select, your arsenal
- **Reorder and combine** — arrangement is part of the design space
- **"Millions of possible combinations"** — the developer's own description of the build space

The confirmed placement rule is the closest thing the game has to weapon modding: **where you place each component in your spell grid changes what the spell actually does.** Official examples: fire placed next to ice becomes **steam**; lightning placed behind a homing component **chases enemies**. Two players holding the same 4 spell forms can walk into a fight with completely different arsenals — that is the entire point of the system.

## The Hub as Your Armory

Between runs, the **Azimuth Temple** hub fills the armory role: **upgrade permanent skills, unlock new combat options, and prepare your loadout** for the next run. The hub is where your "weapon rack" lives — and because it is confirmed that even failed runs feed permanent progression, your arsenal grows no matter how a run ends.

## Which "Weapons" for Which Build

There are no classes in Echoes of Mystralia — your build is your class, and your loadout is your weapon set. Mapping the confirmed archetype directions (see the [Character & Playstyle Guide](/guides/character-playstyle-guide)) to what the showcase implies:

| Build direction | Weapon-style picks | What to look for |
|-----------------|--------------------|------------------|
| **Damage racer** | Lightning-style direct damage | High single-target forms, damage-focused Memory arrangements |
| **Controller** | Ice-style area control | Freezing, slowing, and zone behaviors |
| **Clear-speed farmer** | Fire-style area damage + chain effects | Wide coverage for fast Memory harvesting |
| **Survivor** | Defensive arrangements | Space control and survival-focused hub upgrades |

**Practical early rule:** keep **fire, ice, and lightning coverage** in your loadout until match-ups and status effects are verified — with 7 elements at launch, coverage beats specialization in an unverified meta.

## Could Weapons Appear Later? [EA — subject to change]

Nothing in official announcements suggests a gear layer is coming. The confirmed scope of Early Access is **gameplay-focused updates, balance tuning, and completing the endgame** — all of which concern the confirmed systems, not an equipment overhaul. It is possible Borealys adds weapon-adjacent systems during EA; it is not announced. Plan your power growth around Memories and crafting, and treat any "weapons coming in EA update X" rumor as speculation.
