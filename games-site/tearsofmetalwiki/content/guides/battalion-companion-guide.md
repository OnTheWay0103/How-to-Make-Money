# Tears of Metal Battalion & Companion Guide — Soldiers, Permadeath and Unlocks

> **Applies to Early Access / Updated: September 16, 2026**

> **Scope:** in-run battalion management — the soldiers who march with you, permanent soldier progression, permadeath, and the Companion system. The **between-run, army-side** permanent upgrades (the Barracks, the Statue currency, Commander roster expansion and Companion slots) are covered in the [Barracks & Statues Guide](/guides/barracks-statues-guide).

Your battalion is your army. These Scottish soldiers accompany you on every expedition, and unlike the heroes they can be lost for good. Managing that army — knowing what the troop families actually are, protecting soldiers you have invested in, and understanding how Companions differ from ordinary allies — is one of the most consequential skills in Tears of Metal.

---

## What the sources actually name

Tears of Metal's Early Access sources describe your army with several different vocabularies, and it is worth separating them before planning anything around them.

| Layer | What it tells you | Where it comes from |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|
| **Paper Cult's store page** | You command a "Scottish battalion"; your **soldiers** gain permanent skills between campaigns | Official Steam store page, app 1913120 |
| **Paper Cult's patch notes** | The **Archers'** Volley is an Ally Command; Companion and command stats are read on the **Army selection** screen; allies have **levels** and **ranks** and can be **dismissed** | Official patch notes, July 24 / August 1 / September 8, 2026 |
| **Community guides and reviewers** | The troop families are **Infantry, Spearmen and Archers**, each with its own Ally Command that can be trained at the Barracks | Whisper of the House, Steam reviews |

The practical consequence: **Infantry, Spearmen and Archers are the troop families your Ally Commands are issued to.** Only the Archers' command has been named by the developers themselves (*"the Archers' Volley Ally Command"*), but community guides and Steam reviewers use all three terms consistently, and the Barracks sells training per troop type. This page uses that vocabulary and nothing narrower.

⚠️ **What this page deliberately does not do:** publish a soldier class stat table, per-class HP values, skill-tree maxima, per-act recruitment counts, or a recruitment-building level table. Paper Cult has never published any of those, and the community sources checked for this page do not contain them either. If you see exact numbers of that kind anywhere, treat them as unverified.

⚠️ **A note on older class labels:** some pages on this site predate this vocabulary check and describe your troops with class names that appear in none of the sources listed in this guide's source block. Those labels are unverified — they are not a second, separate roster alongside Infantry, Spearmen and Archers. Where the two descriptions conflict, the source-tiered table above is the one this guide stands behind.

## Ordinary Allies and Companions are two different systems

The single most useful distinction in army management is the one the game never spells out:

| | Ordinary Allies | Companions |
|---|---|---|
| **What they are** | Procedurally recruited line soldiers | Handcrafted named specialists *[Unconfirmed — category described by community guides]* |
| **How you get them** | Recruited during a run, or from settlement upgrades | Unlocked through campaign objectives and events |
| **Progression** | Permanent per-soldier skills that survive as long as the soldier does | Fixed identity; upgraded through the Barracks Companion tiers |
| **If lost** | Gone permanently | Not replaceable in the same run |

Your soldiers are the core fighting force and they grow: Paper Cult's own store page states that *"Your soldiers gain permanent skills between campaigns."* Companions sit on a different axis — they arrive through objectives and are upgraded through the Barracks, not through combat experience.

## Recruiting soldiers

Recruitment happens on two tracks:

- **During a campaign.** You pick up troops as you advance. Paper Cult ships fixes to how allies behave in exactly this context — August 1, 2026: *"Improved the survivability of level 1 allies."* That line also tells you something useful: allies have **levels**, and level 1 allies were dying faster than intended before that patch.
- **Between campaigns, from your settlement.** The store page lists settlement expansion as a headline system: *"Expand your settlement between campaigns to gain access to new shops and buy permanent upgrades."* Settlement buildings that expand recruitment exist, and the [Village Upgrade Guide](/guides/village-upgrade-guide) covers the building-by-building view.

At the **War Preparation** screen you manage the resulting roster. Paper Cult's August 1 patch references *"the two unit Dismiss buttons"* filling at different rates there, and a separate fix for *"units disappearing between sessions after being ranked up without entering another level"* — so ranking a unit up is a real action that happens on that screen.

Where banners spawn, how many appear per act, and what any recruitment building unlocks at each level are **not published** *[Unconfirmed]*. Treat any per-act banner count you read elsewhere as unverified.

## Permanent progression: what is confirmed

Permanent soldier skills are the game's stated long-term investment, and the developer line is unambiguous: *"Your soldiers gain permanent skills between campaigns. Recruit, upgrade and fight alongside them as they become essential assets in your upcoming battles."*

Three things follow with confidence:

1. **Skills persist between campaigns** — for that soldier, not for your account.
2. **Soldiers can be ranked up**, and rank-ups are tracked across sessions (Paper Cult shipped a fix for rank-ups failing to persist).
3. **Losses are permanent.** Community sources are blunt about it — 2UpSkill: *"Procedural soldiers can die permanently. They're gone. Forever."* Paper Cult's own fix for level 1 ally survivability exists because that permanence was biting too hard.

What is **not** published: the skill list, how many points a soldier earns, per-skill caps, or any per-level value. None of that appears in Paper Cult's patch notes or on the store page, so this guide does not reproduce a skill table. If you are planning around a specific skill, read it off the unit's own panel in your build — that is the only authoritative source.

**The rule that survives all of this:** prioritise keeping soldiers who have already accumulated skills. A recruit is replaceable in a way that a promoted soldier is not, and the game gives you a **Dismiss** control at the War Preparation screen — using it on an uninvested recruit costs you nothing structural.

## Companions: what is confirmed, and what is not

Paper Cult has named exactly one companion in the entire Early Access run of patch notes.

On August 7, 2026 the developers teased an addition, and on August 13, 2026 it shipped:

> *"Aodh, the Vigil, an old scholar hauling a book heavier than the armor of most men. Once he's convinced to march with you, he keeps watch when a fight breaks out. The first blows never quite land the way the enemy intends!"*

That is an official description of a **damage-mitigation opening** — the first enemy blows in a fight are blunted — not a stat block. Paper Cult has not published his unlock condition, so treat any specific unlock route you read as unverified *[Unconfirmed]*.

The patch notes for that build list him as the game's **first named companion**, which is the single most important fact for anyone hunting a companion list: the roster started small and has been growing. The September 8, 2026 patch reinforces how early this system still is — it fixed a Companion *"not appearing correctly in the Codex"* and relaxed the Barracks Companion tier prerequisite, both of which are changes you make to a system that is still filling out.

**A note on companion lists.** Community sources do not agree on a roster, and none of them claims to be complete. 2UpSkill's dedicated companion guide describes the *category* in detail but names no companion at all. Whisper of the House names one: **Marriott, leader of Clan McGregor**, unlocked by completing Tools of the Trade. Steam reviewers, writing after the August 13 patch, report that the current build offers only a couple of companions and ask for more.

This page therefore lists **no companion roster**. A list of named companions with per-character unlock conditions and ability values cannot be sourced to Paper Cult or to any community guide checked for this page, and a list that turns out to be invented is worse than no list at all. If you want the current roster, the Codex in your own build is the authority.

## Permadeath management

### What you are protecting

When a soldier is lost, the skills they accumulated go with them. That is the whole stake: not the unit, but the investment.

### Rules that survive contact with the game

1. **Protect the promoted, not the newest.** A soldier with skills banked is worth more than a fresh recruit with better starting stats. Spend the recruit first.
2. **Use the Dismiss control deliberately.** The War Preparation screen lets you cut units. Cutting an uninvested recruit to make room is a legitimate move; the mistake is holding a bloated roster you cannot protect in the field.
3. **Withdraw damaged soldiers.** A low-HP soldier left in the line is a permanent loss waiting to happen. Pull them out and let a fresh unit hold.
4. **Spend Ally Commands on the troop type that is already reaching its target.** Community guides converge on this: Infantry pressure central groups, Spearmen answer protected or shielded enemies, and Archers keep working while you reposition. Upgrading a command you do not issue is the classic wasted purchase.
5. **Do not send units on suicide errands to save resources.** Healing and equipment are recoverable; a promoted soldier is not.

### When a loss is acceptable

Fresh recruits with nothing banked are the units you can afford to spend. Using them to hold a chokepoint, to absorb an opening attack, or to probe an unknown room is a reasonable trade — that is what makes the promoted core survivable.

## Allies in co-op

Tears of Metal runs solo or up to four players, and the store page frames the whole campaign around it: *"Fight solo or up to 4-player co-op to reclaim your island alongside your Scottish battalion."*

One co-op tradeoff is worth planning around before you invite a second player. Steam reviewers report that **the more players are in a campaign, the fewer companions and commanders each player can bring** — so a four-player run is not four full armies. If your plan depends on a specific Companion being present, confirm the party size first. The exact scaling is not published *[Unconfirmed]*.

Each player manages their own battalion, and Ally Commands are issued per troop type rather than globally — which is why two players leaning on the same command branch waste half their Barracks investment. The [Co-op Guide](/guides/co-op-guide) covers how to split the Command branches across a party.

## FAQ

**How many companions does the game have?**
Paper Cult has named one: Aodh, the Vigil, added August 13, 2026 and described in the patch notes as the game's first named companion. Community sources are incomplete and disagree — 2UpSkill's companion guide names none, Whisper of the House names Marriott, leader of Clan McGregor. Steam reviewers writing after the August patch report only a couple in the current build. Your Codex is the reliable count *[Unconfirmed]*.

**Is there an official soldier class list?**
No. Paper Cult's notes name the Archers' Volley Ally Command and the Army selection screen, and community sources describe the troop families as Infantry, Spearmen and Archers. No official class stat table exists.

**Do my soldiers keep their skills if I die?**
Yes — permanent skills are the point of the system, and the store page states soldiers *"gain permanent skills between campaigns."* What you lose is the soldier, if the soldier dies.

**Do soldier upgrades apply to all players in co-op?**
Not confirmed. Community reporting on whether shared village and Barracks upgrades apply to guests as well as the host is unresolved, and Paper Cult has not documented it *[Unconfirmed]*. Confirm in your own lobby.

**Is "Retreat" a real command?**
This guide does not list it. Ally Commands are tied to troop families, and the only one Paper Cult has named by name is the Archers' Volley. A standalone free retreat ping is not documented anywhere in the official patch notes *[Unconfirmed]*.
