# Tears of Metal Battalion & Companion Guide — Soldiers, Permadeath, and Unlocks

> **Applies to Early Access / Updated: August 11, 2026**

Your battalion is your army. These procedurally generated Scottish soldiers accompany you on every expedition, and unlike the heroes, they can die permanently. Managing your battalion effectively — recruiting the right soldiers, pairing them with the right hero, protecting your veterans, and unlocking unique companions — is one of the most important skills in Tears of Metal.

---

## Battalion Overview

Your battalion starts small (2-3 soldiers) but can grow to 8+ soldiers by the end of a successful run. Each soldier has:

- **Name and rank** — Determines base stats (higher rank = higher stats)
- **Health bar** — Soldiers that take fatal damage die permanently
- **Skills** — Permanent bonuses unlocked through combat experience
- **Class** — Determines role and equipment

### Soldier Classes

| Class | Role | Base HP | Damage Type | Best Use |
|-------|------|---------|-------------|----------|
| Pikeman | Frontline melee | Medium | Physical (thrust) | Chokepoint defense, interrupting attacks |
| Grunt | General melee | Medium | Physical (slash) | All-purpose, flanking |
| Shield Bearer | Tank | High | Physical (bash) | **Best class** — holds aggro, blocks chokepoints |
| Trebuchet Spotter | Ranged | Low | Physical (projectile) | **Support only** — marks targets for bonus damage |
| Blood-Steel Knight | Elite melee | High | Physical + Corruption | **Late game** — high damage, high survivability |

**Recruitment reality:** banners appear throughout each act (3 in Act 1, 4 in Act 2, 2 in Act 3), and the Recruitment Tent in the Village offers more between runs. Shield Bearers only appear from Act 2 onward and at Tent Level 2+; Blood-Steel Knights are rare Act 3 / Tent Level 3+ prospects.

## How to Recruit Soldiers

### During Expeditions

Recruitment banners appear in specific locations throughout each act. Exact banner spots are community-mapped and have shifted between updates — the per-act counts and classes below are the reliable part:

| Act | Banners | Soldiers Available |
|-----|---------|-------------------|
| Act 1 | 3 banners (opening coastline) | Pikemen, Grunts |
| Act 2 | 4 banners (mid-campaign region) | Shield Bearers (rare), Grunts |
| Act 3 | 2 banners (The Scar approaches) | Blood-Steel Knights (rare), all classes |

### At the Village (Recruitment Tent)

Between runs, visit the Recruitment Tent in the Village. Higher tent levels unlock:
- **Level 1**: Basic Pikemen and Grunts
- **Level 2**: Shield Bearers and better starting stats
- **Level 3**: Elite prospects (potential Blood-Steel Knights after surviving 2 runs)
- **Level 4**: Guaranteed veteran with pre-existing skills

## Hero-Soldier Pairings

The right soldiers around the right hero turn the game's hardest acts into routine clears.

| Hero | Best Soldier Comp | Why |
|------|-------------------|-----|
| **Wallace** (longsword) | 2 Shield Bearers + 2 Pikemen | Wallace's balanced kit needs a stable frontline; Shield Bearers hold aggro while he works the longsword's reliable parry rhythm |
| **Ruadh Stonecrusher** (hammer) | 1 Shield Bearer + 2 Trebuchet Spotters + 1 Grunt | Ruadh's slow, positioning-heavy swings need marked targets — Spotters feed him bonus damage while the Shield Bearer holds the line |
| **Brienne** (unarmed) | 1 Shield Bearer + 3 Grunts | Brienne's fast attacks flank best; Grunts fill the flanks while the Shield Bearer anchors the middle |

**The universal rule:** every hero comp wants **at least one Shield Bearer**. It is the community's consensus best class — aggro holding and chokepoint blocking are the mechanics that keep your hero alive. The hero's job is damage; the battalion's job is not dying.

## Battalion Battlefield Roles

| Role | Soldier | Assignment |
|------|---------|------------|
| Anchor | Shield Bearer | Front center — holds the enemy line while the hero flanks |
| Flankers | Grunts | Split wide; punish enemies that overextend past the Shield Bearer |
| Interrupts | Pikemen | Watch enemy wind-ups; the thrust interrupts heavy attacks |
| Markers | Trebuchet Spotters | Stay back, mark priority targets for the hero's damage |
| Cleanup | Blood-Steel Knight | Late-fight: mop up the chokepoint after the main line breaks |

**Positioning tip:** assign every soldier a position before the fight — unassigned soldiers drift into chokepoints and die to cleaves that a Pikeman would have interrupted.

## Permanent Skill Progression

Soldiers gain XP from combat, which unlocks skill points. These skills are **permanent** for that soldier — they carry across runs as long as the soldier survives.

> **Numbers caveat:** the skill values below are community-documented approximations and have shifted between updates. Treat them as directional, not exact.

### Skill Trees

| Skill | Effect | Cost | Best For |
|-------|--------|------|----------|
| Iron Hide | +HP per level (max 3) | 1 point/level | Shield Bearers |
| Sharpened Steel | +damage per level (max 3) | 1 point/level | Pikemen, Blood-Steel Knights |
| Battle Hardened | +damage reduction per level (max 3) | 1 point/level | All classes |
| Swift Feet | +move speed per level (max 2) | 1 point/level | Grunts, flankers |
| Veteran's Instinct | +crit chance per level (max 2) | 2 points/level | Damage dealers |

### Priority Skill Allocation

| Soldier Class | First Skill | Second Skill | Third Skill |
|---------------|-------------|--------------|-------------|
| Shield Bearer | Iron Hide (Lv 2) | Battle Hardened (Lv 2) | Swift Feet (Lv 1) |
| Pikeman | Sharpened Steel (Lv 2) | Battle Hardened (Lv 1) | Veteran's Instinct (Lv 1) |
| Grunt | Battle Hardened (Lv 2) | Sharpened Steel (Lv 1) | Swift Feet (Lv 1) |
| Blood-Steel Knight | Iron Hide (Lv 3) | Sharpened Steel (Lv 3) | Veteran's Instinct (Lv 2) |

## Permadeath Management

### Understanding Permadeath

When a soldier's HP reaches 0, they are **dead permanently**. Their name, skills, and progress are gone. This is not a revival mechanic — it is irreversible.

### How to Protect Veterans

Veteran soldiers — those with 2+ skill points allocated — are the backbone of your late-game power. A veteran Shield Bearer with Iron Hide and Battle Hardened is significantly tankier than a fresh recruit.

**Protection strategies:**

| Strategy | How | Why It Works |
|----------|-----|--------------|
| Back-line positioning | Keep veterans behind fresh soldiers | Fresh units absorb first hits |
| Retreat command | Use the "Retreat" ping on a low-HP veteran | They disengage and return to your position |
| Shield Bearer escort | Assign a Shield Bearer to guard your veteran | Aggro draws away from the valuable unit |
| Heal between fights | Use healing banners and potions on low-HP soldiers | Prevent death by attrition |
| Skip hard fights | If a veteran is low HP, avoid optional elite encounters | Better to miss loot than lose a veteran |

### The Rules That Keep Veterans Alive

1. **Pull with the hero, not the battalion.** Let the hero take the first hit of a pack — soldiers aggro the whole group otherwise.
2. **Withdraw damaged soldiers.** A soldier at low HP is a liability; pull them back behind the Shield Bearer instead of letting them eat a combo.
3. **Roster discipline.** Carry a 5-6 soldier roster and rotate: two fresh soldiers per act keeps the veteran core intact.
4. **Rank protects.** Higher-rank soldiers have higher base stats — protect the veterans first; recruits are replaceable.
5. **Blood-Steel Knights are investments.** A Knight that survives 2 runs unlocks elite prospects at Tent Level 3 — losing one sets your roster back an act.

### When to Sacrifice Fresh Recruits

Fresh recruits (no skill points) are expendable. Use them for:
- **Chokepoint holds** — Block enemy paths while your veterans flank
- **Boss aggro** — Send them in first to eat the boss's opening attack
- **Suicide scouting** — Send them into unknown rooms to trigger traps
- **Reinforcement blockers** — Position them at spawn points to delay reinforcements

## Unique Companions

> **Community-reported section:** the unique companions below are reported by players, but Paper Cult has not published an official companion list. Unlock details and ability values are community-documented and may not be exact — verify in-game before planning a run around them.

Unique Companions are handcrafted characters unlocked through hidden objectives. Unlike standard soldiers, companions have unique abilities, dialogue, and cannot be replaced.

### Companion Unlock Locations

| Companion | Unlock Requirement (community-reported) | Class | Unique Ability |
|-----------|-------------------|-------|---------------|
| Fergus the Unyielding | Find his lost shield in the Act 1 ruins (behind a breakable wall) | Shield Bearer | "Unyielding" — resists stagger and blocks more effectively |
| Morag of the Mist | Complete Act 2 without losing any soldiers | Pikeman | "Mist Walker" — stealth-based first strike out of combat |
| Brochan the Piper | Play the bagpipe emote at hidden cairns (one per act) | Grunt | "Piper's March" — nearby soldiers attack faster |
| Ailsa the Cursed | Reach Act 3 with a Corruption Resist emblem equipped | Blood-Steel Knight | "Cursed Blades" — attacks apply Corruption damage over time |
| Torcall the Unseen | Find hidden scout markers scattered across the acts (exact collectible unconfirmed) | Trebuchet Spotter | "True Sight" — reveals hidden enemies and traps nearby |

### Companion Priority

| Priority | Companion | Why |
|----------|-----------|-----|
| 1 | Fergus the Unyielding | Best tank in the game; huge survivability boost |
| 2 | Ailsa the Cursed | Excellent damage for Act 3; Corruption DoT scales well |
| 3 | Brochan the Piper | Team-wide attack speed buff = more DPS for everyone |
| 4 | Morag of the Mist | Conditional but powerful; great for boss opening bursts |
| 5 | Torcall the Unseen | Mostly utility; useful for exploration but not combat |

### Tips for Unlocking Companions

- **Fergus is the easiest unlock** — The breakable wall is near the first village ruins in Act 1. Listen for the hollow sound when hitting walls.
- **Morag requires patience** — You must complete Act 2 without any soldier deaths. Consider running a smaller battalion if yours is large.
- **Brochan's cairns** — The bagpipe emote is found in a chest in Act 1. Cairns are marked by small rock piles with a faint blue glow.
- **Ailsa is the Act 3 reward** — Keep a Corruption Resist emblem equipped through the final act (see the [Corruption Guide](/guides/corruption-guide)).
- **Torcall is a collectible hunt** — Details are the least-confirmed part of this list; if the markers do not spawn, update the game and retry.

## Battalion Commands: When to Use Each

| Command | Bars | Strength | Best Use Case |
|---------|------|----------|---------------|
| Charge (single-bar) | 1 | Fast, moderate damage | Interrupting enemy attacks, quick burst |
| Charge (multi-bar) | 3 | Heavy damage, staggers | Boss stun windows, elite ganks |
| Shield Wall (single-bar) | 1 | Brief damage reduction | Surviving a single boss attack |
| Shield Wall (multi-bar) | 3 | Long duration, full immunity | Tanking Act 3 Corruption pulses |
| Volley (single-bar) | 1 | Quick ranged attack | Picking off low-HP enemies at range |
| Volley (multi-bar) | 3 | Heavy AoE barrage | Clearing a crowded room |
| Retreat | 0 | Immediate disengage | Saving a low-HP veteran from certain death |

### General Command Rules

- **Single-bar for speed** — Against fast enemies, single-bar commands are safer because the wind-up is nearly instant
- **Multi-bar for power** — Against bosses and slow enemies, multi-bar commands provide massive value
- **Do not overlap multi-bars** — Two players using multi-bar commands simultaneously split aggro and waste resources
- **Retreat is free** — Use it liberally. There is no cooldown and no resource cost

## Tactical Usage by Situation

### Chokepoint Defense
1. Position Shield Bearers at the chokepoint entrance
2. Pikemen behind them (Pikemen attack over Shield Bearers' heads)
3. Grunts on the flanks to catch enemies that break through
4. Use Shield Wall (single-bar) when enemies engage
5. Follow with Charge (single-bar) to clear the clump

### Ranged Threats (Trebuchet Spotters, Overseers)
1. Order all soldiers to hold position (default command)
2. Send Grunts as a flanking squad using the "Move" ping
3. Use Volley (single-bar) from your position to suppress
4. Once Grunts engage the ranged enemy, Charge (single-bar) with the main force

### Boss Opening
1. Send one Shield Bearer with Charge (single-bar) to draw aggro
2. Wait a moment for the boss to engage the Shield Bearer
3. Multi-bar Charge with all remaining soldiers
4. Follow up with your own attacks during the stagger

## Common Companion Mistakes

1. **No Shield Bearer.** A hero with only Grunts and Pikemen gets swarmed — the single most common battalion failure.
2. **Letting the Spotters tank.** Trebuchet Spotters have the lowest HP in the game; they're markers, not fighters.
3. **Running the same roster every act.** Soldiers get worn down; rotation is what keeps veterans alive to Act 3.
4. **Ignoring rank.** A fresh recruit with "better" class is still worse than a veteran with skills — skills are permanent bonuses earned through combat experience.
5. **Feeding heroes into chokepoints.** The [Heroes Tier List](/guides/heroes-tier-list) is clear: even S-tier Wallace dies to a surrounded pull. The battalion exists to prevent that.

## Synergy With Builds and Co-op

The companion system compounds with your hero build: Wallace's Pure Physical build (see the [Best Builds Guide](/guides/best-builds)) pairs best with the Shield Bearer wall, while Ruadh's stagger-based kit gains the most from Spotters' marked damage.
