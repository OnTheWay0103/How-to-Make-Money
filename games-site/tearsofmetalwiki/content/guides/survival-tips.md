# Tears of Metal Survival Tips — The Discipline That Keeps You Alive

**Tears of Metal Early Access / Updated: August 11, 2026**

Tears of Metal kills players in three ways: running out of stamina at the wrong moment, missing a parry window, and refusing to retreat. All three are discipline problems, not gear problems. This guide covers the survival fundamentals — the stamina economy, the parry timing table, elemental awareness, and the retreat rules — that keep your battalion and your hero intact through all three acts.

---

## 1. The Stamina Law (Everything Governed by One Bar)

| Action | Stamina Cost | Notes |
|--------|--------------|-------|
| Light Attack | Low | Fast, low damage, builds combo meter |
| Heavy Attack | High | Slow, high damage, breaks guard |
| Dodge Roll | Moderate | Brief invincibility frames (i-frames) |
| Sprint | Continuous drain | Cannot block while sprinting |
| Block | Per-second drain | Reduces incoming damage; chip damage still applies |
| Parry | Moderate | Short timing window; staggers the enemy on success |
| Weapon Art | Very high | Powerful, costly — use deliberately |

**The rule of thumb:** keep enough stamina in reserve to dodge or parry at any moment. With an empty bar you cannot dodge or parry, and a single mistake means eating a full combo. Practically:

1. **End your combos before the bar empties.** Two light attacks + a roll leaves you safe; a full combo + empty bar leaves you dead.
2. **Block costs more than you think.** Holding block through a four-hit combo drains a large chunk of the bar — a parry costs less and pays back.
3. **Sprint is a decision, not a habit.** Sprinting into a pack means no block for the first seconds of the fight.
4. **Keep a reserve, always.** When the bar runs low, disengage — the recovery is faster than the respawn.

## 2. Parry Windows by Enemy Type

Exact parry timings are not officially published — treat any specific window numbers you see online as community estimates. What is reliably reported:

| Enemy Type | Parry Timing | Practice Tip |
|------------|-------------|--------------|
| Humanoid (Grunts, Pikemen) | Generous — telegraphed swings | The best place to learn parrying |
| Beast enemies | Tight — attacks come fast | Parry the lunge, not the approach |
| Corrupted enemies | Deceptive — wind-ups look shorter than they are | Watch the weapon, not the aura |
| Bosses | Pattern-specific | Learn one combo at a time; dodge beats guessing |

**Practice rule:** spend your early Act 1 fights deliberately parrying — the timing is the difference between a free counter and a full combo on your health bar.

## 3. Elemental Awareness — What Is Confirmed

Elements in Tears of Metal come from your equipped emblems — **Fire, Freeze, Lightning, Corruption, and Arcane** are the emblem elements, and status effects (burn, slow/freeze, chain lightning) come from emblems rather than weapons. An official enemy weakness chart has not been published, and community reports are anecdotal rather than charted.

**Why this matters for survival:** the only widely confirmed element-versus-enemy interaction is the Corruption emblem dealing bonus damage to corrupted enemies in Act 3. Claims that matching elements shortens fights are community-reported, not official. Match your emblems (see the [Emblem & Artifact Guide](/guides/emblem-artifact-guide)) and keep a corruption answer ready for Act 3.

## 4. Threat-Level Awareness

| Threat Level | What It Means | Survival Response |
|--------------|---------------|-------------------|
| Low | New spawn zones | Farm freely, practice parries |
| Medium | Standard expedition | Normal discipline: stamina reserve, element match |
| High | Late act, corrupted zones | Retreat rules in effect (below) |
| Extreme | Act 3 Corruption Zone | Full loadout check: consumables, element match, veteran battalion |

The [Threat Level & Difficulty Guide](/guides/threat-level-difficulty-guide) explains how threat scales with act progression — the short version: high-threat zones demand the same fights you'd win at medium, but with zero margin for sloppy stamina.

## 5. The Retreat Rules (Survival's Most Underrated Skill)

1. **Retreat before the first battalion death, not after.** When a soldier drops critically low in a high-threat zone, that's the retreat signal — one death often chains into a wipe.
2. **Retreat is a direction, not a fight.** Disengage to a cleared area, heal, re-pull with fresh stamina. The enemy line does not chase far.
3. **Corrupted zones compound.** The [Corruption Guide](/guides/corruption-guide) details how corruption drains your battalion — corrupted zones are the #1 place players refuse to retreat and pay for it.
4. **Extraction over completion.** A failed objective with a living battalion beats a completed objective with a dead one. Salvage the run, keep the veterans.
5. **The low-HP rule for heroes.** If your hero is critically low with the stamina bar nearly empty — retreat. Those are the deaths that end runs.

## 6. Survival Gear and Consumable Checklist

| Item | Use | Priority |
|------|-----|----------|
| Salves / healing consumables | Post-hit recovery | Always carry 3+ |
| Stamina consumables | Emergency recovery | Carry 1-2 for boss fights |
| Anti-corruption consumables | Corrupted zones | Mandatory in Act 3 |
| Element-match emblems | Damage efficiency | Equip before the zone |

**Budget order:** consumables > weapon merges > defensive gear, for survival purposes — a weapon you can swing is worth more than armor you're too dead to wear. See the [Elite Enemy Guide](/guides/elite-enemy-guide) for where elites and captains drop the best loot.

---

## 7. The Survival Loop, Summarized

Enter every fight with a stamina reserve and a matched loadout → parry on the cues (generous for humanoids, tight for beasts) → retreat on the low-HP rules → restock consumables at the village → repeat. Master that loop and the game's optional challenges become the natural next step — the same discipline with the margin removed entirely.
