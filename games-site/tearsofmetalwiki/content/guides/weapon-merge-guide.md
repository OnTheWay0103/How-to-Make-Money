# Tears of Metal Weapon Merge Guide — Reinforced Tiers, AP, and Best Weapons per Hero

> **Applies to Early Access / Updated: August 11, 2026**

The weapon merge system is the heart of Tears of Metal weapon progression. Combine identical weapons into Reinforced versions, feed the result back into itself, and a common drop becomes a run-defining weapon. This guide covers how the merge system works, the duplicate-dismantling trap that wastes hundreds of weapons, where drops actually come from, how the Ability Point (AP) skill trees work, and the upgrade paths that make your triskelle and Dragon Stone Shards count.

---

## The Merge System Explained

The merge system lets you combine **two identical weapons** into a single, stronger version. Merging is done at the Smithy, and the result keeps the same base weapon — it just gets a higher Reinforced tier with bigger stat bonuses.

### Merge Tier Progression

| Tier | Combine | Result |
|------|---------|--------|
| Base | Default drop | Base |
| Reinforced+1 | Base + Base | Reinforced+1 |
| Reinforced+2 | Reinforced+1 + Reinforced+1 | Reinforced+2 |
| Reinforced+3 | Reinforced+2 + Reinforced+2 | Reinforced+3 |

> Stat bonuses increase with each Reinforced tier. Exact per-tier numbers are community-documented and have shifted between updates — treat any specific figure you see elsewhere as approximate until confirmed in-game.

### Merge Rules

- **Weapons must be identical** — Two Iron Greatswords merge; an Iron Greatsword and a Steel Greatsword do not
- **Rarity does not matter** — A Common + Uncommon of the same weapon merge fine
- **Both weapons must be the same tier** — Reinforced+1 + Base does not work
- **Smithy Level 1 required** — Merging is unavailable until you build the Smithy (see the [Village Upgrade Guide](/guides/village-upgrade-guide))

### The Exponential Cost

Each tier doubles the base weapons required: **2 → 4 → 8 → 16**. By Reinforced+3 you need 8 copies of one weapon (and a possible future Reinforced+4 would need 16). That is why specializing early matters — split your duplicates across two weapons and you will never finish either. Pick your endgame weapon by Act 2 and feed every duplicate you find into it.

## Why You Should NEVER Dismantle Duplicates

Dismantling a duplicate is the most common early-game mistake. It feels like free value, but every duplicate you scrap is a step backward in the merge system — and the penalty compounds. Dismantling two duplicates delays your Reinforced+1 by a full merge, and the gap widens with every tier.

**Rule of thumb:** only dismantle weapons you would never use at any tier. If a weapon could be your endgame pick or a stepping stone toward it, every copy is merge material. Between the exponential 2-4-8-16 costs and the fact that drops are the only way to restock, duplicates are rarer than the scrap value they hand you. A Reinforced+3 weapon is worth more than a dozen dismantles combined.

## Where Weapons Come From

Weapons drop from three main sources:

1. **Battlefield loot** — Elites, captains, and chests are the primary sources of weapon drops. Full-clearing zones (and breaking every crate and barrel) yields far more duplicates than boss-rushing
2. **The village merchant** — A village merchant sells weapons directly for **triskelle**. Its stock and prices improve as you upgrade the village, so check back after every major upgrade
3. **Co-op squads** — Full 4-player squads get **higher drop rates**, which makes the merge system dramatically faster in multiplayer. If you are chasing Reinforced+2 or +3, coordinate a full squad

## Best Weapons per Hero

Each hero's kit leans on one weapon family. Because specific named weapons are not yet publicly documented, treat these as type-level guidance: the "best" weapon is the highest Reinforced tier you can sustain within the right family.

| Hero | Weapon Family | Why It's Best | Target Tier |
|------|---------------|---------------|-------------|
| **Wallace** (longsword) | Longsword | Balanced damage, reach, and the most forgiving block/parry timing in the game — his kit is built around it. Prioritize the heavy-attack branch of its skill tree for crowd-cleaving swings | Reinforced+2 minimum |
| **Brienne** (unarmed) | Unarmed | Brienne fights unarmed, so her damage comes from attack rate and per-hit emblems/artifacts rather than weapon tiers. Stack attack speed and per-hit effects instead of hunting duplicate weapons | N/A — build around emblems |
| **Ruadh Stonecrusher** (giant hammer) | Great hammer | Highest single-hit damage and the game's best stagger. Hammer stagger interrupts captain and boss attacks outright | Reinforced+2 minimum, Reinforced+3 for boss-rushing |

**Merging rule of thumb:** specialize by Act 2. Reinforced+3 needs 8 base copies, so feed every duplicate in your chosen family into one primary weapon. For Wallace and Ruadh that means longsword and hammer drops respectively; for Brienne, duplicate weapons matter less than emblem and artifact stacking (see the [Best Builds Guide](/guides/best-builds) for hero-specific loadouts).

## Hidden AP Costs: Always Price the Full Branch

Ability Points (AP) unlock weapon skill tree nodes, and AP does **not** carry over between runs — every point is per-run. The trap is that late-game skills require purchasing **all connected prerequisites** before they unlock. The cost displayed on a capstone is a lie until you check the branch.

**The rule:** before committing a single point, count every prerequisite in the chain and compare it against the AP you can realistically earn this run. Spending points on Arm Shot and Leg Shot without reaching the capstone is AP burned on a branch you cannot finish — a full branch can cost dozens of AP. If the branch does not fit, invest in a cheaper branch you can cap instead.

**AP spending rules that save runs:**

- Always calculate the full branch cost before spending
- Save AP until you can unlock at least two nodes in a branch — partial investment rarely pays off
- Prioritize branches that end with a unique effect over flat stat bonuses
- Rush the capstone of your preferred branch before spending on secondary branches

## Top Build Paths: Pure Physical Damage

The safest and most consistent build path for heavy frontline heroes is **Pure Physical Damage**:

- **Attack Power** — flat damage scaling on every hit, no conditions
- **Impact** — stagger and interrupt value that keeps enemies from swinging back
- **Guard Break** — shreds block and armor, speeding up every engagement
- **Armor Penetration** — counters the armor-heavy enemies of Act 2 and beyond

This path has no proc conditions, no elemental counters, and no reliance on emblem RNG — it works with any weapon drop and every duplicate you merge into it. Elemental and emblem builds can spike higher, but Pure Physical is the safest AP and merge investment for frontline heroes. ## Dragon Stone Shards and the Material Economy

Every power system in Tears of Metal runs on materials. The ones that matter for weapons:

| Material | Source | Use |
|----------|--------|-----|
| **Weapon duplicates** | Battlefield drops, chests, village merchant | The merge system — the permanent-in-run investment |
| **Triskelle** | Expedition loot, selling unwanted items | Village upgrades, merchant weapons, consumables — banks between runs win or lose |
| **Dragon Stone Shards** | Act bosses and endgame expeditions | Premium village-upgrade currency (Armory Level 3+, Smithy Level 2+, and the Mystic Altar consume them) |

**Dragon Stone Shard rules:** shards are the scarcest material in the game. How you spend them matters more than how many you have:

- **Village upgrades first** — permanent, every run benefits
- **Merchant weapons second** — only if the weapon is your confirmed endgame pick
- **Bank them between runs** — shard income is limited (a handful per act clear, community-reported), and village costs rise steeply at higher levels

**The upgrade order that wastes nothing:**

1. **Merge first, tier second** — merges define your weapon for the run; village upgrades are the permanent part. Spend permanent currency before temporary currency
2. **Specialize one weapon** — the exponential merge math punishes split investment
3. **Bank Dragon Stone Shards for Armory Level 3** — the Armory's inter-run artifact carry is the biggest permanent power spike in the village
4. **Don't tier-craft junk weapons** — scrap spent on a weapon you are not merging toward is scrap wasted

**Common material mistakes:** selling triskelle-earning loot you could break down instead; splitting merges between two weapons (doubles your cost forever); upgrading gear with materials your weapon needs — the [Equipment Guide](/guides/equipment-guide) covers what armor actually benefits from.

## The Armory: Your #1 Village Upgrade

The **Armory** is the highest-priority village upgrade for progression — rated **★★★★★**. It provides **permanent artifact carry between runs**, which stacks with your per-run merge tiers instead of replacing them. Every Armory level raises your floor across all future runs, while merge tiers only last until the run ends.

Upgrade the Armory before the Smithy. The Smithy is only useful once you have duplicates worth merging — and the Armory is what makes every successful run worth more. See the [Village Upgrade Guide](/guides/village-upgrade-guide) for the full priority order and costs.
