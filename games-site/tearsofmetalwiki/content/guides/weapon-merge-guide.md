# Tears of Metal Weapon Merge Guide — Reinforced Tiers and Upgrade Paths

> **Applies to Early Access v1.0 / Updated: August 9, 2026**

The weapon merge system is the heart of Tears of Metal progression. Combine identical weapons into Reinforced versions, feed the result back into itself, and a common drop becomes a run-defining weapon. This guide covers how the merge system works, the duplicate-dismantling trap that wastes hundreds of weapons, where drops actually come from, and the upgrade paths that make your Dragon Stone Shards and AP count.

---

## The Merge System Explained

The merge system lets you combine **two identical weapons** into a single, stronger version. Merging is done at the Smithy, and the result keeps the same base weapon — it just gets a higher Reinforced tier with bigger stat bonuses.

### Merge Tier Progression

| Tier | Combine | Result | Stat Bonus |
|------|---------|--------|------------|
| Base | Default drop | Base | 100% baseline |
| Reinforced+1 | Base + Base | Reinforced+1 | +25% damage, +10% attack speed |
| Reinforced+2 | Reinforced+1 + Reinforced+1 | Reinforced+2 | +50% damage, +20% attack speed |
| Reinforced+3 | Reinforced+2 + Reinforced+2 | Reinforced+3 | +80% damage, +30% attack speed |
| Reinforced+4 | Reinforced+3 + Reinforced+3 | Reinforced+4 | +120% damage, +40% attack speed |

### Merge Rules

- **Weapons must be identical** — Two Iron Greatswords merge; an Iron Greatsword and a Steel Greatsword do not
- **Rarity does not matter** — A Common + Uncommon of the same weapon merge fine
- **Both weapons must be the same tier** — Reinforced+1 + Base does not work
- **Smithy Level 1 required** — Merging is unavailable until you build it

### The Exponential Cost

Each tier doubles the base weapons required: **2 → 4 → 8 → 16**. By Reinforced+4 you need 16 copies of one weapon. That is why specializing early matters — split your duplicates across two weapons and you will never finish either. Pick your endgame weapon by Act 2 and feed every duplicate you find into it.

## Why You Should NEVER Dismantle Duplicates

Dismantling a duplicate is the most common early-game mistake. It feels like free value, but every duplicate you scrap is a step backward in the merge system — and the penalty compounds. Dismantling two duplicates delays your Reinforced+1 by a full merge, and the gap widens with every tier.

**Rule of thumb:** only dismantle weapons you would never use at any tier. If a weapon could be your endgame pick or a stepping stone toward it, every copy is merge material. Between the exponential 2-4-8-16 costs and the fact that drops are the only way to restock, duplicates are rarer than the scrap they hand you. A Reinforced+3 weapon is worth more than a dozen Junk-tier dismantles combined.

## Where Weapons Come From

Weapons drop from three main sources:

1. **Battlefield loot** — Elites, minibosses, and chests are the primary sources of weapon drops. Full-clearing zones (and breaking every crate and barrel) yields far more duplicates than boss-rushing
2. **The Orange Tree Merchant** — A village merchant that sells weapons directly for **triskelle** (e.g., the Celtic Longsword costs 500 triskelle). Its stock and prices improve as you upgrade the village, so check back after every major upgrade
3. **Co-op squads** — Full 4-player squads get **2.5x base drop rates**, which makes the merge system dramatically faster in multiplayer. If you are chasing Reinforced+3 or +4, coordinate a full squad

## Hidden AP Costs: Always Price the Full Branch

Ability Points (AP) unlock weapon skill tree nodes, and AP does **not** carry over between runs — every point is per-run. The trap is that late-game skills require purchasing **all connected prerequisites** before they unlock. The cost displayed on a capstone is a lie until you check the branch.

**Example: Head Shot.** Its real cost is not the capstone's price — it is the full chain:

- Arm Shot (prerequisite) → Leg Shot (prerequisite) → Head Shot (capstone) = **40 AP total**

Spending 20 AP on Arm Shot and Leg Shot without reaching Head Shot is 20 AP burned on a branch you cannot finish. Before committing a single point, count every prerequisite in the chain and compare it against the AP you can realistically earn this run. If the branch does not fit, invest in a cheaper branch you can cap instead.

## Top Build Paths: Pure Physical Damage

The safest and most consistent build path for heavy frontline heroes is **Pure Physical Damage**:

- **Attack Power** — flat damage scaling on every hit, no conditions
- **Impact** — stagger and interrupt value that keeps enemies from swinging back
- **Guard Break** — shreds block and armor, speeding up every engagement
- **Armor Penetration** — counters the armor-heavy enemies of Act 2 and beyond

This path has no proc conditions, no elemental counters, and no reliance on emblem RNG — it works with any weapon drop and every duplicate you merge into it. Elemental and emblem builds can spike higher, but Pure Physical is the safest AP and merge investment for frontline heroes. ## Dragon Stone Shards Economy

Dragon Stone Shards are the premium currency for village progression, earned from **act bosses and endgame expeditions** (roughly 1-3 per act clear). How you spend them matters more than how many you have:

- **Village upgrades first** — permanent, every run benefits
- **Merchant weapons second** — only if the weapon is your confirmed endgame pick
- **Watch patch prices** — the Flamberge dropped from **9 to 3 Dragonstones** in the July 24 patch, so merchant prices change between updates. Never assume today's price is tomorrow's

---

## The Armory: Your #1 Village Upgrade

The **Armory** is the highest-priority village upgrade for the merge system — rated **★★★★★**. It provides **permanent weapon damage**, which stacks with your per-run merge tiers instead of replacing them. Every Armory level raises your floor across all future runs, while merge tiers only last until the run ends.

Upgrade the Armory before the Smithy. The Smithy is only useful once you have duplicates worth merging — and the Armory is what makes every duplicate drop worth more. See the [Village Upgrade Guide](/guides/village-upgrade-guide) for the full priority order and costs.
