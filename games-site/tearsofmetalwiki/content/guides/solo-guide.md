# Tears of Metal Solo Guide — Best Hero, Battalion Tactics & Full Campaign Strategy

> **Early Access / Updated: August 9, 2026**

Tears of Metal is built for four, but it is a complete single-player game — and honestly, solo is where the roguelike tension is sharpest. Every run is you, your battalion, and a 1–2 hour campaign with no mid-run saves. This guide covers the hero picks that actually work solo, how to turn your battalion into a second player, how to manage Threat Level so the game doesn't punish your thoroughness, and the act-by-act solo strategy that gets you through to The Scar.

---

## Is Solo Actually Viable?

Yes — and the game is designed for it.

| Factor | Solo reality |
|--------|-------------|
| **Enemy scaling** | Enemies scale with player count, so solo runs face smaller, thinner hordes than co-op (see the [Co-op Guide](/guides/co-op-guide)) |
| **Battalion power** | Your soldiers are your second player: they hold lines, deal damage, and draw aggro. A good battalion does most of the "teamwork" solo |
| **Difficulty** | Solo is harder than co-op with a good team — but the tradeoff is full build control and zero desync risk |
| **Time cost** | Campaigns are 1–2 hours with no mid-campaign saves. Solo, every mistake is yours to eat (see the [Save System Guide](/guides/save-system-guide)) |
| **Achievements** | Solo-friendly achievements exist, including the challenge of finishing acts with restrictions (see our [Achievements Guide](/guides/achievements-guide)) |

**The honest verdict:** solo is the best way to learn the game. Co-op is where you farm comfortably once you know what you're doing. Most players who clear all three acts alone say the third act solo is the hardest thing in the game — plan accordingly.

## Best Hero for Solo Runs

Our [Heroes Tier List](/guides/heroes-tier-list) ranks all three heroes; here is the solo-specific read:

| Hero | Solo Rating | Why |
|------|:-----------:|-----|
| **Wallace** | **9/10 — the solo pick** | Forgiving defense, balanced damage, no bad matchups. His parry/block timing is the easiest to learn, which matters when there's no teammate to pick you up |
| **Ruadh Stonecrusher** | 8/10 | Highest damage and stagger — excellent for solo boss fights, but slow wind-ups get punished hard by swarms |
| **Brienne** | 7/10 | Fastest attacks and best per-hit synergy, but short range and the game's highest skill floor. Great solo *once mastered* |

### Why Wallace Is the Default Solo Pick

Solo play punishes mistakes more than co-op — nobody revives you mid-encounter, and a bad trade at 30% HP in Act 2 is a dead run. Wallace's longsword kit is the most forgiving: the defensive timing is the friendliest in the game, his reach keeps him out of swarm trouble, and he has no matchup he hard-loses. He's the "boring" correct answer, and that's exactly what solo runs reward.

### When to Pick the Others Solo

- **Pick Ruadh for boss-rushing.** His stagger chains shut down boss patterns (Gilles the Hog and Iseult the Banshee both fold to rhythm-breaking staggers). The cost is every trash fight being slower and riskier — you must be disciplined about pulling groups.
- **Pick Brienne for speed once you're experienced.** Her DPS is steady and her mobility is elite, so mastered Brienne solo clears faster than anyone. Below mastery, she dies to swarms that Wallace would walk through.
- **Rotation note (patch 0.8.57278):** Ruadh is now unlocked by defeating the Banshee. If you're on a fresh save, Wallace is your first hero regardless — which makes him even more clearly the solo starter (see [Patch Notes & Roadmap](/guides/patch-notes)).

## Your Battalion Is Your Second Player

Solo players ignore the battalion at their peril. The game is explicitly designed so you command an army, not just a hero — and solo, that army is doing the work a co-op team would do.

### The Battle Energy Bar

Hits and kills fill a battle energy bar (bottom-left of the screen). This is your solo "team" resource:

- **Full charge → hero super attack:** a full bar can be spent on your hero's special. In solo play, save this for elites and bosses — it's your burst window.
- **Bar segments → battalion commands:** you can spend segments of the bar to order your **spearmen, infantry, or archers** to perform special moves. This is the core of solo crowd control.

### The Three Arms and Their Solo Jobs

| Unit | Solo job | When to command |
|------|----------|-----------------|
| **Infantry** | The frontline sponge | Deploy to hold a chokepoint or draw aggro while you flank |
| **Spearmen** | Anti-charge and anti-captain | Command them when a captain or heavy charges — their counter-strike stops the momentum |
| **Archers** | Range pressure and support | Use them to soften priority targets (banner bearers, spotters) before you engage |

**The golden solo rule:** your battalion commands are not for damage — they're for *attention*. Spending a bar segment to pull aggro onto infantry so you can reposition behind a captain is worth more than any single hero skill.

### Soldiers Are Permanent (and Mortal)

Soldiers gain permanent skills and experience between campaigns — a veteran spearman is an enormous asset. But battalion permadeath is real: when a soldier dies, their skills and experience are gone permanently (see the [Battalion & Companion Guide](/guides/battalion-companion-guide) for the full management system).

**Solo consequence:** you can't "wait for a teammate to take the hit." If your veteran shield line is at low health, retreat them with a command and let fresh recruits hold. Protecting one veteran is worth losing two fresh soldiers.

### Captains and Family Crests

Promoting captains in your army and attaching **family crests** gives persistent bonuses while they stay in your army — examples include faster dodge recharge and stronger basic strikes. Solo, these crests are your cheapest permanent power: a promoted captain with a dodge-recharge crest meaningfully improves survival in every region, and the investment persists between runs.

## Threat Level Control (The Solo Survival Skill)

The single biggest solo killer is the **Threat Level system** — and solo players hit it harder than teams, because you're the one clearing every map corner.

Threat Level is a per-campaign escalation meter that rises with your success:

| What raises it | What it spawns |
|----------------|----------------|
| Clearing map objectives / wiping out groups | Extra captains in subsequent fights |
| Picking up rewards / loot caches | Archer towers in later battles |
| Killing elites and bosses fast | A buffed region boss |

**The solo rule of thumb: clear with intent, not completion.** Every chest you open and every group you wipe makes the next fight harder. On a solo run, decide per region:

1. **Full clear when you need resources** — early act, farming a specific artifact, or pre-boss inventory
2. **Targeted clear when you're strong** — kill only what blocks your route; skip the side packs and the treasure you don't need
3. **Never full clear late** — in Act 3 / The Scar, over-clearing is a death sentence (see the [Threat Level & Difficulty Guide](/guides/threat-level-difficulty-guide) for the full mechanics)

**The solo-specific trap:** without teammates to split aggro, extra captains spawn directly into your face. A region that was fine at Threat 4 can become unwinnable at Threat 8. When the meter climbs, stop looting and move to the boss.

## Act-by-Act Solo Strategy

### Act 1: The Coast (Solo Preparation)

- **Hero:** Wallace (or whoever you picked first)
- **Goal:** learn the rhythm, recruit a battalion, and bank permanent currency. Everything in Act 1 is a tutorial for your soldier economy
- **Priority kills:** banner bearers first (they buff nearby enemies), spotters second (their marks make your soldiers take extra damage)
- **Solo tip:** this is the act to full-clear. You need artifact variety and soldier recruits, and Threat Level penalties are mild here. Don't skip the breakable walls and side buildings — they hold early artifacts and recruitable allies
- **Boss — Gilles, The Hog:** manageable solo with basic blocking discipline. Keep your battalion alive for phase pressure and use your first full battle-energy charge on the boss's first stagger window. Boss arenas have no healing, so enter full (see [Boss Guide](/guides/boss-guide))

### Act 2: The Mid-Campaign Grind

- **Goal:** consolidate. Your weapon should be merged (see [Weapon Upgrade Guide](/guides/weapon-upgrade-guide)), your artifact slots filled, and your battalion veteran-heavy
- **The solo shift:** stop full-clearing. Act 2's Threat Level rises faster, and extra captains with archer towers are where solo runs start snowballing into failure
- **Solo tip:** route bosses before loot. If you're on pace for the campaign, skip the optional pack that would push you to the next Threat tier
- **Boss — Iseult, The Banshee:** the most notorious solo wall of the launch build. Her pattern punishes standing still and over-committing. Dodge sideways, punish recoveries, and save your battle-energy super for the phase transition. Note: beating her is also what unlocks Ruadh Stonecrusher on current builds (see [Patch Notes](/guides/patch-notes))

### Act 3: The Scar (The Solo Filter)

Act 3 — The Scar — is where solo campaigns go to die. Corruption DoT zones drain your health while you stand in them, corrupted enemies apply stacking DoT, and there are no health stations.

**Non-negotiables before entering:**
- **Corruption Resist** — emblem or artifact. Without at least one source, the DoT kills you through potions (see the [Corruption Guide](/guides/corruption-guide))
- **Regen emblem** as a second layer — passive healing offsets the tick damage
- **Merged weapon** — you cannot afford long fights in the final region
- **A heal stock** — boss arenas have no healing; enter the final fight at full

**Solo routing in The Scar:** be aggressively linear. Skip side paths, don't linger in pools, and keep your battalion moving with you. Every minute spent standing is Threat Level feeding the boss buff. See the [Progression Walkthrough](/guides/progression-walkthrough) for the region route.

## Solo Emblems & Artifacts That Pull Their Weight

| Emblem / Artifact | Why it's a solo pick |
|-------------------|----------------------|
| **Physical Damage** | Flat multiplier for Wallace's longsword — no condition, no risk |
| **Critical Strike** | Burst against captains and bosses, where solo fights are decided |
| **Regen** | The solo sustain emblem — heals between encounters without potions |
| **Guardian** | Shield at low HP — the safety net that turns a near-death mistake into a recovery |
| **Corruption Resist** | Mandatory for Act 3, as above |
| **Survivability artifacts** | Damage reduction and health first, damage second. Solo, dead DPS is no DPS (see [Heroes Tier List](/guides/heroes-tier-list) and the [Emblem & Artifact Guide](/guides/emblem-artifact-guide)) |

**The solo build philosophy:** co-op builds can be glass-cannons because teammates cover you. Solo builds need a floor. Two defensive pieces in your loadout is not wasted DPS — it's the difference between dying to one mistake and converting that mistake into a recovered fight.

## Solo Routines That Win Runs

1. **Block out the 1–2 hours.** No mid-campaign saves means starting a campaign you can't finish is a donation. Start fresh, finish fresh (see [Save System Guide](/guides/save-system-guide))
2. **Protect veterans over everything.** One veteran spearman with permanent skill levels beats five fresh soldiers. Retreat them, never sacrifice them
3. **Spend battle energy on commands, not supers — until the boss.** Supers feel great; commands win fights
4. **Watch the Threat meter every region.** When it's near a new tier, stop looting and route to the boss
5. **Bank permanent currency early.** Triskeles and coins carry over whether you win or lose — a failed Act 3 run still pays for village upgrades (see [Village Upgrade Guide](/guides/village-upgrade-guide))
6. **Don't chase every artifact.** Two artifacts that synergize beat a legendary that doesn't (see [Emblem & Artifact Guide](/guides/emblem-artifact-guide))

## Battalion Composition for Solo

Recruitment isn't random filling — it's your build. Solo players should think about the battalion the way co-op players think about team comp (see the [Co-op Guide](/guides/co-op-guide)).

| Unit | Ideal solo ratio | Why |
|------|:----------------:|-----|
| **Infantry** | 40–50% | Your frontline sponge. Solo, they're the shield that lets you flank |
| **Spearmen** | 25–35% | Anti-captain and anti-charge. The units that save you from the game's deadliest normal enemies |
| **Archers** | 15–25% | Pressure and support. Fewer needed — their job is softening priority targets, not holding ground |

**Composition rules that matter solo:**

1. **Never let your frontline be all fresh recruits** — green infantry melt under captain pressure. Keep at least one veteran infantry squad alive between regions
2. **Spearmen beat captains; infantry beats swarms; archers beat spotters** — when a region's enemy mix skews one way (Act 1 coasts: swarms; Act 2 complex: captains; Act 3: everything), lean the ratio
3. **Recruit early, promote always** — the Recruitment Tent and captain promotions are permanent power (see [Village Upgrade Guide](/guides/village-upgrade-guide)). A solo run's most reliable upgrade path is a stronger army, not a stronger hero
4. **Retreat is a command, not a failure** — pulling a battered squad back and letting a fresh one hold is the solo equivalent of a co-op player swapping lanes

**The family crest angle:** crests grant persistent bonuses while a captain stays in your army — faster dodge recharge and stronger basic strikes are confirmed examples. For solo, prioritize survival-crests (dodge recharge) over damage ones on your frontline captain: your hero's survivability is your run's survivability.

## What Kills Solo Runs (And How to Not Die to It)

Solo deaths cluster into five patterns. If a run dies, it's almost always one of these:

| Killer | Why it happens solo | The fix |
|--------|---------------------|---------|
| **Over-clearing into Threat spikes** | No teammate to say "stop looting" | Watch the meter; route to the boss at tier boundaries (see [Threat Level & Difficulty Guide](/guides/threat-level-difficulty-guide)) |
| **Losing veterans to greed** | Pushing a damaged squad one fight too far | Retreat at low health; a dead veteran costs future runs, not just this one |
| **Boss arenas with no heals** | Entered at 60% because "it's fine" | Enter every arena at full HP; bank a potion stock before boss routes |
| **Act 3 DoT outrunning potions** | No Corruption Resist, stood in pools | Resist emblem or artifact, Regen as backup (see [Corruption Guide](/guides/corruption-guide)) |
| **Command hoarding** | Saving battle energy for the "perfect" super | Spend segments on commands constantly; a full bar in a dying run is wasted bar |

**The 10-minute rule:** if a run feels wrong (threat climbing, battalion bleeding, artifacts not coalescing), decide in the next 10 minutes whether it's a full-clear or a bank-run. Bank the permanent currency and end the campaign cleanly rather than feeding a doomed Act 3 (see [Save System Guide](/guides/save-system-guide) on ending runs gracefully).

## Solo vs Co-op: What Actually Changes

| Factor | Solo | Co-op |
|--------|------|-------|
| **Enemy scaling** | Baseline — smaller hordes | Scales with player count; bigger crowds, more loot |
| **Build control** | Complete — every artifact and emblem is yours | Shared — good builds need communication |
| **Battalion attention** | Yours alone; commands are critical | Everyone commands; coordination doubles the pressure |
| **Threat Level** | You control it fully | Any player's clearing raises it for everyone — easier to overshoot |
| **Boss fights** | Pattern + patience; no backup | DPS races; revives depend on teammates |
| **The Scar / Act 3** | The hardest content in the game | Easier with a good team, harsher with a bad one |
| **Failure** | Entirely on you | Shared, and party members can carry a weak run |
| **Meta progression** | Same rates — solo earns the same permanent currencies | Same rates; plus party assists bank currency faster in practice |

**The honest recommendation:** learn solo, farm co-op. Your first campaign should be solo (or with one patient friend) so you actually learn parry timing, command timing, and Threat Level discipline. Once the fundamentals are muscle memory, co-op becomes the comfortable grind — and your solo skills will make you the better teammate.

## Solo Practice Drills (Build the Fundamentals)

If Act 3 keeps ending your runs, drill these in Act 1:

1. **The parry drill** — fight grunt packs for one full region using only parries (no dodge, no block-hold). You'll take damage; that's the point. Parry timing is the solo run's cheapest HP
2. **The command drill** — spend every battle-energy segment the moment it fills, alternating infantry then spearmen. Learn what each command actually does in a fight (see [Battalion & Companion Guide](/guides/battalion-companion-guide))
3. **The restraint drill** — full clear one region, then skip every chest and side pack in the next. Feel the Threat Level difference yourself; it teaches route discipline faster than any guide
4. **The boss drill** — fight Gilles the Hog twice in a row without using your battle-energy super. If you can win on pattern alone, you can win any solo boss fight (see [Boss Guide](/guides/boss-guide))

Each drill trains a skill the solo campaign specifically demands: parry timing, command economy, threat discipline, and boss patience.

## The First Five Runs (A Solo Progression Roadmap)

New solo players waste runs because they don't know what each campaign is *for*. Here's a five-run plan that builds your meta progression deliberately:

| Run | Goal | What to do | What to bank |
|-----|------|------------|--------------|
| **Run 1 — The Learning Run** | Learn the loop | Play Wallace, full clear Act 1, die somewhere in Act 2 | Village upgrades (Armory first — see [Village Upgrade Guide](/guides/village-upgrade-guide)), recruit every soldier you find |
| **Run 2 — The Banking Run** | Meta progression | Rush through Acts 1-2, collect artifacts and gold, end clean before a doomed Act 3 | Permanent currencies (Triskeles), artifact collection for future builds |
| **Run 3 — The Veteran Run** | Battalion power | Focus on recruiting and promoting; let veterans carry fights while you practice parries | A veteran core army + promoted captains with crests |
| **Run 4 — The Act 3 Attempt** | First full clear | Wallace with Corruption Resist + Regen, linear route through The Scar | Your first full campaign completion (and the boss achievements — see [Achievements Guide](/guides/achievements-guide)) |
| **Run 5+ — The Specialization Runs** | Build variety | Try Ruadh (post-Banshee unlock) or Brienne; build around specific emblems | New build archetypes, Ruadh unlock, deeper artifact pool |

**Why this order works:** runs 1-3 exist to make run 4 possible — the game's meta progression (village, veterans, artifacts) is the real difficulty slider. Banking before boss-rushing is the solo player's version of leveling before the dungeon.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I play Tears of Metal solo?**
Yes — the game fully supports single-player, and solo runs face reduced enemy scaling versus co-op.

**Q: Who is the best hero for solo?**
Wallace. His forgiving defense and balanced kit make him the default solo pick; Ruadh is better for solo boss-rushing, Brienne once you master her mobility.

**Q: Is solo or co-op harder?**
Solo is harder at the top end (Act 3 alone is brutal), but co-op with a bad team is harder than solo with a good battalion. Solo is also the best way to learn the game.

**Q: Does the game scale down for solo?**
Enemy numbers scale with player count, so solo faces thinner hordes. Battalion size and threat behavior are unchanged.

**Q: Can I pause in solo?**
Solo play is local and single-player — but there are no mid-campaign saves, so pause all you want; just don't close the game mid-run (see [Save System Guide](/guides/save-system-guide)).

**Q: What difficulty is solo balanced around?**
Solo runs face baseline enemy scaling (the game's default). Co-op adds scaling with player count. The solo experience is the game's reference difficulty.

**Q: Do I need to recruit soldiers to win solo?**
Practically, yes. The game is designed around commanding a battalion — solo without soldiers is a self-imposed hard mode (there's even an achievement-style challenge in that spirit; see [Achievements Guide](/guides/achievements-guide)).

**Q: Which act is hardest solo?**
Act 3 / The Scar, without question — corruption DoT, no health stations, and a buffed region boss punish every solo mistake. Prepare resist and regen before entering.

**Q: Can I get all three heroes solo?**
Wallace starts unlocked. Brienne is available as a starter. Ruadh now requires defeating Iseult, The Banshee — doable solo, but expect the fight to be the wall of your first full campaign (see [Patch Notes](/guides/patch-notes)).
