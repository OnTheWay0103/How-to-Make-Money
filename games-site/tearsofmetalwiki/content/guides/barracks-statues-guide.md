# Tears of Metal Barracks & Statues Guide — Upgrading Your Army

> **Scope:** the *between-run, army-side* permanent upgrade layer — the Barracks, the Statue currency, Commanders, Companion slots and Ally Command training. The Village building overview lives in the [Village Upgrade Guide](/guides/village-upgrade-guide); in-run recruitment, permadeath and battlefield commands live in the [Battalion & Companion Guide](/guides/battalion-companion-guide).

Most Tears of Metal progression guides answer one question — which Village building do I upgrade next — and assume every permanent currency behaves the same way. It does not. Your permanent power is split across **two currencies that fund two different halves of your character sheet**, and only one of them is well documented. That is why players finish campaigns holding a stack of an unexplained currency with no idea what it buys.

This page covers the half Paper Cult barely explains in-game: the army.

---

## Your permanent upgrades are split in two

| Currency | What it buys | Survives death? |
|----------|--------------|:--:|
| **Coins** | Healing, Chests, merchants — this campaign only | ❌ |
| **Triskeles** | Permanent **hero** skills and stat upgrades | ✅ |
| **Statues** | **Barracks**, roster, Companion and Ally Command upgrades | ✅ |
| **Gems / Scrolls** | Permanent campaign, challenge and economy improvements | ✅ |
| **Dragonstone Shards** | Large named unlocks (hero, weapon, equipment, trial) | ✅ |

*✅ = community-reported, not from Paper Cult. Statues trip people up.* Paper Cult confirmed them as a real reward pool in patch v0.8.57278 (July 24, 2026) — *"More Statue Region rewards, and increased quantity."* — so Statues are farmed from **Statue Regions** on the campaign map rather than from ordinary chests. Community guides add that Statue tiles are marked with a statue icon and pay out on a boss kill, and that unlike Coins they are **kept when you die** *[Unconfirmed]*.

The distinction matters: Coins are a run resource. Statues are not.

---

## What the Barracks is

The Barracks is the Village building that spends Statues. Paper Cult has never written a Barracks explainer, but its own patch notes confirm the structure: the September 8, 2026 "Big Bug Fixing Pass" contains the line

> *"The Companion x3 Barracks upgrade now only requires Companion x2, rather than also requiring an upgrade from the previous row."*

Three things follow — the most reliable facts on this page:

1. The Barracks holds **numbered, tiered Companion upgrades** — Companion x1, x2, x3.
2. Those tiers are gated by **row prerequisites**, not just by currency.
3. On September 8 Paper Cult **removed one prerequisite step** on the Companion x3 tier, making it purchasable straight after Companion x2.

If you have been holding off on the Companion line because it looked like it required a detour, that detour no longer exists.

Community reporting (Whisper of the House, GamesHorizon) describes the Barracks as having two sections — a **Barracks** section for the army roster, and a **Commands** section for Ally Command damage — and gives these observed early options:

| Barracks option | What it does | Community-reported source |
|-----------------|--------------|---------------------------|
| **Recruitment Drive** | Select one additional Commander unit before a Campaign | Whisper of the House |
| **Military Advantage** | Increase the Commander roster by 2 | Whisper of the House |
| **Extra Companion** | Allows one more Companion | Whisper of the House |
| **Infantry / Spearmen / Archer training** | +20% Ally Command Damage for the selected troop type | Whisper of the House, GamesHorizon |

The Barracks itself is reported to cost **2 Statues to unlock** *[Unconfirmed]* — that number appears in one community source and nowhere in Paper Cult's patch notes. The per-tier Statue prices, and how far each training line extends, are likewise unpublished *[Unconfirmed]*.

⚠️ Naming caution: Early Access sources describe your army with several different vocabularies. Paper Cult's own notes use **Infantry, Spearmen and Archers**, and name one command outright — *"Fixed the Archers' Volley Ally Command, sometimes stopping its damage prematurely."* This page uses the official terms.

## Commanders, Companions and Allies are three different things

Because the Barracks sells upgrades for all three, they get treated as one system. They are not — and the difference determines which upgrade fixes your problem.

| | Commanders | Companions | Ordinary Allies |
|---|---|---|---|
| **What they are** | Leaders of troop groups; the source of your Ally Commands | Handcrafted named specialists | Procedurally recruited line soldiers |
| **How you get them** | Rostered before a campaign; the roster can be expanded | Unlocked through campaign events and objectives | Recruited during a run, or from settlement upgrades |
| **Upgrade path** | Barracks roster upgrades, promotions | Companion tiers at the Barracks; community reports also point to a Companion House *[Unconfirmed]* | Level-ups and per-soldier skills; permanent while the soldier lives |
| **If it dies** | Can be lost during a campaign | Not replaceable | Permanently gone |

The practical trap: **increasing Commander capacity does not create a Companion slot.** They are separate systems with separate slots. Community screenshots of the War Preparations screen show Companion slots reading *"0 of 2"*, and how the remaining slots open has not been published by Paper Cult *[Unconfirmed]*. So buy the Barracks "extra Companion" upgrade because you have a Companion waiting for a slot — not because you want a bigger army. A bigger army is the roster line.

## The only Companion Paper Cult has named

The August 13, 2026 patch (v0.12.58540) added a Companion by name, and the developers described the ability themselves:

> *"Aodh, the Vigil, an old scholar hauling a book heavier than the armor of most men. Once he's convinced to march with you, he keeps watch when a fight breaks out. The first blows never quite land the way the enemy intends!"*

That is an official description of a **damage-mitigation opening**, not a stat block: the first enemy blows in a fight are blunted. Paper Cult has not published his unlock condition, so treat community unlock routes as unverified *[Unconfirmed]*. The September 8 fix *"Fixed a Companion not appearing correctly in the Codex"* is also worth knowing — if a Companion you unlocked looked missing from your collection, that was a real bug.

## Hero or army? Which to upgrade first

This is the decision the Barracks forces on you, and it is the one the game gives you no help with. The rule that follows from the sources above:

**Upgrade your hero (Triskeles) when the failure was yours:**
- you died to direct damage with damage already good → a survivability stat
- your Block bar emptied during normal pressure → block stamina
- a correctly-timed boss punish still left the boss alive → raw damage

**Upgrade your army (Statues) when the hero survived but the battalion did not deliver:**
- Ally Commands reached the target but barely dented it → **+20% Ally Command Damage** on the troop type you actually issue commands to
- shielded enemies stayed up after your Spearmen arrived → Spearmen training
- ranged pressure continued because your Archers were weak → Archer training
- useful Commanders were excluded from the selection screen → **Military Advantage**, or **Recruitment Drive** if your formation simply cannot cover frontline, shielded targets and rescues at once

Two rules of thumb:

1. **Do not spread Statues.** One upgraded command branch that has a battlefield job beats four half-upgraded branches.
2. **Do not repair a route mistake with a permanent purchase.** If the campaign ended because you extended greedily or died with unspent Coins, the fix is a decision, not a Statue.

## FAQ

**Does the Barracks cost Triskeles?**
No. Community sources separate Triskeles (permanent **hero** skills and stats) from Statues (Barracks, roster, Companion and Ally Command upgrades). Paper Cult's patch notes corroborate Statues as a distinct reward pool via the v0.8.57278 line on Statue Region rewards.

**Are "Small Burning Statues" the same thing as Statues?**
No — a genuine naming trap. v0.11.57863 reads *"Small Burning Statues that reduce Threat now cost one Dragonstone instead of two."* That is a map object bought with Dragonstones to lower Threat, not the army currency.

**How many Companion slots do I get?**
Not published. Community screenshots show two open slots early, but Paper Cult has not documented how the rest unlock *[Unconfirmed]*.
