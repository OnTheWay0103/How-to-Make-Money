# Tears of Metal Healing Guide — Healing Fonts, Chicken, Salves & Coin Budgeting

> **Early Access / Updated: August 29, 2026**

Tears of Metal does not restore your health automatically between fights. Every point of HP you recover is paid for — with coins you picked up on the run, a free pickup you happened to pass, or a potion you crafted back at the village. That is why healing is the game's most complained-about economy: Kotaku calls it **"very expensive or rare"** ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/new-bloody-roguelike-is-scottish-dynasty-warriors-and-its-awesome-2000718370)), Smashpad reports **"scarce healing"** through the early game ([Smashpad](https://smashpad.com/tears-of-metal-early-access-review/)), and Steam reviewers in Japanese put it plainly — **"回復コストが高すぎる"** (healing costs too high) ([gamelog-jp aggregation](https://gamelog-jp.com/game/1913120/)).

This guide breaks down the three real healing sources, what each costs, and the coin-budgeting discipline that keeps your run's economy alive. Numbers are community-measured and Early Access values shift — treat them as directional.

> **Values caveat:** Specific costs below are community-documented and unverified for the current Early Access build. Numbers may change with patches; anything not confirmed in a public source is labeled **[Unconfirmed]**.

---

## 1. Why Healing Is So Expensive in Tears of Metal

There is no passive regen between fights. You restore HP only through sources you find or buy, and the run currency — the **coins** you collect inside an expedition — **does not carry over when you die** ([whisperofthehouse beginner guide](https://www.whisperofthehouse.com/tears-of-metal/beginner-guide)). Two consequences follow:

1. **Healing you buy is a sunk cost.** Coins spent on a font or a salve are gone whether you win or lose the next fight, so every heal competes directly against chests, shops, and the gear that carries a run forward.
2. **HP is the most expensive thing to restore.** Because healing is rare and priced at a premium, the cheapest heal is the hit you never take — block, parry, or dodge instead. The [Survival Tips guide](/guides/survival-tips) covers that discipline.

## 2. The Three Healing Sources (Quick-Reference Table)

| Source | Cost | Heal Amount | Where You Get It | Best For |
|--------|------|-------------|------------------|----------|
| **Healing Font** | ~100 coins ([Unconfirmed]) | Moderate, single-use | Bought/found in the expedition | Topping up before a scripted fight |
| **Chicken** | Free | Small ([Unconfirmed]) | Free pickup in the world | Stretching between fonts — not for emergencies |
| **Salves / potions** | Crafted at the Apothecary (triskelle) | Varies by potion | Village Apothecary, pre-run | Stocking up for boss and Corruption zones |

### Healing Fonts — The Reliable But Expensive Option

Healing Fonts are the game's main paid recovery. Community testing puts them at **roughly 100 coins** each ([whisperofthehouse](https://www.whisperofthehouse.com/tears-of-metal/beginner-guide)) — a real decision point in a run where coins reset on death. They are single-use and restore a solid chunk of health, the right buy when a boss arena is next.

### Chicken — Free, Small, and Not a Lifeline

Chicken is the free option: no coin cost, but the heal is small ([whisperofthehouse](https://www.whisperofthehouse.com/tears-of-metal/beginner-guide)). It is excellent for patching yourself between fights without spending, but it will not save you from an arena where you need a real chunk of HP back. If you are heading into a boss at half health, a font or a salve is the honest answer — the exact chicken heal value is **[Unconfirmed]**.

### Salves & Consumables — Your Pre-Run Insurance

Salves and potions are crafted at the village Apothecary using **triskelle** (the permanent meta-currency that does persist between runs — see the [Village Upgrade Guide](/guides/village-upgrade-guide)). The [Survival Tips guide](/guides/survival-tips) rule still applies: **always carry 3+ salves**. Because they cost the persistent currency rather than your run coins, they are the cheapest form of insurance you can bring — stock up before the expedition starts.

## 3. Coin Budgeting — Healing vs. Loot vs. Upgrades

The core tension in Tears of Metal is persistence: **run coins vanish on death**, while village triskelle is permanent. So the question is never "should I heal?" — it is "what is this heal competing against?"

| Situation | What to Spend Coins On |
|-----------|------------------------|
| Boss arena next | **Heal first.** Arenas have no healing (see below); every coin on a font is cheaper than a failed attempt |
| Low-threat side area | **Skip the font.** Open chests / buy gear — loot compounds across the whole run |
| High Threat Level zone | **Heal before you engage.** Threat-scaled enemies punish a half-empty bar (see [Threat Level Guide](/guides/threat-level-difficulty-guide)) |
| Mid-run, no fight coming | **Save the coins.** There is no interest on a coin hoard, but there is also no boss waiting to take it |

**Rule of thumb:** heal before the arena, not during. Buy healing when the next fight is scripted, gamble on loot when it is optional. Spending your last coins on a font you never need is a mistake — but so is entering a boss room at 40% HP with coins in your pocket.

## 4. Max HP Reduction — The Hidden Tax on Getting Hit

Late in the game, **repeated damage can permanently reduce your maximum HP for the rest of that run** ([2upskill progression guide](https://2upskill.com/tears-of-metal-progression-guide-full-3-acts-walkthrough-best-heroes-and-village-upgrades/)). Every hit you take in the back half of a campaign can shave your ceiling — a loss that does not come back when you heal.

Practical counterplay:

1. **Retreat on the low-HP rule.** A critically low hero with an empty stamina bar is the death that ends runs — disengage instead of trading (see [Survival Tips](/guides/survival-tips)).
2. **Respect Corruption zones.** Act 3 Corruption drains health and can stack this pressure on top of normal damage — carry **Corruption Resist** and a cure (see the [Corruption Guide](/guides/corruption-guide)).
3. **Check Threat Level before committing.** A high-Threat route fights harder enemies that land hits more often — sometimes the lower-reward path protects your max HP.

---

## 5. Boss Arenas Have No Healing — Arrive Full

Kotaku reports the first boss fight offers **no way to heal** ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/new-bloody-roguelike-is-scottish-dynasty-warriors-and-its-awesome-2000718370)), and the [Boss Guide](/guides/boss-guide) confirms there are no heal points in the Act 1 arena — where a single missed block/parry/dodge costs nearly half your health. The rule is non-negotiable:

> **Top off at a font or with a salve before the door. If you enter hurt, you are fighting with a handicap.**

The same logic extends to later bosses, which arrive with Threat Level pressure already active. Entering full is the cheapest heal in the game.

## 6. Co-op Revive Costs — Budget for Resurrections

In co-op, reviving a fallen teammate costs **250 / 350 coins, paid by the surviving player(s)** ([whisperofthehouse co-op guide](https://www.whisperofthehouse.com/tears-of-metal/co-op-guide)). Two things follow:

- **Keep a coin reserve in co-op.** The most expensive revive is the one nobody can afford because the whole party spent its coins on fonts before the boss.
- **Decide who carries the revive fund.** Agree before a boss arena on who keeps coins in reserve, so a downed teammate is a 250-coin fix, not a party wipe.

See the [Co-op Guide](/guides/co-op-guide) for the full team-play picture.

## 7. Village Apothecary Upgrades — The Long-Term Answer

The permanent fix for the healing economy is the **Apothecary**, the village building that crafts potions and consumables — healing, stamina recovery, buffs, and Corruption cures (per the [Village Upgrade Guide](/guides/village-upgrade-guide), which details costs). Prices below are cross-referenced from the Village Upgrade Guide; the stamina potion price lacks an independent public source, so it is marked **[Unconfirmed]**:

| Item | Cost (triskelle) | Use |
|------|------------------|-----|
| Basic Healing potion | 50 | Emergency health recovery |
| Stamina potion | 75 [Unconfirmed] | Extended fights |
| Corruption Cure | 100 | **Near-mandatory for Act 3** |

The community progression reference rates the Apothecary **★★★★☆ as an upgrade priority** ([2upskill](https://2upskill.com/tears-of-metal-progression-guide-full-3-acts-walkthrough-best-heroes-and-village-upgrades/)) — strong, but usually after the Armory and Recruitment Tent, because potions are consumables and permanent upgrades pay back longer. For Act 3, budget the Corruption Cure before anything else.

## 8. Pre-Boss Healing Checklist

The summary list for every boss attempt:

- [ ] **Top off HP** at a font or with a salve before entering the arena
- [ ] **Carry at least one heal consumable** into the fight where the arena allows it
- [ ] **In co-op:** confirm who holds the revive reserve (250/350 coins)
- [ ] **Check Threat Level** — a high-Threat boss punishes a smaller pool harder; take the lower-risk route if your HP is already reduced
- [ ] **Check your max HP** — if the run has been punishing, treat the fight as a full-commit decision, not a gamble
