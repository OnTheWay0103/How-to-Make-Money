# Crimson Moon Solo Guide — How to Play Alone

## Can You Play Crimson Moon Solo?

**Yes — completely.** Solo play is not a watered-down mode or a locked option; Crimson Moon is designed to be played **solo or with one partner**, and **difficulty scales dynamically with player count**. The developers were explicit that co-op is optional.

The difference is not *can* you play solo, but *how* — without a partner you do not get the shared life pool's safety net, the revive, or a second Weapon Art to coordinate with.

## Solo vs. Co-op at a Glance

| | Solo | Co-op (2P) |
|---|------|-----------|
| **Life pool** | Your own health only | Shared pool (your mistakes cost both) |
| **Revive** | None — you must survive | Partner can bring you back |
| **Difficulty** | Scaled for one player | Scaled for two |
| **Weapon Arts** | Your one Art covers everything | Two Arts can chain combos |
| **Pacing** | Deliberate, careful | Faster, more aggressive |

Solo is generally **more demanding but more controllable** — you never lose because your partner drained the shared pool, but you also have no safety net.

## Solo Build Priorities

Because there is no partner to revive you or cover your weaknesses, a solo build must be **self-sufficient**. From the [Builds Guide](/guides/builds-guide) framework:

1. **Weapon Art with utility.** Solo, your one Art needs to handle more than damage. Prioritize Arts with crowd control, space creation, or self-sustain over pure burst.
2. **Survivability Boons.** Without a revive, defensive and sustain Boons matter more solo than in co-op. The [Boons Guide](/guides/boons-guide) covers weighing them per run.
3. **Versatile purified gear.** A solo arsenal should have a reliable set for the content you are pushing, not a niche one-trick build.
4. **Know your escape.** Every solo player needs a "get out" — an Art or Boon that creates distance when a fight turns bad.

## Solo Play Style

### 1. Be deliberate about engagement
Previewers described the combat as methodical. Solo, you control every fight's terms — use the dynamic Incursion maps to pull enemies into favorable positions instead of charging in.

### 2. Master the fundamentals
Blocking, dodging, and executions are your survival tools. The [Beginner Guide](/guides/beginner-guide) covers the core skills. Solo, a well-timed block or dodge is worth more than any damage stat.

### 3. Purify for the long game
Solo progress is slower to recover from setbacks, so be **selective about purification** ([Purification Guide](/guides/purification-guide)). Every piece you keep should serve the build you are committed to.

### 4. Use the hub between runs
Sanctus Clypeus — upgrades, forging, War Table quests — is your solo "recovery station." Always upgrade your purified gear before pushing the next Ward.

## Handling the No-Revive Reality

The hardest adjustment from co-op is the lack of a revive. Solo:

- **Bank the angel transformation** (the Nephilim's powerful form) for emergencies, not as a routine button.
- **Retreat before the fight turns bad** — a controlled retreat costs nothing; a death can end the run.
- **In Incursions, know when to extract.** A partial haul is better than losing everything by overstaying. See the [Incursions Guide](/guides/incursions-guide).

## Common Solo Questions

### Is solo balanced for all content?
The game scales difficulty for player count, and the developers confirmed solo is fully supported. World bosses and the Dead Gods are the hardest solo — prepare with a strong purified build. Specific solo balance will be confirmed by players at launch [Unconfirmed].

### Is solo more fun?
That depends on what you want. Solo gives full control and a purer test of the soulslike combat. Co-op adds the shared-life-pool tension and Weapon Arts combos. Both are officially supported — the [Co-op Guide](/guides/coop-multiplayer-guide) covers the co-op side.

### Can I switch between solo and co-op?
Yes — the game supports both modes, and difficulty scales accordingly. Session/save specifics will be confirmed at launch.
