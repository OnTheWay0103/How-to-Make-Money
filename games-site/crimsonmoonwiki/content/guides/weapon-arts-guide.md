# Crimson Moon Weapon Arts — What We Know & Co-op Coordination

## What Are Weapon Arts?

**Weapon Arts** are the special abilities attached to your weapons in Crimson Moon. Every weapon carries an Art, and the Art is the heart of how that weapon actually fights — it is the difference between "I hit with this weapon" and "this weapon has a signature move."

Because Crimson Moon has **no classes**, your Weapon Art is the closest thing to a combat identity. The official **Builds overview trailer** treated Weapon Arts as the centerpiece of the classless build system, and specifically emphasized **coordinating them in co-op**.

## How Weapon Arts Fit the Build System

Your build is three layers: Equipment, Boons, and Weapon Arts.

- **Weapon Arts = how you fight.** The active ability you press in combat.
- **Boons = what you are empowered with during a run.** They modify or amplify how your Art plays.
- **Equipment = what you keep permanently.** The weapon that carries the Art, plus gear that supports it.

In practical terms: choosing a weapon is choosing a Weapon Art, and your Boons are chosen to make that Art do its job better.

## What Kinds of Weapon Arts to Expect

The game is a melee-first soulslike, so Arts tend to fall into recognizable roles. Exact names and numbers are **[Unconfirmed]** until launch, but the roles previewers and the trailer point to include:

| Role | What the Art Does | Good For |
|------|-------------------|----------|
| **Burst damage** | Big single hit or quick flurry | Finishing staggered enemies, co-op burst windows |
| **Crowd control** | Knockback, stagger, or area lockdown | Keeping enemies off you or your partner |
| **Gap closing** | Lunge / dash toward an enemy | Initiating fights, chasing runners |
| **Space creation** | Knock enemies away, create distance | Escaping a bad position, controlling the pool |
| **Self-sustain** | Heal, shield, or defensive stance | Solo play, surviving longer |

This is a framework, not a confirmed list — the specific Arts and their weapons will be documented after the September 1 launch.

## Choosing a Weapon Art

A good rule for a classless game: **pick the Art that makes the fight you like, then build around it.**

1. **Try every weapon's Art in your first few runs.** The Art is the identity — you cannot judge a weapon by its damage stat alone.
2. **Match the Art to your skill level.** A gap-closing Art demands aggressive play; a space-creation Art is more forgiving.
3. **Let the Art define your Boons.** If your Art is crowd control, take damage Boons to capitalize on the windows it creates. If it is self-sustain, you can afford more aggressive Boons elsewhere.

## Co-op Weapon Arts Coordination

The official Builds trailer made one point repeatedly: **Weapon Arts are meant to be coordinated between the two co-op partners.** The developers want the two of you to chain Arts, not just fight side by side.

Co-op combos to plan around:

- **Lockdown → Burst.** Partner A uses a crowd-control Art to freeze or stagger; Partner B unloads their burst Art into the helpless target.
- **Pull → AoE.** If any Art drags enemies together, pair it with the other partner's area damage.
- **Escape → Recover.** One partner creates space with a knockback Art while the other heals or revives.

Because both partners share the same life pool (see the [Co-op Guide](/guides/coop-multiplayer-guide)), a coordinated Art combo that removes threats quickly is often better than two players doing independent damage.

## Weapon Arts and the Shared Life Pool

In co-op, remember that a missed Weapon Art can cost both of you. A whiffed burst Art means the enemy is still alive and the pool is still being drained. Practicing Art timing matters more in Crimson Moon co-op than in games where each player has their own health bar.
