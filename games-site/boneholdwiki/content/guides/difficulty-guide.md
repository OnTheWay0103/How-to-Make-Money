# Bonehold Difficulty Guide - Scaling Curve, Elite Bosses, and Difficulty Options

Bonehold is a hard game — but the difficulty is specific, learnable, and mostly *not* what new players expect. The traps kill you more than the skeletons; the charge system punishes greed; and there is no save to fall back on. This guide maps the confirmed difficulty curve, explains what actually makes the game hard, and answers the question on every new player's mind: are there difficulty options?

**Honesty note:** Bonehold has no officially published difficulty table. The floor-by-floor expectations below are inferred from confirmed facts (deeper = harder enemies and better loot; elite bosses per floor; trap density) plus roguelike convention — specifics are marked (unverified).

**The honest headline:** the "difficulty slider" in Bonehold is your own play. There is no confirmed difficulty option — instead, the game is hard in exactly four ways, and each one can be learned.

## The Four Real Difficulty Sources

### 1. Traps (the #1 killer — confirmed)

Reviews are explicit: "your biggest enemy is actually the pits in the floor and spikes on the walls." Trap density is high, placements are often behind you, and spike pits/flame walls force waiting. **The fix:** observe before crossing, memorize timer cycles, and use traps against enemies (see the [Traps Guide](/guides/traps-guide)). Trap deaths are knowledge deaths — the same trap stops killing you after you have seen it once.

### 2. Charge Commitment

Most weapons charge: holding the attack button builds power, and the full charge is the damage king — but it locks you in place during the windup. **The fix:** charge only during safe windows (enemy recovery, parry staggers). Reviews confirm the game "wants you to earn your big hits" — greed is the difficulty, patience is the counter.

### 3. Permadeath with No Saves

Every run is all-in. A deep run ends with one mistake, and everything — gear, loot, progress — is gone (confirmed: no checkpoint or save system). **The fix:** this is where the *mental* difficulty lives. Play within your stamina budget, keep escape routes open, and accept that runs end — knowledge carries over even when gear does not (see the [Progression Guide](/guides/progression-guide)).

### 4. RNG Build Puzzles

Your build comes from what drops, not what you plan (confirmed: procedural loot with prefixes; builds are meant to pivot mid-run). **The fix:** learn prefix evaluation (see the [Item Prefixes Guide](/guides/item-prefixes-guide)) — recognizing a run-defining item early is a skill, and it converts RNG from a difficulty source into an advantage.

**The pattern to internalize:** Bonehold's curve is a *knowledge* curve more than a *stat* curve. Floors stop being hard when you know their traps, their enemy mixes, and the windows they give you — which is why reviewers describe the game as beatable "through accumulated experience."

## Difficulty Answers, Summarized

- **Is there an easy mode?** Unconfirmed — no official difficulty settings have been documented (unverified). Don't wait for one.
- **Does it get easier with experience?** Yes, by design — knowledge is the meta-progression.
- **Does anything carry over between runs?** Steam achievements only, confirmed. The rest is your head (see the Progression Guide).
- **Where's the real wall?** The first elite boss and the trap-dense early floors — the two places reviews say players die most.

**The verdict:** Bonehold is hard in four specific, learnable ways — traps, charge commitment, permadeath, and build RNG — and notably *not* hard in the "spongey enemies" sense. Every difficulty source has a confirmed counter, and the game's reviews (86–91% positive sentiment) show the curve is beatable by patient players.
