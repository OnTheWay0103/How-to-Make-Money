# Bonehold Traps Guide - Every Hazard Type and How to Use Them

In Bonehold, the dungeon itself is often deadlier than its inhabitants. Launch reviews are unambiguous: **"your biggest enemy is actually the pits in the floor and spikes on the walls."** This quick-reference guide lists every trap type confirmed by launch coverage, explains why traps kill more players than monsters, and — the fun part — how to use them against the skeletons chasing you.

**Honesty note:** trap *types* are confirmed by multiple launch reviews. Exact trigger timings, damage values, and per-floor trap density are **not officially documented** (待验证) — the details below describe behavior, not numbers.

## Quick-Reference: Every Confirmed Trap

| Trap | Confirmed? | What It Does | Main Danger |
|------|-----------|--------------|-------------|
| Floor pits | ✅ Confirmed | Bottomless openings in the floor; falling in is usually fatal | Easy to back into while fighting — the #1 trap death in reviews |
| Wall spikes | ✅ Confirmed | Spike mechanisms that extend from walls, often timed | Damage on contact; punishes standing near walls during fights |
| Spike pits | ✅ Confirmed | Pit areas with spikes inside; stepping on them deals damage | Often combined with forced jumps or narrow bridges; triggers force you to wait |
| Flame walls | ✅ Confirmed | Fire-emitting wall mechanisms on a timer | Forces repositioning; blocks corridors on a cycle; burns anything standing in them |
| Fire traps | ✅ Confirmed | Floor or wall-mounted fire mechanisms | AoE damage; overlapping with flame walls creates "fire corridors" |
| Other environmental hazards | ⚠️ Reported (待验证) | Localized coverage mentions additional mechanisms | Treat any unnamed hazard the same way: observe before crossing |

**The confirmed pattern:** the dungeon generator places traps *frequently*, sometimes behind the player — you clear a room, turn around, and a spike wall is now between you and the exit. Reviews describe this as the game's most reliable death source, easily beating enemy attacks.

## Why Traps Kill More Players Than Enemies

Reviews converge on the same explanation, and it is worth internalizing:

1. **They appear behind you.** The generator drops traps in your path *after* you commit to a room, so your escape route can vanish mid-fight.
2. **They punish panic.** When a skeleton group pushes you, your instinct is to back up — straight into the pit or spike wall behind you. That is exactly how the fortress kills.
3. **They force waiting.** Spike pits and flame walls cycle on timers; you cannot walk through during the active phase. Reviewers call the forced waiting "slowing the pace and reducing enjoyment" — but waiting is cheaper than dying.
4. **They are repetitive early on.** Early rooms reuse the same trap layouts, which is why players say the first floors feel samey. The upside: the repetition means you learn the patterns quickly.

## The Golden Rule: Observe Before You Cross

Because trap patterns repeat, memorization is the confirmed survival skill:

- **Scan the floor before engaging.** Trap positions are visible if you look; rushing into a room is how you stand in a flame wall mid-charge.
- **Memorize timer cycles.** Flame walls and spike pits cycle on predictable timers. Watch one full cycle before crossing, then cross during the safe phase.
- **Keep an escape lane.** Never fight with a trap behind you *and* enemies in front. Reposition sideways or into the room you already cleared.
- **Trust the repetition.** The same trap layouts appear constantly — the third time you see a corridor, you should already know where the pit is. That knowledge is free survival.

## Using Traps Against Enemies (Confirmed and Glorious)

This is the best-kept-secret that reviews love: **traps damage everyone, including the fortress's own undead.** Reviewers describe pushing chasing skeletons into flame walls and watching them burn to ash — and the same logic applies to every hazard:

| Scenario | What To Do |
|----------|-----------|
| Skeleton group chasing you | Lure them through a flame wall's active phase; they take the burns, not you |
| Elite enemy (confirmed per-floor elites) | Kite it into spike pits or wall spikes to chip its health before you commit to the real fight |
| Caster behind melee crowd | Positioning the melee crowd into a trap clears space so you can reach the caster |
| Cornered by a pit | Hold position and parry rather than back into the pit — one dodge sideways is usually enough |

**Why this works:** traps do not discriminate between you and enemies, so *you* become the one who knows the timer. A well-timed lure converts the game's biggest frustration into its biggest damage tool.

## Trap Safety Checklist

Before entering any unfamiliar room:

- [ ] Trap locations spotted from the doorway
- [ ] Flame/spike timer observed for at least one full cycle
- [ ] Escape route that does not cross a hazard identified
- [ ] A trap you can lure enemies into chosen
- [ ] Stamina reserved for the dodge *away from* the hazard

**The one rule that beats all others:** when a fight starts going wrong, do not back up. Dodge sideways, through the enemies if needed — never straight into the pit behind you.
