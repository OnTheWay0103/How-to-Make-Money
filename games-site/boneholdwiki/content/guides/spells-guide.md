# Bonehold Spells Guide — Spell Scrolls and Magic Systems

Magic is a first-class part of Bonehold's loot and combat system. The developer's description confirms that **spells are procedurally generated loot** alongside weapons and armor, and launch reviews describe builds that weave magic into melee combat in creative ways. This guide covers what is confirmed about the magic system, how to use spells alongside charge attacks, and what remains unconfirmed.

**Honesty note:** the *existence* of spells as loot and the ability to mix magic into any build are confirmed. Specific spell names, scroll mechanics, cooldowns, charges, and damage values are **not officially documented** — any specific spell names you see listed in guides are unverified. This guide gives you the framework, not invented specifics.

## What Is Confirmed About Spells

- **Spells drop as loot.** The game's description explicitly lists "weapons, spells, and armor" as the procedurally generated treasure types. Spells are a drop category of their own.
- **Spells carry prefixes.** Like weapons and armor, spells are described as coming with prefixes that "twist playstyles in unexpected ways."
- **Magic mixes with melee.** Reviews describe hybridization as a core design pillar — including "Death Knight-esque builds" built around slow-charging staves, and magic combining with melee weaponry.
- **The Scorcher and Spellblade are the magic-leaning classes.** Scorcher is fire-themed and magic-focused; Spellblade blends swordplay and magic. (Their exact kits are unconfirmed.)
- **Spells are one-shot finds per run.** As roguelike loot, a spell you find applies to the current run only. *Whether spells are per-run consumables, permanent loadout items, or something else is unconfirmed* — the run-based nature of the game is what is certain.

## How Magic Works in the Combat Loop

Bonehold's combat is charge-based: most weapons charge, and combat flows around dodges, blocks, parries, and traps. Spells slot into that loop as **burst and utility options** — effects that weapons do not provide.

**The confirmed role of spells in combat:**

- **Covering weaknesses.** A melee-focused build can pick up ranged or area spells to handle situations swords handle poorly.
- **Creating combos.** Reviews describe item synergy as Isaac-like — a spell that combos with your weapon's prefix can create "broken-and-beautiful" combinations.
- **Adding build identity.** When the game's loot system hands you spells, the build you assemble around them is the run's identity.

## Spell Archetypes (Unconfirmed Names, Expected Roles)

While no official spell list exists, the game's elemental and fantasy themes, combined with the confirmed class roster, imply common archetypes. Treat these as **expected categories, not confirmed spells**:

| Archetype | Expected Role | Likely Best Fit |
|-----------|---------------|-----------------|
| Fire damage | High damage over time, area denial | Scorcher |
| Ice / control | Slow or immobilize enemies, defensive utility | Any class, strong with melee |
| Lightning | Fast, interrupt-style effects | Aggressive classes |
| Dark / necromantic | High risk, self-healing, summons-style effects | Hybrid builds (the confirmed Death Knight-esque archetype) |
| Support | Healing, mobility, protection | Any class, universally useful |

The one confirmed example from launch coverage: a **hybrid necromantic build using a slow-charging staff**, described as hilarious and effective. This tells you the game's magic includes dark/necromantic themes and that staves are the canonical magic weapon.

## Class Synergies — What Is Confirmed

- **Scorcher:** fire-themed, magic-focused, fragile at close range. Fire spells and burning effects are the obvious synergy — but exact passives are unconfirmed.
- **Spellblade:** hybrid — the class that most naturally weaves spells between sword strikes. Exact spell-melee interaction mechanics are unconfirmed.
- **Any class can engage with magic.** Hybridization is confirmed as a design pillar — a Barbarian wielding spells is not only possible but clearly intended. You do not need a magic class to build around a great spell drop.

**The practical rule:** when a spell or spell-focused item drops that fits your current build's holes — range, area damage, sustain, control — take it, regardless of class. The game rewards this.

## Spell Drop and Farming Strategies

Since spells are loot, finding good ones follows the loot rules (see our [Item Prefixes & Loot Guide](/guides/item-prefixes-guide)):

- **Explore optional rooms.** Side rooms and special rooms are where extra loot lives. If you want more spells, full-clear more.
- **Take spell-focused drops early.** A spell that fits your class can carry a run; a spell that fits your *build* can carry it further.
- **Balance your loadout.** If you have limited slots, carry one damage spell and one utility spell (healing or mobility) rather than five flavors of damage.
- **Expect randomness.** Spell drops are procedural. Do not plan a spell-centric run in advance — recognize one when the loot offers it.

## When to Cast vs Charge Attack

This decision is the core of integrating magic into Bonehold's combat:

| Situation | Use Magic | Use Charge Attacks |
|-----------|-----------|--------------------|
| Group of enemies clustered | Yes — area spells shine here | Partial/full charges with wide swings |
| Enemy at range, closing | Yes — soften them before melee range | No — wait for them to come to you |
| Boss in recovery window | Depends — burst spells are excellent here | Yes — full charge is the top damage tool |
| Boss winding up a big attack | No — save resources, prepare to dodge | No — never commit during enemy windups |
| You are low on health | Utility spells (healing/mobility) if available | Only from safe positions |

**General rule:** spells are for burst moments and covering weaknesses; charge attacks are the sustained damage backbone. The best builds use both in rhythm rather than relying on one.

## Final Verdict

Bonehold's magic system is confirmed to exist as procedurally generated spell loot with prefixes, designed for cross-class hybridization with melee combat. The specifics — spell names, mechanics, and exact class synergies — are not yet officially documented as of early August 2026.

Until the real spell list is documented, the winning approach is: treat spells as flexible tools, pick drops that cover your build's weaknesses, and experiment. The game's confirmed design philosophy rewards exactly that.
