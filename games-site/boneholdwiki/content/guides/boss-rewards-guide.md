# Bonehold Boss Rewards Guide — How to Choose the Best of Three Drops

Since **Patch 1.0.1**, every floor boss in Bonehold drops **three items and lets you keep one**. That changed boss kills from a loot payout into a draft: you are no longer asking "what did I get?" but "which of these three makes the next floor survivable?" With no checkpoints or mid-run saves, it can decide the run.

This guide covers the confirmed reward rules, rarity versus prefixes, when to break your build, and why the guaranteed Epic and Legendary rooms are worth the risk.

## The Reported Boss Reward Rules

Start with what the patch notes actually say, because the details matter:

- **Floor bosses drop three items.** After the kill, you pick one. The other two are lost.
- **Arena and Mini-boss rooms now always award at least Epic items.**
- **Trap rooms now always award a Legendary item.**
- **Rings scale with floor depth.** Early rings are weaker; deeper rings roll stronger values.
- **Enemies on floors 4-10 drop more experience**, so the boss floors are also where level-up timing accelerates.

**[Unconfirmed]** The 1.0.1 and 1.0.2 patch links above could not be re-opened during a 2026-10-23 QA pass. The rules are quoted from those patch notes, but if you are on a different build, verify the room guarantees in-game before planning a run.

Patch 1.0.2 added a second layer that changes how you value boss rewards: **spells now scale with the floor they were found on**, while the magic gear bonus was reduced. In practice, a spell taken from a deep-floor boss is worth more than the same spell found early, and a spell reward competes harder with a weapon or armor pick than it did at launch.

**Honesty note:** Bonehold does not publish an official item list, prefix table, or drop-rate chart. This guide does not invent item names or percentages. It teaches the decision, not a fake tier list.

## Why the Three-Item Draft Is Harder Than It Looks

In a normal ARPG, a boss drop is an upgrade check: is this item better than what I have? Bonehold is not normal, for three reasons.

### 1. Rarity is not the same as power

The official design is built on **procedurally generated loot with prefixes** that "twist playstyles in unexpected ways." A common item with the right prefix can outperform a rarer item with the wrong one. The boss draft often puts a high-rarity item with a useless prefix next to a lower-rarity item that completes your build.

The question is never "which is rarer?" It is **"which one changes what I can do on the next floor?"**

### 2. The run gives you a build

Reviewers consistently describe Bonehold as a game where you check your gear between rooms and rework your build around what dropped. You do not arrive with a plan; you recognize one. The boss draft is the biggest recognition moment in the run.

### 3. Death is final

There are no checkpoints or saves. A greedy pick that leaves you with no sustain, no ranged option, or no answer to a boss pattern is not a temporary setback — it ends the run. The boss reward should cover your **next** problem, not the fight you just won.

## The Evaluation Framework: Five Questions

When the three items appear, run this checklist in order.

### Question 1: Does it fill a hole or raise a ceiling?

- **Fill a hole:** you have no ranged attack, no sustain, no area damage, or no defensive option. A hole is a run-ending risk.
- **Raise a ceiling:** you already have a working build and the item makes its main strength stronger.

In a fresh run, **fill holes first**. In a run that is already stable, take the ceiling.

### Question 2: Is the prefix active or passive?

An **active** prefix changes how you press buttons — extra projectiles, area effects, on-hit triggers, resource generation. A **passive** prefix adds a stat or a small conditional bonus. Active prefixes are usually worth more because they create new interactions with the rest of your gear. Passive stats are easier to replace.

Ask: *will I notice this prefix in the next fight, or only in the inventory screen?*

### Question 3: Does it fit my attack rhythm?

Bonehold combat is charge-based. A prefix that rewards many quick hits wants a fast weapon and aggressive positioning; a prefix that multiplies one big hit wants a slow, charged weapon and safe windows. A powerful item that fights your rhythm is worse than a modest item that matches it.

This is especially true for two-handed weapons, which now have a dedicated inventory icon after Patch 1.0.1. If you are swapping into a two-hander for the first time, take the reward that supports charge play, not the one that assumes you are dual-wielding.

### Question 4: Can I still dodge and block?

Survivability is not a separate stat in Bonehold; it is the ability to keep using your defensive tools. If a reward raises damage but slows your attacks or drains the resource you need for dodges, the next boss will punish it. Reviewers describe bosses as set-piece fights that punish greed — the reward that makes you greedier is a trap.

### Question 5: What does the next floor demand?

You usually know what killed you last run. If the next floor is cramped corridors, area damage and trap awareness matter. If it is open rooms with ranged enemies, a gap-closer or ranged option matters. If it is a boss floor, single-target burst and safe recovery windows matter.

Take the item that answers the **next** floor, not the one that wins the spreadsheet.

## Rarity vs Synergy: A Decision Table

| Situation | Take this | Why |
|-----------|-----------|-----|
| You have one strong weapon and no sustain | The item with healing, lifesteal, or defensive utility | A run ends when you cannot recover |
| You have a stable build and a legendary with a matching prefix | The legendary | Build-defining power spikes are worth chasing |
| The legendary has an anti-synergy prefix | The Epic that fits your rhythm | Rarity does not fix a playstyle mismatch |
| You are on a deep floor and a spell is offered | The spell, if it covers a hole | Spells scale with the floor they were found on (1.0.2) |
| You are about to enter a trap room | The defensive or mobility option | Trap rooms guarantee a Legendary, so survive to claim it |
| All three are sidegrades | Take the one with the active prefix | Active effects create future synergies |

## The Special-Room Guarantees Change the Risk Math

Patch 1.0.1 made two room types much more valuable:

- **Arena and Mini-boss rooms:** at least **Epic**.
- **Trap rooms:** always **Legendary**.

This creates a clear risk/reward loop. A trap room is no longer a coin flip for a mediocre drop — it is a guaranteed Legendary, which means the correct play is often to **clear the trap room before the boss**, not after. The reward can define the run, and the boss draft then becomes a choice between three items rather than a desperate search for one.

But the guarantee does not make trap rooms safe. Reviewers repeatedly mention trap lethality and slow trap timers. Before entering, ask:

1. **Do I have enough health to survive a mistake?** If not, clear other rooms first.
2. **Do I have a movement or defensive option?** Traps punish standing still.
3. **Can I afford to lose this run?** If the run is your deepest, the guaranteed Legendary may still not be worth the risk.

**Pro tip:** treat the trap room as a boss prep room. Clear it, take the Legendary, then reassess your build before the floor boss. The guaranteed item often changes which of the three boss rewards you should take.

## When to Break Your Build

The most common mistake is refusing to break a build that is already working. Bonehold rewards adaptation, but not endless adaptation. Use this rule:

- **Break your build for an active prefix that covers a hole.** Example: you are a melee build with no answer to ranged enemies, and a boss offers a ranged active effect. Take it.
- **Do not break your build for a passive stat increase.** A slightly bigger number is not worth losing the synergy your current gear provides.
- **Do not break your build twice in one floor.** Every swap costs you familiarity and, often, a resource. One deliberate pivot per floor is enough.

Reviewers describe the game's best moments as "broken-and-beautiful" item combinations. Those come from one or two deliberate pivots, not every slot at every boss.

## Common Mistakes

- **Taking the highest rarity automatically.** Rarity is a signal, not a verdict.
- **Ignoring the next floor.** The boss you just beat is not the problem; the floor ahead is.
- **Skipping the trap room because it is scary.** A guaranteed Legendary is one of the best deals in the game — if you can survive it.
- **Hoarding a reward you cannot use.** An item in the inventory does nothing. If it does not fit, take the pick that does.
- **Forgetting ring scaling.** A deep-floor ring can be a real upgrade even if its rarity color looks unimpressive.

## FAQ

**Can I take more than one boss item?** No. The boss drops three and you pick one.

**Are trap-room Legendaries always better than boss rewards?** Not necessarily. A Legendary with the wrong prefix can be worse than an Epic that fits your build.

**Do spells scale with the floor?** Yes, as of Patch 1.0.2. A spell found deeper rolls stronger, which makes deep-floor spell rewards more attractive.

## Final Verdict

The three-item boss draft is the clearest expression of Bonehold's design: randomized loot, permanent death, and a game that asks you to recognize a build instead of planning one. Take the item that fills your biggest hole or creates the most active interactions, not the one with the prettiest color. Clear trap rooms when you can survive them, because a guaranteed Legendary is worth more than a safe floor. And remember the review's warning — bosses punish greed. Your reward choice should make you safer, not greedier.
