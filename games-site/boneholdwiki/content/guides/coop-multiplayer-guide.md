# Bonehold Co-Op & Multiplayer Guide — Is Bonehold Multiplayer?

The short answer: **no.** Bonehold is a strictly single-player game. This guide covers the confirmed facts about Bonehold's multiplayer status, the platforms you can actually play it on, and what your options are if you were hoping for co-op with a friend.

## The Confirmed Answer: Single-Player Only

As of August 2026, Bonehold's official Steam page lists **"Single-player"** as the only gameplay feature. There is no co-op, no online multiplayer, no local multiplayer, and no crossplay in any form. The game was designed from the ground up as a solo dungeon-crawling roguelike, and nothing in its launch materials, trailers, or community coverage suggests multiplayer is planned.

## Why This Matters for the Gameplay Loop

Bonehold being single-player shapes the whole experience in a few concrete ways:

- **Every run is yours alone.** The roguelike loop — clearing procedurally generated floors of the ancient fortress, collecting loot, and inevitably dying — is entirely self-contained. There is no one to revive you and no shared loot to argue over.
- **Death is final per run.** Reviews of the launch build note that Bonehold has **no checkpoint or save system** — when you die, the run is over. This is a deliberate roguelike design choice, but it means a friend cannot "jump in" to salvage a run either.
- **The pacing is deliberate.** Bonehold's combat revolves around charged attacks, dodges, blocks, and parries, with traps and chokepoints encouraging patience over twitchy aggression. This methodical pacing was designed for one player's attention.

## What About Steam Remote Play Together?

Steam **Remote Play Together** lets you stream local multiplayer games to friends, but it only works if the game has local co-op in the first place. Since Bonehold has no local multiplayer, **Remote Play Together will not give you co-op**. The same goes for third-party screen-sharing tools like Parsec — they can stream your screen, but the game itself has no second-player mode.

If you and a friend both own the game, the only shared experience is playing **separate runs side by side** and comparing builds, loot, and boss kills afterward. For a game whose replayability comes from build variety and randomized loot, that can still be a lot of fun — but it is not co-op.

## Is Bonehold Good Solo? — What Reviews Say

If you were hoping for co-op and are now deciding whether to play solo, the launch reception is a useful signal:

- Steam reviews are **"Very Positive"** (roughly 88-90% positive in the first week after the July 28, 2026 release).
- Reviewers praise the **tight, satisfying combat**, the **huge variety of item prefixes and build possibilities** (item synergy has been compared to *The Binding of Isaac*), and strong replay value from procedurally generated loot.
- Common criticisms are the **slow pacing** (charged attacks and trap timers make runs deliberate), **animation jank**, and the **lack of a save/checkpoint system** — which makes runs long and punishing.

In other words: the game was designed to be a complete solo experience, and the reviews suggest it succeeds at that. It just is not a game to buy for a group.

## Why Bonehold Is Solo by Design

Understanding *why* the game is single-player makes the answer easier to accept. Bonehold's design choices all point in one direction:

- **The pacing is built for one player.** The charge-attack system is a game of commitment: you decide when to commit to a slow, powerful attack and when to back off. That tension works because the enemy's attention is entirely on you. In a co-op game, aggro splits, and the whole risk/reward loop of charging changes.
- **The buildcrafting is personal.** Item prefixes "twist playstyles in unexpected ways" — a run's identity forms around *your* drops and *your* decisions. The developer's framing, echoed in reviews, is a single player discovering a build one item at a time.
- **The difficulty curve assumes one character's power.** Reviews describe bosses as set-piece encounters that punish greedy players — a design that assumes one player's resources, stamina pool, and attention.
- **The roguelike structure is self-contained.** With no checkpoints and permanent death per run, every death is entirely yours. That is the genre's intended emotional loop: solitary risk, solitary reward.

None of this means the game is worse for being solo — reviewers rank the tight combat and build variety among the genre's best. It just means the co-op question has a design answer, not just a feature answer.

## Making the Game Feel Social Anyway

Even without built-in multiplayer, there are several ways to share the experience:

- **Side-by-side runs.** The most popular community activity for solo roguelikes: two people play separate runs simultaneously (in the same room or on a call) and compare builds, bosses, and deaths. Bonehold's procedural generation makes every run different, so there is always something new to compare.
- **Race to the deepest floor.** A friendlier competition than head-to-head: whoever clears the furthest or finds the best legendary wins bragging rights.
- **Share build discoveries.** The game's prefix system produces stories ("I found a mace that turns every kill into an explosion") — trading those stories is where most of the social value lives. Reviews describe exactly these "wait until you see this item" moments.
- **Stream or share gameplay.** The game is Windows-only, so screen sharing, streaming, or recording runs is the standard way to show a friend what the game is like — a free demo helps them try it themselves.
- **Community guides and wikis.** This site exists because the game is fun to talk about. Contributing findings, correcting claims, and sharing strategies is a genuinely social activity.

If the social aspect is non-negotiable for you, look for co-op roguelikes that officially support multiplayer before buying — there are several in the genre. If you are okay with parallel play, Bonehold's run-to-run variety keeps parallel runs interesting for a long time.
