# Games Like Bonehold — 8 Similar Roguelike Dungeon Crawlers to Play

Bonehold scratches a specific itch: fast melee combat in a dark fantasy dungeon, random loot that builds itself, and a difficulty curve that punishes greed. If you have finished its depths and want more — or if you tried the demo and want to compare alternatives before buying — this guide lists the eight games most worth your time.

**Honesty note first:** three of these games are **officially tied to Bonehold** — the Steam store page lists bundles with them, which is as strong a similarity signal as a developer can give. The rest are editorial picks chosen because their Steam genres and design DNA match Bonehold's own store tags (verified: Dark Fantasy, Dungeon Crawler, Souls-like, Loot, Action Roguelike, Hack and Slash, 1990's, Pixel Graphics, Hand-drawn, Stylized, Atmospheric). This is a recommendation list, not an official ranking — prices change, so check current Steam pages before buying.

## The Official Bundle Partners (Verified on the Steam Store Page)

Bonehold's store page lists 19 bundles. Four stand out as genuinely similar games, and buying them bundled is often cheaper than standalone.

### 1. Barony — The Co-Op Dungeon Crawler

Barony is the closest thing to "Bonehold with friends." It is a **first-person roguelike dungeon crawler**: procedurally generated levels, dozens of character classes, a huge loot pool, and permanent death. Where it differs from Bonehold: first-person perspective, turn-based-ish pacing is replaced with real-time dungeon crawling, and — the big one — **it supports co-op**, which Bonehold does not.

- **Official tie:** the **Baronehold** bundle (Bonehold + Barony, 10-16% off) is listed directly on Bonehold's Steam page.
- **Pick it if:** you wanted Bonehold's dungeon crawling but wished you could bring friends.

### 2. Mortal Sin — The Brutal First-Person Melee Roguelike

Mortal Sin is a **first-person action-horror roguelike** built around fast melee combat — hacking, slashing, and combo finishers in procedurally generated gothic levels. It shares Bonehold's dark fantasy DNA, its melee-forward combat, and its single-player permadeath structure. It finished its Early Access run with a 1.0 update in 2025 and holds a very positive rating on Steam.

- **Official tie:** the **Mortal Bone** bundle (Bonehold + Mortal Sin) is listed on Bonehold's Steam page.
- **Pick it if:** you love Bonehold's combat focus and want it in a tighter, gorier, first-person package.

### 3. Verho – Curse of Faces — The Retro-Dark Fantasy RPG

Verho is a **dark fantasy RPG** with deliberately retro PS1-era graphics, inspired by classic dungeon crawlers like *King's Field* and *Lunacid*. Its distinctive feature is a **class-based mask system** — masks define your build, much like classes and item prefixes define yours in Bonehold. A word of caution: **it is not a roguelike** — it is a more traditional exploration RPG. Its similarity to Bonehold is atmospheric and aesthetic (dark dungeons, retro 3D, build-defining equipment) rather than structural.

- **Official tie:** the **Mask and Bone** bundle (Bonehold + Verho – Curse of Faces) is listed on Bonehold's Steam page. Verho is also heading to consoles on July 30, 2026.
- **Pick it if:** Bonehold's retro dungeon atmosphere is your favorite part and you want a slower, deeper world to explore.

### 4. Eyes Of The ElderWood — Same Developer, Different Horror

Not a roguelike at all — but this is **Pixel Jackal's other game**, the same developer behind Bonehold. It is a first-person folk horror title, and it has been sold bundled with Bonehold at 10% off on deal trackers. Fans of the studio's art direction and tone will find the family resemblance; fans of Bonehold's *combat* will not.

- **Pick it if:** you want to support the developer and enjoy their visual style, in a horror rather than action package.

## Genre Neighbors (Editorial Picks Matched to Bonehold's Steam Tags)

These four are not officially linked to Bonehold, but their genres line up directly with the game's verified Steam tags — Action Roguelike, Loot, Combat, Dark Fantasy, and Dungeon Crawler.

### 5. Hades — The Action-Roguelike Masterclass

If Bonehold's tight combat and build variety are what hooked you, Hades is the genre's gold standard. Fast real-time combat, god-granted boons that twist your build every run (very much the same feeling as Bonehold's item prefixes), and a story that actually progresses as you die and retry. The boon system is the closest mainstream cousin to the "build-defining loot" loop Bonehold players rave about.

### 6. Dead Cells — The Fast-Paced Weapon Roulette

Dead Cells pairs action-roguelite runs with a huge weapon pool, where every run hands you a new loadout to improvise around. If your favorite Bonehold moments were "a crazy prefix dropped and I rebuilt my run around it," Dead Cells is the same adrenaline in 2D. It trades Bonehold's dungeon-crawler atmosphere for a metroidvania-inspired world, but the loop — kill, loot, die, rebuild — is identical.

### 7. The Binding of Isaac — The Item-Synergy Original

Bonehold's launch reviews explicitly compared its item-driven synergy design to *The Binding of Isaac* — the game that made "every run is a different build" a genre staple. Isaac is top-down and grimly cartoonish rather than 3D melee, but if your favorite part of Bonehold is discovering how random items combine into broken synergies, Isaac is the deepest well in gaming.

### 8. Rogue Legacy 2 — The Class-Rotation Dungeon Crawler

Rogue Legacy 2 is a roguelite dungeon crawler built around rotating classes on every run and building permanent upgrades between deaths. It is the best entry on this list if you specifically loved Bonehold's **class variety** — each class in Rogue Legacy 2 plays dramatically differently, and the lineage system makes every run structurally fresh. Its lighter tone is a pleasant break from Bonehold's grim fortresses.

## How to Choose Based on What You Loved About Bonehold

- **Loved the combat?** Try **Mortal Sin** (melee-forward) or **Hades** (combat-driven runs).
- **Loved the buildcrafting and loot?** **The Binding of Isaac** and **Dead Cells**.
- **Loved the dungeon crawling and wish it had co-op?** **Barony** — the only real co-op option on this list.
- **Loved the retro atmosphere more than the roguelike structure?** **Verho – Curse of Faces**.
- **Finished Bonehold and want the same developer's other work?** **Eyes Of The ElderWood**.

One more honest note: similarity is subjective, and every game above has its own identity — do not expect any of them to *be* Bonehold.
