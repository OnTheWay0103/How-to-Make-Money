# Bonehold Demo Guide - What the Free Demo Includes and How to Use It

Bonehold has a **free demo on Steam** — and for a $9 roguelike, trying before buying is an unusually good deal. This guide covers what is confirmed about the demo, what is still unconfirmed, and exactly how to use the demo to decide whether the full fortress descent is for you.

**Honesty note:** the demo exists and is free (confirmed on the Steam store). The demo's *specific contents* — how many floors, which classes, how much loot — are **not documented in detail** by official sources, so this guide tells you how to test the demo yourself rather than guessing for you.

## What the Demo Lets You Verify

The demo is your chance to answer the questions that reviews and guides cannot decide for you — because taste is personal:

### 1. The Charge Combat Rhythm

The charge system is Bonehold's most divisive feature. Some players love the deliberate pacing; others find it slow. The demo settles this instantly:

- Do you enjoy *waiting* for the right window to hold a charge, or do you want constant action?
- Does the tap → partial → full charge loop feel satisfying in your hands?
- Do you miss when you try to tap a quick attack? (Reviews say a quick stab "won't cut it late-game" — feel that tradeoff yourself.)

### 2. The Trap Pacing

Reviewers consistently name traps as the game's most frustrating element — "forced waiting on spike pits and flame walls" is the #1 recurring complaint. In the demo:

- Play a few rooms and ask: is memorizing trap layouts fun for you, or annoying?
- Try luring skeletons into a flame wall — this is the confirmed payoff that makes the trap system click for many players.

### 3. Class Feel

With several classes available, the demo is your chance to test the officially named ones — **Knight** (defensive, forgiving), **Barbarian** (aggressive, high damage), **Scorcher** (fragile fire mage), **Spellblade** (magic/melee hybrid). Try at least two: the class that *sounds* right is not always the one that *feels* right.

### 4. Performance on Your Machine

This is the most practical demo use: the full game's system requirements contain documented typos (RAM and storage figures are wrong on the Steam page — see our [System Requirements Guide](/guides/system-requirements-guide)). The demo lets you test the real engine on your actual PC:

- Note your frame rate in dense, trap-heavy rooms (the heaviest load).
- Check whether the cursor/menu quirks one review reported affect your setup.
- If it runs smooth on your machine in the demo, the full game will run at least as well.

## How Long Is the Demo?

Unconfirmed — no official duration exists. Based on what the FAQ confirms (early floors, several classes) and typical roguelike demo design, expect a session roughly in the range of the game's average session length (about 1.5–2 hours of playtime reported in player analytics for the full game). Treat that as a rough estimate, not a promise.

## Demo vs. Full Game: What You Are Not Seeing

To set expectations honestly, the demo almost certainly does not show you everything the full game offers:

- **Deep-floor content.** The elite-per-floor bosses and the deeper difficulty curve live past the early floors.
- **The full prefix/loot sandbox.** The procedural item system (confirmed as the heart of buildcrafting) shows its depth over many drops — the demo gives you a taste, not the full banquet.
- **The 98-achievement long game.** Achievement hunting is a full-game project by nature.

## Demo Checklist

Before you buy, use the demo to check off:

- [ ] Charge combat feels good in your hands
- [ ] Trap waiting is tolerable (or even fun when you use them offensively)
- [ ] Your machine holds stable frame rates in dense rooms
- [ ] One class feels like "your" class
- [ ] The dark fortress atmosphere holds your attention for a full session

**The verdict formula:** if the demo leaves you wanting more floors, the full game — with its deeper loot, elites, and bosses — is very likely worth the buy. If the demo's pacing or traps frustrate you, the full game extends those same systems for 10+ hours, and the [Review Guide](/guides/review-guide) has the honest breakdown of who should skip.
