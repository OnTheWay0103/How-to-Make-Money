# How Long Is Bonehold - Game Length, Run Duration, and Replayability

"How long is Bonehold?" is the wrong question — and this guide will tell you why. Bonehold is a roguelike: the "story" is a procedurally generated fortress descent that ends when you die or clear it, and the game's real length lives in the hundreds of runs the loot system encourages. This guide gives you the honest numbers we do have, the expectations that fit each play style, and what "beating" Bonehold actually means.

**Honesty note:** Pixel Jackal has published no official completion time, and a randomized roguelike resists a single "how long to beat" number anyway. The figures below come from launch coverage (including player analytics) and are marked accordingly; everything else is a framework, not a promise.

## The Numbers We Have

| Metric | Value | Status |
|--------|-------|--------|
| Average play session | **~1.6 hours** | Reported in launch player analytics (VaporLens) |
| Price-to-hours reputation | "dozens of hours" | Reported in launch coverage at ~$9 |
| Official completion time | None published | Confirmed absent — no official statement exists |
| Steam achievements | 98 | ✅ Confirmed (list unpublished) |
| Mid-run saves | None | ✅ Confirmed — death or quitting ends the run |

**The session number is the practical one.** Because the game has **no checkpoint or save system** (confirmed by multiple reviews), every session needs to fit inside a run you can finish — and with ~1.6-hour average sessions, that is the realistic block of time you should budget per attempt.

## What "Completing" Bonehold Means

A roguelike's length depends on what you are trying to achieve. Here is a realistic playtime framework:

| Goal | Estimated Hours | What It Takes |
|------|----------------|---------------|
| Learn the game | ~3–5 hours | First bosses down, one class comfortable, early deaths behind you |
| First full clear | ~10–20 hours | Depends heavily on build luck and trap mastery — some players clear far earlier, some much later (unverified) — no official statistics) |
| Clear with all classes | 2–3× the first-clear time | 7 classes (4 officially named), each requiring its own run discipline |
| 100% achievements | 50+ hours | 98 achievements including RNG-dependent loot achievements — a long-term project (see the [Achievements Guide](/guides/achievements-guide)) |
| Legendary hunting & deep runs | Infinite | The confirmed endgame loop — see the [Endgame Guide](/guides/endgame-guide) |

**The honest headline:** there is no official "beating" timer, and reviews describe the game as a "satisfying short-loop dungeon romp" — short per run, long in total. Expect the first full clear to take roughly 10–20 hours of actual play, then multiply for everything after.

## Why Runs Take As Long As They Do

The average session of ~1.6 hours is longer than many roguelike runs because Bonehold's design is deliberately methodical:

1. **Charge combat is slow by design.** Reviews confirm the pacing is the point: you wait for windows, hold charges, and reposition constantly. There is no button-mashing fast lane.
2. **Traps force waiting.** Spike pits and flame walls cycle on timers; reviews explicitly list "forced waiting" as a pace factor. A single floor can take several minutes longer than its combat would suggest.
3. **Room checking is a habit.** Reviewers describe stopping after every room to evaluate prefixes — that inventory time adds up over a run.
4. **No saves means no shortcuts.** You cannot "just finish tomorrow" — the run you start is the run you keep, so players naturally play more carefully (and slower) as runs deepen.

## Replayability: The Real Length

Where Bonehold's length actually lives:

- **The loot system** (confirmed: procedurally generated weapons, armor, spells with prefixes) makes every run a new build puzzle. Reviews compare the item combinations to *The Binding of Isaac* — a game famous for hundreds of hours.
- **98 achievements** give completionists a long, structured tail.
- **7 classes** (4 officially named) each change how the fortress plays.
- **Player analytics** show a "fun-then-drop" pattern: intense early engagement with sessions around 1.6 hours — the length question for most players is less "when do I finish" and more "when do I stop."

## A Realistic Session Plan

Because runs can't be saved mid-way, plan your play:

| Situation | Plan |
|-----------|------|
| 30 minutes free | Skip — a meaningful run needs ~1.5 hours, and a rushed deep run is how you lose good gear |
| 1–2 hours | The sweet spot for a full run attempt |
| 3+ hours | Multiple runs, or one very deep attempt with time to spare |
| Achievement hunting day | Plan 4+ hours: deep runs generate the most achievement progress per hour (see the Achievements Guide) |

## Final Answer

**How long is Bonehold?** A full run takes roughly 1.5–2 hours once you know what you are doing; the first full clear takes about 10–20 hours of play; 100% completion is a 50+ hour project; and the loot-driven endgame is effectively endless. At ~$9 that is an excellent hours-per-dollar ratio — the same conclusion the launch reviews reached with an 8.3/10 score and "dozens of hours" verdicts.
