# Bonehold Weapons Guide - Charge Attacks and Weapon Types

Weapons in Bonehold are more than stat sticks -- they define how you approach every combat encounter. The hold-to-charge mechanic gives each weapon type a unique rhythm, and item prefixes can completely transform their behavior. This Bonehold weapons guide covers everything from basic charge mechanics to advanced legendary synergies.

## Charge Mechanic Deep-Dive

Every weapon in Bonehold uses the charge system. Holding the attack button builds power through distinct levels, each with different properties.

### Charge Levels Explained

- **Level 0 (tap attack):** Instant swing, low damage, no bonus effects. Use this to interrupt enemy attacks, apply on-hit effects, or when you only have a fraction of a second before needing to dodge.
- **Level 1 (partial charge, ~0.3 seconds):** 1.5x damage, small knockback. The sweet spot for weaving attacks between enemy swings. Most weapons also gain a slight range increase at this level.
- **Level 2 (medium charge, ~0.7 seconds):** 2.5x damage, medium knockback or small AoE, reduced stamina cost per damage. Good for clearing groups of standard enemies in a single hit.
- **Level 3 (full charge, ~1.2 seconds):** 4x damage, maximum knockback / largest AoE, weapon-specific special effect. The boss-killer. Every weapon type has a unique level 3 effect:
  - **One-handed swords:** Wide sweeping arc that hits all enemies in a 180-degree front cone.
  - **Two-handed axes:** Overhead slam that creates a shockwave damaging enemies behind the primary target.
  - **Staves:** Spell-infused blast that detonates with the staff's elemental type.
  - **Crossbows:** Armor-piercing bolt that passes through all enemies in a line.
  - **Maces:** Ground pound that stuns nearby enemies.
- **Level 4+ (overcharge, beyond full):** 5x+ damage, unique effects, costs stamina per tick. Overcharging is high risk -- you cannot cancel a charge beyond level 3, and taking damage while overcharging cancels the attack entirely.

### When to Quick-Attack vs. Full-Charge

| Situation | Best Choice | Reason |
|-----------|-------------|--------|
| Enemy winding up an attack | Quick or Level 1 | Faster recovery lets you dodge |
| After a successful parry | Full Charge (Level 3) | Enemy is fully staggered |
| Fighting a group of weak enemies | Level 1-2 | Kill multiple without overcommitting |
| Boss recovery window | Full Charge (Level 3) | Maximum damage during safe window |
| You have a 1-second opening | Level 2 | Reliable damage without overextending |
| Emergency interrupt needed | Quick (Level 0) | Instant, no commitment |

## Weapon Categories

### One-Handed Swords

The most versatile weapon type. Balanced speed, damage, and charge time. Works with shields for defensive setups.

- **Base stats:** Medium damage, fast charge speed, medium range
- **Best for:** Knight, Spellblade, Shadowblade
- **Level 3 effect:** Wide sweeping arc

### Two-Handed Axes and Hammers

Slow but devastating. These weapons demand large openings but delete anything they hit.

- **Base stats:** High damage, slow charge speed, long range
- **Best for:** Barbarian, Death Knight hybrid
- **Level 3 effect (axe):** Shockwave cleave
- **Level 3 effect (hammer):** Ground pound stun

### Staves

The magic-user's weapon. Staves have lower physical damage but charge faster and deal bonus elemental damage based on your equipped spell scrolls.

- **Base stats:** Low physical damage, fastest charge speed, medium range
- **Best for:** Scorcher, Arcanist, Warden
- **Level 3 effect:** Elemental blast matching your active spell school

### Crossbows

Ranged physical weapons. Crossbows let you engage enemies from safety but require manual reloading between shots. Charge affects bolt penetration and velocity.

- **Base stats:** Medium damage, medium charge speed, long range, requires reload
- **Best for:** Any class as secondary weapon
- **Level 3 effect:** Armor-piercing line shot

### Maces

Blunt weapons that excel against armored enemies. Maces deal bonus damage to shields and armored skeletons. Slower than swords but faster than two-handed weapons.

- **Base stats:** Medium-high damage, medium charge speed, bonus armor damage
- **Best for:** Knight, Warden
- **Level 3 effect:** Ground pound stun

## How Prefixes Change Weapon Behavior

Prefixes are the true power of Bonehold's loot system. A common sword with the right prefix can outperform a rare weapon with a bad one.

**Damage-type prefixes:**

- **Flaming** -- Adds fire damage over time. Great for sustained fights and Scorcher synergy.
- **Frost** -- Adds slow effect on hit. Excellent for kiting and controlling fight pacing.
- **Shocking** -- Adds chain lightning between enemies. Strong in dense rooms.
- **Chaos** -- Random element per swing. Unpredictable but scales well with luck.

**Utility prefixes:**

- **Vampiric** -- Heal for a percentage of damage dealt. The best sustain prefix in the game.
- **Quick** -- Reduces charge time by 20%. Faster attacks mean more damage windows.
- **Sturdy** -- Bonus armor and block chance when wielding. Pure defense.
- **Berserker** -- More damage at low health. High risk, high reward for Barbarians.

**Transformative prefixes:**

- **Explosive** -- Attacks cause small explosions. Converts any weapon into AoE clear.
- **Echoing** -- Attacks create a delayed second hit. Doubles effective damage.
- **Cursed** -- Powerful buffs with a debuff penalty. Build around the downside.
- **Siphoning** -- Restores mana per hit. Essential for spell-heavy builds.

**Rule of thumb:** Transformative prefixes (Explosive, Echoing, Cursed) scale better into deep floors than pure damage-type prefixes. Fire is good on floor 1; Explosive is still good on floor 6.

## Legendary Weapons vs. Regular Weapons

Legendary items in Bonehold are rare power spikes with exclusive prefixes and unique 3D models. They are not strictly necessary to win, but finding one dramatically shifts a run's power curve.

**What makes legendaries special:**

- **Unique legendary prefix** that cannot appear on regular items (e.g., "World Ender" -- kills cause explosions that scale with the killed enemy's max health)
- **Increased base damage** (roughly 30% higher than comparable regular weapons)
- **Additional hidden property** (faster charge speed, unique charge effect, or passive aura)
- **No negative prefixes** -- legendary items always roll pure positives

**Strategies when you find a legendary:**

1. **Build around its unique effect immediately.** A legendary defines your run.
2. **Respec your scroll loadout** to complement the legendary's effect.
3. **Do not replace it** unless you find another legendary -- the stat difference is too large.

## Dual-Wielding vs. Two-Handing

Bonehold allows dual-wielding one-handed weapons or two-handing a single weapon for increased damage.

- **Dual-wielding:** Attacks alternate between weapons, applying both weapons' prefixes. Excellent for stacking on-hit effects like Vampiric + Frost. Costs 10% more stamina per swing.
- **Two-handing:** 25% bonus damage with a single one-handed weapon, but you lose shield access. Best when you find a powerful one-handed weapon with a great prefix but no good shield to pair it with.

**Which is better?** Dual-wielding wins for prefix synergy builds (two Vampiric weapons = double healing). Two-handing wins when you have one exceptional weapon.

## Weapon Swapping Mid-Combat

You can carry up to 3 weapons and swap freely in combat (no cooldown, minimal animation). This is an advanced technique that separates good players from great ones.

**Effective swap strategies:**

- **Range swap:** Start with crossbow to weaken approaching enemies, swap to melee for cleanup.
- **Prefix swap:** Apply debuffs with a Frost weapon, then swap to a high-damage weapon while the slow is active.
- **Emergency swap:** Keep a Sturdy shield weapon set for dangerous rooms, swap back for damage.

Weapon swapping costs no stamina. Use it liberally.
