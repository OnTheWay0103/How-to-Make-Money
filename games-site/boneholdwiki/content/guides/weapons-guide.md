# Bonehold Weapons Guide - Charge Attacks and Weapon Types

Weapons in Bonehold are more than stat sticks — they define how you approach every combat encounter. The hold-to-charge mechanic gives each weapon its rhythm, and item prefixes can completely transform their behavior. This Bonehold weapons guide covers the confirmed charge mechanics, the weapon types observed in the game, and how prefixes and legendaries turn a weapon into a build.

**Honesty note:** launch coverage confirms the charge system, the existence of multiple weapon categories (including staves, maces, and crossbows), and the prefix/legendary loot system. Exact charge timings, damage multipliers, and per-weapon special effects are **not officially documented** — treat any specific numbers in other guides as unverified.

## The Charge Mechanic — Confirmed Core

The charge system is the confirmed heart of Bonehold combat. The developer's description emphasizes charged attacks, and launch reviews are explicit that "a quick stab won't cut it late-game" — holding the attack button to build power is mandatory for serious damage.

**Charge framework:**

- **Quick tap:** An instant, low-damage swing. Use it to interrupt enemy attacks, apply on-hit effects, or when you only have a fraction of a second before needing to dodge.
- **Partial charge:** A short hold for moderate damage with mild effects (knockback, small area). The workhorse for clearing groups of weak enemies.
- **Full charge:** Holding until the weapon is fully charged unleashes maximum damage and the weapon's signature effect. The boss-killer — when you have a safe window.
- **Charge pacing is the game's "skill."** Reviews describe the combat as deliberately paced: positioning and timing matter more than raw speed. Learning when *not* to commit is the core skill.

*Exact charge levels, timings, and multipliers are unconfirmed. The tap/partial/full framework reflects what reviews and the developer describe.*

### When to Quick-Attack vs. Full-Charge

| Situation | Best Choice | Reason |
|-----------|-------------|--------|
| Enemy winding up an attack | Quick attack | Fast recovery lets you dodge |
| After a successful parry | Full charge | Enemy is fully staggered |
| Group of weak enemies | Partial charge | Kill several without overcommitting |
| Boss recovery window | Full charge | Maximum damage during a safe window |
| Short opening, unsure of enemy | Partial charge | Reliable damage without overextending |
| Need an interrupt | Quick attack | Instant, no commitment |

## Weapon Categories

The following weapon families are observed in Bonehold's launch coverage and community discussion. The *archetypes* are reasonable expectations for the game's fantasy setting, and staves, maces, and crossbows are specifically mentioned in reviews. Exact stats per category are unconfirmed.

### One-Handed Swords

The classic versatile weapon. Balanced speed, damage, and charge time; pairs naturally with a shield for defensive setups.

- **Character:** Medium damage, fast-ish charge, good all-rounder.
- **Best for:** Knight, Spellblade — and any class wanting a balanced loadout.

### Two-Handed Axes and Hammers

Slow but devastating. These weapons demand large openings but delete anything they hit.

- **Character:** High damage, slower charge, wide swings.
- **Best for:** Barbarian, and aggressive hybrid builds.

### Staves

The magic-user's weapon, confirmed in reviews: the "slow-charging staff" is a real archetype, and one review highlights a hybrid build built around a slow-charging staff "to hilarious effect."

- **Character:** Low physical damage, charge powers magical effects, slower charge time.
- **Best for:** Scorcher, Spellblade — and anyone exploring magic-heavy hybrids.

### Crossbows

Ranged physical weapons, confirmed in reviews (one reviewer picked up a "strange crossbow prefix just to see what chaos would follow"). Crossbows let you engage from safety.

- **Character:** Ranged, medium damage, charge affects the shot's power.
- **Best for:** Any class as a secondary option for softening enemies at range.

### Maces

Blunt weapons, confirmed in reviews: a "common mace" with the right prefix is cited as an example of a run-defining item. Maces are classically strong against armored enemies.

- **Character:** Medium-high damage, slower than swords, blunt impact.
- **Best for:** Knight, and defensive builds.

## How Prefixes Change Weapon Behavior

Prefixes are the true power of Bonehold's loot system — confirmed by the developer ("prefixes twist playstyles in unexpected ways") and by reviews ("a common mace plus a funky prefix can become a run-defining toy").

**What is confirmed:**

- Prefixes attach to weapons, spells, and armor.
- They change how an item behaves — damage type, attack properties, sustainability, or defense.
- A common weapon with the right prefix can outperform a better-rarity weapon with the wrong one.
- Prefixes combine across your loadout, and reviewers compare the synergy to *The Binding of Isaac*.

**What is unconfirmed:** the actual prefix names, their effects, and rarity colors. Any specific prefix names you see in guides (this one included, in older versions) are unverified.

**Evaluation rules that work without names:**

- **Damage-type prefixes** (fire, frost, lightning-style effects) define your damage profile — picking one theme usually beats mixing randomly.
- **Utility prefixes** (faster charge, knockback, area) change how the weapon *feels* — these are the "twist your playstyle" effects the developer describes.
- **Sustainability prefixes** (healing, resource recovery) matter more on long runs than on short ones.
- **Transformative prefixes** (effects that spawn new damage sources, like explosions or echoes) are the kind of effect that can redefine a whole run.
- When a prefix combos with another item you own, take it. Synergy beats raw stats.

## Legendary Weapons vs. Regular Weapons

Legendary items are confirmed as Bonehold's top loot tier: rare drops, described by reviews as "power spikes" that can "break runs open in the best way," with reviewers noting "there are many" of them.

**What makes legendaries special (confirmed):**

- They are rare and dramatically more powerful than regular items.
- Their effects are strong enough to define a run — finding one is an "oh no, oh yes" moment.
- They are best used as build centerpieces, not passive stat upgrades.

**Strategies when you find a legendary:**

1. **Build around its unique effect immediately.** A legendary defines your run.
2. **Rework your other loadout to complement it.** Look for prefixes and spells that combo with the legendary's effect.
3. **Do not replace it casually.** Legendaries are the highest confirmed tier — replacing one with a regular item needs a strong reason.

*Legendary drop rates and exact power values are unconfirmed. Any percentages in other guides are invented.*

## Loadout Strategy

The game lets you carry weapons and swap based on the situation (the exact inventory rules are unconfirmed, but reviews describe experimenting with different weapon types mid-run).

**Effective loadout strategies:**

- **Range + melee coverage:** Keep a crossbow-style ranged option for softening enemies at distance and a melee weapon for the corridor fight.
- **Prefix flexibility:** Carry a second weapon with different effects so you can adapt to what the run demands (a sustain setup for long floors, a damage setup for bosses).
- **Read the room, not the sheet:** Choose your weapon for the *next* room type — corridors favor wide swings, open halls favor range.
