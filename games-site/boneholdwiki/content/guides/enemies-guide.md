# Bonehold Enemies Guide - All Enemy Types and How to Fight Them

The ancient necromantic fortress of Bonehold is packed with undead horrors, and knowing what you are about to fight is half the battle. This guide collects everything that is **confirmed** about Bonehold's enemy roster from official materials and launch coverage — and it is honest about what is not yet documented.

**Honesty note:** Bonehold has no officially published enemy bestiary as of August 2026. The *families* of enemies below are confirmed by the developer's store description and multiple launch reviews; the specific enemy *names*, exact stats, and per-floor spawn tables are **not** officially documented. Where a detail is uncertain, we mark it 待验证.

## The Confirmed Enemy Families

The developer's own description names the three headline threats — "hordes of skeletons, ghosts, and magical abominations" — and launch coverage adds a few more families observed in the fortress. Every enemy you meet will belong to one of these groups:

| Enemy Family | Confirmed? | Where Reported | Overview |
|--------------|-----------|----------------|----------|
| Skeletons | ✅ Confirmed | Developer description, reviews | The fortress's rank-and-file. Fast melee attackers, deadly in groups, easily kited. |
| Ghosts | ✅ Confirmed | Developer description, reviews | Floating undead that pass through the fortress halls; harder to crowd than grounded enemies. |
| Magical abominations | ✅ Confirmed | Developer description, reviews | Mutated, spell-infused monsters with ranged or area magic. Highest priority targets. |

**Rule of thumb:** if a source names a specific enemy ("Floor 3 Skeleton Archer") with stats and exact spawn locations, treat it as unverified. The confirmed reality is simpler: skeletons swarm, ghosts hover and chase, magical abominations cast, and every floor is guarded by an elite.

## Threat Priority - Who Dies First

The confirmed combat wisdom applies to every room: **ranged and spellcasting enemies die first.** This is standard practice for the genre and consistent with how Bonehold's charge combat works — you cannot safely charge a heavy attack while a caster is lining you up from across the room.

| Priority | Enemy Type | Why |
|----------|-----------|-----|
| 1 | Magical abominations / casters | They damage you while you deal with melee pressure; a full charge window spent on a mace swing is a bad trade while a fireball is incoming |
| 2 | Ranged variants (archer-type skeletons, 待验证) | Similar logic: unavoidable chip damage from range forces dodges that waste stamina |
| 3 | Ghosts in groups | They close distance quickly and ignore obstacles — but they are usually not ranged, so they rank below casters |
| 4 | Basic skeletons | Melee-only, kiteable, and perfect parry/charge practice. Let them come to you |

## How to Fight Each Family

### Skeletons

The fortress's bread and butter. Reviews describe fighting through "cramped fortress corridors" with skeletons at every turn — you will parry and dispatch thousands of them over your playtime.

- **They are group fighters.** A lone skeleton is trivial; five skeletons in a corridor is a real fight. The solution is repositioning: back up to a chokepoint and force them to come one at a time.
- **Their attacks are telegraphed.** Skeleton windups are slow enough to learn within the first rooms — this is the game's built-in parry tutorial. Time your block just before the swing lands to stagger and open a full charge window.
- **They die to everything.** Skeletons are fragile. A partial charge clears several at once; a full charge overkills spectacularly. Do not waste heavy cooldowns on basic skeletons.
- **Trap synergy (confirmed):** reviewers explicitly describe pushing chasing skeletons into flame walls and burning them to ash — the most satisfying use of the environment in the game.

### Ghosts

Floating undead that hover through the fortress. They appear in the developer's official description, and reviews confirm them as a persistent threat type.

- **They ignore your chokepoint plans.** Ghosts float — they do not funnel the way skeleton mobs do. Expect them to flank.
- **They chase relentlessly.** You cannot lose them in a doorway. If you need space, break line of sight around a corner and use the recovery time to charge.
- **Fast hits, low individual durability (待验证).** Reviews do not publish ghost stats, but their behavior in coverage reads as a fast, fragile chaser — the kind of enemy you trade with using quick attacks and dodges rather than heavy charges.

### Magical Abominations

The third headline family — corrupted, spell-infused monsters that the developer explicitly names as a core threat.

- **They are the casters.** Expect ranged magic or area effects; reviewers describe dying to their attacks while managing skeleton pressure.
- **Kill them on sight.** When an abomination enters the room, it outranks every melee enemy on the field. Take the brief risk of advancing past the mob to remove the caster, then reset the fight on your terms.
- **Dodge, do not block (待验证).** Whether their spells are blockable is unconfirmed. When in doubt against a spellcaster, dodge sideways — blocking costs stamina you will need for the melee cleanup.

### Elite Bosses Per Floor

Multiple launch reviews confirm a "mighty boss" or elite boss guarding each floor, in addition to the game's grander boss encounters. Think of them as mid-floor skill checks.

- **They are tougher versions of the standard fight.** More health, harder hits, telegraphed special attacks — and they drop better loot, which is why players stop to fight them.
- **The boss framework applies.** Watch the tells first, defend, then charge only during recovery windows. Greed gets punished exactly as hard here as in real boss fights.
- **Do not fight them at low stamina.** The single most common elite-enemy death in roguelikes is walking in tired. Clear the room before engaging, or back out and reset.

## Room-Level Enemy Tactics

Because dungeon layouts are procedurally generated, you will face these families in unpredictable combinations. The confirmed play pattern:

1. **Peek before committing.** Enemies can ambush from side passages — enter rooms carefully.
2. **Read the room's composition.** Casters? Ranged? Pure melee swarm? The correct first move differs for each.
3. **Create a chokepoint or use a trap.** Back into a doorway, or bait the group into a flame wall (see our [Traps Guide](/guides/traps-guide)).
4. **Remove casters, then manage the melee.**
5. **Dodge, don't tank.** Even the tankiest Knight build absorbs a limited number of hits. Rolling costs little and avoids everything.

## Enemy Knowledge as Meta-Progression

One of Bonehold's hidden progression systems is *you*: every run teaches enemy windups, room templates, and spawn patterns that carry over in your head even when the run itself is lost. Reviews confirm the game is beatable through accumulated knowledge — the fortress's enemy families are finite, and your familiarity with them is the one upgrade that never resets.
