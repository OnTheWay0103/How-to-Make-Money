# Bonehold Beginner Guide - Essential Tips for Getting Started

Bonehold throws you into the dark halls of an ancient necromantic fortress with little more than a weapon and a prayer. As a 3D roguelike dungeon crawler from Pixel Jackal, every run is a fresh test of reflexes, strategy, and luck. Whether you are dying to the first skeleton or wondering what all these colored items do, this Bonehold beginner guide covers everything you need to survive your first hours.

## Choosing Your First Class

Bonehold launches with 4 base classes, with 3 more unlockable through progression. For new players, your first class choice matters more than anything else.

**Best starter classes:**

- **Knight** (recommended) -- Heavy armor, sword-and-shield, forgiving health pool. The Knight can block most early-game attacks and deals consistent damage without demanding precise charge timing. Ideal for learning enemy patterns and dungeon layouts.
- **Barbarian** (good alternative) -- Two-handed weapons, rage mechanic, high burst damage. The Barbarian kills things fast, which means fewer chances to take damage. Its rage bar builds as you fight, rewarding aggressive play. The tradeoff is lighter armor and no shield.
- **Scorcher** (not recommended for first run) -- Fire spells, massive AoE, extremely fragile. A single mistake ends your run. Save this class for after you know enemy attack patterns.
- **Spellblade** (intermediate) -- Magic and melee hybrid. Versatile but demands you juggle two resource systems. Better as a second or third class.

**Verdict:** Pick Knight for your first run. You will see more of the dungeon, survive more mistakes, and learn the game's systems at your own pace.

## Understanding the Charge Attack System

The charge system is Bonehold's core combat mechanic. Every weapon has a hold-to-charge attack that changes properties the longer you hold the button.

**Charge levels:**

- **Quick tap (level 0):** A fast, low-damage swing. Use this when enemies are about to attack and you need to interrupt them.
- **Partial charge (level 1-2):** Moderate damage with a mild effect (slight knockback, small AoE). Good for clearing groups of weak enemies.
- **Full charge (level 3):** Maximum damage, special effects (elemental explosion, wide sweep, armor break). The go-to for elites and bosses.
- **Overcharge (level 4+):** Risk-reward. Holding beyond full charge increases damage further but costs stamina. Time it wrong and you take a hit mid-windup.

**Master rule:** Do not always full charge. Quick attacks into a dodge roll are often safer than standing still charging while a skeleton winds up its own attack.

## Basic Combat Flow

Every encounter in Bonehold follows a rhythm:

1. **Assess the room** -- Enter carefully. Enemies can ambush from side passages.
2. **Identify threats** -- Archers and casters die first. Melee enemies can be kited.
3. **Create openings** -- Block or parry an enemy attack, then punish during their recovery.
4. **Charge during safe windows** -- Only commit to a full charge when no enemy is actively targeting you.
5. **Dodge, don't tank** -- Even the Knight cannot absorb endless hits. Rolling costs little stamina and avoids all damage.

**Parrying:** Time your block just before an enemy's attack lands to stagger them. This opens a guaranteed full-charge window. Practice on the first floor's basic skeletons.

## How Procedural Dungeons Work

Each Bonehold run generates a dungeon from a pool of hand-crafted room templates. You will never see the same layout twice.

**Key dungeon rules:**

- Each floor has 3-5 rooms before a boss door
- Room contents (enemy types, traps, loot, shrines) are randomized
- Side rooms may contain optional treasure or elite enemies
- The deeper you go, the harder the enemies and better the loot
- Boss rooms are set-piece encounters with unique mechanics

**Floor structure:**
Floor 1 (easy) -- Skeleton guards, basic traps, common items
Floor 2 (moderate) -- Armored skeletons, magical traps, uncommon items
Floor 3+ (hard) -- Elite variants, environmental hazards, rare and legendary items

## What Happens When You Die

Death in Bonehold is permanent per run. You lose your current gear, scrolls, and progress. **However**, meta-progression persists permanently.

**What carries over after death:**

- Unlocked classes (unlock once, keep forever)
- Permanent upgrades purchased between runs (health, damage, loot quality)
- Achievement progress
- Game knowledge -- every death teaches you something

**What resets each run:**

- All equipped items and weapons
- Spell scrolls in your inventory
- Gold found during the run
- Floor progress

This roguelike loop means your character gets stronger over time even when individual runs fail. Do not get discouraged by early deaths -- they are part of the design.

## First Boss Tips

The floor 1 boss is a skeleton commander with a greatsword. It has three attacks:

1. **Overhead slam** -- Slow, telegraphed. Dodge sideways, then full charge its back.
2. **Horizontal sweep** -- Wide arc. Block or parry if you are Knight, dodge through if you are Barbarian.
3. **Summon skeletons** -- At 50% health, it calls 2-3 basic skeletons. Kill them quickly to avoid being swarmed.

**General boss strategy:** Stay mid-range, bait the overhead slam, dodge, attack during recovery. Do not chase the boss around the room -- let it come to you.

## How to Evaluate Item Prefixes

Item prefixes modify weapons and gear with additional effects. They are the key to scaling power in Bonehold.

**Early-game prefixes to prioritize:**

- **Vampiric** -- Heal on hit. Sustains through long dungeon floors.
- **Sturdy** -- Increased defense. Makes surviving mistakes easier.
- **Quick** -- Faster charge speed. Lets you get attacks off in tighter windows.
- **Flaming** -- Fire damage over time. Strong against unarmored enemies.

**Late-game prefixes:**

- **Chaos** -- Random secondary effects. High variance, high ceiling.
- **Cursed** -- Powerful effect with a downside. Build around the drawback.
- **Legendary** -- Exclusive to legendary items. These define entire builds.

## Your First 5 Runs Strategy

Do not expect to clear the game in your first attempt. Here is a realistic progression:

1. **Run 1:** Die on floor 1. Learn the basic enemy types and charge timing. Unlock nothing.
2. **Run 2:** Reach the floor 1 boss. Learn its attack patterns. Spend meta-progression gold on +health.
3. **Run 3:** Defeat floor 1 boss. Die on floor 2. Purchase +damage upgrade.
4. **Run 4:** Explore floor 2 fully. Learn elite enemy patterns. Unlock your second class.
5. **Run 5:** Reach floor 3. Face new enemy types. Start experimenting with prefixes.

By run 5, you should have a solid grasp of the combat loop, your preferred class, and enough meta-progression to make consistent progress. From here, the real dungeon crawling begins.
