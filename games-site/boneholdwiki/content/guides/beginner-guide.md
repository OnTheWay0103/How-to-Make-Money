# Bonehold Beginner Guide - Essential Tips for Getting Started

Bonehold throws you into the dark halls of an ancient necromantic fortress with little more than a weapon and a prayer. As a 3D roguelike dungeon crawler from Pixel Jackal, every run is a fresh test of reflexes, strategy, and luck. Whether you are dying to the first skeleton or wondering what all these items do, this Bonehold beginner guide covers everything you need to survive your first hours.

**An honesty note first:** Bonehold launched on July 28, 2026, and some of its systems are not yet fully documented by official sources. Where a detail in this guide is unconfirmed, we say so plainly instead of inventing it.

## Choosing Your First Class

Bonehold has **7 classes**, but only **4 are officially named**: **Knight, Barbarian, Scorcher, and Spellblade**. The other three are unconfirmed as of this guide's writing — including whether they need to be unlocked.

**Best starter classes:**

- **Knight** (recommended) -- Heavy armor, sword-and-shield, forgiving health pool. The Knight can block most early-game attacks and deals consistent damage without demanding precise charge timing. Ideal for learning enemy patterns and dungeon layouts.
- **Barbarian** (good alternative) -- Two-handed weapons, high burst damage. The Barbarian kills things fast, which means fewer chances to take damage. The tradeoff is lighter armor and no shield.
- **Spellblade** (intermediate) -- Magic and melee hybrid. Versatile, but demands you manage magic and melee together. Better as a second or third class.
- **Scorcher** (not recommended for first run) -- Fire spells, massive AoE, extremely fragile. A single mistake ends your run. Save this class for after you know enemy attack patterns.

**Verdict:** Pick Knight for your first run. You will see more of the dungeon, survive more mistakes, and learn the game's systems at your own pace.

## Understanding the Charge Attack System

The charge system is Bonehold's core combat mechanic, and it is **confirmed** as the heart of combat: most weapons work by holding the attack button to charge, then releasing for a stronger hit. Launch reviews are explicit that a quick stab "won't cut it late-game" — charging matters.

**Charge basics:**

- **Quick tap:** A fast, low-damage swing. Use this when enemies are about to attack and you need to interrupt them, or when you have no time to commit.
- **Partial charge:** A short hold for moderate damage with mild effects. Good for clearing groups of weak enemies without overcommitting.
- **Full charge:** Hold until the weapon is fully charged for maximum damage and the weapon's special effect. The go-to for elites and bosses — when you have a safe window.

**Master rule:** Do not always full charge. Quick attacks into a dodge roll are often safer than standing still charging while an enemy winds up its own attack. Reviews describe the pacing as deliberately slow — the game wants you to *earn* your big hits with positioning.

*Specific charge level counts, exact timings, and stamina costs are unconfirmed — the tap/partial/full framework above is the confirmed core.*

## Basic Combat Flow

Every encounter in Bonehold follows a rhythm:

1. **Assess the room** -- Enter carefully. Enemies can ambush from side passages.
2. **Identify threats** -- Ranged and spellcasting enemies die first. Melee enemies can be kited.
3. **Create openings** -- Block or parry an enemy attack, then punish during their recovery.
4. **Charge during safe windows** -- Only commit to a full charge when no enemy is actively targeting you.
5. **Dodge, don't tank** -- Even the Knight cannot absorb endless hits. Rolling costs little and avoids all damage.

**Parrying:** Time your block just before an enemy's attack lands to stagger them. This opens a guaranteed full-charge window. Launch reviews confirm parries are one of the most rewarding mechanics in the game. Practice on the first floor's basic skeletons.

## How Procedural Dungeons Work

Each Bonehold run generates a dungeon from a pool of hand-crafted room templates. You will never see the same layout twice — this is confirmed by the developer's description of procedural generation.

**Key dungeon rules (confirmed and expected):**

- **Confirmed:** Room contents (enemy types, traps, loot) are randomized per run; the game's description promises "procedurally generated treasures."
- **Confirmed:** The deeper you go, the harder the enemies and the better the loot — standard for the genre and consistent with reviews of the difficulty curve.
- **Expected (unconfirmed):** side rooms with optional treasure, elite enemies, and shrine-like safe rooms are common roguelike features and consistent with the fortress setting — but exact room types are not officially documented.

*Specific floor counts and per-floor enemy tables are unconfirmed. Do not trust guides that claim "floor 3 has X" — no official source documents the floor structure.*

## What Happens When You Die

Death in Bonehold is **permanent per run**, and this is one of the most important confirmed facts: launch reviews note the game has **no checkpoint or save system**. When you die, the run is over — your current gear, items, and progress are gone.

**What is confirmed:**

- All items and progress gained during the run are lost on death.
- You start a fresh run with a fresh character and fresh loot.
- Steam achievements (98 exist) track progress globally across runs — the platform handles that, not the game.

**What is unconfirmed:**

- Whether any "meta-progression" exists (permanent upgrades purchased between runs, unlockable classes, persistent currencies). **No official source confirms any meta-progression system.** Some guides claim the game has a between-run upgrade hub with currencies like "Bone Fragments" — those claims are unverified speculation.

The roguelike loop means you improve through knowledge even when individual runs fail: every death teaches you enemy patterns, room types, and item interactions that carry over in your head. Do not get discouraged by early deaths — they are part of the design.

## First Boss Tips

The floor 1 boss is a skill check, and it teaches the fundamentals of boss combat. **We cannot confirm the boss's name or exact moves** (no official boss list exists yet), but the confirmed strategy framework applies:

- **Bosses punish greed.** Do not chase the extra hit — the charge system punishes overcommitment hard.
- **Learn the tells.** Spend the first moments of the fight dodging and watching the windup animations. Identify the big telegraphed attack, the combo, and the "adds spawn" moment before committing to damage.
- **Parry or dodge the big attack, then punish.** The safest damage windows are after the boss misses or finishes a combo.
- **Expect summons.** Most roguelike bosses call adds at a health threshold. Kill them quickly to avoid being swarmed — but only when it is safe.
- **Stay mid-range.** Let the boss come to you. Chasing the boss around the arena is how players walk into attacks.

## How to Evaluate Item Prefixes

Item prefixes modify weapons and gear with additional effects. This is **confirmed** as the key to scaling power in Bonehold — the developer describes prefixes that "twist playstyles in unexpected ways," and reviews call the prefix system the heart of buildcrafting. A common weapon with the right prefix can outperform a fancier one.

**What we can and cannot say:**

- **Confirmed:** Prefixes exist on weapons, spells, and armor, and change how items behave.
- **Confirmed:** Legendary items are rare, build-defining power spikes.
- **Unconfirmed:** The actual prefix names (any specific names like "Vampiric," "Flaming," or "Sturdy" you see in guides are unverified), rarity colors, and drop rates.

**Early-game evaluation rules that hold regardless of names:**

- Prefer prefixes that improve how you already fight over ones that demand a new playstyle.
- Sustainability effects (healing, recovery) matter more in long runs than raw damage.
- If a prefix combos with another item you own, take it.
- When a legendary drops, consider rebuilding the run around it.

## Your First 5 Runs Strategy

Do not expect to clear the game in your first attempt. Here is a realistic progression:

1. **Run 1:** Die on the first floor. Learn the basic enemy types and charge timing.
2. **Run 2:** Reach the first boss. Learn its attack patterns. Die with knowledge.
3. **Run 3:** Defeat the first boss. Discover what the second floor has in store.
4. **Run 4:** Explore deeper, learn elite enemy patterns, try a second class.
5. **Run 5:** Push past your previous best. Start experimenting with prefixes and loadouts.

By run 5, you should have a solid grasp of the combat loop, your preferred class, and enough enemy knowledge to make consistent progress. From here, the real dungeon crawling begins.
