# Low-Budget Repairs Demolition Guide — Tear Out Walls & Old Fixtures Fast

Every renovation in Low-Budget Repairs starts the same way: with a flat full of things that need to come out. Old tiles, ruined wallpaper, busted fixtures, and furniture that blocks the work — the demo's demolition loop is short, but it hides more profit than any other phase of a job. Demolition is also where the game's most famous stunts live: the furniture that goes out the window, and the dumpster that gives it back.

> Based on the July 2026 demo and pre-launch coverage of the open playtest (started July 30, 2026). The full game launches August 13, 2026 — anything unverified is marked **[Needs launch verification]**.

## What Demolition Exists in the Demo

The demo's demolition is practical, not structural. You're tearing out **finishes**, not buildings:

- **Tile removal** — the hammer's classic job. Stuck tiles come off with persuasion; the [tool guide](/guides/tool-guide) lists the hammer for exactly this.
- **Wallpaper stripping** — the chisel and scraper's job. The chisel stands in for the scraper for free (demo-confirmed tool substitution).
- **Old fixture removal** — toilets, sinks, cabinets, and light fixtures that the contract replaces come out before the new ones go in.
- **Furniture removal** — the big, satisfying part. See the window trick below.
- **Damage clearing** — kicked-in drywall, broken plaster, and water-ruined sections get opened up before repair.

The one thing the demo does *not* confirm is **interior wall demolition** — knocking actual load-bearing or partition walls down to change a flat's layout. **[Needs launch verification]** Nothing in the demo or official coverage suggests walls come down between rooms, so plan around finishing walls, not removing them.

## The Window Trick: Furniture Out, Fast

Pre-launch coverage of the game is explicit: **furniture that doesn't fit through the door goes out the window** — and the throw carries real risk, because what goes down can damage what's below. This is the demo's most famous mechanic and the fastest furniture removal in the game:

- **It's faster than carrying.** A sofa down the stairs is minutes of hauling; a sofa out the window is one throw. Time is money — the [money-making guide](/guides/money-making-guide) counts every hour.
- **There's a risk below.** "Risking damage to things below" is confirmed. A busted car roof or a damaged courtyard becomes your problem, and visible damage outside the flat is damage the neighbors notice.
- **Check what's below before you throw.** Courtyard clear and nothing parked = free removal. Something fragile below = carry the furniture down the stairs like a normal person.
- **The window trick pairs with the inspection risk table** — the [inspection guide](/guides/inspection-guide) notes nobody inspects the courtyard. Yet. **[Speculative — awaiting launch confirmation]** — a launch build may change exactly whose window it is.

## The Dumpster: Where Furniture Goes to Be Reborn

The game's confirmed furniture economy has a second side: **dumpster finds.** Pre-launch coverage describes replacing old furniture with "flea-infested dumpster finds" — the game lets you pull usable furniture out of the dumpster and put it in the flat.

- **Check the dumpster before you buy new.** If a contract needs furniture and the dumpster has a piece that covers the requirement, that's a free item — the most profitable source in the game.
- **Flea-infested is fine, visibly broken is not.** The inspector judges by appearance from the doorway; a cleaned-up dumpster find passes. A shattered one doesn't.
- **This is the demolition mirror:** what you throw out one job becomes the next job's bargain. The [tiling guide](/guides/tiling-guide) confirms the same loop for tiles — tiles from a demolished room get reused in visible spots.

## Removing Old Fixtures the Cheap Way

Fixtures follow the same rule as everything else: **remove only what the contract replaces, and never buy new tools for it.**

- **Toilets, sinks, and cabinets** come out with the valve off (plumbing) and a cheap hammer (demolition). Turn off the water before cutting into any supply line — the [plumbing guide](/guides/plumbing-guide) valve rule applies to removal, not just repair.
- **Light fixtures** come out with the power off at the breaker panel — see the [electrical guide](/guides/electrical-work-guide) for the breaker rule.
- **Salvage what you remove.** Old tiles, handles, and fixtures that aren't broken are future patch materials. A job that reuses last job's salvage spends almost nothing.
- **Don't demolish what's being kept.** Read the contract before the hammer comes out. The demo's demo-of-demolition failures are almost all "took out what I was supposed to paint."

## Debris Handling: The Dumpster vs. The Hallway

Once things are torn out, the debris has to go somewhere, and the demo gives you two options with very different profiles:

- **The dumpster** — proper, but costs time per trip. Hauling bags down the stairs eats minutes from your day.
- **The hallway** — the [cost-cutting guide's](/guides/cost-cutting-tips) confirmed trick: stack bags in the common area and save the trips. Nobody bills you for it, and it's the lowest-risk shortcut in the game. **[Demo-verified]**

The balance: hallway for big jobs where time matters, dumpster when you're on a job where the client watches closely or the hallway is shared with the client's floor. A neat stack reads as temporary; a spill reads as unprofessional — and the inspection guide's cleanliness category applies to the flat, not the hallway. Yet.

## Demolition Tools: Keep It Cheap

From the [tool guide](/guides/tool-guide):

- **Cheap hammer** — the demolition workhorse. Head loosens, but it's the cheapest tool in the game for a reason.
- **Cheap chisel** — wallpaper, tile persuasion, and standing in for the scraper.
- **Utility knife** — cutting wallpaper sheets, tape, and boxes.
- **Skip quality for demolition.** Blunt-force work wears out cheap tools fast, but failure is cheap: a loosened hammer head costs seconds, not a job. Quality money goes to the drill and screwdrivers.

## Demolition Mistakes That Cost Profit

1. **Demolishing what the contract keeps** — the most expensive mistake in the game. Read the contract first.
2. **Throwing without checking below** — one shattered windshield erases the job's profit.
3. **Buying new furniture before checking the dumpster** — the free option is one flight of stairs away.
4. **Skipping the valve/breaker before cutting** — a cut pipe floods the flat; a cut live wire is the electrical guide's worst case.
5. **Over-demolishing** — stripping everything to studs when the contract only names the tiles. More work, more debris, same flat fee.

## Demolition Cheat Sheet

- Read the contract → valve off / breaker off before cutting → remove only what's named → salvage anything reusable → check the dumpster before buying → throw furniture out the window only when the courtyard is clear → haul or hallway the debris → clean up before painting and tiling.

Demolition is the phase where time is money and money is salvage. Do it fast, keep what's useful, and the rest of the job starts with a head start.
