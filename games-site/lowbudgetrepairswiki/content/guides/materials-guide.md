# Low-Budget Repairs Materials Guide — What to Buy and Where to Save

Tools you buy once; materials you buy for every job — and they're where beginners lose the most money. This guide covers every material category in Low-Budget Repairs: what it does, what the cheapest acceptable version is, and where a few zlotys more are actually worth it. Based on the July 2026 demo; launch is **August 13, 2026**. For how the store itself works — bargain bin, stock rotation, brands — see the [hardware store guide](/guides/hardware-store-guide).

## Paint: Your Biggest Expense, Your Biggest Saving

Paint is the largest recurring material cost, and the game's signature savings live here:

- **Dilute it.** One can of paint stretched with thinner or water at roughly **1:5** covers a whole room and passes inspection. At 1:10 it starts looking patchy. Stir thoroughly — unmixed thinner leaves streaks inspectors notice.
- **The "show wall" rule.** Buy one decent can for the wall opposite the entrance (the most-looked-at surface) and diluted cheap paint for everything else. One right can beats a full redo.
- **Paint over, don't prepare.** The demo doesn't require sanding or primer. Scrape loose paint, then paint.
- **Knock-off brands are fine.** Off-brand paint at 1:5 dilution looks the same on the wall as name-brand at 1:5. The brand premium is for the show wall only.
- **Skip invisible surfaces.** Behind radiators, behind furniture, behind doors — the client can't see it, so it doesn't exist. The [painting guide](/guides/painting-guide) has the full technique.

## Thinner: The Force Multiplier

Thinner (or water, per demo players) is the cheapest material in the game and turns one can of paint into two. Buy it in bulk — multi-packs are cheaper per unit, and the store's stock rotates. Keep a bottle in your kit at all times; you'll use it on nearly every job.

## Adhesives and Fillers: The Corner-Cutter's Aisle

This aisle is where most "repairs" actually happen:

- **Caulk** — the cheapest crack and gap fix. Cracks in plaster, gaps around tiles, skirting seams. Passes the damage check when it's the same color as the wall.
- **Silicone** — for wet areas: bathroom corners, around the tub, sink edges. Dries flexible; beats caulk wherever water lives.
- **Spackle / filler** — only when a hole is too big for caulk and a poster can't cover it. Cheapest tub, one pass, paint over it.
- **Glue** — furniture repairs and anything the job says "fix, don't replace." A glued drawer handle is a fixed drawer handle.
- **Wallpaper paste** — only if the contract demands wallpaper. Otherwise, painting over wallpaper is always cheaper (see below).

The rule: **fillers buy you time, not quality.** A caulk line over a crack passes the inspection; it does not stop the crack from growing. That's fine — you're paid before it matters.

## Tiles and Grout: Mismatched Is a Lifestyle

- **Tiles** — the bargain bin is the only place to buy them. Chipped edges and mismatched patterns are acceptable; the inspection checks coverage, not aesthetics ([tiling guide](/guides/tiling-guide)). Buy only what you need to replace — not the whole wall.
- **Adhesive** — cheapest brand. The demo doesn't check the brand of glue holding a tile.
- **Grout** — cheap bag, mixed roughly. Grout hides mismatched edges and missing spacers, so it's the material that makes sloppy tiling invisible. Never skip it on visible rows.
- **Level and spacers** — you don't need them. "A little unevenness gives the room some character."

## Wallpaper: Scrape or Paint Over?

- **Paint over it** whenever the job allows — it costs one diluted can and an hour.
- **Scrape it** only when the contract explicitly requires new wallpaper, or when the old pattern is so strong it shows through paint.
- A chisel replaces a wallpaper scraper for free — no dedicated tool purchase needed.

## Plumbing Parts: The Duct Tape Era

- **Duct tape** — the game's most profitable material. A taped pipe passes the client's tap test as long as it holds. Wrap firmly, several layers, extending past the damage.
- **Sealant** — the second-line fix for joints and threads; costs less than a new fitting.
- **Pipes and fittings** — only when the pipe is truly broken through, and then only the damaged section with the cheapest connecting fitting. Never replace a whole line.
- The one rule: **function is binary.** If it holds water, it passes — whatever it looks like. The [renovation jobs guide](/guides/renovation-jobs-guide) covers which jobs need real parts.

## Furniture: Refurbish, Build, or Throw

The most expensive material category, and the most improvised:

- **Refurbish** — if the contract says "refurbish" instead of "replace," sand-and-paint (or glue-and-paint) beats buying anything. Always check the contract wording first.
- **Flat-pack** — the buy-when-required option. Cheapest line, and assembly is labor you're paid for.
- **The window** — furniture that doesn't fit through the door goes out the window. The game never bills you for the courtyard mess; the neighbors' comments are flavor text.

## Materials Substitution Table

When you're short one thing, the demo lets most materials substitute roughly:

| You need | Substitute | Works when |
|---|---|---|
| Wallpaper scraper | Chisel | Always |
| Grout | Caulk (painted over) | Small gaps, dry areas |
| Spackle | Caulk | Holes under poster size |
| Pipe section | Duct tape + sealant | Cracked joint, low pressure |
| Primer | Nothing — paint directly | Always (demo) |
| New furniture | Refurbished existing | Contract says "refurbish" |
| Matching tile | Any tile | Always (mismatch is fine) |
| Paint for the show wall | Second coat of diluted | When the color is close |

The result is uglier than the "proper" material, but inspectors rarely look that closely — and the savings are pure profit.

## Where to Save vs. Where to Spend

| Spend cheap | Spend a little more |
|---|---|
| Paint (diluted, most walls) | One show-wall can |
| Tiles, grout, adhesive | — |
| Caulk, silicone, spackle | — |
| Tape, sealant, fittings | — (only real pipe sections when forced) |
| Wallpaper (skip entirely) | — |
| Furniture (refurbish first) | — |
| Thinner (buy in bulk, always) | — |

In practice, almost every material is a "spend cheap" item — the only exception is the show-wall paint, and even that is optional when the bin has a usable can.
