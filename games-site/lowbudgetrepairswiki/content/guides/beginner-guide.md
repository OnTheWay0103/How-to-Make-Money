# Low-Budget Repairs Beginner Guide — Getting Started Tips

Welcome to **Low-Budget Repairs**, the satirical first-person renovation simulator from Gray2RGB and PlayWay S.A. You play a handyman in **1990s Poland**, running a one-man renovation "business" where the goal is never to do a good job — it's to do a job that *looks* good enough to get paid. The game launches **August 13, 2026** on Steam, so this guide is based on the demo and developer previews; we'll update it as details confirm.

## What Is Low-Budget Repairs?

Low-Budget Repairs is a first-person renovation sim with a comedic twist. Set in a run-down 1990s Polish apartment block — panel-building hallways, cramped flats, everything painted the same shade of beige — you take renovation jobs from neighbors and local characters.

The comedy comes from the game's attitude: you're not a craftsman, you're a *survivor*. Every zloty counts. Paint gets watered down, holes get covered with posters, mismatched tiles are a lifestyle choice. If you've ever played a house-flipping game and thought "I could do this cheaper," this game lets you act on that thought — consequences and all. The game already has over **1 million wishlists** on Steam.

## The Core Gameplay Loop

The whole game runs on one simple loop:

```
Accept job → Buy cheap supplies → Do the work (cut corners) → Get paid → Repeat
```

1. **Accept a job** — Clients post renovation jobs: repaint a room, retile a bathroom, fix a leaky pipe, or renovate a whole flat.
2. **Buy supplies** — Head to the hardware store. The bargain bin is your best friend.
3. **Do the work** — Paint, tile, assemble furniture, fix plumbing. How well you do it is up to you.
4. **Get paid** — The client inspects the result (roughly) and pays the agreed flat fee.

The key mechanic: **you get a flat fee, and everything you spend comes out of your profit.** There's no quality bonus, so the whole game becomes a puzzle of "what can I get away with?" We cover that in depth in our [money-making guide](/guides/money-making-guide).

## Your First Jobs: Walkthrough

The early jobs teach the basics with a sensible difficulty curve.

### Job 1: The Repaint

Your first job is typically a simple room repaint:

- Strip or cover old wallpaper where needed
- Prep the walls (or skip it — the demo doesn't require sanding)
- Paint with a roller — the game simulates real painting, so you cover the wall in strokes
- **Only paint what you can see** — behind the radiator, behind furniture, behind the door. The client won't check
- Clean up and collect your fee

### Job 2: The Bathroom Retile

This job introduces tiling and adhesives:

- Remove old tiles (a chisel or hammer works)
- Apply adhesive and place new tiles
- **Mismatched tiles are acceptable** — size is what matters, and you don't strictly need spacers or a level
- Caulk the corners — cheap caulk over gaps beats a proper fix every time

### Job 3: The Leaky Pipe

The first plumbing job introduces the game's duct tape era:

- Turn off the water (the demo's most common "why is the flat flooded" mistake)
- Find the leak and either replace the section or, if the pipe is hard to reach, wrap it in duct tape
- **Duct tape works** — a taped pipe passes inspection, at least in the demo

## Basic Controls and Mechanics

The demo controls are standard first-person: **mouse** to look and aim, **WASD** to move, **left mouse** to use your current tool, **right mouse** for alternate actions, **E** to interact, **Tab** for job info, **I** for inventory and tool belt, **Q** for the tool wheel, **R** to drop. See the [controls guide](/guides/controls-guide) for the full keybindings.

Key mechanics to know early:

- **Tool belt**: You carry a limited set of tools — swap quickly, don't lug everything at once.
- **Carrying**: You can pick up and carry objects. Yes, including furniture — and yes, including throwing it out the window to save time. The game never punishes you directly, though the neighbors may comment.
- **Inspection**: Clients "inspect" your work. Based on the demo, the inspection is surface-level — visible problems get noticed, hidden ones don't.

## How to Navigate the Hardware Store

The hardware store is your only supply source (based on the demo), organized like a real 1990s Polish hardware store — a bit chaotic, but learnable. Sections: **paint** (cans, rollers, brushes, thinner — your biggest expense), **tiles and building materials**, **tools** (cheap and quality versions), **plumbing**, **adhesives and fillers** (glue, caulk, silicone, spackle), and **furniture** (flat-pack pieces for clients who want "new" items on a budget).

Plus the **bargain bin**: discounted, often damaged or off-brand stock. **Check it first every trip** — demo players report it frequently holds usable paint, tiles, and tools at a fraction of the price.

There's no shopping-list UI, so note what the job needs before you leave — running back costs time, and time is money. See our [hardware store guide](/guides/hardware-store-guide) for the full breakdown.

## Tips for Your First Renovation

- **Dilute the paint.** Adding thinner (or water, per demo players) stretches a can further. Demo players report a roughly 1:5 ratio looks fine on the wall; heavier dilution starts to look patchy.
- **Buy the bargain bin first.** Even damaged paint that needs stirring paints the same.
- **Don't sand, don't prime.** Skipped prep is invisible profit.
- **Skip non-visible work.** Behind radiators, behind furniture — if the client can't see it, it doesn't exist.
- **Cover, don't repair.** Holes get posters. Cracks get caulk. Mold gets paint.
- **Watch the clock.** Clients get impatient, and your flat fee doesn't change — slower work is less money per hour.

## How Profit Works

This is the most important concept in the game:

```
Profit = Job fee − (supplies + repairs + waste)
```

You accept a **flat fee** per job. Every zloty spent on paint, tiles, tools, or replacement materials comes straight out of your pocket; money you don't spend is profit.

This flips renovation logic on its head: a quality fix costs more (less profit), a "good enough" fix costs less (more profit), and an invisible fix costs nothing (maximum profit). The tension is risk: **if the client notices a bad job, they may refuse to pay or demand a redo** — eating your profit anyway.

The safest beginner strategy: cheap materials, clean visible work, and no risky shortcuts until you understand what inspectors notice. Then branch into the aggressive strategies in our [cost-cutting tips guide](/guides/cost-cutting-tips).

## Final Words

Low-Budget Repairs is a game about laughing at renovation norms — a satire of both the fixing-up genre and the real-life shortcuts of 1990s Poland. Play it straight or play it as a scummy contractor who paints over mold; the game supports both.

The full game launches **August 13, 2026**. The demo is free on Steam if you want to practice your corner-cutting before then.
