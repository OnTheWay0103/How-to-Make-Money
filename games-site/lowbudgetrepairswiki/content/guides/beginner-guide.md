# Low-Budget Repairs Beginner Guide — Getting Started Tips

Welcome to **Low-Budget Repairs**, the satirical first-person renovation simulator from Gray2RGB and PlayWay S.A. You play a handyman in **1990s Poland**, running a one-man renovation "business" where the goal is never to do a good job — it's to do a job that *looks* good enough to get paid. The game launches **August 13, 2026** on Steam, so this guide is based on the demo and developer previews; we'll update it as the full release confirms details.

## What Is Low-Budget Repairs?

Low-Budget Repairs is a first-person renovation sim with a comedic twist. Set in a run-down Polish apartment block in the 1990s — panel-building hallways, cramped flats, everything painted the same shade of beige — you take on renovation jobs from neighbors and local characters.

The comedy comes from the game's attitude: you're not a craftsman, you're a *survivor*. Every zloty counts. Paint gets watered down, holes get covered with posters, and mismatched tiles are a lifestyle choice. If you've ever played a house-flipping game and thought "I could do this cheaper," this game lets you act on that thought — consequences and all.

The game already has over **1 million wishlists** on Steam, and the demo has been widely played. The setting — 1990s Polish panel apartments, with their clunky radiators and distinctive balconies — is a big part of the charm.

## The Core Gameplay Loop

The whole game runs on one simple loop:

```
Accept job → Buy cheap supplies → Do the work (cut corners) → Get paid → Repeat
```

1. **Accept a job** — Clients post renovation jobs: repaint a room, retile a bathroom, fix a leaky pipe, or renovate a whole flat before a family moves in.
2. **Buy supplies** — Head to the hardware store. The bargain bin is your best friend.
3. **Do the work** — Paint, tile, assemble furniture, fix plumbing. How well you do it is up to you.
4. **Get paid** — The client inspects the result (roughly) and pays the agreed flat fee.

The key mechanic: **you get a flat fee, and everything you spend comes out of your profit.** There's no quality bonus, so the whole game becomes a puzzle of "what can I get away with?" We cover that in depth in our [money-making guide](/guides/money-making-guide).

## Your First Jobs: Walkthrough

The early jobs teach the basics with a sensible difficulty curve.

### Job 1: The Repaint

Your first job is typically a simple room repaint:

- Strip or cover old wallpaper where needed
- Prep the walls (or skip it — the demo doesn't require sanding)
- Paint with a roller — the game simulates real painting, so you cover the wall in strokes
- **Only paint what you can see** — behind the radiator, behind furniture, behind the door. The client won't check
- Clean up and collect your fee

### Job 2: The Bathroom Retile

This job introduces tiling and adhesives:

- Remove old tiles (a chisel or hammer works)
- Apply adhesive and place new tiles
- **Mismatched tiles are acceptable** — same size is what matters, and you don't strictly need spacers or a level for the client to accept the work
- Caulk the corners — cheap caulk over gaps beats a proper fix every time

### Job 3: The Leaky Pipe

The first plumbing job introduces the game's duct tape era:

- Turn off the water (the demo's most common "why is the flat flooded" mistake)
- Find the leak and either replace the section or — if the pipe is hard to reach — wrap it in duct tape
- **Duct tape works** — a taped pipe passes inspection, at least in the demo

## Basic Controls and Mechanics

The demo controls are standard first-person:

- **Mouse**: look around and aim your tool
- **WASD**: move
- **Left mouse**: use your current tool
- **Right mouse**: alternate tool action
- **E / F**: interact — pick up items, open doors, talk to clients
- **Tab or I**: open inventory and tool belt
- **R**: drop the item in your hands

Key mechanics to know early:

- **Tool belt**: You carry a limited set of tools — swap quickly and don't lug everything at once.
- **Carrying**: You can pick up and carry objects. Yes, this includes furniture — and yes, it includes throwing furniture out the window to save time. The game never punishes you directly, though the neighbors may comment.
- **Inspection**: Clients "inspect" your work. Based on the demo, the inspection is surface-level — visible problems get noticed, hidden ones don't.

## How to Navigate the Hardware Store

The hardware store is your only supply source (based on the demo), organized like a real 1990s Polish hardware store — a bit chaotic, but learnable:

- **Paint section**: cans, rollers, brushes, thinner — your biggest recurring expense
- **Tiles and building materials**: tiles in a few patterns and sizes, adhesive, grout, cement
- **Tools**: everything from hammers to drills, in cheap and quality versions
- **Plumbing**: pipes, connectors, tape, and sealants
- **Adhesives and fillers**: glue, caulk, silicone, spackle
- **Furniture**: flat-pack furniture for clients who want "new" pieces on a budget
- **The bargain bin**: discounted, often damaged or off-brand items. **Check this first every trip** — demo players report it frequently holds usable paint, tiles, and tools at a fraction of the price

There's no shopping list UI, so note what the job needs before you leave — running back to the store costs time, and time is money. See our [hardware store guide](/guides/hardware-store-guide) for the full breakdown.

## Tips for Your First Renovation

- **Dilute the paint.** Adding thinner (or water, per demo players) stretches a can further. A 1:5 ratio looks fine on the wall; 1:10 starts to look patchy.
- **Buy the bargain bin first.** Even damaged paint that needs stirring is cheaper than full price — and it paints the same.
- **Don't sand, don't prime.** Skipped prep is invisible profit.
- **Skip non-visible work.** Behind radiators, behind furniture, under the couch — if the client can't see it, it doesn't exist.
- **Cover, don't repair.** Holes get posters. Cracks get caulk. Mold gets paint.
- **Watch the clock.** Jobs don't have hard timers in the demo, but clients get impatient, and your flat fee doesn't change — slower work is less money per hour.

## How Profit Works

This is the most important concept in the game:

```
Profit = Job fee − (supplies + repairs + waste)
```

You accept a **flat fee** per job. Every zloty spent on paint, tiles, tools, or replacement materials comes straight out of your pocket. Money you don't spend is profit.

This flips renovation logic on its head: a quality fix costs more (less profit), a "good enough" fix costs less (more profit), and an invisible fix costs nothing (maximum profit). The tension is risk: **if the client notices a bad job, they may refuse to pay or demand a redo** — eating your profit anyway.

The safest beginner strategy: cheap materials, clean visible work, and no risky shortcuts until you understand what inspectors notice. Once you have a feel for it, branch into the aggressive strategies in our [cost-cutting tips guide](/guides/cost-cutting-tips).

## Final Words

Low-Budget Repairs is a game about laughing at renovation norms — a satire of both the fixing-up genre and the real-life shortcuts people took in 1990s Poland. Play it straight and craft a beautiful flat, or play it as a scummy contractor who paints over mold — the game supports both.

The full game launches **August 13, 2026**. The demo is free on Steam if you want to practice your corner-cutting before then.
