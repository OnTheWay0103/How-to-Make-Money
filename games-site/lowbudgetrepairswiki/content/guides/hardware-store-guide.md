# Hardware Store Guide — Where to Buy Supplies in Low-Budget Repairs

The hardware store is your only supply source in Low-Budget Repairs, and how you shop there decides whether each job makes money. This guide covers how the store works, what's on the shelves, the legendary bargain bin, and which items deserve full price.

The game launches **August 13, 2026** on Steam; this guide is based on the demo and player reports.

## How the Hardware Store Works

The store is a 1990s-style Polish hardware store: shelves and bins, a counter at the back, and no fancy shopping list UI. You walk the aisles, pick up items, and pay at the register. Key facts from the demo:

- **Stock rotates** — the shelves and the bargain bin change over time. Items you see today may be gone tomorrow.
- **Pickup is manual** — grab items by hand and carry them to the register. There's no cart UI (based on the demo), so plan your trips.
- **No list syncing** — the game doesn't auto-remind you what the job needs. Check the job description before you leave the flat.
- **Buying in multiples** — multi-packs of consumables (tape, caulk, thinner) cost less per item.

The golden shopping rule: **check the bargain bin first, always.** It's the single highest-leverage habit in the game.

## Types of Supplies Available

The store carries six categories:

1. **Paint** — cans in various colors (and the ubiquitous 1990s Polish beige), rollers, brushes, trays, thinner. Your biggest recurring expense.
2. **Tiles and building materials** — tiles in a few patterns and sizes, tile adhesive, grout, cement, drywall, and filler.
3. **Tools** — everything from hammers to drills, in cheap and quality versions, plus occasional used tools in the bargain bin. See our [tool guide](/guides/tool-guide) for the full rundown.
4. **Plumbing** — pipes, connectors, tape, sealant, and the infamous duct tape.
5. **Adhesives and fillers** — glue, caulk, silicone, spackle, wallpaper paste. The corner-cutter's aisle.
6. **Furniture** — flat-pack furniture for jobs that require new pieces. The most expensive category.

## The Bargain Bin Strategy

The bargain bin is a discount shelf with random, often damaged stock: dented paint cans, chipped tiles, used tools, near-expiry sealants, and off-brand everything. Based on the demo:

- **Prices are steeply discounted** — player reports suggest 40–60% off equivalent new stock.
- **Most damage is cosmetic.** A dented paint can still paints; chipped tiles tile fine (see our [cost-cutting guide](/guides/cost-cutting-tips) — the game literally doesn't care if tiles match).
- **Check it before every job.** The best shopping sequence: get the job → check the bin → buy the rest new. A bin with the right paint can turn a break-even job into a profitable one.
- **Beware mislabels.** Demo players report occasionally mislabeled cans — open and stir paint before you commit.

## Knock-Off Brands vs Name Brands

The store sells both established brands and knock-offs. Based on the demo's parody approach (the game is a satire, after all), the difference is:

- **Knock-offs** — cheaper, and the quality shows: paint that covers thinner, tools that wear faster, sealant that dries brittle. For the game's purposes, knock-off paint at 1:5 dilution and name-brand paint at 1:5 dilution look roughly the same on the wall.
- **Name brands** — cost more, cover better, and their tools last. Worth it only when the visible result must carry the job, or when a tool's failure would be expensive (see [when to invest](/guides/tool-guide)).

The satire is the point: 1990s Poland was full of suspiciously similar brands with slightly-off logos, and the game plays that for laughs. Player reports suggest the brand name is cosmetic for most supplies — the real difference is price.

## The 1990s Polish Supply Scarcity Mechanic

Here's where the game gets historically fun: the store's stock reflects the real supply situation of 1990s Poland, right after the fall of communism. That means:

- **Stock is unreliable.** The exact paint color or tile you need may not be on the shelf. This is a core mechanic, not a bug — it forces you to improvise.
- **Supply comes in waves.** Restocks arrive periodically, and popular items (beige paint, white tiles) sell out first.
- **Shortages force corner-cutting.** Out of the right color? The game's answer: buy a close color, or buy what the bargain bin has and make it work. This is by design — the scarcity *is* the challenge.
- **Bulk hoarding is viable.** Players report buying extras of frequently-needed items (thinner, tape, caulk) when they're in stock, since you can't count on them being there next trip.

Think of it as a mini logistics game layered on the renovation game: the store never has everything, and the difference between a profitable job and a money-loser is often what you managed to grab on restock day.

## Best Items to Always Buy Cheap

These are never worth full price:

- **Paint (color-critical jobs aside)** — diluted and cheap covers fine for most walls. Buy the bargain bin's stock first.
- **Rollers, brushes, trays** — consumables. Cheap ones shed bristles, which costs seconds, not zlotys.
- **Tape, caulk, silicone, thinner** — the corner-cutter's kit, and multi-pack discounts make cheap versions the clear winner.
- **Tiles** — mismatched cheap tiles pass inspection (see the [cost-cutting guide](/guides/cost-cutting-tips)).
- **Tools for one-job use** — a chisel beats a purpose-bought scraper. Never buy a quality tool for a job type you haven't done before.
- **Flat-pack furniture substitutes** — if the job allows refurbishing existing furniture instead of buying new, that's always cheaper (and covered in the [cost-cutting guide](/guides/cost-cutting-tips)).

## Items Worth Spending On

A short list of things that justify full price:

- **A quality drill** — the one tool whose failure mid-job is expensive, and whose speed pays back on every job. See the [tool guide](/guides/tool-guide) for the full reasoning.
- **The exact paint color when the client will see it** — if the wall facing the door must match a specific color, a close-but-wrong shade is visible. One can of the right paint is cheaper than a redo.
- **Paint for the "show wall"** — buying one quality can for the entrance wall and cheap diluted paint for the rest is the optimal split players report.
- **Items in bulk when in stock** — given the scarcity mechanic, paying full price for multi-packs of tape and thinner when they're available beats hunting for them mid-job at any price.

## Final Shopping Rules

1. Check the bargain bin before every purchase.
2. Buy consumables in bulk when in stock — scarcity won't wait.
3. Never buy quality tools except the drill (and a roller if painting is your main income).
4. Let the job's visible surfaces dictate where quality money goes — one "show wall" can of the right paint saves a redo.
5. Embrace the shortages. The game is built for improvisation, and some of the funniest (and most profitable) jobs come from making do with what the store actually had.
