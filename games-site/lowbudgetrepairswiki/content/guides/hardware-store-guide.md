# Hardware Store Guide — Where to Buy Supplies in Low-Budget Repairs

The hardware store is your only supply source in Low-Budget Repairs, and how you shop decides whether each job makes money. This guide covers how the store works, what's on the shelves, the bargain bin, and what deserves full price. Based on the demo; the game launches **August 13, 2026** on Steam.

## How the Hardware Store Works

The store is a 1990s-style Polish hardware store: shelves and bins, a counter at the back, no shopping-list UI. Walk the aisles, pick up items, pay at the register. Key facts from the demo:

- **Stock rotates** — shelves and the bargain bin change over time.
- **Pickup is manual** — carry items to the register; no cart UI, so plan trips.
- **No list syncing** — the game won't remind you what the job needs. Check the job description before leaving the flat.
- **Bulk discounts** — multi-packs of consumables (tape, caulk, thinner) cost less per item.

The golden shopping rule: **check the bargain bin first, always.**

## Types of Supplies Available

1. **Paint** — cans (including the ubiquitous 1990s Polish beige), rollers, brushes, trays, thinner. Your biggest recurring expense.
2. **Tiles and building materials** — a few tile patterns and sizes, adhesive, grout, cement.
3. **Tools** — everything from hammers to drills, in cheap and quality versions, plus used tools in the bargain bin. See our [tool guide](/guides/tool-guide).
4. **Plumbing** — pipes, connectors, tape, sealant, and the infamous duct tape.
5. **Adhesives and fillers** — glue, caulk, silicone, spackle, wallpaper paste. The corner-cutter's aisle.
6. **Furniture** — flat-pack furniture for jobs that require new pieces. The most expensive category.

## The Bargain Bin Strategy

The bargain bin is a discount shelf with random, often damaged stock: dented paint cans, chipped tiles, used tools. Based on the demo:

- **Prices are steeply discounted** — player reports suggest 40–60% off equivalent new stock.
- **Most damage is cosmetic.** A dented can still paints; chipped tiles tile fine — and the game doesn't care if they match (see our [cost-cutting guide](/guides/cost-cutting-tips)).
- **Check it before every job.** One matching can of bin paint can turn a break-even job profitable.
- **Beware mislabels** — open and stir paint before committing.

## Knock-Off Brands vs Name Brands

The store sells both established brands and knock-offs. The game is a satire, and the difference plays for laughs:

- **Knock-offs** are cheaper and it shows: paint covers thinner, tools wear faster, sealant dries brittle. For gameplay, knock-off paint at 1:5 dilution and name-brand at 1:5 look roughly the same on the wall.
- **Name brands** cost more, cover better, and their tools last. Worth it only when the visible result must carry the job, or when tool failure would be expensive ([when to invest](/guides/tool-guide)).

Player reports suggest the brand is mostly cosmetic — the real difference is price. The game leans into 1990s Poland's suspiciously similar off-brand logos.

## The 1990s Polish Supply Scarcity Mechanic

The store's stock reflects real supply in 1990s Poland, right after the fall of communism:

- **Stock is unreliable.** The exact paint or tile you need may not be on the shelf — a core mechanic, not a bug, that forces improvisation.
- **Supply comes in waves.** Restocks arrive periodically, and popular items (beige paint, white tiles) sell out first.
- **Shortages force corner-cutting.** Out of the right color? Buy a close color, or take what the bargain bin has and make it work.
- **Bulk hoarding is viable.** Buy extras of frequent needs (thinner, tape, caulk) when in stock — you can't count on them next trip.

Think of it as a mini logistics game: the store never has everything, and profit vs loss often hinges on what you grabbed on restock day.

## Best Items to Always Buy Cheap

- **Paint (for most walls)** — diluted and cheap covers fine; buy the bargain bin's stock first.
- **Rollers, brushes, trays** — consumables; shedding bristles costs seconds, not zlotys.
- **Tape, caulk, silicone, thinner** — the corner-cutter's kit, and multi-pack discounts make cheap the clear winner.
- **Tiles** — mismatched cheap tiles pass inspection (see the [cost-cutting guide](/guides/cost-cutting-tips)).
- **One-job tools** — a chisel beats a purpose-bought scraper.
- **Furniture substitutes** — if the job allows refurbishing existing furniture, that's always cheaper.

## Items Worth Spending On

- **A quality drill** — the one tool whose mid-job failure is expensive and whose speed pays back everywhere (see the [tool guide](/guides/tool-guide)).
- **The exact paint color where the client will see it** — a close-but-wrong shade on the entrance wall is visible; one right can beats a redo.
- **Paint for the "show wall"** — one quality can for the entrance wall, cheap diluted paint for the rest.
- **Bulk consumables when in stock** — with scarcity, paying full price for multi-packs when available beats hunting mid-job at any price.

## Final Shopping Rules

1. Check the bargain bin before every purchase.
2. Buy consumables in bulk when in stock — scarcity won't wait.
3. Never buy quality tools except the drill (and a roller if painting is your main income).
4. Let the visible surfaces dictate quality spending — one "show wall" can saves a redo.
5. Embrace the shortages — some of the funniest and most profitable jobs come from making do with what the store actually had.
