# Hardware Store Guide — Where to Buy Supplies in Low-Budget Repairs

The hardware store is your only supply source in Low-Budget Repairs, and how you shop there decides whether each job makes money. This guide covers how the store works, what's on the shelves, the bargain bin, and which items deserve full price. Based on the demo and player reports; the game launches **August 13, 2026** on Steam.

## How the Hardware Store Works

The store is a 1990s-style Polish hardware store: shelves and bins, a counter at the back, no shopping-list UI. You walk the aisles, pick up items, and pay at the register. Key facts from the demo:

- **Stock rotates** — shelves and the bargain bin change over time.
- **Pickup is manual** — carry items to the register yourself; no cart UI, so plan your trips.
- **No list syncing** — the game won't remind you what the job needs. Check the job description before leaving the flat.
- **Bulk discounts** — multi-packs of consumables (tape, caulk, thinner) cost less per item.

The golden shopping rule: **check the bargain bin first, always.**

## Types of Supplies Available

1. **Paint** — cans in various colors (and the ubiquitous 1990s Polish beige), rollers, brushes, trays, thinner. Your biggest recurring expense.
2. **Tiles and building materials** — tiles in a few patterns and sizes, adhesive, grout, cement, drywall, filler.
3. **Tools** — everything from hammers to drills, in cheap and quality versions, plus used tools in the bargain bin. See our [tool guide](/guides/tool-guide).
4. **Plumbing** — pipes, connectors, tape, sealant, and the infamous duct tape.
5. **Adhesives and fillers** — glue, caulk, silicone, spackle, wallpaper paste. The corner-cutter's aisle.
6. **Furniture** — flat-pack furniture for jobs that require new pieces. The most expensive category.

## The Bargain Bin Strategy

The bargain bin is a discount shelf with random, often damaged stock: dented paint cans, chipped tiles, used tools, near-expiry sealants. Based on the demo:

- **Prices are steeply discounted** — player reports suggest 40–60% off equivalent new stock.
- **Most damage is cosmetic.** A dented can still paints; chipped tiles tile fine — and the game doesn't care if they match (see our [cost-cutting guide](/guides/cost-cutting-tips)).
- **Check it before every job.** Best sequence: get the job → check the bin → buy the rest new. One matching can of bin paint can turn a break-even job profitable.
- **Beware mislabels.** Demo players report occasionally mislabeled cans — open and stir paint before you commit.

## Knock-Off Brands vs Name Brands

The store sells both established brands and knock-offs. The game is a satire, and the difference plays for laughs:

- **Knock-offs** are cheaper and the quality shows: paint that covers thinner, tools that wear faster, sealant that dries brittle. For gameplay purposes, knock-off paint at 1:5 dilution and name-brand paint at 1:5 dilution look roughly the same on the wall.
- **Name brands** cost more, cover better, and their tools last. Worth it only when the visible result must carry the job, or when a tool's failure would be expensive (see [when to invest](/guides/tool-guide)).

Player reports suggest the brand is mostly cosmetic — the real difference is price. 1990s Poland was full of suspiciously similar brands with slightly-off logos, and the game leans into that.

## The 1990s Polish Supply Scarcity Mechanic

The store's stock reflects the real supply situation of 1990s Poland, right after the fall of communism:

- **Stock is unreliable.** The exact paint color or tile you need may not be on the shelf. This is a core mechanic, not a bug — it forces improvisation.
- **Supply comes in waves.** Restocks arrive periodically, and popular items (beige paint, white tiles) sell out first.
- **Shortages force corner-cutting.** Out of the right color? Buy a close color, or take what the bargain bin has and make it work.
- **Bulk hoarding is viable.** Players report buying extras of frequently-needed items (thinner, tape, caulk) when in stock, since you can't count on them next trip.

Think of it as a mini logistics game on top of the renovation game: the store never has everything, and the difference between profit and loss is often what you grabbed on restock day.

## Best Items to Always Buy Cheap

- **Paint (for most walls)** — diluted and cheap covers fine; buy the bargain bin's stock first.
- **Rollers, brushes, trays** — consumables; cheap ones shed bristles, which costs seconds, not zlotys.
- **Tape, caulk, silicone, thinner** — the corner-cutter's kit, and multi-pack discounts make cheap the clear winner.
- **Tiles** — mismatched cheap tiles pass inspection (see the [cost-cutting guide](/guides/cost-cutting-tips)).
- **One-job tools** — a chisel beats a purpose-bought scraper.
- **Furniture substitutes** — if the job allows refurbishing existing furniture, that's always cheaper.

## Items Worth Spending On

- **A quality drill** — the one tool whose mid-job failure is expensive, and whose speed pays back on every job (full reasoning in the [tool guide](/guides/tool-guide)).
- **The exact paint color where the client will see it** — a close-but-wrong shade on the wall facing the door is visible. One can of the right paint is cheaper than a redo.
- **Paint for the "show wall"** — one quality can for the entrance wall and cheap diluted paint for the rest is the optimal split players report.
- **Bulk consumables when in stock** — with the scarcity mechanic, paying full price for multi-packs when available beats hunting for them mid-job at any price.

## Final Shopping Rules

1. Check the bargain bin before every purchase.
2. Buy consumables in bulk when in stock — scarcity won't wait.
3. Never buy quality tools except the drill (and a roller if painting is your main income).
4. Let the job's visible surfaces dictate where quality money goes — one "show wall" can of the right paint saves a redo.
5. Embrace the shortages — some of the funniest and most profitable jobs come from making do with what the store actually had.
