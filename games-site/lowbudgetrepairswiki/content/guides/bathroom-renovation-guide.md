# Low-Budget Repairs Bathroom Renovation Guide — Toilets, Sinks & Showers

The bathroom is where Low-Budget Repairs teaches you everything: it's the demo's signature job, the first contract most players ever take, and the room where the game's three biggest systems — plumbing, tiling, and painting — all collide. Learn to do it cheap here, and every later room is just a variation.

> Based on the July 2026 demo. The full game launches August 13, 2026 — new fixtures and job variants are expected, and anything unconfirmed is marked **[Needs launch verification]**.

## The Bathroom Work Order (Learn This Once)

The demo's flooded bathroom job defines the correct sequence for every bathroom job, and the order is the whole trick:

1. **Stop the water** — find the flat's valve and shut it off before touching anything. See the [plumbing guide](/guides/plumbing-guide) for the valve rule; it prevents 90% of bathroom failures.
2. **Fix the plumbing** — the cheapest fix that holds: duct tape, sealant, or replacing only the damaged section. If it passes the tap test, it's done.
3. **Dry the damage** — water damage spreads into walls and floors; deal with it before covering anything.
4. **Tile the floor** — the classic inspection focus. Walls usually pass with paint.
5. **Paint the walls** — water stains get covered properly; see below.
6. **Replace or repair fixtures** — toilet, sink, and shower hardware last, so you don't redo them around wet work.
7. **Tidy and re-test** — run every tap yourself, then call the client.

Skip a step and you redo it: paint before the leak is fixed, and the leak ruins the paint.

## Toilets and Sinks: Repair Before Replace

Fixtures work on the same logic as everything else in this game: **the client cares that it functions and looks acceptable, not where it came from.**

- **Repair beats replace.** A running toilet or a dripping sink valve is plumbing work — fix the mechanism, don't buy the fixture. The demo's plumbing loop (tape, sealant, cheapest fitting) applies to fixture internals the same way it applies to pipes.
- **Hide the cheap fix behind the fixture.** A taped pipe or sealer seam behind the sink cabinet is invisible at inspection; a visible drip is an automatic fail. The [inspection guide](/guides/inspection-guide) confirms function is the only plumbing category that matters.
- **Replace only when the client demands it or the fixture is broken beyond repair.** Cracks, missing pieces, and fixtures the contract explicitly names get replaced — with the cheapest version that looks acceptable.
- **Skip the premium fixture tier.** No inspection has ever measured chrome. **[Demo-verified]** — the demo's inspectors judge appearance from standing height, and a clean budget fixture reads the same.

## Showers and Baths: The Water-Tightness Test

Showers are the bathroom's second plumbing check: water runs down the wall and floor, and leaks find every shortcut.

- **Waterproofing matters here more than anywhere.** The demo's tiling is forgiving on *walls*, but a shower that leaks through into the flat below is a failure no grout line can hide.
- **Caulk the seams properly.** Shower trays, bath edges, and wall corners need a real caulk seal — the [cost-cutting guide's](/guides/cost-cutting-tips) cheap-caulk trick works, but a shower seam must actually hold water.
- **Mismatched tiles go at the back of the shower** — the least-visible spot in the room, per the [tiling guide](/guides/tiling-guide). Group the worst tiles there and keep the tiles at eye level on the main wall in one color family.
- **Test with water before the client arrives.** Fill the shower tray, let it sit, check the flat below if you can. A 30-second test beats a redo.

## Tiling the Bathroom Specifically

The bathroom is the only room where floor tiling is close to mandatory — the demo's flooded bathroom job requires floor tiles, and walls pass with paint. That asymmetry is the budget:

- **Buy the cheapest pack that covers the floor area.** Coverage is what's inspected, not quality.
- **Don't tile walls unless the contract names them.** Bathroom walls usually pass painted. Check the contract before the shop trip.
- **Use the classic hiding spots:** behind the toilet, under the sink cabinet, at the back of the shower. Mismatched or damaged tiles live there.
- **Grout generously.** Wide joints and edge gaps read as "grout line" from the doorway. The [tiling guide](/guides/tiling-guide) covers the full method, including skipping spacers and levels.

## Water Damage: The Stains That Bleed Through

Water damage is the bathroom's most common "painting" problem, and it has a specific failure mode: **thin paint over a water stain bleeds through and fails inspection.** The demo's fix is a thick, full-coverage coat — enough paint that the stain physically cannot show. From the inspection guide's risk table: a dilute coat over a stain is high-risk; a thick coat over a stain is safe.

For serious damage, remember the demo's demolition-then-repair lesson: remove what's broken (tiles, plaster) before covering what isn't. Painting over an actively crumbling wall just moves the problem to the inspection.

## What the Bathroom Inspection Actually Checks

Clients check five things in the bathroom, in rough priority:

1. **Plumbing function** — they turn the taps. One drip fails the job.
2. **Floor tile coverage** — missing tiles or open gaps fail; crooked or mismatched passes.
3. **Wall appearance** — stains bleeding through, unpainted patches, and half-finished edges fail; thin coats pass.
4. **Fixtures** — broken or visibly patched fixtures read as damage.
5. **Cleanliness** — wet floors, debris, and open paint cans set a critical mood. Dry the floor before the client walks in.

## Bathroom Budget Plan

- One shop trip: cheapest floor tile pack, cheapest paint that covers, tape/sealant, and only the fixture parts you must replace.
- Spend where visible: the floor tiles at the doorway and the wall facing the door. Cheap out everywhere else.
- One visible shortcut per room: pick posters, mismatched tiles, or caulk lines — not all three.
- Keep the plumbing honest: tape the pipes, but make sure nothing drips. Plumbing failures are the most expensive redo in the game.

## Bathroom Cheat Sheet

- Valve off → fix the leak → dry everything → tile the floor (cheap pack, one corner outward) → paint the walls (thick over stains) → repair fixtures → caulk the shower seams → test every tap → dry the floor → call the client.
