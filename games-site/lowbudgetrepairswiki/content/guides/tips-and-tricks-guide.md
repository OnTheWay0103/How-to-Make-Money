# Low-Budget Repairs Tips & Tricks — 25+ Shortcuts from the Demo Community

Low-Budget Repairs rewards one skill above all: **getting away with it.** The demo's community has spent weeks finding the shortcuts that work, the tricks that pass inspection, and the mistakes that cost whole jobs. This is the compiled list — the quick wins, speed tricks, and warning signs worth knowing before launch on **August 13, 2026**. Each section links to the deep-dive guide behind it.

## The Ten Golden Rules

1. **Check the bargain bin before every job.** Damaged paint and chipped tiles are 40–60% off and perform identically. This one habit decides most margins.
2. **Dilute the paint, stir it well.** One can + thinner at ~1:5 covers a room. Unmixed thinner leaves streaks — the number-one visible tell.
3. **Paint only what the contract requires.** Behind radiators, furniture, and doors is invisible, so it doesn't exist.
4. **Turn off the water valve before plumbing.** The demo's most common fail is flooding the flat you're meant to be fixing.
5. **Duct tape holds.** A firmly taped pipe passes the tap test. Function is binary: if it doesn't drip, it passes.
6. **Mismatched tiles are fine.** Size and coverage matter; color and leveling don't. Grout hides the rest.
7. **Cover, don't repair.** Holes get posters, cracks get caulk, mold gets paint. Always price the cover-up before the repair.
8. **Furniture goes out the window.** Hauling it down stairs is wasted time. The courtyard mess is flavor text.
9. **Tidy before the inspection.** Paint drips and open cans fail the cleanliness check — five minutes, cheapest insurance in the game.
10. **One shop trip per job.** Every return visit is time you could spend on the next contract. Buy everything, plus cheap spares, in one go.

## Speed Tricks

- **Leave debris in the hallway.** Bags in the common area are nobody's responsibility; hauling to the dumpster is wasted time.
- **Use the wrong tool in a pinch.** A chisel scrapes wallpaper, a hammer chisels tiles, caulk acts as grout. The result is uglier — inspectors rarely look that closely.
- **Skip prep entirely.** No sanding, no primer, no masking. The demo accepts raw painting over anything.
- **Walk the flat once, then the store once.** Planning the buy list before leaving is the biggest time saver in the game — the store has no list UI and won't remind you.
- **Batch jobs in the same building.** Less walking between contracts, and neighbors who see you working may offer new ones.

## Money Tricks

- **Bulk-buy consumables when in stock.** Tape, caulk, and thinner multi-packs cost less per item, and the store's stock rotates — hoard the cheap stuff.
- **Keep leftovers.** A spare can of paint or a leftover tile is a free fix on a future job. Storage costs nothing.
- **Buy one "show wall" can.** The wall opposite the entrance gets the decent paint; every other wall gets diluted cheap stock. ([materials guide](/guides/materials-guide))
- **Refurbish before replacing.** If the contract says "refurbish," sand-and-paint beats buying anything. Read the wording before you bid.
- **Take repaint jobs first.** They're the 80–90% margin jobs that build the float for riskier contracts. ([job profit guide](/guides/job-profit-guide))
- **Decline jobs the store can't supply cheaply.** A job requiring full-price materials is a 30% margin job. The board refreshes.

## Painting Tricks

- **Two coats on the entrance wall only.** The most-looked-at surface carries the room; everywhere else, one thin coat passes.
- **Roll in long, even strokes.** Patchy-looking rollers get noticed; consistent (even thin) coverage passes.
- **Paint corners with the brush, walls with the roller.** Brush-only walls look visibly different — the demo's painting sim rewards the two-tool approach.
- **Scrape loose paint first.** Bubbling paint paints badly and flakes show under fresh coats.

## Tiling Tricks

- **Knock out tiles behind the toilet first.** If you're going to be sloppy with removal, be sloppy where the client won't look.
- **Grout covers sins.** Mismatched edges, missing spacers, uneven rows — grout hides them all. Never skip grout on visible rows.
- **Tile the visible wall first.** If you run out of tiles, an invisible corner missing a tile beats a visible one missing a tile.

## Inspection Tricks

- **Re-test everything you can trigger** — taps, doors, drawers — before calling the client. A 20-second check beats a free redo.
- **Sweep the flat from the doorway.** That's exactly how the inspector sees it. Anything visible from the door gets fixed; anything else gets skipped.
- **Don't stack shortcuts on one wall.** One cheap trick passes; five obvious mistakes get noticed. Spread the risk across rooms and surfaces.
- **Tidy last.** The cleanliness check happens at the end — do your mess-making first, clean up second, call the client third. ([inspection guide](/guides/inspection-guide))

## Mistakes That Cost Profit

- **Forgetting the water valve** — floods the flat, fails the job, costs the cleanup. Most common demo fail by far.
- **Stirring diluted paint badly** — streaks the inspector sees. Stir thoroughly, every batch.
- **Overbuying** — two cans and 20 tiles when you needed half. The contract tells you what's required; believe it.
- **Return trips to the store** — each one is a job you didn't start. One trip, everything, always.
- **Failing the same client twice** — reputation controls your job board; a second fail dries up the neighborhood.
- **Stacking too many visible shortcuts** — one cheap trick passes, five obvious ones don't. Vary your risk.
