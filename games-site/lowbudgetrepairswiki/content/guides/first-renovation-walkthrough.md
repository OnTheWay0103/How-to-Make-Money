# Low-Budget Repairs First Renovation Walkthrough — Flooded Bathroom Step by Step

Your first contract in Low-Budget Repairs is almost always the **flooded bathroom** — the demo's signature job, and the perfect introduction to the whole game. It teaches you the full loop in about **30–60 minutes**: accept the job, shop for supplies, stop the leak, repair the damage, get inspected, get paid. This walkthrough takes you through every step, with the exact corner-cutting that makes the job profitable. Based on the July 2026 demo; the full game launches **August 13, 2026**.

## Step 0: Read the Contract and Walk the Flat

Before you spend a single zloty, **walk the entire flat and read the job description twice**. The contract lists what the client actually requires — usually for the flooded bathroom:

- Stop the leak (plumbing)
- Replace visibly broken tiles
- Repaint the water-damaged walls

Note what it does **not** require: new fixtures, new floor, sanding, primer, repainting undamaged rooms. Anything not in the contract is money you don't need to spend. This is the single most important habit in the game — see the [renovation jobs guide](/guides/renovation-jobs-guide) for how contracts are structured.

## Step 1: The Shopping List

Make your list before leaving the flat. For this job you need, at minimum:

1. **Pipe tape or sealant** — the "fix" for the leak (a cheap fitting if the pipe is visibly broken)
2. **Tiles** — only enough to replace the broken ones; mismatched tiles are acceptable
3. **Tile adhesive** — cheapest brand
4. **Paint + thinner** — one can stretched with thinner at roughly 1:5 (see the [materials guide](/guides/materials-guide))
5. **Roller + tray** — cheap versions; the bargain bin often has usable ones

**Check the bargain bin first, every trip.** A dented paint can and chipped tiles from the bin are 40–60% off and perform identically on the wall. If the bin has tiles that roughly match, this job's margin jumps instantly. Full shopping details are in our [hardware store guide](/guides/hardware-store-guide).

Buy everything in one trip. The store's stock rotates, and every return visit is time you aren't making money.

## Step 2: Stop the Water — Before Anything Else

The demo's most common new-player disaster is skipping this: **turn off the flat's water valve before touching the pipe.** If you don't, your "repair" sprays water everywhere, the damage gets worse, and you pay for it in cleanup time (or a flood the client notices).

With the water off:

- Find the leak — a burst section, a joint, or a drip from a fitting.
- **Duct tape the leak.** Wrap the pipe firmly, several layers, extending past the damage. A taped pipe passes inspection as long as it actually holds — the demo's plumbing check is behavioral: the client turns the tap, and if nothing drips, you pass.
- If the pipe is truly broken through, replace only the damaged section with the cheapest fitting that connects — never the whole line.

Do not move on until the tape/fitting is dry and holding. Plumbing is the one category where function beats cosmetics.

## Step 3: Dry and Clean the Damage

Water damage spreads; deal with it before painting over it:

- **Dry the worst areas.** Towels, cloths — the demo doesn't simulate drying time, but wet plaster paints badly and looks blotchy.
- **Scrape loose paint** where the wall is bubbling. A chisel does this job — no need to buy a dedicated scraper.
- **Remove the damaged tiles** with a hammer and chisel. Knock them out cleanly where you can; behind the toilet and under the sink, the client can't see, so they don't need to be clean at all.

## Step 4: Replace the Broken Tiles

- Apply adhesive and press the tiles in place. **No level, no spacers.** A slightly uneven row adds "character" and the client rarely notices from standing height — the [tiling guide](/guides/tiling-guide) covers exactly what survives the inspection.
- **Mismatched tiles are fine.** Size and coverage matter; matching color does not.
- **Grout the gaps.** Skip fancy grout — a cheap bag mixed roughly is fine. Grout hides the mismatched edges and the missing spacers.

Only replace tiles the contract requires and the client can see. Tiles behind the toilet, under the sink, or in the corner behind the door are optional.

## Step 5: Paint the Water-Stained Walls

The repaint is where the job's profit is made or lost — paint is your biggest expense.

- **Dilute the paint.** One can of paint + thinner (or water) at roughly **1:5** covers the whole room and passes inspection. Stir thoroughly — unmixed thinner leaves streaks the client *will* notice.
- **Paint only what the contract requires.** Behind the radiator, behind the door, behind the toilet — invisible, so nonexistent.
- **Two coats on the entrance wall.** The wall opposite the door is the most-looked-at surface in the room; give it a second, less-diluted coat. Every other wall gets one.
- **Paint over the water stains** — that's the point of the repaint. A thick coat of cheap paint hides the brown edges; the client never checks what's underneath. The full technique is in the [painting guide](/guides/painting-guide).

## Step 6: Tidy Before the Inspection

Five minutes of cleanup is the cheapest insurance in the game. The inspection checks **cleanliness** — paint drips on the floor, open cans, tools lying around, and debris all read as "unprofessional" and set the client's mood before they look at the walls.

- Pick up tools, close paint cans, wipe visible drips.
- Bag the debris. Bags left in the hallway are fine — the common areas are nobody's responsibility, and hauling to the dumpster is wasted time.
- If there's furniture you couldn't be bothered to move, that's fine — just make sure the paint behind it doesn't show at the doorway.

## Step 7: The Inspection and Getting Paid

Call the client. They walk the flat and look — from standing height, with the lights on, at what's visible:

1. **Plumbing:** they turn the tap. No drip = pass, even if it's tape underneath.
2. **Tiles:** missing or visibly crooked tiles fail; slightly uneven grout passes.
3. **Paint:** bare patches fail; streaky or thin walls usually pass.
4. **Damage:** uncovered holes or stains fail; posters, caulk, and thick paint over them pass.
5. **Cleanliness:** the mess you left matters.

What happens next: full payment, a grumble with payment, or a fail (no payment, possibly a redo — see the [inspection guide](/guides/inspection-guide)). If you followed this walkthrough, you should get the fee in your pocket with a grumble at worst — and "tough luck, the money's already in your pocket" is the game's entire philosophy.

## Example Budget: Where the Profit Comes From

A typical flooded bathroom (demo values, rough):

| Item | Proper approach | Low-budget approach |
|---|---|---|
| Pipe repair | New pipe section | Duct tape + cheap fitting |
| Tiles | Matching, leveled, spaced | Mismatched bin tiles, no spacers |
| Paint | 2 cans, full strength | 1 can + thinner, 1:5 |
| Wall prep | Sand, prime, fill | Scrape loose paint only |
| **Est. supplies** | **~60% of the fee** | **~30–40% of the fee** |

That gap — the money you didn't spend — is the entire game. Every job is this same puzzle, scaled up. ## Common First-Job Mistakes

- **Forgetting the water valve** — you flood the flat and the client sees it. Most common demo fail.
- **Not stirring diluted paint** — streaks are visible; the client notices.
- **Painting everything** — behind-radiator paint is pure waste.
- **Overbuying** — two cans of paint and 20 tiles when you needed half that. Buy what the contract requires.
- **Skipping cleanup** — the cheapest five minutes in the game, and players skip it constantly.

That's the whole job. Practice it in the free demo before the **August 13, 2026** launch — it's the same contract, and everything you learn carries into the full game.
