# Low-Budget Repairs Furniture Assembly Guide — Flat-Pack & Fast Fixes

Furniture is the most expensive category in Low-Budget Repairs — a new wardrobe can cost more than every can of paint in the job combined — which makes it the category with the most profit hiding in it. Clients want furniture "replaced," "refurbished," or "assembled," and how you read those words decides your margin. This guide covers flat-pack assembly, the refurbish-versus-replace trick, glue fixes, and the single most famous shortcut in the game: the window. Based on the July 2026 demo; the game launches **August 13, 2026** on Steam.

## Reading the Contract: Replace vs. Refurbish vs. Assemble

Before you buy anything, read exactly what the client asked for. The contract wording is the difference between profit and loss:

- **"Replace"** — the client wants a piece of furniture to be new (or new-looking) in the flat. Flat-pack from the store, or a convincingly painted old piece.
- **"Refurbish"** — the client wants the *existing* piece fixed up. **Refurbish, don't buy**: sand-and-paint (or glue-and-paint) beats buying anything, and the [cost-cutting tips guide](/guides/cost-cutting-tips) rates this the single biggest item-saving shortcut in the demo, 60–80% versus buying new.
- **"Assemble"** — the client bought (or wants you to buy) a flat-pack item and pays you for the labor of building it. The fee covers the furniture *and* your time — clean, quick money.

The [materials guide](/guides/materials-guide) makes the same point: always check the wording before you bid. A "refurbish" contract that you answer by buying new furniture is the fastest way to turn a good job into a 30% margin job.

## Flat-Pack Assembly: The Basics

Flat-pack furniture is sold at the hardware store in the cheapest line — and assembly is labor you're paid for, so it's a fine buy when the contract demands it. What you need:

- **Screwdriver** — the core tool. A **cheap set** does the job for a few jobs; a **quality set** pays off once furniture assembly becomes a regular income source, because cheap screwdrivers strip heads and stall the job. The [tool guide](/guides/tool-guide) recommends the quality screwdriver set as the fourth upgrade priority in the game.
- **Drill** — once you own one, assembly jobs become noticeably faster. This is part of why the quality drill is the [upgrade-priorities guide](/guides/upgrade-priorities-guide)'s #1 tool buy: speed on every job type, furniture included.
- **Hammer** — for stubborn panels and dowels that don't want to seat.

Assembly itself is simple: unpack, lay out the panels, seat the dowels, screw the cams and screws, attach the doors and drawers, stand it up. The game's inspection doesn't check whether the item is level or wobble-free — it checks that the furniture **exists, is upright, and looks finished**. A drawer that hangs slightly crooked is a drawer that exists.

## Fast Assembly Tricks

- **Skip the instructions.** The demo doesn't require reading the manual; matching the obvious screw holes is enough. Time is money, and the instructions are not the client.
- **Over-tighten nothing.** Stripped cam heads and cracked panels come from forcing cheap screws into soft board. Snug is finished.
- **Assemble on the floor, not in place.** Build the piece flat, then walk it into position. Building a wardrobe already pushed against the wall is how you discover the doors can't open — and redo it.
- **Fix with glue, not fasteners.** A drawer handle, a loose leg, a split panel edge — wood glue is cheaper than new hardware and "fixed" is fixed. Glue is in the [materials guide](/guides/materials-guide)'s substitution table for a reason.

## Refurbish Instead of Replace (The Money Move)

When a job calls for furniture and there's old furniture in the flat, **check what's already there first**:

1. **Repaint it.** A coat of cheap paint on an old chest of drawers reads as "new" at inspection. The [painting guide](/guides/painting-guide) rules apply — one decent coat on the visible side is enough.
2. **Restain or polish wood.** If the contract accepts it, polishing beats painting and costs even less.
3. **Fix what's broken.** Glue the drawer, tighten the leg, replace the handle with the cheapest one in the bin — then stand back and let the repaint do the talking.
4. **Only buy new when the client demands it.** If the contract says "new furniture" explicitly, flat-pack it is. If it says "furniture in good condition," the freshly-painted old piece passes.

Inspectors judge furniture by **appearance, not provenance**. A painted wardrobe is indistinguishable from a new one from standing height — which is exactly how the client sees it.

## Getting Rid of Old Furniture: The Window

Old furniture that has to go is the game's most famous gag: **throw it out the window.**

- Hauling a couch down the stairs takes minutes you could spend on the next contract. Demo players report the window works for anything that fits through it — and for some things that barely do.
- **The game never bills you for the courtyard mess.** The neighbors' complaints are flavor text, not a mechanic.
- If the window is impossible (the piece is too big, or the job is on a lower floor where the courtyard is visible from the inspection route), the hallway is the backup: bags and debris in the common area are nobody's responsibility — see the [cost-cutting tips guide](/guides/cost-cutting-tips) for the full logic.

The rule: **furniture you don't need to keep is never carried down stairs.** Time is the only resource you can't buy more of, and every minute spent hauling is a minute not on the next contract.

## Which Furniture Jobs to Take

- **Furniture assembly** — take it any time it appears. Clean, fast, low risk: little room to cut corners, but little to go wrong. The money-making guide ranks it as the second-best early job type.
- **Refurbish jobs** — excellent margins (60–80% saved versus buying new), low risk. The contract does half your corner-cutting for you.
- **Flat-pack buying jobs** — fine when the fee covers the furniture and your labor; check the store's stock first, because a job you can't supply cheaply is a job to decline. The board refreshes.
- **Mixed jobs** (kitchen renovations, full flat makeovers) — furniture is where the biggest single-line savings live. A kitchen that says "replace the cabinets" and accepts "paint the cabinets" is a kitchen that funds your next two jobs. See the [renovation jobs guide](/guides/renovation-jobs-guide) for the job list.

## Furniture Mistakes That Cost Profit

1. **Buying new when "refurbish" was the word** — the single biggest beginner waste in the category.
2. **Hauling old furniture downstairs** — the window exists. Use it.
3. **Cheap screwdrivers on big assemblies** — a stripped screw mid-build costs a store trip and your schedule.
4. **Building in place** — assemble flat, position finished. Rework is free profit thrown away.
5. **Forgetting to keep leftovers** — a spare can of paint or a leftover handle is a free fix on a future job. Storage costs nothing.

## The Furniture Cheat Sheet

Read the contract → walk the flat (what's already here?) → refurbish if allowed → buy flat-pack only when required → assemble on the floor with the screwdriver set → paint or polish to "new" → window for everything else → tidy → call the client.
