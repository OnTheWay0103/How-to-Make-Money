# Best Cost-Cutting Tips & Shortcuts in Low-Budget Repairs

Low-Budget Repairs is a game where cutting corners is the entire point. You're paid a flat fee per job, so every zloty saved on supplies is a zloty of profit — and the game's inspectors only catch what they can see. This guide compiles every known shortcut from the demo and player reports, each with the technique, the savings, and the risk of getting caught.

The game launches **August 13, 2026** on Steam. Until then, the demo is your lab.

## 1. Watering Down Paint

**Technique**: Mix thinner (or water, per demo players) into your paint. A 1:5 ratio (one part thinner, five parts paint) covers acceptably; stir thoroughly to avoid streaks. Keep a slightly thicker mix for the wall facing the door.

**Savings**: One can becomes effectively 1.5–2 cans — roughly 40–50% off your paint spend per room.

**Risk**: **Low.** Streaks on large walls happen, but a second roller pass fixes most. Only heavily diluted paint (1:10 or worse) fails close inspection.

## 2. Mismatched Tiles, No Spacers, No Level

**Technique**: Buy whatever tiles the bargain bin has — pattern and color don't need to match, only size. Skip spacers and leveling; lay tiles fast, even if grout lines wander.

**Savings**: 30–50% of tile costs, plus spacers and leveling tools.

**Risk**: **Medium.** A patchwork bathroom can trigger complaints. Demo players report mixed patterns pass if the grout is fresh and the job looks "intentional." Keep mismatches subtle (same color family) on high-fee jobs.

## 3. Duct Tape Fixes for Plumbing

**Technique**: For leaking joints or cracked pipe sections, wrap the problem in duct tape — multiple layers, pressed firm. This is the demo's most common fix.

**Savings**: 70–80% versus replacing a pipe section plus connectors.

**Risk**: **High.** This is the easiest shortcut to fail. Leaks find tape; a soaked floor is hard to hide, and the redo is expensive. Keep tape fixes out of sight (behind the sink cabinet) and consider a proper fix for visible pipes.

## 4. Bargain-Bin Supplies

**Technique**: Check the hardware store's bargain bin on every trip. It holds off-brand, damaged, or near-expiry items — dented paint cans, chipped tiles, used tools — at steep discounts. Stir, shake, and sort; demo players report most bin items work fine.

**Savings**: 40–60% on whatever you buy there. Across a full renovation, it can mean the difference between break-even and profit.

**Risk**: **Low.** Main hazards are mislabeled paint (check the color by opening the can) and tools near breaking point — both cheap to recover from.

## 5. Painting Only Visible Walls

**Technique**: Paint only what the client will look at. Skip behind the radiator, behind furniture, the wall behind the door, and the inside of closets.

**Savings**: 20–30% of paint usage on a typical room — often a full can's worth.

**Risk**: **Low.** Inspectors don't move furniture (based on the demo). Just read the job terms before committing to move anything yourself.

## 6. Posters Over Drywall Holes

**Technique**: When drywall is damaged — kicked-in holes, dents, failed anchors — cover it with a poster instead of patching. The game treats posters as decoration.

**Savings**: 70–80% versus drywall patch, filler, sanding, and repaint.

**Risk**: **Medium.** A poster in the middle of a bare wall looks deliberate; a poster over a giant hole at eye level looks like what it is. Use posters on smaller damage, or where they're already "behind something."

## 7. Leaving Trash in Common Areas

**Technique**: Instead of hauling debris to the dumpster, stack bags in the hallway or stairwell outside the flat.

**Savings**: 10–15 minutes of hauling per job — more jobs per day.

**Risk**: **Very low.** Player reports suggest no one bills you for hallway mess, though neighbor dialogue may comment. The lowest-risk shortcut in the game, and the most in-character.

## 8. Using Wrong Tools in a Pinch

**Technique**: The demo lets tools approximate each other: a chisel or screwdriver scrapes wallpaper, a hammer persuades stuck tiles, a utility knife stands in for a scraper. Skip buying specialized one-use tools.

**Savings**: Avoids small tool purchases per job — a few percent, but it compounds.

**Risk**: **Low.** Results are uglier and work is slower, but nothing fails because you used the "wrong" tool. The main cost is time.

## 9. Cheap Caulk/Silicone Over Cracks

**Technique**: For hairline cracks, gaps around pipes, or seam lines, run cheap caulk or silicone over the gap instead of filler, sanding, and repaint. Squeeze, smooth with a finger, done.

**Savings**: 50–70% versus proper crack repair in materials, plus all the sanding time you skip.

**Risk**: **Medium.** Fresh caulk is visible up close. For cracks at eye level, paint a thin coat over the caulk to blend it. Never caulk something structurally broken — a sagging shelf or cracked beam will fail.

## 10. Refurbishing Old Furniture Instead of Buying New

**Technique**: When a job calls for "new" furniture, check what's already in the flat first. Restaining, repainting, or polishing the existing piece often satisfies the contract. New flat-pack furniture is only needed when the client explicitly demands it or the old piece is missing.

**Savings**: 60–80% versus buying new — the single biggest item-saving shortcut in the demo.

**Risk**: **Low.** Inspectors judge furniture by appearance, not provenance. A freshly painted chest of drawers passes as new to most clients.

## How to Combine Shortcuts Safely

Each shortcut raises the chance the client notices *something*, and visible shortcuts stack. Safe combinations from player reports:

- **Invisible shortcuts stack freely**: skip behind radiators + watered-down paint + bargain bin + hallway trash — none are visible at inspection.
- **One visible shortcut per room**: posters, mismatched tiles, or caulk lines — pick one per room so the job stays coherent.
- **Never stack on plumbing**: a tape fix plus visible shortcuts invites a full inspection, and plumbing failures are the most expensive redo.

The golden rule: make the first thing the client sees — the entrance wall, the bathroom floor — look clean and fresh, and everything hidden can be as cheap as you like. Full release on **August 13, 2026** may change inspection behavior; we'll update this guide if it does.
