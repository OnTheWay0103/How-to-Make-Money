# Low-Budget Repairs Upgrade Priorities — What to Fix First

Every renovation job in Low-Budget Repairs is a list of things to fix, but the list hides an order: **function before damage, damage before cosmetics, visible before hidden.** Do the work in the wrong order and you repaint a wall, then flood it again — profit spent twice. This guide lays out the order of operations that keeps every job cheap and passing. Based on the July 2026 demo; the full game launches **August 13, 2026**.

## The Golden Order of Operations

For any job, work in this sequence:

1. **Stop the source of damage.** Leaks, drips, broken pipes. Water damage spreads; the leak must die before anything else. In the [flooded bathroom walkthrough](/guides/first-renovation-walkthrough), this is turning off the valve and taping the pipe — before you touch a single tile.
2. **Dry and clean.** Wet walls paint badly, and dirt under fresh paint shows up as a patch the inspector sees. This step costs nothing but time, and skipping it ruins the steps after.
3. **Repair or cover structural damage.** Holes, cracked plaster, missing tiles. Repair what's visible, cover what's cheap to cover (see the [cost-cutting tips guide](/guides/cost-cutting-tips) — posters and caulk are legitimate materials).
4. **Cosmetic work last.** Painting, tiling, wallpaper. Cosmetics are the final layer — nothing you do here survives if a leak resumes underneath.

The logic: **each step protects the one after it.** Paint is wasted on a wall that gets re-flooded, and tiles are wasted on a floor that gets soaked again. Function-first is also the cheapest order — a taped pipe costs zlotys, but a re-painted bathroom costs an entire job.

## What Inspectors See First (Room Priority)

The inspection is a walkthrough: the client enters the flat, moves through rooms in order, and looks from standing height with the lights on ([inspection guide](/guides/inspection-guide)). That makes the **first rooms and first surfaces the priority**:

1. **Entrance hall** — the client's first impression. The wall opposite the door is the single most-looked-at surface in any flat. It deserves the one quality can of paint in your budget — the "show wall" rule from the [materials guide](/guides/materials-guide).
2. **Living room** — where the client spends the most time during the walkthrough. Visible holes, stains, and mess here fail jobs.
3. **Kitchen** — the splashback and worktop get close looks; the areas under the worktop overhang and behind appliances do not.
4. **Bathroom** — the only room with a behavioral test: the tap. Function beats looks here, but cracked tiles in plain sight still fail.
5. **Bedrooms and storage rooms** — least-checked. Cheapest materials, fewest skipped shortcuts needed.

Within each room, the same logic: **eye level first, floor second, corners third, hidden areas never.** A client notices a streaky wall at eye level; they rarely crouch to check skirting, and they never move furniture.

## Surface Priority: What to Fix Before What

| Priority | Surface | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Plumbing and leaks | Fails on a tap test; damages everything below |
| 2 | Visible holes and cracks | Fails the damage check; easy to cover cheaply |
| 3 | Required paint coverage | Unpainted required walls fail; thin paint passes |
| 4 | Tile coverage | Missing tiles fail; uneven grout passes |
| 5 | Cleanliness | One tidy pass before inspection; cheapest insurance |
| 6 | Optional surfaces | No failure risk — skip until everything above is done |

## What to Skip Entirely

Time and money both flow to what's invisible:

- Behind radiators, behind furniture, behind doors
- Inside closets, under sinks, behind appliances
- Areas above eye level (top shelves, ceiling edges) unless the contract names them
- Any surface the contract doesn't list as required

Skipping these isn't sloppy — it's the game's core mechanic. The client's inspection is surface-level, and [the money-making guide](/guides/money-making-guide) explains why every zloty saved is profit.

## Tool and Loadout Upgrade Priorities

Your upgrades aren't just the flat — your own kit matters, and it has its own order. From the [tool guide](/guides/tool-guide):

1. **Quality drill** — the one tool whose mid-job failure is expensive, and whose speed pays off in every job type. Buy it first when you have the cash.
2. **Quality roller + tray** — only once repaints are your main income; cheap rollers shed bristles mid-wall.
3. **Bulk consumables** — tape, caulk, thinner in multi-packs. The store's stock rotates, so buy when in stock.
4. **Quality screwdriver set** — furniture assembly jobs become profit once you stop stripping heads.
5. **Everything else cheap** — hammers, chisels, scrapers do the same job for a fraction of the price.

## Early Progression Priorities

Before the launch on **August 13, 2026**, use the demo to lock in the sequence:

1. **Repaint jobs first** — near-zero risk, 80%+ margins, builds your float. ([job profit guide](/guides/job-profit-guide))
2. **Furniture assembly** — clean, fast money that funds your drill.
3. **Tiling jobs when the bin has tiles** — the best profit-per-job in the demo.
4. **Plumbing only with a float** — one failed tap test erases two jobs of profit.
5. **Full makeovers last** — once you know every shortcut, the boss fight pays like a week of small jobs.

The golden order is the same at every scale: **stop the damage, cover what you can, paint what they see, skip the rest.** Master that, and every flat — from a flooded bathroom to a full makeover — is just the same list, longer.
