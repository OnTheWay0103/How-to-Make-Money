# Low-Budget Repairs Kitchen Renovation Guide — Cabinets, Countertops & Appliances

The kitchen is where Low-Budget Repairs stops being a tutorial. It's the game's first multi-system room — paint, tile, woodwork, and plumbing all in one contract — and the demo shows that its budgets are noticeably more generous than the bathroom's. That generosity is the trap: **a bigger budget isn't permission to spend it.** Kitchens profit exactly like every other room: finish the visible work cheaply and pocket the difference.

> Based on the July 2026 demo, where the kitchen renovation job appears as a full contract type. The full game launches August 13, 2026 — anything unconfirmed is marked **[Needs launch verification]**.

## What a Kitchen Job Actually Involves (Demo-Confirmed)

From the demo's kitchen renovation contract, the typical task list is:

- **Repaint cabinets and walls** — the biggest surface area, and the cheapest part of the job if you do it right.
- **Tile the splashback** — usually the only tiling the contract names.
- **Replace or resurface the worktop** — the one surface clients actually look at closely.
- **Sometimes: plumbing and appliance swaps** — sink connections and old appliances.

The scope is medium, the profit is moderate-to-high, and the demo's best shortcut set is already known — this guide walks through each surface in the right order.

## Cabinets: Paint Them. Never Replace Them.

The single most profitable kitchen rule: **the demo lets you repaint cabinets instead of replacing them, and inspectors judge furniture by appearance, not provenance.** A freshly painted cabinet front reads as new — the same logic as the [cost-cutting guide's](/guides/cost-cutting-tips) furniture-refurbishing rule, applied to the whole kitchen.

- **Paint the doors and frames you can see.** Behind-the-door interiors and cabinet backs are invisible from the doorway. Skip them.
- **Repaint, don't strip.** Thick coats over the existing finish cover better and faster than sanding down to bare wood. This is a kitchen, not a museum.
- **Fix handles and hinges cheaply.** A straightened hinge or a bargain-bin handle passes; a missing handle fails. Spend five zlotys, not five hundred.
- **One visible shortcut per room.** If the cabinets are your shortcut (painted-over scuffs, mismatched doors), keep the worktop honest — see below.

## Countertops: The Surface That Gets Looked At

The worktop is the kitchen's one surface clients genuinely examine — food is prepared on it, and it sits at eye level. The demo's contract allows **resurfacing as an alternative to replacement**, and that's the profitable route:

- **Resurface before replace.** A clean, even surface over the old worktop satisfies the contract at a fraction of the cost. The game treats it as a real repair, not a cheat. **[Demo-verified]** — the demo's kitchen job explicitly offers the resurfacing option.
- **Replace only when the contract demands it** — or when the old worktop is broken beyond covering. Even then, the cheapest acceptable version wins; nobody measures laminate thickness.
- **Make the worktop the honest part of the job.** If you're mismatching tiles behind the stove and painting over cabinet damage, the worktop is where you spend the visible money. One clean surface anchors the whole kitchen.

## Splashback: Tile Only What the Contract Names

The kitchen splashback is the room's tiling moment — and the demo's rule is exact: **tile only the splashback the contract lists.** Not the whole wall, not the wall behind the cabinets, not the floor.

- **The tiling guide's full shortcut kit applies:** cheapest pack, no level, no spacers, grout hides everything.
- **Hide mismatched tiles under the worktop overhang** — the demo-confirmed spot where the counter edge shadows the worst tiles.
- **Mismatches also go behind the stove and appliances.** Nobody moves the fridge to check the tile color.
- **Skip the kitchen floor unless the contract names it.** Per the [tiling guide](/guides/tiling-guide), the kitchen floor is often paintable or coverable — and rarely required.

## Appliances: Refurbish, Swap Cheap, Skip the Premium

The demo's kitchen jobs "sometimes" include appliance swaps, and the same cost-cutting logic applies as everywhere else:

- **Refurbish what's already there** when the contract allows — a cleaned, polished appliance reads as new, exactly like painted furniture.
- **Swap only what the contract names.** The client pays a flat fee; every appliance you replace is bought from your own profit.
- **Never buy the premium appliance tier.** Function (does it work) and appearance (does it look clean) are what get inspected. The brand badge isn't checked.
- **[Needs launch verification]** — the exact appliance types, brands, and price tiers in the release build are unconfirmed.

## Kitchen Plumbing: The Sink Is the Danger Zone

Kitchens are the second home of the plumbing check — and the demo's flooded bathroom lesson applies in full:

- **Valve rule first.** Find the kitchen's shutoff before touching the sink connections. The [plumbing guide](/guides/plumbing-guide) covers the rule that prevents 90% of disasters.
- **Sink leaks are hidden-leak friendly** — behind the cabinet, under the counter — which is exactly why they're the most tempting place to shortcut. Keep tape fixes out of sight and *test the taps before the client arrives*.
- **A dripping kitchen tap is an automatic inspection fail**, same as the bathroom. Function is binary.

## Kitchen Work Order (The Right Sequence)

1. **Survey and read the contract** — required surfaces only; the kitchen usually lists cabinets, splashback, and worktop, with plumbing/appliances sometimes.
2. **Plumbing first** — fix the sink connections and shut offs before anything else; wet work before paint.
3. **Cabinets** — paint the visible fronts; fix handles cheaply.
4. **Splashback** — tile only what's listed; worst tiles under the overhang.
5. **Worktop** — resurface, or replace with the cheapest acceptable version.
6. **Appliances** — refurbish or swap only what's named.
7. **Final pass** — re-test the taps, tidy, and stage the kitchen before the client walks in.

## Kitchen Budget Plan

- One shop trip: cheapest paint for cabinets, cheapest tile pack for the splashback, worktop materials, and tape/sealant.
- The worktop is the visible spend; everything else gets the cheap route.
- Hide mismatches and repairs behind appliances, under the overhang, and inside cabinets.
- Keep leftover paint and tiles — future jobs in the same block often need exactly your leftovers.

## Kitchen Cheat Sheet

- Read the contract → fix the plumbing → paint the cabinets → tile only the splashback → resurface the worktop → swap only named appliances → test the taps → tidy → call the client.

Kitchens pay more because they're bigger and more complex — but complexity is a cost you control, not a fee you deserve. The demo's kitchen contract is the perfect training ground; the release build's new job variants and prices will be added to this guide after **August 13, 2026**.
