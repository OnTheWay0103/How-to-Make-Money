# Low-Budget Repairs Tiling Guide — Cheapest Methods & Shortcuts

> Based on the July 2026 demo. The full game launches August 13, 2026 — some details may change in the release build.

Tiling is where Low-Budget Repairs separates the renovators from the perfectionists. Tiles are expensive, tiling is slow, and the inspector — as always — checks that the surface is **covered**, not that it's *right*. Here's how to tile a bathroom or kitchen on a budget without crying over a single crooked line.

---

## Tiling Mechanics

Tiles are placed on floor and wall surfaces with adhesive, then grouted to fill the joints. Each job lists how much surface needs tiling, and you buy tiles by the pack from the shop.

The core loop: spread adhesive → place tiles → grout → move on. Tools like levels, tile cutters, and spacers exist, but the demo's inspector never demands precision — which means most of those tools are optional purchases on a budget run.

---

## Tiling Without a Level or Spacers (The Quick and Dirty Method)

A level and spacers make your tiling straight. Skipping them makes it *fast*, and the inspector mostly can't tell the difference:

- **Work from one corner outward.** If the first row is straight-ish, the rest follows — small cumulative wobble looks fine in a 1990s flat.
- **Eyeball the gaps.** Spacers create perfectly even joints; tiling by eye creates joints that are "close enough." Grout hides the difference completely.
- **Accept a slightly uneven floor.** The demo's tiling snaps tiles into place with real forgiveness. Crooked rows happen; the inspector doesn't measure angles.
- **Don't cut tiles unless you must.** If a tile doesn't fit, finish the row early and grout the space — a gap filled with grout reads as "grout line," not "mistake."
- **Save your money.** A level, spacers, and a tile cutter are the first things a careful renovator buys and the first things a low-budget renovator skips.

## Cheap Tile Sources

Tile prices vary wildly, and the cheap options are usually all you need:

- **The cheapest pack per square meter wins.** Coverage matters more than looks — nobody inspects the pattern.
- **Buy slightly less than you "need."** Small exposed gaps are easily grouted or hidden, so overbuying "just in case" is wasted money.
- **Check the client's flat for salvage.** Tiles already in the flat (behind furniture, in closets, in rooms being demolished) can be reused in visible spots.
- **Leftover packs from earlier jobs.** Stash the odd leftover tile — a future job's patch fix never needs to match.

---

## Mismatched Tile Strategy

Mixing tile colors and patterns is not a failure — it's a *strategy*:

- **The inspector checks coverage, not color consistency.** A bathroom with two different tile colors passes as long as every surface is tiled.
- **Group same-colored tiles together** in the least visible areas: behind the toilet, under cabinets, at the back of the shower.
- **Put mismatches at floor level**, where they're half-hidden by the grout line and shadow.
- **Embrace it narratively.** A 1990s Polish block flat with patchwork tiles reads as realistic. You're not building a showroom; you're passing an inspection.

---

## Grouting Shortcuts

Grout is cheaper than tiles, which makes it the perfect friend:

- **Grout only the visible joints.** Joints under cabinets and against walls never get a second look.
- **Use grout to fix big mistakes.** A wide gap between mismatched tiles disappears under a generous grout line.
- **One fast pass beats a careful one.** A thin surface coat looks the same from standing height.
- **Skip perfect cleanup.** Grout smears on tile edges are unnoticeable once dry.

---

## Covering Gaps and Uneven Surfaces

Uneven floors and broken edges are everyday problems in a 1990s block, and they all have a budget answer:

- **Caulk or sealant** fills small gaps at walls, corners, and pipe entries — cheaper and faster than cutting a tile to fit.
- **Thin tiles over bumps.** A slightly uneven floor tiled with thin tiles looks flat from the doorway.
- **Furniture is a tile too.** Put a cabinet or toilet over the bad section and nobody knows it exists.
- **Never replace subfloor.** Fixing the floor under the tiles is expensive, invisible, and uninspected.

## When Tiling Is Required vs Optional

Not every surface in a job needs tiles. Read the contract, then tile only what it lists:

| Area | Typically required? | Notes |
|------|---------------------|-------|
| Bathroom floor | Usually | The classic inspection focus |
| Bathroom walls / splashback | Often | Check the contract before buying |
| Kitchen splashback | Often | Required in most kitchen jobs |
| Kitchen floor | Sometimes | Can be paint or linoleum instead |
| Living room floor | Rarely | Usually optional, often a paint job |
| Bedroom floor | Almost never | Skip it entirely |

The demo's flooded bathroom job only requires floor tiles — walls pass with paint. If in doubt, tile the minimum the contract names and no more.

---

## Quick Tiling Checklist for a Budget Run

- Buy the cheapest pack that covers the required area.
- Tile from one corner by eye — no level, no spacers, no cutter.
- Grout every gap generously; clean nothing.
- Mismatched tiles go behind toilets and under cabinets.
- Caulk the corners, skip the subfloor, pass the inspection.
