# All Renovation Jobs in Low-Budget Repairs — Complete List & Tips

> Job list based on the July 2026 demo and official previews. The full release (August 13, 2026) will add more contracts — exact payouts are unconfirmed until launch.

Every contract in **Low-Budget Repairs** is a renovation job with a client, a flat, and a budget. Your job is to finish it for less than the budget and pocket the difference — the bigger the gap between what you spend and what the client pays, the bigger your profit.

The demo shows five core job types. Here's what each one involves, how to cheat it, and what it's worth.

---

## The 5 Job Types

### 1. Flooded Bathroom Repair

The demo's signature job — a leaky flat with water damage everywhere.

- **Typical tasks:** stop the leak, dry the worst areas, replace damaged tiles, repaint water-stained walls, sometimes swap a pipe or fitting.
- **Best shortcuts:** paint over water stains instead of treating them; replace only visibly broken tiles and let grout cover the rest; patch the pipe with the cheapest fitting that stops dripping.
- **Difficulty:** Easy — the smallest scope in the game.
- **Est. profit:** Low to moderate. It's the starter job, so it pays accordingly.

### 2. Plumbing Emergencies

Leaks, bursts, and drips — the fastest jobs, and the easiest to rush.

- **Typical tasks:** locate the leak, shut off the water, replace pipes or fittings, clean up damage, repaint the affected wall.
- **Best shortcuts:** replace only the leaking section, not the whole line; use the cheapest fitting that doesn't drip; paint over the water damage rather than repairing the plaster.
- **Difficulty:** Easy to medium — tight on time, but small in scope.
- **Est. profit:** Moderate for the time spent. Fast turnaround jobs are reliable money early on.

### 3. Living Room Refresh

The "easy money" job — no real damage, just tired walls and floors.

- **Typical tasks:** repaint walls, patch small holes, refresh skirting, sometimes a floor covering.
- **Best shortcuts:** the [Painting Guide](/guides/painting-guide) rules apply fully here — skip behind furniture and radiators, dilute paint, one coat where possible.
- **Difficulty:** Easy.
- **Est. profit:** Low per job, but fast. A good rhythm job for building reputation early.

### 4. Kitchen Renovation

A bigger scope with more systems involved.

- **Typical tasks:** repaint cabinets and walls, tile the splashback, replace or resurface the worktop, sometimes plumbing and appliance swaps.
- **Best shortcuts:** paint cabinets instead of replacing them; tile only the splashback the contract names; hide mismatched tiles under the worktop overhang (see the [Tiling Guide](/guides/tiling-guide)).
- **Difficulty:** Medium — multiple surfaces, more decisions.
- **Est. profit:** Moderate to high; kitchens are where budgets start being generous.

### 5. Complete Flat Makeover

The big one — the whole flat from top to bottom.

- **Typical tasks:** repaint every room, tile floors where required, replace damaged fixtures, fix plumbing, make the whole flat passable.
- **Best shortcuts:** plan the buy in one trip — visit the shop once, buy everything, don't return for one missing can; skip every optional surface; do the ugly rooms with the cheapest materials since nobody inspects detail.
- **Difficulty:** Hard — the largest scope, and it tests everything you learned.
- **Est. profit:** High. One flat makeover can pay like several small jobs.

## Job Difficulty Progression

The demo's jobs scale in a clear order:

1. **Flooded bathroom** — teaches tiling, painting, and the inspection system.
2. **Plumbing emergencies** — adds time pressure and tight budgets.
3. **Living room refresh** — a low-stress money job that lets you build up savings.
4. **Kitchen renovation** — multi-surface work, more planning.
5. **Flat makeover** — the boss fight of renovation: everything at once, one inspection.

Each job also gets harder versions — bigger flats, worse damage, tighter budgets — as you progress.

---

## How to Unlock More Jobs

The demo shows the start of the progression loop; the full game expands it. What's known:

- **Complete jobs successfully.** Failed or botched inspections set your reputation back and can cost you future work.
- **Accept everything early.** Job availability in the demo scales with how many contracts you've done, so taking small jobs unlocks bigger ones.
- **Mind the budget.** Finishing well under budget is how you grow — spending the client's money on premium materials doesn't earn extra reputation.
- **More jobs at release.** The August 13 launch adds jobs beyond the demo's five core types, so expect the list above to be the base, not the ceiling.

## General Profit Tips for Every Job

- **Buy once.** Return trips to the shop eat into the time you could spend on the next contract.
- **Cheapest materials that pass.** Premium paint and perfect tiles are never required to pass an inspection.
- **Read the contract for required surfaces.** Optional surfaces are where budgets die.
- **Keep leftovers.** A spare can of paint or a leftover tile is a free fix on a future job.
