# Low-Budget Repairs Electrical Guide — Wiring, Breakers & Light Fixtures

Electricity is Low-Budget Repairs' newest system — and the one with the loudest failure state. Plumbing floods a flat; electricity, done wrong, takes out the whole block. The game's playtest announcement put it bluntly: **"do it right… or see what happens."**

> Based on the July 2026 demo and the open playtest that started July 30, 2026. The electricity system is playtest-era, so less is confirmed than for painting, tiling, or plumbing. Anything unverified is marked **[Needs launch verification]** — the full game launches **August 13, 2026**.

## The Breaker Rule (Electrical's Version of the Water Valve)

Plumbing has one rule that prevents 90% of disasters: turn off the water. Electrical work has the same rule, and it lives at the **breaker panel**:

**Cut the power to the circuit before you touch anything wired.**

The playtest's breaker panel is where every electrical job starts. Each switch controls a circuit — lights, outlets, kitchen, and so on — and the game expects you to flip the right one off before working. Skip this and you risk the system's signature failure: a live wire mistake that kills the power (and the job). When in doubt, flip the main switch and work in the dark — a flashlight beats a fried circuit.

## The Breaker Panel: The One Mechanic Everyone Knows

The breaker panel is also the source of the game's most famous stunt. In the trailer, the player **locks the switches into the "on" position** — wedging them so they can't trip — which knocks out power to the entire neighborhood. It's played for comedy, but it tells you something important about the mechanics:

- **Breakers can be interacted with in detail.** Not just "flip on/off" — you can lock, wedge, and bypass them.
- **The panel affects more than one flat.** Your wiring work has neighbors.
- **There are consequences beyond the job site.** Whether the client sleeps without electricity is a player choice, per pre-launch coverage — meaning electrical shortcuts can make the client's night miserable in a way a painted-over stain never will.

## What Electrical Work Probably Looks Like (Playtest-Informed)

The playtest notes mention the new electricity system alongside improved building mechanics — painting, drilling, gluing, assembling. Electrical jobs in the release build likely follow the same shape as the demo's other trades: a defined scope, a few visible components, and an inspection at the end. Expect, based on the trailer and playtest:

- **Light fixtures** — removing old ones, rewiring connections, mounting new fixtures. The most visible electrical work; a bare wire or dangling fixture won't pass any inspection.
- **Outlets and switches** — swapping covers, replacing faulty units, testing function. **[Needs launch verification]**
- **Breaker panel work** — the entry point and the danger zone for every job above.
- **Wiring repairs** — damaged cables, exposed conductors, connections that need replacing rather than taping.

None of the exact task lists, prices, or inspection rules are confirmed yet. Treat the above as the shape of the system, not the facts.

## The Cost-Cutting Trap: Electricity Can't Be Hidden

Every other trade in this game rewards the same strategy: do the visible work well, cheap out on everything hidden. Electrical work resists that strategy for three reasons:

1. **It's functional, like plumbing.** The inspection may not open your junction box, but it will flip the light switch. If the lamp doesn't light, the job doesn't pass — tape and hope won't fool a switch test.
2. **Failures escalate publicly.** A plumbing leak floods one flat. A locked breaker blacks out the block, and the whole neighborhood knows who did it. Electrical shortcuts have the most visible failure state in the game.
3. **Cheap fix options are mostly unverified.** Duct-taping a pipe is demo-confirmed. Whether taping a wire is viable (or whether the game makes it fail instantly) is **[Needs launch verification]** — and the playtest's "do it right… or see what happens" warning suggests the game wants electricity to be the trade you can't half-ass.

## Electrical Tools: What You'll Want

From the [tool guide](/guides/tool-guide), most of what you need already exists in the demo's lineup:

- **Screwdriver (quality)** — the core electrical tool. Fixtures, outlet covers, panel access. A stripped screw mid-panel is the kind of failure that costs a job, so this is a case for the quality tier.
- **Drill (quality)** — mounting new fixtures and switches. The tool guide already recommends spending once on a quality drill; electrical work is another reason.
- **Utility knife** — stripping cable jackets and cutting tape. Cheap, replaceable.
- **Cheap hammer** — demolition of old fixtures. The cheap tier is fine here; see the [demolition guide](/guides/demolition-guide).

Dedicated electrical gear — voltage testers, wire strippers, insulated tools — hasn't been confirmed in any build. **[Needs launch verification]** Don't buy specialized tools for a job type you haven't seen yet.

## Electrical Mistakes That Cost Profit

1. **Working with the power on.** The playtest's warning is the game's own foreshadowing — the failure state exists to punish this.
2. **Locking breakers as a "trick."** It makes a great trailer, but the consequence is a neighborhood blackout. Reputation damage like this compounds: the [inspection guide](/guides/inspection-guide) notes that neighbors talk.
3. **Leaving visible damage.** A new fixture with old, damaged wiring around it invites the same "cover vs. repair" judgment as a leaky pipe — except the client can *see* the fixture and test the switch.
4. **Buying specialty tools before you know the system.** Every unconfirmed tool is a zloty that could be a can of paint. Wait for the release build.
5. **Forgetting function beats appearance.** As with plumbing, an electrical job passes on function: switch works, light turns on, nothing sparks. Polish the visible parts, make the function real, and skip whatever the contract doesn't name.

## The Electrical Cheat Sheet

- Find the panel → flip the right breaker off (or the main switch) → do the work → restore power → test every switch and fixture yourself → tidy up → call the client.

The demo's other trades teach you the loop; electricity is the same loop with higher stakes. Learn the breaker rule in the playtest if you can — the [release hub](/guides/release-hub) has the launch date, and this guide will be updated with verified wiring, fixture, and price details the moment the full game is in players' hands.
