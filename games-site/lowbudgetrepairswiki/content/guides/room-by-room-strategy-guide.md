# Low-Budget Repairs Room-by-Room Strategy — Which Rooms to Tackle First

Every renovation job in Low-Budget Repairs is a sequence of rooms, and the order you work them decides both your profit and your sanity. The demo's job list teaches a clear progression — flooded bathroom, plumbing emergencies, living room refreshes, kitchen renovations, and the complete flat makeover — and each room type has its own cost profile. This guide ranks them by what they pay, what they cost, and what the inspection actually looks at.

> Based on the July 2026 demo. The full game launches August 13, 2026 — late-game rooms and job variants are unconfirmed and marked **[Needs launch verification]**.

## The Demo's Job Order Is the Difficulty Curve

The demo unlocks contracts in a deliberate order, and it's worth respecting even when easier money appears:

1. **Flooded bathroom repair** — the tutorial. Teaches tiling, painting, plumbing, and the inspection in one compact room.
2. **Plumbing emergencies** — adds time pressure and the valve rule.
3. **Living room refresh** — the first pure money job: repaint, patch, done.
4. **Kitchen renovation** — multi-system work with a generous budget.
5. **Complete flat makeover** — everything at once, one inspection, the biggest fee.

Skipping ahead without the cash cushion is how runs die — a flat makeover's mistakes wipe out more than a bathroom's ever could.

## Room-by-Room ROI (From Demo Behavior)

| Room | Typical cost per job | Typical fee | Time | Verdict |
|------|---------------------|-------------|------|---------|
| Bathroom | Low (tile pack + paint + tape) | Low–moderate | Medium | Learning room; low ceiling, cheap to run |
| Living room | Lowest (paint only) | Low | Fastest | The bank account — run these constantly |
| Kitchen | Moderate (cabinets, splashback, worktop) | Moderate–high | Long | The profit engine — generous budget, big savings |
| Bedroom | Lowest (paint or skip) | — | Fast | Almost always optional — see below |
| Hallway | Very low | — | Fast | The staging area — entrance wall matters |
| Full flat | High (everything) | Highest | Very long | The boss job — one inspection, all-or-nothing |

## Bathroom First: The Tuition Room

The bathroom is the demo's starter job for a reason — it's small, so mistakes are cheap. Take it first and take it seriously:

- It teaches the **valve rule** and the tap test, the two plumbing facts that matter everywhere else. The [plumbing guide](/guides/plumbing-guide) has both.
- It teaches **tiling the cheap way** — one corner, no spacers, grout hides everything. See the [tiling guide](/guides/tiling-guide).
- It's the room where you learn that **floor tiles are the required surface and walls pass with paint** — the single most profitable fact in the game.

Spend your first session here, replaying the flooded bathroom until the work order is automatic. Every room after it is faster.

## Living Room Refreshes: The Bank Account

The living room is the demo's easy-money job, and it's the room you should run when you need cash fast:

- **Paint-only work.** No plumbing, no tiling — the [painting guide's](/guides/painting-guide) rules apply in full: skip behind furniture and radiators, dilute, one coat where possible.
- **Fast turnaround.** The shortest job in the game, which makes it the best reputation builder — more jobs unlock faster when you complete more contracts.
- **The trap:** low fee per job means it only pays if you run it fast. A slow living room refresh is worse than a kitchen done slowly — the [job profit guide](/guides/job-profit-guide) compares per-hour rates.

Use living rooms to build the cushion, then graduate to kitchens.

## Kitchens: The Profit Engine

The kitchen is where the demo's budgets get generous — and where the profit lives:

- **The big savings are built in:** paint the cabinets instead of replacing them, tile only the splashback, resurface the worktop. Our [kitchen guide](/guides/kitchen-renovation-guide) is the full playbook.
- **The fee is high enough that speed matters less.** A kitchen done well pays like two living rooms, so the per-hour math still works even at a careful pace.
- **It's the gateway to makeovers.** The kitchen contract is the hardest demo job short of the full flat — finishing it means you're ready for the boss.

## Bedrooms, Hallways, and Other Optional Rooms

The most profitable fact about the "extra" rooms is that they're usually **optional**:

- The [tiling guide](/guides/tiling-guide) confirms: bedroom floors are almost never required, living room floors are rarely required, and optional surfaces are where budgets die.
- Read the contract and **don't renovate what isn't named**. A bedroom you skip is a bedroom you don't pay for.
- When a room *is* named but simple — hallway, closet, entrance — do the minimum that passes inspection: paint the visible walls, cover damage, leave the rest. The [inspection guide](/guides/inspection-guide) has the risk table for every shortcut.

## The Full Flat Makeover: Planning the Order

The complete flat makeover is the demo's boss job: every room, one inspection, and a fee that pays like several small jobs. The order inside the job matters more than the job itself:

1. **Walk the whole flat and read the contract.** List every required surface before buying anything.
2. **Fix all systems first.** Plumbing and (in the release build, per the playtest) electrical issues before any paint or tile — wet or live work ruins finished work.
3. **Work back to front.** Start in the farthest rooms (bedrooms, kitchen) and finish at the entrance. The last room you touch is the first one the client sees.
4. **One shop trip.** Buy everything in the plan at once; return trips on a makeover are the biggest time leak in the game.
5. **Save one shortcut per room.** The makeover is the purest test of the "one visible shortcut per room" rule from the [cost-cutting guide](/guides/cost-cutting-tips) — visible shortcuts stack into an obvious pattern.

## Which Jobs Should You Actually Take?

- **Early game:** living room refreshes and plumbing emergencies (if you have a cash cushion) — fast, safe, and reputation-building.
- **Mid game:** kitchens — the best profit-per-hour the demo offers, once you know the cheap routes.
- **Late game:** flat makeovers — the all-or-nothing payout, only when your supplies, tools, and habits are solid.
- **Never:** a job whose systems you can't afford to redo. Flooding a flat on an empty bank is a run-ender. **[Demo-verified]** — the demo's failure economy punishes the risky take.

## Room Strategy Cheat Sheet

- Bathroom = learn the systems. Living room = build the bank. Kitchen = make the profit. Makeover = cash in the education.
- Read the contract: optional rooms are skipped, not renovated.
- Fix systems before finishes, work back to front, and leave the entrance wall for last.
- One visible shortcut per room, always.
