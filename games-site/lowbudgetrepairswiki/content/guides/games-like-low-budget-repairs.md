# Games Like Low-Budget Repairs — 7 Similar Renovation & Handyman Simulators

Low-Budget Repairs launches on August 13, 2026 — and if you've already burned through the demo's jobs and need something to fix while you wait, or you're still deciding whether the game is for you, this comparison covers the simulators that scratch a similar itch. The table below sticks to well-known titles and what's publicly confirmed about them; prices are approximate list prices and can shift during sales.

## The Short Answer

If you like the *fantasy* of Low-Budget Repairs — fixing up spaces, cutting corners, and getting paid — **House Flipper** is the closest mainstream game, **House Flipper 2** is the prettier upgrade, and **PowerWash Simulator** is the most relaxing neighbor to it. If you like the *satire* — the shady-deal energy of a handyman working a block of flats — **Thief Simulator** (from the same publisher family) has the same "what's the worst I can get away with" DNA.

## The Deep Cuts, Compared

### House Flipper — the genre benchmark

The original House Flipper (Frozen District / PlayWay, 2018) invented the mass-market fantasy Low-Budget Repairs is parodying: buy a ruined property, clean, paint, tile, and flip it for profit. Its price point (~$19.99) is the same tier Low-Budget Repairs is expected to land on (see our [price & editions guide](/guides/price-editions-guide)). The difference is tone: House Flipper wants you to *feel good* about the work; Low-Budget Repairs wants you to *get away with* the work. If you've played House Flipper and thought "what if I watered down the paint and skipped the level," you already understand the pitch.

### House Flipper 2 — the premium upgrade

House Flipper 2 (2023) does everything the first game does with modern physics, better visuals, and a sandbox mode. It's roughly $10 more than the expected Low-Budget Repairs price, and it's the honest answer if you want the renovation power fantasy at its most polished. Low-Budget Repairs is the budget-friendly, joke-forward alternative — same genre, opposite vibe.

### PowerWash Simulator — the chill neighbor

PowerWash Simulator (FuturLab, 2022) shares the most with Low-Budget Repairs on *structure*: accept jobs, complete them, get paid, unlock more. It strips out the money strategy entirely — you can't cut corners because there's only one job to do — which makes it the most relaxing game on this list. If the stress of "will the client notice the diluted paint?" sounds like a feature, Low-Budget Repairs is your game; if it sounds like a bug, PowerWash is the safer buy.

### Thief Simulator — same publisher, same cheek

Thief Simulator (2018) is the clearest sibling: same PlayWay publishing family, same comedy of getting away with something, same first-person task loop — except you're burgling houses rather than renovating them. Fans of Low-Budget Repairs' "the money's already in your pocket" attitude will recognize the energy instantly.

### Car Mechanic Simulator 2021 — the economy-sim cousin

For players drawn to the *business* side — buy parts cheap, do the work, charge the client — Car Mechanic Simulator 2021 offers the same loop with cars. It's less funny and more methodical, but if the profit optimization in Low-Budget Repairs is what hooks you, CMS 2021 has the deepest economy of the bunch.

### Renovator — the honest-work alternative

Renovator (2021) is a first-person renovation sim that takes the work seriously: tools, materials, technique, and doing the job properly. It's the anti-Low-Budget-Repairs — no corner-cutting mechanics, no satire — which makes it a useful comparison for players who want the manual labor fantasy *without* the shadiness.
