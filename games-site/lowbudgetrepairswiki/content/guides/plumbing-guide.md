# Low-Budget Repairs Plumbing Guide — Fix Leaks & Pipes on the Cheap

Plumbing is the scariest job category in Low-Budget Repairs — and the most misunderstood. Players avoid it because flooding a flat is the demo's most common fail, but plumbing is also where the game's biggest savings live: the difference between a proper pipe replacement and a roll of duct tape can be **70–80% of the job's supply cost**. This guide covers every known pipe fix, the water valve rule that prevents disasters, and how the plumbing check actually works. Based on the July 2026 demo; the game launches **August 13, 2026** on Steam.

## The One Rule That Prevents 90% of Failures

**Turn off the flat's water valve before you touch any pipe.**

The demo's most common new-player disaster is skipping this: you grab the leaking pipe, it sprays water everywhere, the damage spreads, and you either fail the job or eat the cleanup time. The valve is usually reachable in the flat — find it on your first walkthrough, before the job starts. The step-by-step is in our [first renovation walkthrough](/guides/first-renovation-walkthrough), because the flooded bathroom job is where every player learns this the hard way.

With the water off, nothing can get worse. Everything else is just cost-minimization.

## How the Plumbing Check Works: The Tap Test

The inspection's plumbing check is **behavioral, not visual**. Based on the demo, the client turns the tap (or runs the relevant water) and watches for drips:

- **No drip = pass.** The fix can be tape, sealant, or a loose-fitting dream — if it holds water, it passes.
- **A drip = fail.** One drop is enough to fail the plumbing check, even if everything else in the flat is perfect.
- **Function is binary.** There is no "mostly fixed" grade. Either it holds or it doesn't.

That's the whole game's plumbing philosophy: **if it holds water, it passes — whatever it looks like underneath.** A taped pipe behind the sink cabinet that never drips is, as far as the game is concerned, a fixed pipe.

## The Cheap Fix Ladder (Cheapest First)

For any leak, work down this list and stop at the first fix that holds:

**1. Duct tape (cheapest, most profitable).** Wrap the leak firmly — several layers, each extending past the damaged area, pressed down so the whole wrap is one solid band. Duct tape is the game's signature plumbing fix: a roll costs a fraction of a pipe section and passes the tap test as long as it actually holds. Demo coverage shows leaks patched exactly this way, sometimes with a bucket catching the last drips while the tape cures. **Use it for joints, cracks, and small burst sections.**

**2. Sealant (second line).** For threads, joints, and fittings where tape won't seal flat, a tube of sealant costs less than a new fitting and does the same job. Apply around the joint, let it set, test the tap. Sealant is the fix for "the fitting is fine, the seal isn't."

**3. Replace only the damaged section.** When a pipe is truly broken through — split open, crushed, missing a chunk — tape alone won't hold. Buy **only the damaged section** of pipe plus the cheapest connecting fitting. Never replace a whole line: the rest of the pipe is fine, and the client never inspects plumbing you didn't touch.

**4. Replace the fitting.** Stuck or stripped fittings get swapped for the cheapest compatible one. A fitting is a few zlotys; a flooded flat costs a whole job.

The [materials guide](/guides/materials-guide) has the full breakdown of what each fix costs, and the [cost-cutting tips guide](/guides/cost-cutting-tips) rates the duct-tape fix's savings (high) and risk (high) honestly.

## What the Demo's Plumbing Jobs Look Like

Plumbing appears in two of the demo's core job types (full list in the [renovation jobs guide](/guides/renovation-jobs-guide)):

- **Flooded bathroom repair** — the signature starter job. Stop the leak, dry the damage, replace broken tiles, repaint water-stained walls. The plumbing part is one taped pipe; the rest is cleanup.
- **Plumbing emergencies** — faster, tighter jobs: locate the leak, shut off the water, fix the pipe, clean up, repaint the affected wall. These are **time-pressured**: the client wants it done fast, and slow work is less money per hour.

Both are **small in scope but easy to fail** — not because the plumbing is hard, but because one forgotten valve or one unsealed wrap fails the whole job. The demo's reward for doing them right is real: plumbing fees are high because plumbing is "scary," and your costs stay near zero with tape and sealant.

## Tools You Actually Need for Plumbing

From the [tool guide](/guides/tool-guide):

- **Plunger and pipe wrenches** — the dedicated plumbing tools. Cheap versions are fine; they're used once per job at most.
- **Utility knife** — cutting tape and sealant tubes.
- **A bucket** — catches drips while a fix sets, and carries water out of the flat after a flood.
- **Skip the premium versions.** Plumbing tools fail cheaply (a stuck wrench is seconds of work, not a dead job), so the cheap tier is the right call unless you're running plumbing jobs constantly.

## Flood Risk: The Real Danger

The risk that makes players avoid plumbing isn't the work — it's the **flood**:

- A leak you fix with the valve off stays fixed. A leak you "fix" with the valve on gets worse while you watch.
- Water damage spreads: soaked floors, stained walls, damaged tiles. Every one of those becomes a repair you're now paying for.
- **One flooded flat can wipe out two jobs of profit** — cleanup time, replacement materials, and a possible fail.

The [upgrade-priorities guide](/guides/upgrade-priorities-guide) makes the same point: function before damage, always. Stop the source of water before you dry, clean, or paint anything. Paint is wasted on a wall that gets re-flooded.

## When to Take Plumbing Jobs

- **Early game: skip them** if you're low on cash. The flood risk isn't worth wiping out your float.
- **Once you have a cash cushion:** take them — they're among the best money-per-hour jobs because the fee is high and the supplies are nearly free with tape.
- **Never stack shortcuts on plumbing.** The [cost-cutting guide](/guides/cost-cutting-tips) is explicit: a tape fix plus visible shortcuts invites a full inspection, and plumbing failures are the most expensive redo in the game. Keep the visible part of the job clean — tidy bathroom, dry floor — and the tape stays invisible behind the cabinet.

## Plumbing Mistakes That Cost Profit

1. **Forgetting the water valve** — floods the flat. The demo's #1 fail by far.
2. **One-layer tape wraps** — a single strip doesn't hold pressure; the tap test drips, the job fails, and you redo it with the supplies you already bought.
3. **Replacing the whole line** — the client pays a flat fee; the extra pipe sections come out of your pocket.
4. **Skipping the re-test** — run the tap yourself before calling the client. A 20-second check beats a free redo. (The [inspection guide](/guides/inspection-guide) covers what else to re-test.)
5. **Taking plumbing jobs with an empty bank** — the fee is tempting, but one flood ends your run.

## The Plumbing Cheat Sheet

- Find the valve → turn it off → find the leak → tape it (several layers) → or seal it → or replace the damaged section only → dry the damage → repaint the stains → test the tap → clean up → call the client.
