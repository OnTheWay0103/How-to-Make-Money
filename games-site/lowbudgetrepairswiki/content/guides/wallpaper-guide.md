# Low-Budget Repairs Wallpaper Guide — Hang, Scrape & Paint Over

If you've seen the game's trailer, you know the joke: 1990s wallpaper is everywhere, and it is awful. "Why did people in the '90s love wallpaper so much?" the developers themselves ask. In Low-Budget Repairs, wallpaper is a featured task across multiple job types — and like everything else in the game, it has three prices: the proper way, the cheap way, and the way that costs nothing at all. This guide covers all three, and when each one passes inspection. Based on the July 2026 demo; the game launches **August 13, 2026** on Steam.

## The Three-Way Decision

Every wall with wallpaper on it offers the same choice:

**1. Paint over it — cheapest, and correct most of the time.** A diluted can of paint over old wallpaper costs a fraction of stripping and re-hanging, and the [materials guide](/guides/materials-guide) is explicit: *painting over wallpaper is always cheaper.* If the job allows it, this is the answer.

**2. Scrape it — only when the contract demands.** You strip old wallpaper when the job requires new wallpaper, or when the old pattern is so strong it bleeds through paint no matter how many coats you add.

**3. Hang new wallpaper — when the contract says so.** Wallpapering is a listed task in the game, not just a coat of paint. It costs paste, paper, and time — so you do it exactly as well as the inspection demands, and no better.

The decision rule: **if paint can cover it, paint covers it.** Wallpaper work only happens when the contract names it.

## How to Scrape Old Wallpaper (The Chisel Trick)

Stripping wallpaper is the demo's simplest removal job, and it has the game's best tool substitution built in:

- **A chisel replaces a wallpaper scraper for free.** The [tool guide](/guides/tool-guide) lists the chisel as the improvised scraper; the [cost-cutting tips guide](/guides/cost-cutting-tips) confirms "a chisel scrapes wallpaper." No dedicated tool purchase needed.
- A utility knife scores stubborn strips; the flat scraper is the "proper" tool if you already own one — never buy one for a single job.
- **Don't scrape what you don't need.** The contract tells you which walls are required. Behind the radiator, behind furniture, inside closets — wallpaper there stays, exactly like unpainted wall does. The inspection looks from standing height at visible surfaces ([inspection guide](/guides/inspection-guide)), and walls behind furniture are invisible walls.

Scrape cleanly where the new paper goes; scrape "good enough" everywhere else. Bare patches under new wallpaper don't exist if the paper covers them.

## How to Hang New Wallpaper on a Budget

When the contract demands wallpaper, the cheap way still passes:

- **Buy the cheapest roll that covers the required walls** — plus wallpaper paste. The bargain bin occasionally has damaged rolls at steep discounts; damaged edges can be trimmed, and trimmed edges are invisible.
- **Measure once, cut long.** Cutting a strip short means a second strip — and a visible seam. Cutting long means a trimmed edge, which reads as a seam, not a mistake.
- **Paste, hang, smooth.** The game's wallpapering sim is forgiving about bubbles and wrinkles from a distance; it notices *missing* paper on a required wall, not imperfect paper.
- **Match patterns roughly.** The inspector does not verify pattern alignment (see the [painting guide](/guides/painting-guide)'s color-matching section for the same logic on paint). "Looks intentional" beats "actually matched."
- **Hide the seams.** Corners and behind furniture are where strips meet badly — put the ugly seams there, behind the radiator, where the client never looks.

## Painting Over Wallpaper: When It Works

The cheapest wallpaper treatment in the game is also the most common:

- **One diluted coat covers most 90s patterns.** Flat, pastel-ish patterns vanish under a single coat at the standard ~1:5 dilution. The [materials guide](/guides/materials-guide) has the ratios.
- **Strong patterns need strategy.** Big flowers, dark stripes, or textured paper bleed through thin paint. Two options: a second coat, or the "show wall" rule — one quality can for the wall opposite the entrance, diluted cheap stock everywhere else ([materials guide](/guides/materials-guide)).
- **Textured wallpaper paints badly.** If the paper is heavily embossed, scraping may genuinely be cheaper than three failed coats — the paint pools in the texture and every streak shows.
- **Glossy wallpaper doesn't take paint.** If the paper is shiny, paint beads and flakes. Scrape the glossy paper, then paint the bare wall.

Paint-over is the default answer, but it fails visibly on glossy and heavily textured paper — learn to spot those two, and you'll never waste a job's paint budget on a wall that needed scraping anyway.

## Wallpaper in Specific Jobs

- **Flooded bathroom repair** — water-damaged wallpaper peels at the seams; the contract usually calls for repainting the damaged walls, which is a scrape-and-paint or paint-over situation. The full job is in the [bathroom renovation guide](/guides/bathroom-renovation-guide).
- **Living room refreshes** — the most common wallpaper jobs: repaint or re-paper the tired walls. Refresh contracts are easy money ([renovation jobs guide](/guides/renovation-jobs-guide)), and "refresh" almost always accepts paint-over.
- **Kitchens and full makeovers** — wallpaper in multiple rooms means multiple decisions. Batch the decision: scrape the glossy and textured rooms, paint over the rest, buy paper only for the rooms the contract names.

## Wallpaper Mistakes That Cost Profit

1. **Buying a wallpaper scraper** — the chisel does it free.
2. **Stripping walls the contract would accept painted** — removal is hours you're not paid for. Read the job wording first.
3. **Painting over glossy paper** — the paint beads, the wall fails, you redo it with stripping on top of the wasted can.
4. **Short wallpaper strips** — every short cut means a visible seam or a second strip. Cut long.
5. **Scraping every wall in the flat** — behind the radiator and the wardrobe, the old paper stays. Invisible is not required.

## The Wallpaper Cheat Sheet

Read the contract → check the paper (flat and matte = paint over; glossy or textured = scrape) → scrape with the chisel where needed → one diluted coat, two on strong patterns → buy paper only where the contract demands → hang it roughly straight, seams in corners → tidy → call the client.

Wallpaper is the game's reminder that the cheapest fix is often the one that doesn't fix anything at all — it just covers it.
