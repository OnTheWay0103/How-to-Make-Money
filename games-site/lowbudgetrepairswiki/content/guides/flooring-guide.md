# Low-Budget Repairs Flooring Guide — Cheap Fixes for Every Floor

Floors are where beginners overspend and undershoot in equal measure: they tile entire rooms the contract never named, and they panic about floor damage the inspector never checks. The truth is simpler than both habits — floors follow the same rule as walls, with a different set of shortcuts. This guide covers which floors actually need work, how to tile a floor cheaply, and what to do with damaged boards, scratches, and stains when tiling isn't required. Based on the July 2026 demo; the game launches **August 13, 2026** on Steam.

## Do You Actually Need to Touch the Floor?

The first question is never "how do I fix the floor" — it's **"is the floor in the contract?"** From the [renovation jobs guide](/guides/renovation-jobs-guide) and [tiling guide](/guides/tiling-guide), the demo's floor requirements look like this:

| Room | Floor required? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Bathroom | **Usually** | The classic inspection focus — the flooded bathroom job requires floor tiles |
| Kitchen | Sometimes | Paint or linoleum works instead of tiles |
| Living room | Rarely | Usually optional — "refresh" jobs might name a floor covering |
| Bedroom | Almost never | Skip it entirely |

And the inspection check is forgiving: clients look at floors **from standing height** ([inspection guide](/guides/inspection-guide)) — they notice missing tiles and unfilled gaps, but they rarely notice minor grout inconsistencies or a slightly uneven floor, and they never measure. **Tile only what the contract names** — every extra pack of tiles is profit thrown away.

## How to Tile a Floor the Cheap Way

When the contract does require floor tiles, the [tiling guide](/guides/tiling-guide) rules apply fully:

- **No level, no spacers.** The demo's tiling snaps tiles into place with real forgiveness. Crooked rows happen; the inspector doesn't measure angles. "A little unevenness gives the room some character."
- **Mismatched tiles are fine at floor level.** Put mismatches on the floor where grout lines and shadow hide them — a patchwork floor reads as realistic, not shoddy. Size and coverage matter; color does not.
- **Thin tiles over bumps.** A slightly uneven floor tiled with thin tiles looks flat from the doorway. The tile becomes the level.
- **Never replace the subfloor.** Fixing the floor *under* the tiles is expensive, invisible, and uninspected. It is the definition of wasted spend.
- **Grout hides everything else.** Cheap grout mixed roughly fills the mismatched edges and wandering rows — the [tiling guide](/guides/tiling-guide) calls grout the material that makes sloppy tiling invisible. Never skip it on visible rows.

The flooded bathroom job — the game's starter contract — is your practice ground: replace the visibly broken floor tiles, leave the rest, grout over the seams. The step-by-step is in the [bathroom renovation guide](/guides/bathroom-renovation-guide).

## When Paint and Linoleum Beat Tiles

Not every floor job needs tiles:

- **Kitchens often accept paint or linoleum instead.** The contract may name a floor covering, not a tile count. A kitchen floor painted or covered with cheap linoleum passes at a fraction of the tile cost — check the wording before you buy a pack of tiles.
- **"Floor covering" jobs** (like living room refreshes) usually mean the client wants the floor to look handled. A fresh covering, a cleaned surface, or a rug can satisfy the requirement.

The rule of thumb from the [materials guide](/guides/materials-guide): **buy only what the contract lists, and substitute the cheaper material whenever the contract allows.**

## Repairing Damaged Floors Without Replacing Them

For floors that are damaged but not being re-tiled:

- **Ugly scratches → area rug.** The [money-making guide](/guides/money-making-guide) ranks this the game's best floor fix: a rug over a scratched floor costs a fraction of sanding and refinishing, and saves 5x or more versus a proper repair. Inspectors judge the visible result, not the floor underneath.
- **Missing or broken boards → replace only the damaged boards.** A few replacement boards beat redoing the floor, and the client rarely walks the floor looking for board mismatches. (From the [job profit guide](/guides/job-profit-guide): "new floorboards" is a *mandated spend* — if the contract demands real boards, the fee needs to cover them.)
- **Water damage → dry it, then cover or repaint.** The flooded bathroom teaches this: dry the worst areas, scrape loose paint, and handle the visible surface. Floors survive paint-over the same way walls do ([painting guide](/guides/painting-guide)).
- **Skirting boards → refresh, don't replace.** "Refresh skirting" appears in the demo's living room job — a repaint of the skirting reads as fresh, and it's nearly free.

## The Inspection Order: Floor Second, Never First

The [upgrade-priorities guide](/guides/upgrade-priorities-guide) sets the priority: **eye level first, floor second, corners third, hidden areas never.** A streaky wall at eye level fails a job; a slightly uneven floor passes. Spend your care in that order:

1. Visible walls and damage at eye level — the inspection focus.
2. Floor: missing tiles, unfilled gaps, big stains — the things noticed from standing height.
3. Corners and skirting — cosmetic, cheap to refresh.
4. Under furniture, behind appliances — never checked, never touched.

The floor matters most in the **bathroom** — it's the classic inspection focus — and least in bedrooms. Budget your materials in that order, and the cheapest floors get the cheapest treatment.

## Flooring Mistakes That Cost Profit

1. **Tiling rooms the contract never named** — the biggest floor waste in the game. Every extra pack of tiles is pure loss.
2. **Replacing the subfloor** — expensive, invisible, uninspected.
3. **Sanding and refinishing a scratch that a rug covers** — the 5x rule from the money-making guide: always price the cover-up before the repair.
4. **Leveling and spacing tiles** — the inspector doesn't measure angles. You're paying for craftsmanship nobody grades.
5. **Panicking about a slightly uneven floor** — from standing height, it reads as "character."

## The Flooring Cheat Sheet

Read the contract → check which rooms name the floor → bathroom gets the tiles (bought from the bargain bin, mismatched accepted, no level, no spacers) → kitchen accepts paint or linoleum → rugs cover scratches → damaged boards get replaced one-by-one → skirting gets a refresh coat → skip everything below furniture → tidy → call the client.

Floors are the game's lesson in scope discipline: the cheapest floor job is the one that does exactly what the contract names and nothing more.
