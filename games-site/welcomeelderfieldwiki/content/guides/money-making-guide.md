# Welcome to Elderfield How to Make Money

Every farming life-sim has an income loop, and Elderfield's is built on the systems the developer confirms on the store page: **farm, mine, forage, fish, cook**. Exact prices are not published (the game launches September 10, 2026), so this guide ranks the *income sources* by their confirmed reliability rather than by numbers.

## Your Income Sources

### 1. Crops (Steady Base Income)

Farming is the economic engine. Confirmed systems [Official]:

- **Cultivate land and harvest** — ordinary crops (corn, pumpkins) plus unsettling ones (teeth and eyeballs).
- **Crops sell** for money [Community/Editorial].
- **Stop tentacles** — keeping your farm clear keeps crop space productive.

**Advice:** A consistent, repeated crop is more reliable than chasing exotic horror crops early. Volume beats price until you know the market.

### 2. Fishing (High Value, Gated)

Fishing is one of the best **food + income** sources once unlocked [Community]. It is gated behind the fishing-rod quest (lake via the sewer — see the [Fishing Guide](/guides/fishing-guide)), but the payoff is a repeatable, low-cost income stream.

**Advice:** Unlock fishing early and fish as a daily routine. It turns low-energy days into productive ones.

### 3. Mining & Foraging (Material Income)

Mining and foraging are confirmed resource sources [Official]. In the demo, gathering **can trigger fights** (cutting grass, breaking rocks), so material income carries a small combat cost [Community].

**Advice:** Mine/forage in bursts, carrying a basic loadout, and process materials into sellable or craftable goods.

### 4. Livestock (Daily Passive Income)

The ranch's otherworldly livestock produce daily [Official]. Livestock is a classic life-sim income tier: high setup, steady passive return.

**Advice:** Build up livestock once your crop/fishing base is stable; it smooths out daily income.

### 5. Cooking & Crafting (Value-Add)

Cooking and crafting are confirmed systems [Official]. Processing raw ingredients into food/items typically sells for more than the raw materials — and food also supports your rest economy.

**Advice:** Don't sell everything raw. Cook and craft for both income and self-sufficiency.

### 6. The Mall Dungeon (Loot Windfall)

The demo's first dungeon (the local mall) contains loot and combat rewards [Community]. Dungeon runs are a **lumpy but large** income source compared to daily farming.

**Advice:** Clear the mall when you are prepared — the loot spike is worth the combat risk, especially in Cozy Mode.

## An Income Priority Order

For a smooth start, prioritize in this order:

1. **Fishing** — unlock early, fish daily (high value, low cost).
2. **Crops** — consistent volume-based income.
3. **Cooking** — process produce for better margins.
4. **Livestock** — steady passive income once established.
5. **Mining/Foraging** — material income with fight risk.
6. **Mall runs** — occasional loot spikes.

This is a pre-release ranking based on confirmed systems and genre norms; exact profitability will be updated after launch.

## What Money Is For

Money feeds the progression systems [Official/Editorial]:

- **Seeds and farm upgrades** — expand crop capacity.
- **Home & character customization** — a confirmed system [Official].
- **Equipment and combat items** — your turn-based combat kit (see the [Combat Guide](/guides/combat-guide)).
- **Relationship gifts** — friendship systems typically cost money to progress.

## An Early-Game Money Plan (Demo-Tested)

A concrete, low-risk plan you can follow in the demo right now [Editorial]:

1. **Day 1–2: Farm + unlock the rod.** Plant a consistent crop, and make the short sewer trip to grab the fishing rod (use the shop-manhole shortcut to minimize fights).
2. **Day 3–5: Fish daily.** Fishing is your steadiest income. Sell surplus, cook some.
3. **Stockpile food before the mall.** The mall is the demo's dungeon and loot spike — go in with healing.
4. **Clear the mall once.** The loot bump funds the next phase (better gear, seeds, customization).
5. **Reinvest into livestock.** Once your crop/fishing base is stable, livestock adds passive daily income.

This plan works because it starts with the **lowest-risk, highest-return** sources (fishing, crops) and only then takes on the mall's combat risk.

## Selling vs Processing: When It Matters

Not everything should be sold raw [Editorial]:

- **Sell** what has no better use — surplus crops and fish you won't eat.
- **Process** materials into crafted/cooked goods when their market value exceeds the raw materials plus effort.
- **Keep a reserve** of food and combat items — money in the bank is only useful if you survive to spend it.

In a game where rest and combat consume items, a bare-metal "sell everything" strategy leaves you underprepared for the mall and the night. Balance income with self-sufficiency.

## Common Money Mistakes

- **Skipping fishing** — the rod quest is short and fishing is the best early income.
- **Selling everything raw** — cooking/crafting adds value and food supports rest.
- **Over-expanding crops before you can water/harvest them** — volume you can't maintain wastes money.
- **Ignoring tentacles** — they can cost you crop space and income.
