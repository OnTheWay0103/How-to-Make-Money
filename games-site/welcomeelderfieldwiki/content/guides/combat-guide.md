# Welcome to Elderfield Combat Guide

Welcome to Elderfield's combat is **turn-based** and resource-driven. The official Steam description calls it **"in-depth turn based combat"** and frames Challenge Mode around testing your **skills and resource building**. Because the full game launches September 10, 2026, exact skill names, damage values, and enemy stats are [Unconfirmed] — this guide covers the confirmed structure and how to approach it.

## When Do You Fight?

Combat in Elderfield is not always a choice:

- **Dungeon encounters** — the demo's first dungeon is the local mall, which contains real encounters.
- **Ambient triggers** — **cutting grass or breaking rocks can start a fight** [Community].
- **Old God content (Challenge Mode)** — the store description implies the harder mode pushes you into more and tougher combat.

In **Cozy Mode**, fights are lighter and avoidable more often. In **Challenge Mode**, combat is a core pillar.

## The AP System

Combat uses **action points (AP)** that gate what you can do in a turn [Community]. The general shape:

- **Each action costs AP** — attacking, using an item, buffing, or fleeing all consume from the same pool.
- **Resource use matters** — the store description emphasizes "resource building," so your gathered materials are effectively your combat fuel.
- **Strategic buffs** — buffs are a named mechanic; applying them before/while attacking is expected to matter in harder fights.

Exact AP pools, per-action costs, and turn order rules are **not published** — expect them to be verified right after launch [Unconfirmed].

## Equipment & Items

Combat draws on **"diverse equipment"** (store description) and the items you craft and cook:

- **Equipment** — weapons/gear that define your offensive options. The demo shows a small equipment set; the full catalogue is [Unconfirmed].
- **Consumables** — food and crafted items that heal or buff in combat. Your farm and cooking loop directly feeds these.

This is why the farming/economy guide matters: **your combat kit is built from what you grow and gather.**

## Fleeing Is Legitimate

A notable, confirmed design choice: **fleeing is a legitimate option** in combat [Community]. You are never forced to fight a fight you cannot win. Early game, especially in the demo, **fleeing is often the correct call** when:

- Your AP and items are low.
- You are out of healing.
- You triggered an ambient fight while gathering and are unprepared.

In Challenge Mode, fleeing may cost you rewards or progress [Unconfirmed] — weigh the tradeoff.

## Combat Tips for the Demo

- **Bring supplies** to the mall. The demo dungeon is a real difficulty spike if you arrive empty-handed.
- **Learn the AP rhythm** on ambient fights (grass/rocks) before tackling the mall.
- **Use buffs early** rather than hoarding them — fights are short and resource-driven.
- **Flee from anything you cannot handle** — there is no shame in retreat in a cozy-horror game.

## Combat by Mode

**Cozy Mode:**
- Combat is optional-ish and light. Use it to learn the AP system safely.
- Prioritize farming/fishing income over combat optimization.

**Challenge Mode:**
- Prepare for every encounter: stock items, keep equipment upgraded, use buffs.
- Expect fights to be the main progression gate — "the Old Gods are waiting."

## Building a Combat Kit (Pre-Release)

Because combat draws on "diverse equipment" and gathered resources, your **farm and craft loop is your loadout**:

- **Food** — healing and recovery in and out of combat. Stock more than you think you need before the mall.
- **Buff items** — "strategic buffs" are a named mechanic; crafting buff consumables is expected to matter in harder fights [Unconfirmed].
- **Equipment** — the demo shows a small gear set; upgrade and diversify as the town's areas open up.
- **Flee supplies** — since fleeing is legitimate, keep a movement/escape option in your pocket for bad fights.

A simple rule: **never enter a dungeon without at least one full heal and one buff.** The mall punishes unprepared runs.

## Reading a Fight (When to Stay vs Flee)

Pre-release decision framework based on the confirmed systems [Editorial]:

**Stay and fight when:**
- You have full AP, items, and health.
- The enemy is in a dungeon (you came prepared to fight).
- You are on a resource run and the fight is easy.

**Flee when:**
- Your AP or items are low.
- You triggered an ambient fight while gathering and are not ready.
- The enemy type looks like a difficulty spike (first time you see a new horror, it usually is).

The cozy-horror tone rewards **avoidance as a strategy** — dying less is a real advantage in a game where rest itself carries costs.
