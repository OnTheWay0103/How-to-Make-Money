# How Cooking Works in Welcome to Elderfield

Cooking in **Welcome to Elderfield** sits at the intersection of three systems: combat preparation, relationship building, and income. The store page lists it alongside fishing and marriage as a core life-sim activity, and the game's turn-based combat expects you to carry food and crafted supplies into fights.

There is also a second layer to the answer. The developer has announced a **Cooking profession** as part of a larger professions rework, and it will change how food quality and food pricing work. That update is **not live yet**. This guide separates what cooking does today from what has been announced, so you can plan a farm without confusing the two.

## Cooking in the Live Game

Welcome to Elderfield launched on September 10, 2026. The live build is **v1.0.2a** according to community documentation [Community]. In that build, cooking is a crafting activity: you take crops, fish, forage, and animal products, turn them into meals, and use or sell the results.

The game does not treat cooking as a standalone minigame. It is a processing step in the same loop as farming and fishing:

1. **Grow or gather** the raw ingredient.
2. **Process it** at the appropriate station.
3. **Cook it** into a meal.
4. **Use it** for combat recovery, rest support, gifts, or sale.

The [Farming & Economy Guide](/guides/farming-economy-guide) covers the production side; this guide focuses on the cooking step itself.

## How to Unlock Cooking

Cooking is gated behind the game's early farming questline. According to community documentation [Community]:

1. Complete **Farmer Hans's "The Farmer's Path"** quest.
2. Deliver **Wheat** from your first planting.
3. The reward unlocks the **Grinder** and access to **Chef Elroy's recipe shop**.

The Grinder is the processing station that turns crops into usable cooking ingredients. Chef Elroy's shop is the first reliable recipe source. Until you finish "The Farmer's Path," you can gather ingredients but you cannot turn them into the full range of meals.

This is an early-game gate, not a late-game unlock. If you are following the [Beginner Guide](/guides/beginner-guide), finishing Hans's first task should be one of your first three priorities.

## Where Recipes Come From

Recipes are not all sold in one shop. The community wiki documents several sources [Community]:

| Source | What It Provides |
|--------|------------------|
| **Chef Elroy's shop** | The core recipe list, unlocked after "The Farmer's Path" |
| **Herb's "Casting your Line"** | Fishing-related recipes, including Tempura, Baked Fish Filet, and Light Salad; a second stage adds Fog Broth |
| **Chef Elroy after "The Skull Chalice"** | Pumpkin Pie and Pumpkin Muffin |
| **Gretta's "Cursed Cuisine"** | Stew of Six Curses |
| **Fishing treasure** | A chance at the Void Dinner recipe |
| **Starter recipes** | Hotdog, Bread, Vegetable Soup, and Bitter Gel |
| **The Saloon** | Sells some beginner recipes |
| **Keg / artisan processing** | Aged wine and cheese rather than meals, but part of the same food economy |

Treat this as a map of where to look, not a complete recipe database. The community wiki is player-maintained, and some entries are marked as uncertain on the source page itself [Community]. If a recipe is not appearing, check quest completion first — several are quest rewards rather than shop stock.

## What Food Does

Food serves four jobs in the current build [Official][Community]:

### 1. Restores HP and MP

Meals are consumables. The announced cooking changes explicitly refer to food restoring HP and MP, which confirms that the current food system is built around recovery [Official]. In practice, this makes cooked food a combat resource, not just a roleplay item.

### 2. Supports buffs

The store page describes combat with "strategic buffs and diverse equipment," and community guides report that cooked meals grant combat effects such as max HP, defence, and resistance bonuses [Community]. The exact buff values are not published in an official table, so treat any specific number you see in a player guide as community data rather than a guarantee.

### 3. Builds relationships

Dishes are some of the most efficient gifts in the game. Community documentation lists loved and hated dishes for individual townsfolk — for example, Harvest Stew is loved by Tanner and Josh but hated by Sam, while Void Dinner is loved by Sam [Community]. The [NPCs & Romance Guide](/guides/npcs-romance-guide) covers the relationship side in more detail.

The practical takeaway: check the recipient's preferences before gifting. A loved meal is a large friendship swing; a hated meal wastes the ingredients and the action.

### 4. Sells for money

Cooked food can be sold, but the current economy is not a simple "grow crop, cook, profit" loop. The announced pricing change explains why.

## Selling Food: The Current Rule and the Announced Change

The developer's October 19 post includes a specific economy change for the upcoming update [Official]:

> Food will always sell for the same price as the total ingredients required to craft it.

That sentence matters more than it looks. It means cooking will **not** multiply the sale value of raw ingredients by itself. If a dish uses ingredients worth 100 gold, the dish sells for 100 gold. The profit has to come from somewhere else: perks, quality, or the fact that cooking lets you use ingredients you would not otherwise sell.

The same post says cooking perks can add further value [Official]. So the intended progression is:

- **Base cooking:** converts ingredients into food at ingredient-cost parity.
- **Perk investment:** pushes the sale value above the ingredient cost.
- **High-quality food:** restores more HP/MP and sells for more.

Until the update ships, the current build's pricing may differ. Do not rebuild your entire farm around the announced rule yet [Editorial]. The safe play is to cook for use and gifts first, and treat mass production for sale as a lower-margin strategy.

## The Coming Cooking Profession

The Cooking profession is part of a larger **Levels-to-Professions rework** announced in the October 15 developer post [Official]. The rework replaces the old leveling structure with skill-tree-style choices and adds **more than 130 individual profession perks**. A new **Combat profession** is also planned to address difficulty and combat feedback.

For cooking specifically, the October 19 post confirms [Official]:

- Higher cooking levels improve the **chance of high-quality food**.
- High-quality food **restores more HP and MP**.
- High-quality food **sells for more**.
- Food pricing is being standardised to ingredient cost, with perks providing the upside.

The developer has not announced a release date for this update. A French-language roadmap report dated October 11, 2026 describes the same package — combat rebalancing, a skill-level revamp, a new cooking skill, official mod support, and quality-of-life changes — and also gives no date [Community/Media].

In other words: the Cooking profession is **announced, not shipped**. Do not expect the quality tiers or the ingredient-cost pricing rule to be active today.

## What to Do Now

[Editorial] A practical plan for the current build:

1. **Finish "The Farmer's Path" early.** The Grinder and Chef Elroy's shop are the foundation of the whole cooking loop.
2. **Cook for combat, not just for sale.** Food is recovery and buffs. Carrying meals into the mall or the Catacombs is worth more than squeezing a few gold out of a dish.
3. **Keep a gift shelf.** Store a few loved dishes for the townsfolk you are befriending. Check preferences before gifting.
4. **Do not mass-produce for profit yet.** The announced ingredient-cost pricing rule may remove the margin you are relying on. Wait for the rework before building a cooking business.
5. **Stock ingredients now.** Crops, fish, and forage that you do not need immediately can wait for the Cooking profession's quality system.
6. **Do not skip fishing.** The [Fishing Guide](/guides/fishing-guide) explains the rod route; fish are a major cooking input and several recipes come from fishing quests.

## What Is Still Unconfirmed

As of October 23, 2026, the following are **not confirmed** [Unconfirmed]:

- The release date of the Cooking profession and the wider professions rework.
- The exact quality tiers and their HP/MP or sale multipliers.
- The full perk list and how many points a player can earn.
- Whether current recipes, food items, or saved ingredients will convert cleanly into the new system.
- Whether the ingredient-cost pricing rule applies to all dishes or only to the new profession's output.

The live cooking loop — unlock via Farmer Hans, recipes from quests and Chef Elroy, food as recovery and gifts — is confirmed by community play and the store page. The profession layer is a announced roadmap item, not a current mechanic.

## FAQ

**Do I need to cook to progress?**
It is not the only progression path, but food supports combat recovery, rest, and relationships. Skipping it makes the combat and social loops harder, not impossible [Editorial].

**Where is Chef Elroy?**
Community documentation places his recipe shop in town and unlocks it through Farmer Hans's "The Farmer's Path" quest. Follow the early farming questline and the shop becomes available [Community].

**Can I make money by cooking?**
In the current build, yes, but the margin is not guaranteed. The announced rule sets food's sale price to the total ingredient cost, with perks providing the upside [Official]. Treat cooking as a use-and-gift system first.

**What is the best food for combat?**
Community guides favour dishes with HP and MP recovery plus defensive buffs. Exact values are community-reported [Community]. Carry a mix rather than relying on one dish.

**Will my recipes carry over to the Cooking profession update?**
Not confirmed [Unconfirmed]. The rework changes the leveling structure, so some recipe or quality behavior may change.

**Is cooking available in the demo?**
The demo includes a cooking/crafting loop, but the full recipe list and the profession system are not part of the demo [Community]. See the [Demo Guide](/guides/demo-guide) for what the vertical slice includes.
