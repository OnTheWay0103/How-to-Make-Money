**In Silver Pines your melee weapon is a consumable.** It degrades as you use it, and you restore it by applying **tape** — through an input sequence you perform in real time, with whatever is chasing you still chasing you.

Melee is the weapon class you can always fall back on, because firearms run out and tape does not. That makes understanding durability the difference between a manageable run and one that collapses the moment a fight goes long.

> **Sourcing note.** This system is **not** described on the Steam store page. It is **Community-reported** — consistent across demo players and preview coverage, and one of the most commonly discussed mechanics in the demo. Where the exact numbers are unknown we say so rather than estimating.

## How Durability Works

The system, as demo players and preview coverage describe it:

1. **Melee weapons have finite durability.** Every swing consumes some of it
2. **When durability runs out, the weapon is no longer usable** until repaired
3. **Repair is done with tape** — a consumable you carry and buy from vending machines
4. **Repair is a manual sequence, not a button press.** Preview coverage describes needing to *"add tape to a knife to repair it or increase its durability"* through explicit input, in the same family as the reload minigame
5. **The repair happens in real time.** The game does not pause while you do it

That fourth point is the design decision that generates the most discussion. A repair you perform under pressure is a different thing from a repair you trigger from a menu — it converts "my weapon is broken" from a resource problem into a moment-to-moment risk.

### What players consistently complain about

The most repeated friction point in demo feedback is **repair timing**: players describe being unable to finish repairing a melee weapon before enemies reach them. Note that this is a complaint about difficulty, not a bug report — the timing is clearly intentional, and it is exactly the kind of friction a survival horror game chooses on purpose.

If it turns out not to be for you, the demo is free — the [demo guide](/guides/silver-pines-demo-guide) covers what else it contains.

## Repair Discipline: Five Habits

These are conclusions drawn from the systems above and from demo player experience, not official instructions.

### 1. Repair early, not when you need to

Repairing at full durability wastes tape; repairing at zero durability in a fight loses you the fight. The practical answer is to repair **before you enter an area you have not scouted** rather than reacting to a broken weapon. You are far more likely to have a quiet ten seconds before a fight than during one.

### 2. Repair with your back to safety

Since the repair does not pause the game, do it where you can see the approach. Preview coverage is unanimous on this point: do not start a repair or a reload with an enemy nearby.

### 3. Never travel without tape

Tape is the item that turns your melee weapon back on. It is bought from vending machines with the same coins that pay for your saves — see the [coin economy guide](/guides/silver-pines-coins-vending-machines-economy) — which means running out of tape is usually a budgeting failure from several minutes earlier, not a bad-luck moment.

### 4. Treat a broken weapon as an emergency

If your melee weapon is broken and you have no tape, you have lost your reliable damage source. Ammunition is scarce and reloading is slow. At that point the correct play is usually to **disengage and go back**, not to push on.

### 5. Do not repair mid-combo

Attacks have noticeable cooldown delays [Community-reported], so there is already a rhythm to melee. Interrupting it to repair is how you take the hit you were avoiding.

## Melee, Stamina and the Rest of the Combat Loop

Melee does not exist in isolation, and the combat guides on this site split it deliberately:

- **[Stamina, Dodging and Mementos](/guides/silver-pines-stamina-dodge-mementos-guide)** — swinging a melee weapon costs stamina, as does dodging, and running costs nothing. An empty bar means no dodge, which means damage
- **[Guns, Ammo and Reloading](/guides/silver-pines-guns-ammo-reloading-guide)** — the ranged alternative, with its own slow manual reload and the same real-time exposure problem
- **[The save system](/guides/silver-pines-save-system-phone-booths)** — why fighting is optional more often than it looks, and why disengaging is a legitimate strategy

The short version of how they fit together: **melee costs durability and stamina; firearms cost ammunition and time; both cost exposure.** There is no free attack in Silver Pines.

## Should You Fight at All?

The official store description frames the game as *"fight or avoid the horrors of the town"* — avoiding is presented as a first-class option, not a failure state. Given that melee degrades, stamina is short and the game does not pause, **avoidance is frequently the correct resource decision**.

The counter-argument is also real: preview coverage notes that **leaving enemies alive makes returning to an area harder**, because the area is still dangerous when you come back for the thing you missed. In a metroidvania you will come back.

The workable compromise: **avoid enemies on routes you plan to run, and clear enemies on routes you plan to loot.** Fighting everything is unsustainable; fighting nothing makes the map progressively worse.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do weapons break in Silver Pines?**
Melee weapons degrade with use and stop working until repaired [Community-reported].

**How do you repair a weapon?**
With tape, through a manual input sequence performed in real time [Community-reported].

**Is there a quick repair button?**
No. Preview coverage describes an explicit input sequence rather than a single press [Community-reported].

**Can you repair during a fight?**
You can try, but the game does not pause [Community-reported], and players consistently report that repairing under pressure is where it goes wrong.

**How much durability does a weapon have?**
Never published [Unconfirmed]. No official source gives any durability figure.

**What weapons are in Silver Pines?**
Demo players report a cutter (utility knife) plus a pistol and a shotgun [Community-reported]. There is no official weapon list [Unconfirmed].

**Is there a better melee weapon than the cutter?**
No official weapon progression has been published, and no verified source describes a melee upgrade path [Unconfirmed].

**Should I use melee or guns?**
Melee costs durability and stamina; firearms cost scarce ammunition and a slow reload. Use firearms when a fight is genuinely worth winning from range, and melee when it is not worth the bullets.
