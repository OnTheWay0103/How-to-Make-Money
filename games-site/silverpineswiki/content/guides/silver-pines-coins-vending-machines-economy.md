**Silver Pines runs on one currency, and it has two uses that are permanently in competition.** Scavenged change buys supplies from vending machines, and the same change pays for your saves at phone booths. There is no second currency, no crafting-and-selling loop and no way to earn coins by fighting — the money you find is the money you have.

> **Sourcing note.** The demo has been live since June 1, 2026, so the economy is one of the better-documented systems in the game — but it is documented by **players**, not by the developer. The Steam store page describes scarce resources and a limited inventory; it does not describe the coin system. Everything below is **Community-reported** unless it says otherwise, and no coin price has ever been officially published **[Unconfirmed]**.

---

## Where Coins Come From

Coins are a **physical pickup**. You find them in the environment rather than earning them from a vendor or a quest. That has practical consequences:

- **Income is finite and front-loaded.** There is no repeatable source, so every coin spent is a coin that will not be replaced
- **Searching is the income strategy.** The official store description makes this explicit: *"Search everywhere for items because every bullet, bottle of medicine and upgrade matters."* That sentence is about the whole resource system, and coins are part of it
- **Skipping rooms costs money.** In a game where you are often better off avoiding a fight, the temptation is to avoid the room as well. The economy punishes that

⚠️ **Whether coins respawn or can be re-farmed is not documented** — no official source and no consistent player report **[Unconfirmed]**. Budget as though your income is finite, because that is the safe assumption either way.

## Where Coins Go: Two Demands, One Purse

| Sink | What you get | Why it matters |
|------|--------------|----------------|
| **Phone booths** | A save. Progress is banked; death no longer costs you this stretch | Protects everything you have already done |
| **Vending machines** | Supplies — reported to include healing items, tape and ammunition | Keeps you alive long enough to do anything else |

The two sinks pull in opposite directions in a specific way: **buying supplies reduces your ability to protect progress, and protecting progress reduces your ability to buy supplies.** Neither choice is wrong. What matters is that you make it on purpose.

### What's actually in a vending machine

The store page describes scavenging for resources, and demo players consistently report that **vending machines sell supplies in exchange for change** [Community-reported]. Across preview coverage and player reports, the items named are:

| Supply | Reported use | Confidence |
|--------|--------------|:--:|
| Healing items | Restore health | Community-reported |
| Tape | Repairs melee weapon durability | Community-reported |
| Ammunition | Firearm rounds | Community-reported |

Exact **prices, stock levels and machine locations have never been published** by any official source **[Unconfirmed]**. Any list claiming to show "all vending machine prices in Silver Pines" is inventing them.

## How to Decide: A Spending Framework

No official figures exist, so this is a decision framework rather than a table of values. Treat every number in it as **[Unconfirmed]**.

### The order that generally wins

1. **Tape, if your melee weapon is close to breaking.** A weapon that has failed is not a weapon, and melee is the resource you can always rely on
2. **Whatever you need to survive the next specific obstacle**, if you know what it is. Buying a shotgun shell for an enemy you have not met is guessing
3. **The save**, before you attempt anything you have not done before
4. **Everything else.** Stockpiling is a luxury for players who are not one bad encounter from a rollback

### The trap to avoid

The most common way to ruin a Silver Pines run is to **treat vending machines as shops and phone booths as an afterthought**. It is intuitive — a machine has a menu, a phone booth is a thing you walk past — and it is exactly backwards. A save protects everything you have; a healing item protects only the next few minutes.

The second most common mistake is the mirror image: **hoarding coins so aggressively that you arrive at every fight under-equipped.** Coins in your pocket with no healing items and no tape are not savings, they are dead weight.

### Practical rules of thumb

- **Never spend down to zero** unless the save is the thing you are buying
- **Check a booth before you check a machine**, so you know what a save costs before you commit the money elsewhere
- **Carry tape, not a tape stockpile.** Inventory space is limited; the [inventory guide](/guides/silver-pines-inventory-storage-guide) covers the swap system
- **When in doubt, save.** Progress is the only resource in the game that cannot be re-found

## Coins and the Save System

Because saving is a coin sink, the whole economy is downstream of the [save system](/guides/silver-pines-save-system-phone-booths). Two consequences are worth spelling out here:

**Distance from your last booth is an unbanked debt.** The coins you spent on supplies are gone, but the ground you covered since your last save is at risk. Pushing further without saving increases the amount you could lose.

**The "one more room" instinct is expensive.** In a game with autosave, one more room is free to attempt. Here it carries the whole stretch behind it.

## Is the Economy Too Tight? — What Players Actually Say

The demo holds **802 reviews at 95.9% positive** as of September 16, 2026 — demo-only data, not a score for the full game. The released full game has its own, separate review summary (1,735 reviews at 97% positive as of October 24, 2026), though Steam does not break those reviews down by topic. Taken together, the overwhelming majority of players are not describing the economy as unfair. The criticisms that do exist are worth knowing before you buy:

- **Pacing complaints rather than balance complaints.** The recurring theme in negative coverage is that early melee combat feels slow and heavy, and that repair and reload sequences take long enough to be dangerous
- **Repair timing frustration.** Multiple players describe being unable to finish repairing a melee weapon before enemies reach them — a friction point that is clearly deliberate, and clearly divisive
- **Preview coverage has been mixed on the opening.** PC Gamer described the first hour as derivative and early melee as slow, while other outlets praised the navigation and puzzle design. Both reactions are about feel rather than economy
- **No one is complaining about the coin system being opaque**, which suggests the design communicates itself well in play

If you want to judge for yourself, the demo is free — see the [demo guide](/guides/silver-pines-demo-guide).

No selling loop has been reported by demo players, and none is described on the store page [Unconfirmed].

**Is there more than one currency?**
No second currency has been reported. Coins are the only money in the game [Community-reported].
