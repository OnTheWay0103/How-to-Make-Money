**In Silver Pines, firing a gun is a decision you make before you do it — and reloading is a decision you make in front of whatever you were shooting.**

Reloading is manual, it is round-by-round, and it happens in real time. The game does not pause. Preview coverage is unusually consistent on this point: reloading leaves you exposed, it takes longer than players expect the first time, and doing it with an enemy nearby is how runs end.

> **Sourcing note.** The Steam store page confirms only that ammunition and resources are scarce — *"every bullet, bottle of medicine and upgrade matters."* The reload mechanic itself is **Community-reported** from demo players and preview coverage. No official source has published weapon names, ammunition counts or reload timings **[Unconfirmed]**.

## Why the Reload Design Changes How You Fight

A conventional shooter treats reloading as a short pause in your damage output. Silver Pines treats it as **a vulnerability window you chose to open**.

Three consequences follow:

### You reload before a fight, not during one

The correct time to top up is when nothing is trying to reach you. Players who reload reactively — after the magazine runs dry mid-encounter — are the ones who describe dying to reload animations.

### Running dry is a plan, not an accident

Because reloading is slow, the last few rounds in a gun are worth less than the first few, in a very literal sense: firing them buys you time you then have to spend reloading. If you know a fight is going to need more than half a magazine, the moment to reload is *before* you start.

### Ammunition scarcity makes every shot a resource decision

Steam's store description lists bullets **first** among the things that matter. Ammunition is not a pickup you accumulate comfortably; it is a purchase, made with the same coins that fund your saves and your tape. See the [coin economy guide](/guides/silver-pines-coins-vending-machines-economy) for how that competition works.

## When Is a Bullet Worth Spending?

No official numbers exist, so this is reasoning rather than a damage table. Every numeric value in it is **[Unconfirmed]**.

**Spend ammunition when:**

- **The alternative is losing health you cannot easily recover.** Healing is also bought with coins; a bullet and a healing item cost from the same purse, and the bullet is usually cheaper than the damage
- **The enemy is one you cannot safely melee** — long reach, fast, or one that punishes the cooldown delays in melee attacks
- **You are protecting unbanked progress.** If you are far from your last phone booth, a bullet that ends a fight is worth more than a bullet saved
- **It ends the fight.** Chip damage across a long melee exchange usually costs more durability and stamina than it saves

**Save ammunition when:**

- **The fight is optional and you can leave.** The official description frames the game as *"fight or avoid"* — avoiding is a supported answer
- **You are on a route you will run again.** Clearing a corridor you only pass through is often not worth the rounds
- **You have not yet decided what the next obstacle is.** Buying shells for a problem you have not met is guessing

## The Exposure Problem: Guns, Menus and Maps

Reloading is not the only thing that costs you time. Silver Pines **does not pause** for anything [Community-reported]:

- The **inventory** runs in real time — reorganising mid-fight gets you hit
- The **map** runs in real time
- The **reload** runs in real time
- Drinking coffee, which players report restores stamina, runs in real time too

That last one is worth underlining because it is a trap: the thing you consume to gain stamina leaves you stationary while you consume it. The same source warns explicitly against doing it with an enemy nearby.

**The general rule:** every action in Silver Pines that takes time is an action your enemies get to use. Plan menu time the way you plan ammunition.

## Melee or Firearms? A Working Division of Labour

| Situation | Better answer |
|-----------|---------------|
| A single weak enemy, nothing else around | **Melee** — saves ammunition, costs durability and tape |
| Something fast or long-reaching | **Firearms** — melee's cooldown delays punish you |
| A corridor you plan to run past | **Neither** — avoid it, per the store page's own framing |
| Protecting a long stretch of unbanked progress | **Firearms** — a saved bullet is worth less than a lost hour |
| You have no tape left | **Firearms** — a broken melee weapon is not an option |
| You are out of ammunition and tape | **Leave.** This is the state where runs end |

The two weapon classes share one property: **neither is free.** Melee costs durability and stamina; firearms cost scarce ammunition and a slow, exposed reload. Silver Pines does not offer a cheap way to win a fight — that is the design.

## Practical Reload Discipline

1. **Top up before you open a door or enter a new area**, not after something appears
2. **Reload with your back to a wall you have already cleared**, where you can see the approach
3. **Do not reload to full if you need to move.** Partial reloads are a legitimate choice when the alternative is standing still
4. **Pair ammunition purchases with tape purchases.** Arriving at a fight with full shells and a broken melee weapon is only half-equipped
5. **Watch for Mementos.** Preview coverage reports they can improve reload speed [Community-reported] — the full Memento list is **[Unconfirmed]**
