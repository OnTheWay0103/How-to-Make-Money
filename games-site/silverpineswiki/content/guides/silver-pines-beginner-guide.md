**Silver Pines is not a hard game in the way an action game is hard. It is a game that punishes habits you brought from other games.**

Almost everything that kills a run comes from one of four reflexes: reloading when you should have reloaded earlier, spending coins as if they were money, treating menus as safe, and treating fights as mandatory. None of those reflexes is a mistake in a normal game. All of them are mistakes here.

What follows is twelve rules. They are built from the official Steam store description where the store page states a design intent, and from demo player experience and preview coverage where the detail is community-reported — we mark which is which.

---

## The Rules

### 1. Bank your progress before you push, not after

There is **no autosave** in Silver Pines, and saving happens only at coin-operated phone booths [Community-reported]. Dying rolls you back to your last save, and everything since — resources, ground covered, puzzles solved — is gone.

The rule: **if you have been exploring for a while, you are carrying a debt. Settle it.** Save before a boss, before a new area, before anything you have not done before.

*Why this is rule one:* every other mistake in this game costs you a few resources. This one costs you the last hour.

### 2. Treat coins as two currencies that happen to be the same coins

The same change pays for your saves **and** buys supplies from vending machines [Community-reported]. That means a vending machine is never just a shop — it is a decision about how much unbanked progress you are willing to hold.

**Never spend down to zero** unless the save is what you are buying.

### 3. Search the rooms, even when the route is dangerous

The official store description is blunt about it: *"Search everywhere for items because every bullet, bottle of medicine and upgrade matters."*

That is a design instruction. Skipping a room to avoid a fight costs you supplies, and supplies are the thing the game is about. The compromise is in rule four.

### 4. Avoid enemies on routes you will run; clear enemies on routes you will loot

The store page frames the game as *"fight or avoid the horrors of the town"* — avoiding is a supported answer, not a failure.

But preview coverage adds the other half: **leaving enemies alive makes the area harder when you come back**, and in a metroidvania you always come back. So: keep the corridors you pass through repeatedly quiet, and clear the ones you intend to search.

### 5. Reload before the fight, never during it

Reloading is **manual, round-by-round and happens in real time** [Community-reported]. It is a vulnerability window you open deliberately, not a short pause in your damage output.

Top up when nothing is trying to reach you. Full breakdown in the [guns and reloading guide](/guides/silver-pines-guns-ammo-reloading-guide).

### 6. Carry tape, and repair before you need to

Melee weapons in Silver Pines **degrade with use** and are restored with **tape** through a manual input sequence [Community-reported]. A broken melee weapon means your reliable damage source is gone, and ammunition is scarce by design.

Tape is bought with the same coins that fund your saves. Running out of tape is usually a budgeting failure from several minutes earlier. See the [melee and durability guide](/guides/silver-pines-melee-weapons-durability-repair).

### 7. Never empty your stamina bar on offence

Stamina in Silver Pines is spent on **dodging and melee swings** — and running does not drain it at all [Community-reported]. When the bar is empty you cannot dodge, and a character who cannot dodge takes damage.

**Keep one dodge in reserve at all times.** Fight in bursts: attack, break off, recover, return. Details in the [stamina guide](/guides/silver-pines-stamina-dodge-mementos-guide).

### 8. Remember that the game never pauses

The inventory does not pause it. The map does not pause it. Reloading does not pause it. Drinking coffee does not pause it [Community-reported].

Every action that takes time is an action your enemies get to use. Read your map in cleared corridors. Open your inventory behind a closed door, not in the middle of a room.

### 9. Disengage early, not late

Because running is free, leaving a fight always costs you nothing but position. The failure mode is staying one exchange too long — the third hit you were sure you would land is the one that empties your stamina and ends the run.

If a fight is going badly, **leave**. The store page endorses it.

### 10. Treat a locked door as a note, not a wall

Silver Pines is a metroidvania: the town is *"filled with locked doors and hidden paths"*, and *"with better tools and weapons more and more of the town becomes accessible"* (official store description).

**Mark what you cannot resolve instead of trying to solve it.** The game gives you a means of marking puzzles and locations on the map [Community-reported]. Use it every time. Getting stuck is almost always "you do not have the item yet", not "you are missing the trick".

### 11. Buy the upgrade, not the stockpile

The store description names bullets, medicine and **upgrades** as the things that each matter. An upgrade you own is permanent; a stockpile is consumable and occupies inventory space you do not have.

When you are choosing between one more healing item and something permanent, the permanent thing usually wins.

### 12. Your inventory is a plan, not a bag

Inventory space is limited — that is on the store page, not in a player report — and key areas contain **storage containers** where you can swap items [Community-reported].

Before a trip, decide what you are likely to meet and carry for that. Take the key for the door you are heading to, tape for the fights you will take, and ammunition for the weapon you intend to fire. Everything else can wait in a container. See the [inventory guide](/guides/silver-pines-inventory-storage-guide).

## The First Hour, Roughly

The demo covers a prologue slice that runs about an hour and a half [Community-reported], and its shape is a reasonable preview of the opening of the full game:

1. **The setup** — Red Walker arrives looking for Eddie Velvet, a musician who has vanished
2. **Arrival and the tutorial beats** — movement, interaction, inventory, and the save system
3. **A quieter exploration stretch** — the calm before the storm, and where you should be searching rooms and banking progress
4. **A motel sequence with monsters** — where the combat and resource loop actually bites
5. **A boss encounter** — the demo's climax, and the moment rule four matters most

If you want to know what the demo contains before you spend an evening on it, the [demo guide](/guides/silver-pines-demo-guide) covers it.

## What Not to Worry About

A few things people ask about that are not worth your attention yet:

- **Weapon names, damage numbers and enemy statistics** — none have been published by any official source **[Unconfirmed]**. Do not plan around a spreadsheet that does not exist
- **A tier list of weapons or Mementos** — the game is not out, and the vendor has published no arsenal and no Memento list **[Unconfirmed]**. Our [Survival Priorities](/tier-list) page ranks the resource habits instead, and says so
- **The best ending** — all endings are reachable on a single save file, and they reward thorough exploration rather than a specific choice (Game8 developer interview). You cannot lock yourself out by playing naturally

## Quick Reference

| Situation | Do this |
|-----------|---------|
| Just found a phone booth | Save, if you can afford it |
| Just found a vending machine | Ask what the save costs first |
| About to enter a new area | Reload, repair, save |
| Something is chasing you | Run — it is free |
| Stamina is low | Disengage; do not swing |
| A door will not open | Mark it and leave |
| Weapon is nearly broken | Repair now, not in a fight |
| Carrying an unidentified key | Take it to a container, or find its door |
| Progress feels long unbanked | Go back and save |
