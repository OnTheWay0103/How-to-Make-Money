**The official Silver Pines store description says it in one line: "Inventory space is limited. Search everywhere for items because every bullet, bottle of medicine and upgrade matters."**

That is not marketing copy — it is a design statement, and it is the sentence that tells you what kind of game this is. Silver Pines is built on the classic survival horror premise that you can never carry everything you find, so every trip out of a safe area involves leaving something behind on purpose.

---

## The Two Storage Layers

Silver Pines separates what you **own** from what you **carry**, which is the single most important structural fact about its inventory.

| Layer | What it is | Confidence |
|-------|-----------|:--:|
| **Personal inventory** | A small grid you carry at all times. Compact items take one square; bulkier items take more | Confirmed (store page) / Community-reported (grid detail) |
| **Storage containers** | Fixed containers in key areas that hold items outside your personal grid, and let you swap your loadout | Community-reported |

The official description confirms the first layer directly — "inventory space is limited" is on the store page. The storage container layer comes from preview coverage, which describes *"storage containers in key areas, so you can swap items"* **[Community-reported]**.

### Why the distinction changes how you play

Once you internalise that you own more than you can carry, the game stops being a scavenger hunt and becomes a **loadout problem**:

- Finding an item is not the same as having it. It goes into storage until you decide to take it
- A long trip becomes a plan: what am I likely to meet, and what do I need for that specifically
- Backtracking to a container is a real cost — it is travel time through dangerous ground — so the plan you leave with matters

⚠️ **How many storage containers exist, where they are, and what they hold is not documented** by any official source **[Unconfirmed]**. We do not publish a container location list, because no verified list exists.

## What Actually Takes Up Space

No official source has published slot counts or item sizes, so the following describes **categories**, not numbers. Every specific figure in this section is **[Unconfirmed]**.

**Consumables you will always be carrying something of:**

| Category | Why it competes for space |
|----------|--------------------------|
| Healing items | The thing that converts a bad encounter into a survivable one |
| Tape | Restores melee weapon durability; without it your melee weapon is a countdown |
| Ammunition | Firearm rounds; scarce by design and the most coin-hungry purchase |
| Keys and cards | Puzzle items that gate progress; they occupy space while carrying no combat value |
| Mementos | Ability buffs [Community-reported]; unclear whether they occupy inventory space or sit outside it **[Unconfirmed]** |

That last row is a genuine open question. Mementos are described by players as collectible buffs rather than puzzle keys — the same preview coverage that describes them notes they are *"not used for puzzles"* — but whether they consume grid space is not documented **[Unconfirmed]**.

## The Loadout Rules That Follow

These are not official instructions; they are the conclusions that follow from the systems above plus demo player experience. Treat them as guidance, not gospel.

### Carry tape, not a tape collection

A melee weapon with no tape is a melee weapon with a timer on it. But a stack of tape you never use is space you needed for ammunition. One repair's worth, topped up at a container, is usually the right shape.

### Take the key you need, not the keys you have

Puzzle items are dead weight in combat. If you know where a key goes, take it on the trip where it gets used. If you do not, it belongs in a container until you find out — unless the walk back is more dangerous than carrying it.

### Match your ammunition to your plan

Firearm ammunition is scarce by design — the store page names bullets first in its list of things that matter. Carrying shells for a weapon you are not planning to fire this trip is a wasted slot.

### Leave room to pick things up

An inventory that arrives full leaves you nothing to pick up. In a game whose store description tells you to *"search everywhere"*, the worst state to be in is "found something, cannot take it, do not want to walk back". Leave slack.

### Do not open the inventory in a dangerous room

This is an inventory rule with combat consequences: **Silver Pines does not pause.** The map, the menu and the inventory all run in real time [Community-reported], so reorganising your bag mid-encounter is a way to get hit.

## Can You Upgrade Your Inventory?

Some form of capacity increase is **widely reported by demo players** — it is one of the most-searched questions about the game — but no official source has ever named the item, described the mechanic or published a count **[Unconfirmed]**.

What we can say honestly:

- The official store description refers generally to *"upgrades"* mattering, alongside bullets and medicine **[Confirmed as a category, not as an inventory upgrade]**
- Players consistently describe **finding** capacity expansions rather than buying them **[Community-reported]**
- **How many exist, what they are called, what they cost and where they are is entirely undocumented [Unconfirmed]**

If a guide gives you a numbered list of Silver Pines inventory upgrades with map locations, it is guessing. There is no official location data for this game yet, for anything.

### The "hip pouch" question

"Silver Pines hip pouch" is a real search term, so it is worth answering directly: **we have no verified source that names an item called a hip pouch in Silver Pines.** The phrase circulates in community discussion of the inventory system, and it is plausible as a name for a capacity upgrade, but plausible is not the same as verified. **[Unconfirmed — no official or consistently-reproduced community source names this item.]**

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How big is the Silver Pines inventory?**
The store page confirms space is limited; the exact grid size has not been published [Unconfirmed]. Preview coverage describes it as grid-based, where items occupy differing numbers of squares [Community-reported].

**Is there an item box in Silver Pines?**
Yes, in the sense that storage containers in key areas let you store and swap items [Community-reported]. The specific name used in-game is [Unconfirmed].

**Can you upgrade inventory space?**
Widely reported by demo players, but never documented officially [Unconfirmed].

**What is a hip pouch in Silver Pines?**
No verified source names an item by that name [Unconfirmed].

**What should I carry?**
Tape, the healing you actually need, ammunition for the weapon you plan to use, and the key for the door you are heading to. Everything else belongs in storage.

**Do you lose stored items when you die?**
Nothing suggests storage is affected by death — the rollback is to your last save, and containers are part of the world state [Community-reported, and the standard behaviour for this sub-genre] **[Unconfirmed]**.

**Does the game pause when the inventory is open?**
No [Community-reported]. Plan menu time as carefully as you plan ammunition.

**Is searching everywhere actually necessary?**
The store page says so directly: *"Search everywhere for items because every bullet, bottle of medicine and upgrade matters."* Treat that as a design instruction rather than flavour text.
