# Silver Pines Wiki — Survival Horror Metroidvania Guide Hub

**Everything confirmed about the survival horror metroidvania from Wych Elm and Team17 — released on October 8, 2026 for PC via Steam at $24.99, with console versions also announced.**

Silver Pines was released on **October 8, 2026**. The Steam version is **PC (Windows)** and costs **$24.99 USD**; console versions were announced for **PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X|S, Nintendo Switch and Nintendo Switch 2**. It is developed by **Wych Elm** and published by **Team17** — a distinction worth stating precisely, because the two are commonly collapsed into one. The Steam build is Windows-only.

A **free standalone demo has been on Steam since June 1, 2026** and remains available: as of **September 16, 2026** it held **802 user reviews at 95.9% positive**, an Overwhelmingly Positive rating. That is demo-only data. The released full game has its own, separate Steam review summary: as of **October 24, 2026**, **1,735 user reviews at 97% positive**, also Overwhelmingly Positive.

> **Naming warning.** "Silver Pines" is also the name of a mobile home park, a healthcare group, a ranch resort chain, a rehabilitation centre, and — for World of Warcraft players — it is one letter away from Silverpine Forest. This site is about the **video game by Wych Elm**, not about any of those. That is also why our page titles are anchored to `silver pines game` rather than the bare name.

## What is Silver Pines?

Silver Pines is a **survival horror metroidvania**. You play **private investigator Red Walker**, who arrives in a half-abandoned town in the middle of nowhere to track down **Eddie Velvet** — a musician who has vanished. As the case uncovers a dark past, Walker slips into something stranger than a missing-person job.

The two halves of that genre label are both real, and both matter:

**The survival horror half.** Ammunition is scarce. Your inventory is small and grid-based. The official store description says it plainly: *"Inventory space is limited. Search everywhere for items because every bullet, bottle of medicine and upgrade matters."* You scavenge spare change to buy supplies from vending machines — and the same coins are spent to **save your progress**, because Silver Pines has **no autosave**. Death costs you everything since your last save.

**The metroidvania half.** The official description describes a town *"filled with locked doors and hidden paths"* where *"with better tools and weapons more and more of the town becomes accessible."* That is tool-gated progression: areas you walk past early become reachable later, and the map fills in as you go.

The tone is Lynchian small-town Americana — Twin Peaks, Silent Hill and Alan Wake are the reference points the press keeps reaching for. In a Game8 interview, Wych Elm developer **Mattias Snygg** confirmed the game has **multiple endings**, but that they are not branching-choice endings: all of them can be reached on a single save file, and they reward exploring the town thoroughly.

## Key Systems at a Glance

Each line below carries its sourcing, because Silver Pines has been playable since release and its systems are described in two different registers — official and player-reported.

**Confirmed by the developer / Steam store data:**

- **Resource scarcity** — the official description names bullets, medicine bottles and upgrades as things that each matter
- **Limited inventory** — stated directly on the store page
- **Tool-gated exploration** — locked doors and hidden paths that open as your tools improve
- **Multiple endings** — reachable on one save file (Game8 developer interview)
- **Full controller support** — plus DualShock and DualSense support, Achievements, Cloud and Family Sharing
- **No difficulty setting announced**, no co-op, no multiplayer

**Community-reported** (consistent across demo players and preview coverage, but not stated on the store page):

- **Phone booths are the only save points**, and there is no autosave
- **Coins are shared currency** — the same change buys vending-machine supplies and pays to save
- **Grid-limited inventory with storage containers** in key areas for swapping items
- **Melee weapons have durability** and are repaired with **tape**, through a manual input sequence
- **Reloading is manual and per-bullet**, and happens in real time
- **Stamina is spent on dodging and melee swings**; running does not drain it
- **The game does not pause** — not for the inventory, not for the map
- **Mementos act as ability buffs** (faster reloading, better health recovery)

**Weapons reported by demo players:** a **pistol**, a **shotgun** and a **cutter** (utility knife). No official source has published a weapon list [Unconfirmed].
