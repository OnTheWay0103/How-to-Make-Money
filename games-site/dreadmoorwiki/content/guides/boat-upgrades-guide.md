# DREADMOOR Boat Upgrades Guide — Dry Dock, Modules & Engine

Your boat is not a vehicle in DREADMOOR. It is the progression system. Fishing spots, salvage, contracts, and story locations are all gated by how far and how safely the trawler can travel, and almost every meaningful upgrade runs through the **Dry Dock** at the Trading Post.

This guide explains the demo’s upgrade chain, the math behind the two headline modules, and the mistakes that leave players with a crafted part sitting in storage instead of installed on the boat.

## What Upgrades Exist?

The official Early Access feature list names five boat upgrade categories:

- **Engine**
- **Propeller**
- **Hull**
- **Searchlight**
- **Afterburner**

The official press material also mentions **rod** and **lure** upgrades, which are fishing gear rather than boat modules. The demo only lets you complete a subset of the boat list: Hull Protection and the Upgraded Engine are the two required progression upgrades, with Fish Hold improvements, Propeller upgrades, and Afterburner research appearing as optional or later options.

That gap matters. A guide that presents a full five-tier boat tree with stats for every module would be inventing numbers. This page sticks to what the demo shows and labels the rest as Early Access categories without published stats.

## Unlocking the Dry Dock

The Dry Dock is locked when you first arrive. The unlock chain is:

1. **Talk to John the Fifth** at his workshop.
2. **Repair the Moonshine Still.** The repair requires removing a lodged prosthetic leg, gathering **Coral Fiber** on Angler’s Island with the Axe, collecting **Bug Slime** from small blue creatures with the Grappling Hook Gun, and salvaging Metal Plates.
3. **Receive the Dry Dock Key.**
4. **Restore power.** Repair the Fuse Box with one Cable, then activate the separate Power Switch.
5. **Place the boat on the Boat Lift.**

Do not skip the power step. The Fuse Box and the Power Switch are separate interactions, and the lift will not operate until both are done.

## Cleaning and Repairing the Hull

Once the boat is on the lift, the demo teaches three maintenance systems:

| System | Tool | What it does |
|--------|------|--------------|
| Hull cleaning | Pressure Pump + Water Cannon | Removes Polyps from the hull |
| Hull repair | Repair Gun loaded with Bug Slime | Repairs damaged hull sections individually |
| Module installation | Dry Dock interface | Fits crafted modules into slots |

The important detail is that hull damage is **section-based**. You repair the damaged sections you can see, not a single global health bar. That is why a quick visual check before a crossing is more useful than waiting for a warning prompt.

## The Two Headline Modules

### Hull Protection

Hull Protection is the demo’s clearest tradeoff:

- **Protection: 2**
- **Boat Speed: −1**
- **Install slot:** side-front

It is crafted at John’s Lathe after you research Weak Hull Protection. The protection value reduces the frequency and severity of hull damage; the speed penalty is permanent while the module is installed.

**When it is worth it:** new routes, night crossings, and any trip where a repair detour would cost more than the lost speed. The demo’s dangerous water is not forgiving, and a damaged hull can force you back to the Dry Dock.

**When it is not:** a short, known, safe farming route where you are optimizing for trip count. In that case, the engine alone is faster.

### Upgraded Engine

The Upgraded Engine is the single largest quality-of-life upgrade in the demo:

- **Base boat speed:** 2 kn
- **Speed after Upgraded Engine:** 4 kn
- **Prerequisite:** Carburetor
- **Carburetor price:** 50

The prerequisite chain is where players get stuck. John lost the Carburetor to Gill and Bozur in a card game. To get it:

1. Catch and deliver **3 Harpooners** using Rainbow Fin at the marked predatory-fish location.
2. After delivery, **buy the Carburetor separately for 50**. The contract reward does not place it in your inventory automatically.
3. Raise the boat on the lift, remove the Standard Engine, and place it in Module Storage.
4. Craft the Upgraded Engine at John’s Lathe using the Carburetor and other materials.
5. Install it in the empty engine slot.

The demo explicitly shows the speed change from 2 kn to 4 kn. That is a 100% increase, which means every route takes roughly half the time. It also shortens exposure on night crossings, even though it does not make them safe.

## The Module Storage Trap

Crafted modules do not always go straight into your inventory. In the demo, a crafted module can end up in the **Storage Locker** or **Module Storage**, and the boat slot remains empty until you deliberately install it.

If a crafted upgrade seems to have vanished:

1. Check the Storage Locker at the Dry Dock.
2. Check Module Storage near the Boat Lift.
3. Open the installation interface and confirm the correct slot.

The Hull Protection module installs in the **side-front** slot. The engine installs in the engine slot after the Standard Engine is removed. Do not craft a second module before checking storage; duplicate parts waste materials.

## Upgrade Order — A Practical Path

Our recommended order for a fresh demo run:

1. **Unlock the Dry Dock.** Nothing else matters until the lift works.
2. **Repair the hull.** A clean, repaired hull is the baseline for every later trip.
3. **Craft Hull Protection** before your first trip to The Throat or any unfamiliar water.
4. **Complete the Harpooner contract and buy the Carburetor.**
5. **Craft and install the Upgraded Engine.**
6. **Only then consider optional modules** such as Fish Hold improvements, Propeller, or Afterburner research.

This order front-loads survival and travel speed. Cosmetic or comfort upgrades can wait; the engine and hull compound every other activity.

## Materials Checklist

Before you start the upgrade chain, gather or confirm:

- **Metal Plates** — salvage around the Trading Post and floating containers
- **Coral Fiber** — Angler’s Island, harvested with the Axe
- **Bug Slime** — small blue creatures, collected with the Grappling Hook Gun
- **Rubber** — tires and salvage; useful later in the chain
- **Chromatic Scales** — Thresher, from suitable peaceful fish
- **Fish Meat** — Grinder; also the fuel supply
- **Scraps of Knowledge** — reward from John after the Chromatic Scales step
- **Cable** — one is needed for the Fuse Box

The two bottlenecks are usually **Chromatic Scales** and **Coral Fiber**. Scales compete with Rainbow Fin bait; Coral Fiber requires a dedicated trip to Angler’s Island. Plan both before you commit to a crafting session.

## What the Demo Does Not Show

The demo does not include:

- Propeller stat values
- Fish Hold capacity numbers
- Searchlight installation or its effect on night visibility
- Afterburner duration, cooldown, or fuel cost
- A full material cost table for any module beyond the two headline upgrades
- Whether modules can be removed and reused without a loss

The official Early Access list confirms these categories exist, but not their numbers. Treat any specific values you see on a pre-launch page with suspicion unless it names the build it measured.

## Common Boat-Upgrade Mistakes

1. **Buying the Carburetor before finishing the Harpooner contract.** The purchase is gated by the delivery.
2. **Forgetting to install the crafted engine.** Check Module Storage and the engine slot.
3. **Crafting Hull Protection twice.** Confirm the first one is installed before crafting another.
4. **Installing Hull Protection on a pure speed route.** The −1 kn penalty is real; use it where protection pays.
5. **Ignoring hull sections after a collision.** Repair before the next long trip, not after.
6. **Spending all Chromatic Scales on Rainbow Fin.** Keep a reserve for hull reinforcement.
7. **Sailing to The Throat with a full hold and an empty tank.** Upgrade or repair first; the gate puzzle is not a good place to discover a logistics problem.
