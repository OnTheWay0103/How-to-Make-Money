# DREADMOOR Combat Guide — Harpoons, Threats & What Is Confirmed

DREADMOOR is marketed as a fishing survival horror game, and the official Early Access feature list confirms **real-time combat against mutated creatures on both sea and land**, with harpoons and other tools. It also confirms that night water is dangerous, that tentacles can attack the boat, and that a destroyed boat drags you to the nearest dock while you lose cargo.

The demo, however, is almost entirely a fishing and repair tutorial. Hands-on coverage of the demo and playtest describes no conventional combat encounters, no health meter, and only scripted monster moments. That creates a documentation problem: the game’s combat exists in official material, but there is not enough verified detail to write a weapon-tier list.

This guide separates the two. It covers what is confirmed, what the demo actually shows, and how to manage threats without pretending to know damage numbers that have not been published.

## Confirmed Combat Facts

These points come from official store and announcement material:

| Fact | Status |
|------|--------|
| Real-time combat against mutated creatures | Confirmed |
| Combat happens on sea and on land | Confirmed |
| Harpoons are a combat tool | Confirmed |
| Reinforced harpoons can be crafted | Confirmed |
| Night water is more dangerous | Confirmed |
| Tentacles can attack the boat at night | Confirmed |
| A destroyed boat drags you to the nearest dock | Confirmed |
| Cargo is lost when the boat is destroyed | Confirmed |
| Other weapons and their stats | Not published |

The last row is the important one. We know harpoons exist. We do not know the full weapon list, damage values, ammo economy, reload times, or whether there are melee weapons beyond the Axe used for harvesting.

## What the Demo Actually Shows

The October 2026 demo is a vertical slice built around the Trading Post, the Dry Dock, Angler’s Island, and The Throat. In that build:

- The **Axe** is a harvesting tool for Coral Fiber, not a demonstrated combat weapon.
- The **Grappling Hook Gun** is used to collect Bug Slime and salvage, not to fight.
- The **fishing rod** is the primary interaction.
- Monster presence is scripted and atmospheric: distant tentacles, a leviathan skull, unsettling set pieces.
- No health bar, hunger meter, thirst meter, or oxygen meter was observed by preview coverage.
- Sailing all night repeatedly produced only distant tentacle sightings in the preview build, with no attack or consequence in that session.

That does not mean combat is fake. It means the demo was tuned to teach the survival loop first. The Early Access build is the one that is supposed to deliver the combat sandbox.

## The Threat Framework: Avoid, Deter, Escape, Absorb

Until weapon stats are published, the useful way to think about DREADMOOR combat is a four-layer defense. Each layer is cheaper than the one after it.

### 1. Avoid

Most threat management happens before a fight starts.

- **Do not sail at night without a reason.** Night is explicitly the dangerous window. If a trip can be made in daylight, make it in daylight.
- **Do not overload the boat.** A full hold means losing more cargo if the boat is destroyed.
- **Do not explore unfamiliar water with a damaged hull.** Repair first.
- **Do not assume the demo’s calm nights are representative.** The preview build did not punish night sailing heavily; the official feature list says the launch build will.

Avoidance is boring, which is why it works.

### 2. Deter

Deterrence is about making your boat a less attractive target.

- **Keep the engine upgraded.** Speed is the most reliable defensive stat in a game where escape is possible.
- **Install Hull Protection** when you expect trouble. Protection 2 at a cost of 1 kn is a reasonable trade for a dangerous route.
- **Keep a searchlight slot in mind.** The Early Access upgrade list confirms a searchlight category, but the demo does not show its effect. We are not going to claim it scares creatures.
- **Do not carry a hold full of valuable fish through known dangerous water.** Process or sell first.

### 3. Escape

When something engages you, the first question is whether you can leave.

- **Turn away early.** Do not wait until the hull is badly damaged to break contact.
- **Use open water.** Running aground or into a dead end turns a chase into a repair bill.
- **Accept the loss of a fishing spot.** A missed catch is cheaper than a destroyed boat and lost cargo.
- **Head for the nearest dock.** The official material says a destroyed boat drags you to the nearest dock. It does not say the trip is free; cargo is lost.

Escape is not cowardice in a survival game. It is the intended counterplay to an enemy you have not geared for.

### 4. Absorb

If you cannot avoid, deter, or escape, the hull is the last layer.

- **Repair before the trip, not during it.** Hull damage is section-based, and repairs happen at the Dry Dock.
- **Carry repair materials.** Bug Slime feeds the Repair Gun; Metal Plates and other salvage feed the upgrade chain.
- **Treat Hull Protection as insurance.** It reduces the chance that one bad encounter ends the run.
- **Do not stack cargo value in a damaged boat.** Offload before a risky crossing.

## Harpoons: What We Know and What We Do Not

Harpoons are the only confirmed weapon category. The official material mentions harpoons and **reinforced harpoons** as craftable gear, which implies a progression path from a basic harpoon to at least one upgraded version.

What is not confirmed:

- How many harpoon tiers exist
- Whether harpoons are thrown, fired from a launcher, or used as melee
- Whether they are recoverable after use
- Damage, range, reload, or ammo capacity
- Whether different harpoons are effective against different creature types

If a pre-launch article gives you exact harpoon damage numbers, check whether it cites the build. We have not found an official source for those numbers, so we do not publish them.

## On-Foot vs On-Water Combat

The official feature list says combat happens on both sea and land. That implies two different risk profiles:

- **On water:** the boat is both your weapon platform and your escape route. A fight can damage the thing you need to leave. Cargo is at risk. Night attacks are explicitly confirmed.
- **On foot:** you are away from the boat, possibly on an island with salvage or a story objective. The demo shows islands with docks, so mooring and exploring is a real loop. Without confirmed health or dodge mechanics, the safe assumption is that on-foot combat is more dangerous than boat combat, not less.

We will document the actual on-foot systems after Early Access launches.

## Night Crossings: The Highest-Risk Routine

Night is the one combat-adjacent system with enough official detail to plan around.

Confirmed behavior:

1. Night water is more dangerous.
2. Tentacles can attack the boat.
3. If the boat is destroyed, you are dragged to the nearest dock.
4. You lose cargo in that process.

Practical protocol:

- **Check fuel before dark.** A night crossing with a low tank combines two failure states.
- **Check hull condition before dark.** Do not enter the night with unrepaired sections.
- **Empty or reduce the hold if the cargo is valuable.** The loss is cargo, not just time.
- **Know the nearest dock.** The game may relocate you, but knowing the route helps you plan the recovery.
- **Do not fish at night for profit unless the catch justifies the risk.** Different species may appear, but the boat is exposed the whole time.

## What to Do After a Boat Loss

The official material does not describe a death loop in detail, so this is a recovery checklist rather than a guaranteed procedure:

1. Confirm where you were relocated.
2. Check what cargo survived.
3. Repair the hull before the next trip.
4. Refuel before the next trip.
5. Replace lost bait materials before attempting the same route.
6. If the loss was caused by night travel, change the route or the timing, not just the loadout.

Do not immediately re-sail the same route to “get it back.” The conditions that destroyed the boat have not changed.
