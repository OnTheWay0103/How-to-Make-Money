# DREADMOOR Survival Guide — Fuel, Hull & Night Crossings

Survival in DREADMOOR is not a stack of meters. The demo does not show an oxygen bar, a hunger bar, a thirst bar, or a conventional health bar. What it does show is a boat that needs fuel, a hull that takes damage, and a night that the game repeatedly warns you about.

This guide covers the survival systems that are actually confirmed, the routines that keep a run alive, and the mechanics that are still unconfirmed. If you came here looking for an oxygen-management table, the honest answer is that no such system has been verified.

## The Confirmed Survival Loop

DREADMOOR’s survival pressure comes from four places:

1. **Fuel** — the boat needs it, and it is produced from fish.
2. **Hull condition** — damage accumulates and must be repaired at the Dry Dock.
3. **Materials** — bait, repairs, and upgrades all consume salvage and processed fish.
4. **Night** — the water becomes more dangerous, and the boat can be attacked.

There is no confirmed hunger, thirst, temperature, or oxygen meter. Sleeping exists as a time-skip at the Bunkhouse, not as a fatigue meter you have to manage moment to moment. That makes DREADMOOR’s survival loop closer to a logistics game than a traditional survival game.

## Fuel: Turning Fish Into Motion

The demo’s fuel chain is the most important routine to learn. It has more steps than most players expect:

1. **Catch fish.** Any fish that can be processed works; the opening Snappers are the simplest source.
2. **Process the fish at the Grinder** into **Fish Meat**. A whole fish is not fuel and is not a recipe ingredient.
3. **Feed the Fuel Larva** with Fish Meat.
4. **Use the Fuel Gun** to transfer fuel into the motorboat.
5. **Check the tank.** Feeding the Larva and filling the boat are separate actions.

The last step is where runs fail. It is entirely possible to feed the Larva, assume the boat is fueled, and discover an empty tank after you have already left the dock. Check the tank before casting off, every time.

### How Much Fuel Should You Carry?

No official fuel capacity or consumption rate has been published for Early Access, and the demo does not expose a numeric tank gauge in the sources we checked. A practical policy:

- **Before a known route:** fill the tank and keep enough Fish Meat for one more fill.
- **Before an unfamiliar route:** fill the tank and carry a reserve of whole fish or Fish Meat.
- **Before a night crossing:** fill the tank even if the route is short. A night crossing with a low tank combines two failure states.
- **Before The Throat:** fill the tank and repair the hull. The gate area is not a place to troubleshoot logistics.

If you are ever unsure, over-prepare. Fuel is cheap compared with a lost cargo run.

## Hull Condition: The Slow Failure State

Hull damage is not a single health bar. The demo treats the hull as a set of sections, and repairs happen at the Dry Dock.

The maintenance loop:

| Step | Tool or station | What it does |
|------|-----------------|--------------|
| Clean | Pressure Pump + Water Cannon | Removes Polyps from the hull |
| Repair | Repair Gun loaded with Bug Slime | Repairs damaged hull sections |
| Protect | Hull Protection module | Protection 2, Boat Speed −1 |
| Inspect | Visual check at the dock | Find damage before the next trip |

The Repair Gun consumes **Bug Slime**, which you collect from small blue creatures with the Grappling Hook Gun. That makes Bug Slime a survival resource, not just a crafting curiosity. Keep a stock.

Hull Protection is optional but valuable on dangerous routes. The speed penalty is real, so treat it as insurance for exploration and night travel rather than a permanent default for short farming runs.

## Salvage: The Other Survival Currency

The Grappling Hook Gun is your salvage tool. It retrieves floating debris, Metal Plates, and Bug Slime. Its default reeling speed is slow, and an upgrade exists, but the demo does not publish the upgraded value.

Survival-relevant salvage includes:

- **Metal Plates** — bait recipes, repairs, and upgrade materials
- **Rubber** — from tires; used later in the upgrade chain
- **Cable** — one is needed for the Dry Dock Fuse Box
- **Coral Fiber** — Angler’s Island, harvested with the Axe
- **Chromatic Scales** — processed from suitable peaceful fish

Do not treat salvage as optional loot. The upgrade chain and the repair loop compete for the same materials, and a run that ignores salvage will stall at the next crafting gate.

## Sleep and Time

The demo uses sleep as a time-skip at the Bunkhouse. Sleeping advances to the next morning and triggers the first fishing assignment. It is not presented as a fatigue system you must manage.

That means time management is mostly about **day versus night**:

- Do your fishing and salvage in daylight when you can.
- Use sleep to skip a night you do not need to sail.
- Do not sleep through a deadline you have not checked. Contract objectives may be time-sensitive; read the objective text before skipping time.

No official statement confirms that contracts expire on a timer, so do not assume a hard deadline. The safe habit is simply to check the objective before sleeping.

## Night Crossings: What Is Confirmed

The official Early Access material is explicit:

- Night water is more dangerous.
- Tentacles can attack the boat.
- If the boat is destroyed, you are dragged to the nearest dock.
- You lose cargo in that process.

Hands-on coverage of the playtest complicates the picture: a preview session that sailed all night repeatedly saw only distant tentacle sightings, with no attack or consequence. That may be a demo-tuning issue, a preview-build issue, or a sign that night attacks are rarer than the marketing suggests.

The correct planning assumption is the official one: **night is dangerous, and the boat can be lost.** If the launch build turns out to be gentler, you have lost nothing by being prepared.

### Night Checklist

Before any night crossing:

- Tank full
- Hull repaired
- Repair materials in stock
- Hold not overloaded with high-value cargo
- Nearest dock known
- Route planned, not improvised
- A reason to be sailing at night that is worth the risk

If you cannot check every box, wait for morning. The game does not reward impatience.

## Pre-Departure Checklist

Use this before every long trip, not just night trips:

| Check | Why |
|-------|-----|
| Fuel tank | The most common cause of a stranded run |
| Hull sections | Damage compounds and forces a repair detour |
| Bait stock | A fishing trip without the right bait is a wasted trip |
| Fish Meat reserve | Fuel, bait, and contracts all consume it |
| Hold space | Salvage and contracts both need room |
| Contract objective | Whole fish vs processed fish changes the plan |
| Weather and time | Night changes the risk profile |

This checklist is deliberately short. A long checklist you do not use is worse than a short one you do.

## What Is Not Confirmed

As of October 24, 2026, the following are **not** verified:

- An oxygen meter or underwater breathing system
- Hunger, thirst, temperature, or fatigue meters
- A conventional player health bar
- A fuel capacity number or burn rate
- Whether fuel can be bought directly from a trader
- Whether food can be cooked for buffs
- Whether sleep has a fatigue or rest requirement beyond time-skipping

If you see a guide that lists oxygen tank upgrades or hunger thresholds for DREADMOOR, check its sources. We have not found official or demo evidence for those systems.

## Emergency Procedure

When something goes wrong, work in this order:

1. **Stabilize the boat.** Stop taking damage; move away from the threat.
2. **Check fuel.** A stranded boat in dangerous water is worse than a damaged one at dock.
3. **Check hull sections.** If the hull is critical, head for the Dry Dock instead of the fishing spot.
4. **Drop the optional objective.** A contract can be retried; a destroyed boat costs cargo.
5. **Return, repair, refuel, restock.** Do not immediately repeat the same trip.

The goal is not to win every encounter. It is to keep the boat and the cargo moving.
