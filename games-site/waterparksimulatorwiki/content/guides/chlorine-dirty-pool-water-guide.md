# Waterpark Simulator Chlorine Guide: Dirty Pool Water Fixed

Chlorine in Waterpark Simulator is a single bottle you throw into a pool that has **already** turned yellow-brown — it is a fix, not a preventive. You buy jugs at CayMart, carry one to the water, and throw it in; the pool clears instantly. Draining and refilling does the same job for free if you can afford the downtime.

## What chlorine does — and what it does not

The item is easy to misread because it sounds like the usual "keep your water clean" toggle. It is not.

- **It is reactive.** Chlorine only registers on water that has visibly gone bad. Throw it into a clear pool and nothing happens, which is the single most common reason players think the item is broken.
- **It clears tint, nothing else.** Bottles, cans, and trash sitting in the pool stay there. Scoop them first or the water goes bad again within minutes.
- **It does not raise the pool's long-term hygiene.** The park's cleanliness score still depends on what your cleaners and filters are doing between visits.
- **It does keep the pool open.** That is the whole point. A drain closes the attraction; a bottle does not.

CayPlay's Steam page frames the work this way: you personally handle "cleanup and pool waste," and cleanliness is one of four inputs to the park score next to slide quality, food, and amenities ([Official](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3293260/)). Chlorine is the mid-shift answer to that pressure, not a replacement for staffing.

## Where to buy chlorine

Chlorine is a **shop item, not a quest reward**. The route is:

1. Open the park shop (CayMart).
2. Find the chlorine jug in the supplies range and buy it like any other consumable.
3. It arrives as a physical object you can carry — the 2026-08-28 patch notes confirm "shop supplies and chlorine" survive quitting and reloading, so a jug you leave on the ground is still there next session ([Official](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/3293260/view/1842212951303274)).

Community write-ups say chlorine is gated behind a **research unlock** added with the Splash & Dash update in October 2025, sitting a few steps into the upgrades tree. One guide puts it around Research Level 7 and roughly 16 research points into a Modern Toilets and Mop Upgrade path; another puts the jug at about **$25 per use** ([Community](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3293260/discussions/0/598539452432963497)). Those numbers vary by difficulty and patch — treat the exact level and price as **[Community]** rather than fact. What is consistent across sources is the shape: research first, then a shop purchase, then a physical bottle.

If chlorine is missing from the shop, check the research tree before assuming a bug. The upgrade path is listed in our [research tree priority order](/guides/research-tree-guide).

## How to use a bottle, step by step

1. **Confirm the pool is actually dirty.** Look for the yellow-brown tint and the vapour rising off the surface. Clear water will not accept the bottle.
2. **Clear the pool floor first.** Any floating trash will re-contaminate the water, so send a cleaner or scoop it yourself.
3. **Carry the jug to the pool edge.** It is a holdable item, so you can walk it across the park rather than teleporting it.
4. **Throw it in.** The water clears within a second or two.
5. **Re-check the pool a few minutes later.** If it darkens again immediately, something is still spawning dirt — usually an uncollected trash source or a filter that is not running.

One technical note worth knowing: before patch 1.0.43, holding several bottles at once produced several conflicting throw prompts, so the throw could land on the wrong target or refuse to fire. CayPlay fixed exactly that — "Fixed holding multiple chlorine bottles causing multiple throw options" ([Official](https://www.vgspoilers.com/game/waterpark-simulator/patches)). If you are on an older build, carry one bottle at a time.

## Chlorine versus drain-and-refill

Both work. They cost different things.

| Situation | Chlorine | Drain and refill |
|---|---|---|
| Pool is full of guests, mid-day | **Yes** — no downtime | No — closes the attraction |
| Park is closed, no guests queued | Optional | **Yes** — free |
| Water is dirty but trash remains inside | Clean the trash first | **Yes** — clears everything |
| You are short on cash | Skip it | **Yes** — costs nothing |
| A quest specifically asks for chlorine | **Yes** — only this counts | No |

The community consensus in the Steam thread is blunt: because refilling is free, several players never buy chlorine at all and simply drain on a schedule. Chlorine earns its price in two situations only — a busy mid-shift save, and the one quest step below.

## The Yogi step that will not count

The endgame Yogi questline includes a step that asks you to treat dirty water with chlorine. Players hit a wall on it for a structural reason: **clean water does not count**, and a well-run park never has dirty water, because maintenance staff keep the pools clear.

The workarounds community players describe ([Community](https://waterparksimulatorgame.wiki/guides/yogi-quests/)) are all variations on forcing a dirty pool:

- Temporarily pull maintenance staff off pool duty so the water degrades on its own, then treat it and reassign them.
- Seed the problem deliberately — drop trash bags into one small pool and wait for the tint.
- Use the smallest pool in the park for the whole step, so the cleanup and the re-dirtying both happen fast.

Once the water is visibly brown, throw the bottle in and watch the objective tick. If nothing ticks, the water was not dirty enough yet — wait for the vapour before wasting another jug.

## Can staff handle the chlorine for you?

Yes, according to community reports: maintenance employees can be trained to apply chlorine themselves, which turns it from a manual chore into a supply cost. The caveat is scale — a small crew cannot keep up with a large swim area, so a park with four pools and two maintenance hires will still stall. The 2026-08-28 patch note about supplies persisting on the floor also matters here: a jug dropped near a pool stays there, which makes staging bottles around the park practical rather than wasteful.

If your staff are the bottleneck rather than the supplies, the hiring order in our [staff management guide](/guides/staff-management-guide) is the better fix.

## Why water quality deserves a routine

Water quality sits at the top of the hygiene priority order on this site, ahead of slides, trash, and puddles, because a dirty pool drags the rating down faster than almost anything else and the guests who leave do not come back the same day. The [visitor problems solver](/guides/visitor-problems-guide) uses that same order: water quality, then pools and filters, then trash, then overdue tasks.

A practical routine that does not require chlorine at all:

- One cleaner assigned to pool decks, not the whole park, on any shift above two stars.
- Trash bins placed so the yellow radius covers the pool edge — guests outside the radius will not walk to the bin.
- A drain scheduled at close on the pool that took the most traffic that day.
- One chlorine jug staged at the busiest pool during heatwave and event weather, when guest volume spikes and accidents come with it.

## FAQ

**Is chlorine free?** No. It is a shop purchase, and community reports put a jug around $25. Draining and refilling costs nothing but closes the pool.

**Does chlorine clean the whole park?** No. It only affects the pool you throw it into, and only clears the water — not the trash, not the deck, not the filters.

**Do I need chlorine to reach five stars?** Not necessarily. Cleanliness is one rating input and staff plus scheduled draining covers it. Chlorine is the convenience and quest answer.

**Why can I not find chlorine at all?** Most often it is a research gate rather than a bug. Confirm the unlock, then check the shop's supplies tab.

## What is not confirmed

Exact jug price, the exact research level and point cost, and how many pools a single bottle is meant to serve are all **community-reported and vary by build**. Everything marked [Official] above comes from CayPlay's Steam page or patch notes; everything else is player testing.
