# Co-op Multiplayer Guide — Play With Friends (1.0)

Multiplayer co-op was the most-requested feature during Early Access, and the 1.0 update delivered it: **up to 4 players** can run a park together, from scratch or from an existing save. This guide covers how it works and how to run a shared park without chaos.

Last updated August 8, 2026 (1.0).

## How Co-op Works

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Player count | Up to **4 players** |
| Starting | Create a new shared park, or load an existing park |
| Ownership | Everyone shares the same park, budget, and staff pool |
| Voice chat | Optional **proximity voice chat** |
| Cross-play | PC + console cross-play support hasn't been officially detailed; check the current patch notes before assuming it works across platforms |
| Progression | Park progression is shared — one park, one star rating |

The golden rule of co-op: **one park, one budget**. Anyone can spend park money and hire/fire staff, so communicate before big purchases or you'll build two wave pools on accident.

## Communication Tools (1.0)

- **Proximity voice chat** — talk to players near you
- **Trumpet** — get attention across the park
- **Megaphone** — announce things park-wide
- **Walkie-talkie system** — the important one: chat with any player anywhere on the map

For distance communication, the walkie-talkie is the reliable tool — proximity chat fades as players split up.

## Role Splits That Work

Division of labor makes or breaks a shared park. Proven splits:

### The 2-Player Split

| Player | Job |
|--------|-----|
| **Park Manager** | Tickets, pricing, research, staff, building plans |
| **Groundskeeper** | Cleaning (mop/water gun), repairs, restocking, troublemaker ejection |

### The 4-Player Split

| Player | Job |
|--------|-----|
| **Host / Manager** | Research, budget, ticket pricing, Museum visits |
| **Cleaner** | Puddles, trash, pool refills |
| **Builder** | Slide design, layout, decor |
| **Guest Services** | Stalls, restocking, ejections, safety (life rings, lifeguarding) |

## The Museum Problem (Solve It Together)

The Museum questline requires leaving your park unattended — a real problem solo, a non-problem in co-op:

- Send the **Manager** across the street to do Museum quests while everyone else covers the park
- Do quests **before opening or at closing** if you're short-handed
- The quests are mostly maintenance tasks — if the park runs itself, progress is fast

## Tips for Smooth Co-op

1. **Agree on one builder.** Two people placing paths at once creates spaghetti parks. Design together, build one at a time.
2. **Pick a budget boss.** One player approves big purchases. Everyone else buys stalls and small stuff freely.
3. **Stagger staff breaks** — if all staff nap at once and both players are across the street, the park burns.
4. **Use the walkie-talkie** — not proximity chat — for "pool empty at the east slide" style calls.
5. **Duck water battles (1.0)** — the duck guns are a silly way to pass time between shifts.
6. **Carry people.** 1.0 lets you pick up guests, staff, and other players — give friends rides across the map when the park is huge.

## Co-op Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It Hurts |
|---------|--------------|
| Everyone building simultaneously | Layout chaos; no one knows where anything is |
| No budget discipline | Shared wallet empties; can't afford staffing |
| Both players in the Museum at once | Park unattended during open hours |
| Ignoring voice chat | Missed emergencies turn into ambulance fees |
| Hiring without checking slots | The over-allocation tax stacks with multiple managers |

## The Verdict

Co-op is the best way to experience the endgame: the Museum questlines, night mode events, and beach map all shine with a partner — and the cleanup burden of a 4-star park is genuinely built for two pairs of hands. Start a fresh park with friends for the full experience (existing saves can be loaded, but building up together is more fun).
