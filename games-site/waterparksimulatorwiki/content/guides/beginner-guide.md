# Waterpark Simulator Beginner Guide — First 3 Hours

Waterpark Simulator drops you into a rundown waterpark with a sledgehammer, a hammer, a mop, and a dream. The goal: build attractions, sell tickets, keep guests happy, and grow your star rating until you own a five-star waterpark empire. It sounds simple, but the game is a juggling act — and the first few hours decide whether your park snowballs into a money machine or drowns in puddles and angry reviews.

This beginner guide walks through your **first 3 hours** in order: the opening tutorial, the daily routine, your first builds, and the mistakes to avoid. Everything here is based on the 1.0 release (July 31, 2026) and player reports from Early Access.

## Before You Start: Difficulty Modes

You can pick from **Sandbox, Easy, Normal, or Hard** when creating your park. For your first playthrough, choose **Easy or Normal** — they give you enough starting cash and forgiving guest demands to learn the systems without going bankrupt. Sandbox removes money pressure entirely and is great for learning slide design, but you will not experience the management loop that the game is really about. Hard is for later, once you know the economy.

Also decide solo vs. co-op: the 1.0 update added **4-player co-op**, and starting a shared park with friends genuinely eases the early workload — one person can babysit the entrance while another cleans and builds.

## The Opening Tutorial (~10 Minutes)

The tutorial teaches you the absolute basics:

- **Serve customers** — hand out bracelets/tickets at the entrance
- **Sell items** — open the food stall and serve guests
- **Clean puddles and trash** — the mop and trash bags
- **Build** — place your first small slide or pool with the build menu (B)

Pay attention to the hygiene side. The tutorial's trash-bag step has a known bug in some versions where bags cannot be picked up — if that happens to you, restart the day or check the pinned bug thread on Steam; it is a known Early Access-era issue that patches have addressed.

**Important:** complete the tutorial fully. It unlocks the core tools and teaches the hammer-holds-to-repair mechanic that you will use all game.

## The Core Gameplay Loop

Everything in Waterpark Simulator orbits one loop:

```
Build attractions → attract guests → sell tickets & food
→ keep guests happy (cleanliness, repairs, safety)
→ earn XP → research upgrades → raise star rating
→ charge higher entry fees → repeat
```

Your star rating (up to 5 stars, with "Diamond Stars" added in 1.0) is the master dial. More stars = you can charge more per ticket = more profit per guest. You earn stars by completing tasks, keeping the park clean, and running profitable days.

## The Daily Routine (First 3 Hours)

A day in your park has a natural rhythm:

### 1. Pre-Opening (Morning)

- **Repair everything** with your hammer — broken pools, stalls, and slides. Damaged attractions annoy guests and can hurt them (broken pools + cannonballs = ambulance fees).
- **Restock food stalls** (a hot dog pack costs $25; topping stock: plain, ketchup, mustard, both).
- **Throw a few life preservers into pools** — panicking visitors automatically bump into them, saving you rescue time.
- **Hand out flyers** near the street (each costs $5) to draw a wave of guests — but do not spam them in the first days; you cannot handle the crowd yet.

### 2. Open Hours (Daily, Closes ~6 PM)

- Open the park (via the tablet, Tab), then:
  - **Clean constantly** — mop puddles immediately (guests slip on them, lose happiness, and ragdoll into each other), and fill trash bags completely before tossing for maximum value.
  - **Watch for troublemakers** — look for the devil emoji above a guest's head. Ticket-cheaters (no bracelet), drunks (green bubbles), vandals (spray cans), and perverts (cameras) need to be pushed out with **Right Mouse**. You get ~$30 per intruder ejected.
  - **Refill pools that run low** — pools do not auto-refill; if a pool empties, guests cannonball into the shallow bottom and get injured. That triggers ambulances, which charge a fee.
  - **Run your research icons** — collect the floating science icons around the park for research points.

### 3. Closing (After 6 PM or Night Mode)

- The park closes around 6 PM in classic mode — guests leave, and you get a quiet window.
- **Repair everything** again (upgraded hammer makes this fast).
- **Do research** at the Research Table in your office — spend your research points before ending the day.
- **Deposit/plan** — decide what to build next day.

In **1.0 night mode** you can keep the park open after dark — but employees are paid more, so only run nights when the extra revenue beats the wage bill.

## Your First Builds (Priority Order)

Do not try to build everything. A focused park beats a sprawling one:

1. **One pool + one small slide** — your first ticket-drawing attractions.
2. **A hot dog stand** — the best early profit-per-square-meter. Place it near the ticket booth.
3. **Trash cans placed with intent** — when placing one, the arrow shows the approach direction and the yellow zone shows its range. Guests outside the zone will not use it, so cover high-traffic areas.
4. **A diving board** — cheap and popular; keep life preservers in the pool below.
5. **Upgrade your hammer** at the Research Table ASAP — faster repairs every closing.

Resist the urge to add a second pool before you can keep the first one clean. Expansion adds guests, and guests add cleaning work. A two-pool park that is always dirty earns less than a one-pool park that shines.

## Research Points in the First Hours

Research points come from three places:

- **Floating science icons** around your park (collect them as you move around)
- **Ticket sales** as the day progresses
- **Daily actions** — cleaning, repairs, and completing tasks

You spend them at the **Research Table** in your office, which has **Attractions, Park, and Maintenance** trees (exact categories may shift with patches). Early priorities:

| Priority | Unlock | Why |
|:--:|------|------|
| 1 | **Hiring Level 1** (Maintenance) | Unlocks the cashier — frees you from ticket booth duty |
| 2 | **Showers / hygiene basics** | Guests stay clean and happy |
| 3 | **Food stalls** | Hot dog + lemonade stands are profit machines |
| 4 | **Trash bin / trash bag upgrades** | Faster, more efficient cleanup |
| 5 | **Water gun** | Cleans much faster than the mop (but note: you do not earn research points from water-gun cleaning — fine later, costly very early) |
| 6 | **Floor Tool** | A great early time-saver for laying park floors |

One early-game trick players use: deliberately let vandals spray graffiti for a while — cleaning it up yields solid research point gains at the start.

## Ticket Types — Sell the Right One

You do not just sell one ticket. From the ticket booth you choose the right pass for each customer:

- **Teen / Adult / Senior** passes
- **4 / 6 / 8 hour** durations
- **With or without Slide Pass**

Selling the wrong type makes guests leave unhappy. Get in the habit early of matching the guest (age and apparent plans) to the pass. A **cashier handles this automatically** once hired — another reason Hiring Level 1 is the top research priority.

## Troublemaker Quick Reference (First Hours)

| Type | How to Spot Them | What to Do |
|------|-----------------|------------|
| Ticket cheater | No bracelet on wrist | Right-click push them out (~$30 reward) |
| Drunk | Green bubbles, staggering | Push out before they cause chaos |
| Vandal | Spray can in hand | Push out, then clean the graffiti (bonus research points) |
| Pervert | Camera in hand | Push out immediately |
| Panicking guest | Flailing in pool | Push off diving boards / let them hit a life preserver |

The **boxing glove at the front desk** (and later the water pressure gun) is a faster ejection tool than pushing.

## Mistakes That Kill New Parks

1. **Overbuilding** — too many pools and slides means too much cleaning. Expand when your current park is clean and profitable.
2. **Ignoring puddles** — slips tank guest happiness and reviews.
3. **Selling wrong tickets** — guests leave angry; cashier fixes this.
4. **Empty pools** — injured guests, ambulance fees, and misery.
5. **Spamming flyers early** — you cannot serve the crowd you attract.
6. **Spending every research point on attractions** — Hiring Level 1 and hygiene unlocks pay back more in the first days.
7. **Ignoring the hygiene HUD** — clean water and clean slides matter more than trash disposal speed.

## Your First 3 Hours — What "Done" Looks Like

By the end of hour 3 you should have:

- A cashier hired (Hiring Level 1 researched)
- One pool, one slide, a hot dog stand, and well-placed trash cans
- A stable daily profit (not necessarily huge — stable)
- Your first few research unlocks (hygiene + food stalls)
- No ambulance bills in the last two days
- A star rating ticked up at least once

From there, the game opens up: bigger slides, wave pools, lazy rivers, and — once you hit **2.5 stars** — the Museum across the street, which starts the 1.0 endgame questlines.
