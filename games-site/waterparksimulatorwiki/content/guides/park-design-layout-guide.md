# Park Design & Layout Tips — Build a Park That Runs Itself

A well-laid-out park is a park that mostly manages itself: guests flow, queues are short, puddles are concentrated, and your cleaning time collapses. A badly laid-out park makes every system in the game fight you. These are the layout principles that work in Waterpark Simulator 1.0.

Last updated August 8, 2026.

## The Three Golden Rules

1. **One entrance funnel, one clear main path.** Guests should enter, see the best slide immediately, and naturally walk a loop back to the food. If guests can wander into dead-ends, they get lost, get tired, and leave unhappy.
2. **Cluster by function.** Wet attractions together, food together, seating together. Mixing them scatters your cleaning and restocking runs.
3. **Coverage beats decoration.** Every area of your park should be in range of a trash can and easy to reach with the mop/water gun. Decor is last.

## Entrance & Ticket Area

- **Ticket booth at the very front**, with the **hot dog stand right next to it** — guests buy food the moment they enter, and you capture revenue before their first step.
- Keep the entrance plaza **flat and wide** — bottlenecked entrances create trample-and-slip chaos.
- Place 2-3 trash cans in the plaza: guests arrive carrying wrappers from stalls.

## The Core Loop Layout

The most reliable park shape is a **figure-8 or loop**:

```
[Entrance] → [Main Path] → [Slide Zone] → [Pool Zone]
     ↑                                      |
     └──────── [Food Court] ←───────────────┘
```

- **Slide exits feed pools.** Every slide should dump its riders into or right beside a pool. That keeps wet guests where they belong and concentrates the puddle problem into one cleanable zone.
- **Diving boards behind pools**, not in front — boards are a spectator crowd magnet and block the path if front-facing.
- **Food court mid-loop**, not at the entrance and not at the far end. Guests get hungry after a ride or two; place stalls where the loop's midpoint is.

## Trash Can Placement (The Missed Detail)

When you place a trash can, the game shows you two things:

- **An arrow** — the direction guests approach from
- **A yellow zone** — the can's effective range

**Any guest outside that yellow zone will not use the can.** They drop trash on the ground instead. Practical rules:

- Face the arrow toward oncoming foot traffic
- Place cans at every junction, slide exit, and stall queue
- Place cans at regular intervals along busy stretches of main path

This single mechanic quietly decides whether your park is always messy (outside coverage) or mostly clean (inside coverage).

## Slide Design & Placement

- **Design for throughput, not just spectacle.** Long slides are great; a single tiny slide at the entrance creates a 30-person queue that never drains. Early on, two medium slides beat one enormous one.
- **Build tall where it doesn't block paths** — multi-story slides are the showpieces, but their footprint (and the crowd they draw) should sit at the park's rear so entrance traffic never clogs.
- **A big slide needs a big exit zone.** Riders need space to get out of the splash zone, or you get pile-ups and panic chains.
- Custom slides with **loops and funnels** are the S-tier earners (see the [Tier List](/guides/slide-ride-tier-list)) — build them when your cleaning and staff coverage can handle the crowd they attract.

## Water Management

- **Pools need manual refills** — put refill/cleaning workstations within reach of every pool's fill point, or you will do laps every evening.
- **Life preservers in every pool** (multi-ring stands via research). One ring near the diving board handles most incidents.
- **Slope paths away from pools.** Water that flows onto paths creates puddle fields at the worst possible spot — the walkway. If puddles keep appearing on a path, that's a layout problem, not a cleaning problem.

## Food & Vending Placement

- **Stalls at queue points** (slide exits, pool edges) — guests waiting in line buy food.
- **Vending machines at dead spots** — the corners where no staffed stall makes sense. Zero upkeep, zero breaks, but lower margin; they're coverage, not cash cows.
- **Restock path in mind:** stalls grouped together mean one restock trip serves them all.

## The Beach Map (1.0)

The 1.0 update added a **Miami-neon beach map** — a new park entrance, a city backdrop, and its own layout logic. Notes:

- New parks on the beach map are separate saves; layouts don't transfer.
- The beach layout gives you more open space for mega-slides, but the map's long coastline means **path loops are even more important** — a straight-line beach park wastes half the map.
- Theme it (Ocean decor from the Museum) for the big endgame setup.

## Layout Mistakes That Kill Parks

| Mistake | Symptom | Fix |
|---------|---------|-----|
| Entrance bottleneck | Constant slips and trampling at the gate | Widen the plaza; move stalls off the immediate path |
| Slides pointing into paths | Puddle fields on walkways | Reroute slide exits into pools |
| Trash cans out of coverage | Trash everywhere despite cans | Re-check arrows + yellow zones |
| Food far from rides | Guests leave hungry and unhappy | Food court at loop midpoint |
| Overcrowded single zone | Queue chaos, panic chains | Split capacity: second slide or pool before adding more guests |
| Decor first, infrastructure never | Pretty park, terrible reviews | Infrastructure (cans, rings, benches) before decoration |

## The Ideal Endgame Park (1.0)

By the late game your park should look like:

- One **mega-slide centerpiece** (or a Museum theme slide) at the rear
- A **wave pool** or large pool complex mid-park
- A **food court + vending ring** at the loop's midpoint
- **Full trash coverage**, benches along every path, life rings in every pool
- A **zipline network** for fast travel between zones (1.0)
- Theme decor layered last, zone by zone
