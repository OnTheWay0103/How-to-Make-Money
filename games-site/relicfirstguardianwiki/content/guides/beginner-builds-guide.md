# Beginner Builds Guide — Your First Loadouts in Arsiltus

The Relic: First Guardian has **no XP and no levels** — so a "build" is not a stat allocation; it is the combination of your weapon family, the relics you equip, the skills you buy, and the unique gear you find. For a beginner, that freedom is the danger: with 70+ relics and 12 skill trees, it is easy to build a character that does everything and survives nothing. This guide gives you two simple frameworks that work from the opening hours, and the priorities that keep them working.

---

## How a Build Works (No Levels Edition)

| Build Component | What It Does | How You Get More |
|-----------------|--------------|------------------|
| Weapon family | Your moveset + 2 skill trees (12 total across 5 families) | Chosen at start; two trees per family |
| Relics | 70+ passive fragments; freely swappable | Found in exploration; slots unlock via Shards of Awakening → Elisa at camps |
| Skills | Bought with Relic Energy | From Brutals and Ancient Relic Blessings |
| Unique gear | One-of-a-kind weapons/armor, some with Drawbacks | World drops; forged at the blacksmith |

**The beginner's key insight:** because relics are freely swappable, your build is not a promise — it is a loadout you adapt per fight. The frameworks below tell you what to *prioritize*, not what to commit to.

## Pick Your First Weapon

| Family | Beginner Rating | Why |
|--------|-----------------|-----|
| **Sword & Shield** | ★★★★★ | Native block + the game's generous parry = the safest kit in the game |
| **Longsword** | ★★★★ | Balanced; the versatile middle ground |
| **Battle Staff** | ★★★ | Skill-spamming ranged play, but no block — it needs relics first |
| **Dagger (dual blades)** | ★★★ | Fast and aggressive, but fragile |
| **Two-Handed** | ★★ | Big damage, slow swings — punishes new timing |

**Start with Sword & Shield.** The game's defense economy makes block and parry the most forgiving tools, and the parry window is confirmed generous by launch reviews. You can graduate to faster weapons once you know enemy timing.

---

## Framework 1: The Guardian (Sword & Shield) — Defense First

The beginner framework, built on the two confirmed rules: **attacks cost zero stamina, and stamina is defense-only.**

| Slot | Priority |
|------|----------|
| Skills | HP and stamina nodes first; then block/parry-supporting nodes |
| Relics | Survivability fragments — defense, sustain, stamina regen |
| Gear | High-stability shields and armor; skip Drawbacks that tax stamina |

**Play pattern:** block or parry every attack, punish with free attacks (they cost no stamina), and never let the bar hit zero mid-combo. This framework survives every boss long enough for you to learn it.

**First 3 relics to look for:** any defense fragment, any stamina fragment, any health fragment — names are being documented post-launch, so judge by effect type.

## Framework 2: The Duelist (Longsword) — Learn by Parrying

The next step: same defensive foundation, more damage.

| Slot | Priority |
|------|----------|
| Skills | Parry-tied skills, then general damage nodes |
| Relics | Hybrid — half damage, half survivability; cooldown reduction is strong |
| Gear | Whatever fits; this framework adapts to finds |

**Play pattern:** identical defense, but you spend the openings on heavier combos. The Duelist is the framework that carries you through a full first playthrough without re-spec'ing.

**Why not the others yet?** The Staff needs relic support to shine (its no-block kit punishes early deaths); Two-Handed builds require stamina discipline you have not built yet. Both are excellent *second* playthroughs.

## Skill Purchase Priority (Relic Energy)

1. **HP nodes first** — the confirmed way to raise survivability (the [How to Increase Health Guide](/guides/how-to-increase-health) covers the four methods in full).
2. **Stamina nodes second** — stamina is your only defense currency; a bigger bar is a longer life.
3. **Skill tree damage nodes third** — damage scales your clear speed, not your survival.
4. **Cooldown nodes last** — they matter most once your skills are already strong.

**Rule of thumb:** if a Brutal is killing you, your next skill purchase should be survival, not damage. The Relic Energy you spend on dying is wasted anyway.

## Gear and Drawbacks: The Beginner Rules

Unique gear is one-of-a-kind — there are **no duplicate drops**, so you cannot farm a "better version." That makes gear decisions permanent-ish:

| Gear Rule | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| Forge favorites | The blacksmith is the only way to push a favorite weapon's damage up |
| Read Drawbacks | Powerful items can carry drawbacks (stamina loss, a weakness) — always read before equipping |
| Never tax stamina | For beginners, any Drawback that costs stamina is a no-go |
| Swappable relics cover the rest | Resistances, cleanses, and sustain all live on relics — swap, don't suffer |

---

## The First-Hour Build Checklist

- [ ] Sword & Shield equipped (or Longsword if confident)
- [ ] HP/stamina skill nodes bought as Relic Energy arrives
- [ ] Survivability relics equipped — not damage relics
- [ ] Shards of Awakening found and taken to Elisa at a camp (more relic slots)
- [ ] Shards of Esser collected from temple altars (more potions — you start with 3)
