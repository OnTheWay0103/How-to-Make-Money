# Weapons Guide — All 5 Weapon Families Explained

The Relic: First Guardian offers **five weapon families**, each with its own moveset, playstyle, and skill tree. Confirmed facts about the system: there are **12 skill trees total — two per weapon family** (not 12 per weapon), skills are bought with **Relic Energy**, and **every weapon in the world is unique** — no random drops, no duplicates. Some powerful weapons carry **Drawbacks** (e.g., stamina loss or a weakness).

At launch (August 2026) the families' playstyles are confirmed by official material and reviews; specific skill-tree names and per-weapon stats are **unconfirmed** and still being documented.

---

## The 5 Families at a Glance

| Family | Playstyle (confirmed) | Defensive Option |
|--------|----------------------|------------------|
| **Sword & Shield** | Defensive, parry-focused | Block + parry (best defense in the game) |
| **Longsword** | Balanced, versatile | Parry (no block) |
| **Dagger (dual blades)** | Fast, aggressive, bursty | Dodge (no block) |
| **Two-Handed** | Heavy damage, slow | Dodge (no block) |
| **Battle Staff** | Mage-like, skill-heavy | Dodge (no block) |

Every family shares the game's core rules: **attacks cost zero stamina** (stamina is for defense only), and **skills run on cooldowns**.

---

## Sword & Shield — The Balanced Guardian

**Confirmed**: the defensive family. It is the only family with a **native block**, and the game's parry system pairs with the shield for the strongest defensive toolkit.

**Strengths**: safest playstyle; block and parry make it the best family for learning boss patterns; the parry window is described by reviews as generous.
**Weaknesses**: lower damage ceiling than aggressive families; can teach a passive habit that hurts against dodge-gate bosses (see the [Boss Strategies guide](/guides/boss-strategies)).

**Best for**: beginners, first soulslike players, anyone who hates dying to patterns.

---

## Longsword — The Versatile Duelist

**Confirmed**: the balanced middle ground — decent damage, decent speed, decent range.

**Strengths**: adapts to any encounter; the parry playstyle rewards timing without the fragility of daggers.
**Weaknesses**: no block — you must parry or dodge for defense.

**Best for**: players who want a reliable all-rounder while they learn the roster.

---

## Dagger (Dual Blades) — The Agile Assassin

**Confirmed**: the fastest family, built for aggressive burst play. (Official material sometimes calls this family "dagger" and reviews call it "dual blades" — same family.)

**Strengths**: highest attack speed; excellent for exploiting brief openings and applying pressure; the family that most rewards aggressive play — which the game's zero-stamina-attacks economy encourages.
**Weaknesses**: short range; no block; punished hard by mistakes. Reviews note heavy-feeling enemy aggression, so positioning discipline is required.

**Best for**: experienced players who want speed and burst.

---

## Two-Handed — The Heavy Hitter

**Confirmed**: slow, heavy, high-damage weapons. Preview coverage noted heavy-feeling slow attacks on this family as a trade-off for power.

**Strengths**: big damage per hit; the natural choice for players who prefer trading and big swings.
**Weaknesses**: slow recovery — whiffing is costly; no block; harder to use against fast, aggressive bosses.

**Best for**: players who like deliberate, hard-hitting combat.

---

## Battle Staff (Combat Staff) — The Skill-Caster

**Confirmed**: the mage-like family. Launch reviews describe it as the "skill-spamming" weapon — built around using skills, which run on cooldowns rather than resources.

**Strengths**: strongest synergy with the skill/cooldown system; ranged and AoE options; safer damage from distance.
**Weaknesses**: previews suggest it needs **relic support** to scale — early on it is less competitive until you collect the right fragments.

**Best for**: players who want to lean into the cooldown-skill system and don't mind a slower start.

---

## The 12 Skill Trees (Two per Family)

Confirmed: the game has **12 exclusive skill trees — two per weapon class** (sometimes described as each weapon having a "two-sided skill tree"). Key facts:

- Skills are purchased with **Relic Energy** (earned from defeating **Brutals** and finding **Ancient Relic Blessings**).
- Trees are not just offensive: some nodes **increase Health Points or Stamina**, and some **buff specific Relics**.
- You can blend trees into a unique class — official material frames the two-tree structure as the game's "class system."

**Unconfirmed**: the names of the 12 trees, node costs, and per-node effects. Guides naming trees like "Iron Wall" or "Wind Blade" are speculative.

---

## Unique Weapons and Forging

Every weapon you find is **one-of-a-kind** — there is no loot grinding for a better drop. Confirmed consequences:

1. **Exploration is how you find weapons** — hidden paths and secret areas hold uniques.
2. **The blacksmith upgrades your favorites** — with currencies and materials (exact costs unconfirmed). Since copies never re-drop, forging is how a weapon grows with you.
3. **Drawbacks are real** — some weapons carry costs (stamina loss, a weakness). Weigh them before equipping.
4. **No rarity tiers confirmed** — common/rare/legendary color-coded tiers are not verified; item value is individual, not categorical.

## Common Questions

**Q: Which weapon is best?**
A: **Unconfirmed** at launch — no verified tier data exists. All five families are described as viable; choose by playstyle. Early community sentiment and reviews lean toward sword & shield for learning and daggers/staff for aggressive play.

**Q: Can I use more than one weapon?**
A: The game's design encourages switching families between fights (each has its own tree progress). Mid-combat weapon switching is **unconfirmed**.

**Q: How do I unlock more skills?**
A: Spend **Relic Energy** in each weapon's skill tree — earned from Brutals and Ancient Relic Blessings.

**Q: Do weapons have levels?**
A: No levels in this game. Weapons improve via **blacksmith forging** (unconfirmed exact costs).

**Q: Should I upgrade my weapon early?**
A: Yes — forging your starter/favorite unique is likely the best early material sink, but confirm exact costs in-game first; wasted materials hurt because items never re-drop.
