# Probably Stolen: Underground Exchange and Reputation

The underground market in Probably Stolen is not a room you stumble into; it is a licence tier you unlock. Official store copy lists "Unlock advanced licenses to access the underground market" as a feature, and notes that some trades need "heavy machinery and permits". This page covers that path, the reputation ledgers behind it, and what remains unpublished.

> Last verified: October 28, 2026 · Written from official pre-release material. The development build is not in our hands, so everything marked [Unconfirmed] has no published answer.

## The licence gate is the real entrance

The store page states the route plainly: advanced licences open the underground market. It also explains why the shopfront cannot carry you on its own, because "most of your best margins live in legal gray zones". The same page sets the two halves against each other:

> "Some trades need heavy machinery and permits, others just a good hiding spot and a straight face."

Permits are the slow, inspectable route; hiding is the fast, raid-prone one. Weapons "require permits and modifications", which is why the gunsmith line is described as "high margins, security inspections".

Licences are listed as a feature rather than a quest reward, and the store page ties them specifically to underground access. What is not documented: the licence price, how many tiers exist, which vendor sells them, and whether faction standing is a prerequisite. All of that is [Unconfirmed].

| Access step | Status | What the official text actually says |
|---|---|---|
| Run a legal storefront | Confirmed | "Run your store in a dystopian station" |
| Buy product-line permits | Confirmed for weapons | "Weapons require permits and modifications" |
| Unlock advanced licences | Confirmed as a feature | "Unlock advanced licenses to access the underground market" |
| Licence cost, tiers, vendor | [Unconfirmed] | No official figure or location published |
| Entrance location on the station | [Unconfirmed] | Never described in any official post |

## Two reputation ledgers, not one

Probably Stolen tracks reputation on more than one axis, and mixing them up is how players get caught. The store page warns that "Reputation buys access and protection, but it also paints a target on your back", which only makes sense once you separate the ledgers.

**Security reputation** is the authorities' view of you. We know it exists because a patch note removed a way to farm it: in 48.3.12 the studio wrote that "A cleared inspection that resulted from showing contraband in the display case will no longer grant Security reputation". The stated reason was to stop players "farming free Security reputation by repeatedly displaying contraband and then hiding it in the smuggler bay to pass inspection". So passing inspection was a Security reputation source, and hiding goods in the smuggler bay was the exploit.

**Black Market reputation** is the other side's view of you. Two official numbers attach to it: the Thief's stock "now scales with Black Market reputation" (UPDATE 049), and at -40 reputation "players now retain access to the janitorial service, but the cost is doubled" (48.3.10).

Those effects are the whole documented payoff, and only one is a shop. The Thief's inventory grows with your standing, so the black market is not a fixed catalogue you save up for; it pays back in stock quality rather than discounts, and no scaling formula is published, so the rate is [Unconfirmed]. The janitorial service marks the floor instead: the market does not cut you off, it prices you out. What it cleans up is [Unconfirmed], though the obvious reading is the aftermath of a raid.

| Ledger | Documented movement | Documented effect | Unknown |
|---|---|---|---|
| Security | Cleared inspections (before 48.3.12); display-case contraband no longer counts | No effect is published; the only data point is that inspections used to grant it | Rates, decay, and whether it gates permits |
| Black Market | Not described | Thief stock scaling; janitorial service retained at -40 but at double cost | How to gain it, what else it unlocks |
| Factions | Not described | "exclusive deals" from "the right factions" | Named factions, thresholds, which deals |

The table is deliberately lopsided. Official material says what reputation does at the edges and almost nothing about how it accumulates.

## Faction deals and who is across the counter

The store page describes faction play as relationship building rather than a bar to fill: "Navigate cutthroat politics and build connections with the right factions to get the exclusive deals". It also says your counter is a stage for them:

> "Today's customer could be a security fisher for evidence, a revolutionary seeking leverage, or a fallen elite testing your usefulness."

Those three archetypes matter because they map onto the four things you can do with any customer: "Approve it, refuse it, report it, or look away; someone is always taking notes."

| Customer archetype | Official description | Why they matter to a smuggler |
|---|---|---|
| Security fisher | Collecting evidence | The sale is a trap; this is where evidence generation matters |
| Revolutionary | Seeking leverage | Wants something you have; leverage implies pressure later |
| Fallen elite | Testing your usefulness | The high-budget buyer, and the one worth selling counterfeit goods to |

What no official source says is which factions exist, what the exclusive deals are, or the standing each requires. Faction names circulating elsewhere appear in no Steam post or store copy, so treat them as [Unconfirmed].

## Hiding contraband without farming reputation

Hiding is a documented mechanic with an exploit history. The store page lists it directly: "Hide stolen goods and contraband from the authorities (or bribe your way out if you get caught)". That gives you two failure responses, hiding and bribery, and one warning: "contraband brings raids".

The 48.3.12 note is the most instructive patch in the set, because it shows how the intended loop works. Players were putting contraband in the display case to trigger an inspection, then moving it to the smuggler bay to pass, and collecting Security reputation for the cleared inspection. The studio closed it to stop "farming free Security reputation", which tells you two things: the case and the bay are separate containers with different audiences, and a clean inspection is no longer a reputation source by itself.

The practical rule: hide to survive, not to farm. The replacement reputation sources are [Unconfirmed].

## Bargaining: the trade-off is the mechanic

Haggling is listed as a feature with a warning attached: "Haggle for the best deals, but remember that every bargain comes with a trade-off". UPDATE 049 says the studio "Adjusted how bargaining affects customer budgets", and in the same patch "Some T3 buyers from the display case now scale their budget".

Read those two lines together and you get the shape of it: bargaining moves the customer's budget rather than the sticker price, and some high-tier buyers' budgets scale, so a bargain against a T3 buyer is worth less than it looks. The direction of the 049 adjustment is not stated, so whether bargaining now raises or lowers budgets is [Unconfirmed].

Bargaining is not free money, then. Bargain where the trade-off is cheap, on repeat customers and on lines where you control supply, and take the listed price on one-off high-value sales.

## Perks that change the risk maths

The patch notes are the only place perk numbers appear, and they do not all move the same way.

| Perk | Official change | What it means at the counter |
|---|---|---|
| Model Citizen | "credit bonus from reporting criminal customers increased from 20% to 50%" | Reporting now pays a 50% credit bonus instead of 20%; the informant build got real |
| Intimidating | "evidence generation reduction increased from 25% to 33%, and starting point cost reduced from 3 to 2" | Less evidence per interaction and a cheaper buy, so it fits earlier in a build |
| Addiction perks | "trading penalty increased from 5% to 8%" | A nerf, and a warning: the addiction line is a tax you pay at the counter |

The Intimidating line also confirms a build cost structure: perks are bought with points at the start, and this one now costs two instead of three. Whether you can earn more points later is [Unconfirmed].

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I reach the underground exchange?

Through advanced licences, which the store page lists as an unlockable feature. The station location, the licence price and the exact unlock condition are [Unconfirmed].

### Does bad Black Market reputation lock me out?

Not according to the only official data point. At -40 reputation you keep the janitorial service, but it costs double. The market appears to price you out rather than close.

### Can I still farm Security reputation with the display case?

No. Since playtest patch 48.3.12, a cleared inspection that came from showing contraband in the display case no longer grants Security reputation.

### Does bargaining change what a customer can spend?

Yes, and some T3 buyers now scale their budget too. The direction of the change and its size are [Unconfirmed].

### Are the faction names published anywhere official?

No. Faction deals are confirmed as a system, but no faction has been named in a Steam post or on the store page.
