# Probably Stolen Aquafarmer Guide: Tanks, Roe, Caviar

> Last verified: October 28, 2026 · Built from the official Aquafarmer announcement; unpublished values are marked [Unconfirmed]

The Aquafarmer in Probably Stolen begins with a basic water circulation system, a stock of tilapia and a supply of fish food. Then it becomes three jobs: keeping tank water inside each species' habitat requirements, running roe to fry to mature fish, and choosing which fish to butcher and which to open with Surgery Tools for caviar.

## What the scenario starts you with

The announcement is unusually concrete about the opening state:

> "You begin with a basic water circulation system, a stock of tilapia, and a supply of fish food."

Three assets, three consequences. You have a working system rather than parts, so the first tank already runs. You have fish, so the breeding clock starts on day one. You have feed, which means feeding is a running cost that will run out.

The official one-liner names the two halves of the route: "Breed fish. Manage their tanks and water quality." Every task in this guide belongs to one of those halves.

Aquafarmer is one of nine 1.0 starting scenarios, and after picking it you choose from four difficulty options. The goal is the same on every route: earn enough to buy out and own the shop before rising rent forces your hand.

## The tank is the thing you upgrade

The announcement names exactly three variables you manage:

> "Each has its own habitat requirements, so you'll need to manage the tank's water quality, filtration, and energy cell"

- **Water quality.** Species-specific and something you must manage. Safe ranges per species are [Unconfirmed].
- **Filtration.** Named as part of tank management. Filter tiers, throughput and replacement are [Unconfirmed].
- **Energy cell.** Named as a component of the tank. Draw, duration and recharging are [Unconfirmed].

The reading that follows: because requirements are per species, the tank is a configuration rather than a container. Upgrading means making a tank suit a species, not buying a better fish. When you add eels or sturgeons, expect to reconfigure rather than simply expand.

That also sets where early attention goes: buying more fish into a tank that is not configured for them is the expensive mistake, and reconfiguring a tank you own is the cheap one.

## The species ladder

> "There are different varieties of tilapia before expanding into more valuable species, such as eels and sturgeons."

| Stage | Documented species | Documented role | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Start | Tilapia, several varieties | Starting stock | Varieties confirmed; names [Unconfirmed] |
| Expand | Eels | Named as a "more valuable" species | Requirements, prices [Unconfirmed] |
| Expand | Sturgeons | "More valuable", and the species named for caviar | Requirements [Unconfirmed] |

"More valuable" is the developers' own phrasing and the only official value ordering, so treat it as a direction rather than a number. No eel or sturgeon price appears in the official notes.

Two practical notes. The tilapia tier already contains several varieties, so sorting within the starter tier is the first optimisation available. And since each species has its own habitat requirements, adding a species is a tank project rather than a purchase.

## Breeding: roe, eggs, fry, mature fish

> "your fish may reproduce by laying and fertilizing roe. The eggs will eventually hatch into swarms of fry, which will grow into mature fish."

Four things sit inside that sentence.

**"May" is a possibility, not a schedule.** A cycle with no roe is not automatically a bug.

**Laying and fertilising are separate steps,** which reads as needing both parties present. Pairing rules and sex ratios are [Unconfirmed].

**"Eventually" and "will grow" describe waiting,** and neither timing is published, so plan capacity from what you observe.

**"Swarms of fry" moves the bottleneck.** Once a hatch succeeds, the constraint shifts from breeding to tank capacity, so decide where fry go before the eggs hatch.

## Harvest: knife for meat, Surgery Tools for caviar

> "Use a knife to slaughter and butcher fish for their meat. Certain fish may also provide unique resources beyond meat. You can also use Surgery Tools on sturgeons to harvest caviar."

| Tool | Target | Output | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Knife | Any fish | Meat | Confirmed |
| Knife | "Certain fish" | "Unique resources beyond meat" | Confirmed; which fish is [Unconfirmed] |
| Surgery Tools | Sturgeons | Caviar | Confirmed; yield is [Unconfirmed] |

The design point is that one animal has two different harvests depending on the tool. A sturgeon butchered with a knife gives meat; a sturgeon opened with Surgery Tools gives caviar. That is a resource-allocation decision, and it means your best fish should not be treated as food.

Editorially, slaughter and butcher read as consuming the animal, so keep a breeding block you never harvest; nothing official describes restocking.

Surgery Tools are a separate acquisition from the knife, so the caviar step carries a gear prerequisite. Where they come from and whether they wear out are [Unconfirmed].

## The maintenance rhythm

No official schedule exists, so this routine is ours, built around the three named variables.

1. **Water quality first, every session.** It ties to species requirements, so it is the variable that can invalidate a tank.
2. **Confirm filtration is running.** Named as part of tank management, so check it rather than assume it.
3. **Check the energy cell.** Also named, and the component that stops everything else working.
4. **Feed, and count what is left.** You start with a supply of fish food, so the count is your real timer. How feed is replenished is [Unconfirmed].
5. **Handle each hatch before it becomes a capacity problem.** "Swarms of fry" is a warning about volume.
6. **Decide the product before the tool.** Knife or Surgery Tools, meat or caviar, never both from one fish.

One quality-of-life note from the same patch cycle: the simultaneous inventory window cap rose from 10 to 25, which matters when you are moving feed, breeding stock and harvests at once.

## Common failure points

| Failure | Why the official text implies it | Prevention |
|---|---|---|
| New species in an unchanged tank | "Each has its own habitat requirements" | Reconfigure before the fish arrive |
| Water quality left to drift | It is listed as something you manage | Check it first, every session |
| Energy cell runs dry | It is a named tank component | Inspect it with the other two variables |
| Expecting roe every cycle | Reproduction "may" happen | Keep more breeding stock than you need |
| Butchering the sturgeon you wanted caviar from | Knife gives meat; caviar needs Surgery Tools | Choose the product first |
| Harvesting the whole breeding block | Slaughter reads as consuming the fish | Hold fish back from the knife |

| Route | Official one-liner | What it asks of you |

The right-hand column is our reading. Official difficulty guidance names only the ends: Apprentice is "Best for beginners" and Rock Bottom is "Maximum difficulty". Nothing ranks Aquafarmer.

Structurally, this route carries the most continuous obligations of the nine. A shop counter waits for a customer; a tank does not.
