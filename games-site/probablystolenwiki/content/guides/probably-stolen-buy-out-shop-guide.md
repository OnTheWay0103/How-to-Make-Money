# Probably Stolen: How to Buy Out the Shop and Win

Probably Stolen's win condition is a purchase. The 1.0 announcement calls it the Game Ending: you "purchase and own the store", and the game hands you THE STATS. Rent rises regardless of how well your week went, so the run is a race between your margin and the rent clock.

> Last verified: October 28, 2026 · Built from official store copy and Steam announcements. The buy-out price and rent figures have never been published, so they are marked [Unconfirmed] rather than guessed.

## The only published win condition

The studio stated it directly in the 1.0 feature list:

> "Game Ending — Where you can purchase and own the store. However, now when achieving this massive goal, you'll get THE STATS. See your most profitable sale, the days survived, total worth, perks and more numbers to compare it to your more successful friends."

Two things stand out. First, the buy-out is framed as a goal you achieve, not an upgrade you buy when convenient, which is why the short description tells you to "Earn enough to buy out your shop before rising rent forces your hand". Second, the reward is a scoreboard: four run-wide numbers plus "more", framed as something to compare with friends.

What the announcement does not say: the price, whether the offer arrives as a menu or a conversation, and whether you can decline and keep playing. Those are [Unconfirmed].

## Rent is the clock, not a bill

The store page dedicates its own paragraph to rent and states it bluntly: "Rent increases over time, regardless of how well your week went."

The rest of the paragraph is a causal chain:

> "If you fall behind, the consequences are practical. Your store loses attractiveness, fewer customers walk in, revenue drops, and the gap widens. Recovering from that slide often means taking on riskier deals just to stabilize your cash flow."

That is the difficulty curve, and it explains why the buy-out is a race rather than a grind. Falling behind does not take money directly; it takes your customers, which takes next week's money, which pushes you toward contraband.

| Stage | What the studio says happens | What to watch |
|---|---|---|
| Paying on time | Nothing stated; rent still rises over time | Your margin per week, not your total cash |
| Falling behind | Store loses attractiveness, fewer customers walk in | Customer count before revenue |
| Slide | Revenue drops and the gap widens | Gap growth, not the absolute debt |
| Recovery | Riskier deals to stabilise cash flow | Whether the risk is a licence you hold |

The initial rent, any grace period and whether you can be evicted are all [Unconfirmed]. Not one official post gives a number, though the official developer page does describe the station's own cut rising over time alongside rent.

## Three routes to the buy-out

Every documented economy feeds the same number, and the store page says the lines are not interchangeable: "Every product line has its own profit curve and its own way of ruining you."

| Route | Documented mechanics | Capital profile | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Legal retail | Water "must be tested and filtered"; weapons need permits and modifications; chemicals rely on recipes | Slow, steady, permit-gated | Inspections before resale, plus product liability |
| Black market | Advanced licences open the underground market; the Thief's stock scales with Black Market reputation | Cheapest entry, needs standing | "contraband brings raids" |
| Vending machines | New in 1.0: "Unlock the Vending Machines that sell goods overnight after your close shop to generate more profit" | Upfront unlock, then passive | Unknown; no numbers published |

**Legal retail** is the route the store page describes most fully. Cigarette packs "need visual inspection before resale", water "must be tested and filtered", rare items "need proper appraisal to avoid overpaying". Each is a skill check between you and the margin, which is why the gunsmith line is summarised as "high margins, security inspections".

**Black market** has the best documented scaling and the documented raid risk; the store page's advice is to fence "discounted, stolen goods if you believe Security won't reach you". The [black market and reputation guide](/guides/probably-stolen-black-market-reputation-guide) covers the licence gate and both reputation ledgers.

**Vending machines** are the 1.0 addition and the only confirmed passive income. The wording matters: they sell "overnight after your close shop", so they turn stock you already own into money while the shop is shut. They are not free money; they stop inventory sitting idle.

No profit figure exists for any of the three. Any comparison of credits per hour is [Unconfirmed].

## Which start makes the buy-out shortest

After picking a starting scenario you "can choose between 4 difficulty options", so the scenario sets where you begin and the difficulty decides how much pressure you face. The official one-liners tell you which is which.

| Scenario | Official description | Read for a buy-out run |
|---|---|---|
| Apprentice | "Best for beginners. Be a generalist. Upgrade as you go." | Most forgiving; slowest specialisation |
| Fence | "Stolen goods." | Starts closest to the black market route |
| Water Merchant | "Test, purify, re-label or don't. Liability is yours." | Liability-heavy; re-labelling is the margin lever |
| Gunsmith | "Weapons and mods. High margins, security inspections." | High margins, gated by permits and inspections |
| Rock Bottom | "You start with nothing. Maximum difficulty." | Challenge run, not a first buy-out |

For a first buy-out, pick the line whose risk you can already manage rather than the best rumoured margin. The [starting scenarios guide](/guides/probably-stolen-starting-scenarios-guide) breaks down all nine.

## An order of operations for the buy-out

1. **Read the rent before you buy stock.** Rent rises regardless of your week, so the first calculation is weekly rent against expected margin.
2. **Pick one line and master its check.** Inspection, filtration, appraisal or recipe; a line you cannot check absorbs your cost.
3. **Never let the gap open.** Recovery means riskier deals, and riskier deals are how a stable shop becomes a raid.
4. **Add passive income before expanding.** Vending machines sell overnight, the only documented way to earn while closed.
5. **Use the counter decisions deliberately.** You can "Approve it, refuse it, report it, or look away", and reporting pays a 50% credit bonus with Model Citizen.
6. **Keep one big sale in reserve.** THE STATS tracks your most profitable single sale.

## THE STATS: reading the scoreboard

The buy-out does not just end the run; it grades it. Four fields are named, plus "more numbers".

| Field | What it measures | How to move it before the ending |
|---|---|---|
| Most profitable sale | The single best transaction of the run | High-value single items: appraised rarities, counterfeit goods sold to elites, genetically modified breeds |
| Days survived | How long the run took | Better margins finish sooner; the clock is rent, not playtime |
| Total worth | Value accumulated by the end | Cash plus assets; whether unsold stock counts is [Unconfirmed] |
| Perks | The perk set you finished with | Perks cost starting points, and Intimidating now costs 2 instead of 3 |
| "More numbers" | Not itemised | [Unconfirmed] |

Optimising for one field costs another: a total-worth run hoards inventory, a most-profitable-sale run chases single big tickets. Decide which number you want judged on before day one.

## The ending you can lose

Not every ending is a win. Playtest patch 48.3.10 changed a failure state: "Players can no longer lose the game with the 'Augmented' ending for missing too many augs, unless playing on Hard Mode."

So the Augmented ending is a loss condition tied to missing augs, and outside Hard Mode it no longer ends your run. The note predates 1.0 and the 1.0 announcement does not contradict it, so on normal difficulty a low-aug run keeps going. Hard Mode restores the older rule. How many augs is "too many" to miss is [Unconfirmed].

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need to buy the shop to finish the game?

The Game Ending is the documented goal: you purchase and own the store and the game shows THE STATS. Whether other endings exist beyond the Augmented loss condition is [Unconfirmed].

### How much does the buy-out cost?

No figure has been published. Any specific price is either taken from one save file or invented.

### Can rent actually end my run?

The store page describes consequences, not eviction: attractiveness, customers and revenue fall. Whether a hard failure exists is [Unconfirmed].

### Is the vending machine worth unlocking?

It is the only confirmed overnight income, so it fits any run that ends the day with unsold stock. Its cost and output are [Unconfirmed].

### Which difficulty should a first buy-out run use?

The game offers 4 difficulty options after the scenario choice. Hard Mode re-enables the Augmented loss condition, the only documented mechanical difference between difficulties.
