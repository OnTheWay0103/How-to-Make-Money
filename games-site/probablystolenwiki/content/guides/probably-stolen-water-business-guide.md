# Probably Stolen Water Purification and Wine Guide

> Last verified: October 28, 2026 · Built from official pre-launch announcements; unpublished values are marked [Unconfirmed]

The Water Merchant scenario in Probably Stolen hands you one line of instruction: test the water, purify it, then re-label it or do not, and accept that the liability is yours. This guide walks that chain, covers the Premium Spring Water tag rule and the Wine Extract liquid, and compares the water line with the other routes.

## What the Water Merchant actually is

The official one-liner is deliberately unsentimental:

> "Water Merchant: Test, purify, re-label or don't. Liability is yours."

It ships as one of nine 1.0 starting scenarios, listed in the comparison table below, and after picking one you choose from four difficulty options. The Water Merchant was introduced as the new scenario of the September 14 public demo, so its shape predates launch even though its numbers do not.

The surrounding economy is the game's economy. The store page says you craft, trade and smuggle "goods of questionable origin" and must earn enough to buy out your shop before rising rent forces your hand. The 1.0 announcement describes the ending as purchasing and owning the store. Water funds that buy-out; it is not a separate game.

## The four verbs, and what they leave out

The official description is a sequence of decisions, not a recipe.

| Step | What the official text confirms | What it does not publish |
|---|---|---|
| Test | A test step exists and comes before the sale | Testing tool, output, purity grades — [Unconfirmed] |
| Purify | Purification is distinct from testing | Method, station, failure states — [Unconfirmed] |
| Re-label | Selling under a different label is a choice | Label items, costs, detection rules — [Unconfirmed] |
| Don't | Skipping the re-label is equally available | No stated consequence beyond liability — [Unconfirmed] |

The pattern holds across this game's official material: systems are announced, values are not. No official note contains a purity threshold, a purification yield or a container price; any such figure you see elsewhere is worth checking.

Because testing precedes purification, budget for stock you cannot yet identify: water you cannot test is water you cannot honestly label.

## The water trading guide comes from Dr. Jackson

A playtest patch note records a specific fix: "Water trading guide can now be bought from Dr. Jackson." The instruction set is a purchase, not a starting item, which suggests the early water trade is meant to be learned by doing.

Dr. Jackson is a vendor with gated stock. The Update 049 note says "The Toolbox can be bought from Dr. Jackson after day 14", so his inventory is time-gated in at least one case. Whether the water trading guide sits behind a similar threshold is [Unconfirmed].

## Premium Spring Water and the Water tag

The clearest published rule about the water line is a tag rule: "Premium Spring Water now has the Water tag, but non-Upper Level customers and tourists will not accept it."

**Items carry tags.** Water is a tag a product can hold, and this patch adds it to a premium product.

**Customers are stratified.** The note names Upper Level customers and tourists separately. The same patch mentions "some T3 buyers from the display case" scaling their budget, so tiering runs deeper than those two names.

**A premium product is not a universal product.** Your best water can be the wrong item for the customer in front of you.

What does not follow is how the game decides who walks in; nothing published ties customer mix to price, display or reputation. Plan for mixed traffic and mark assumptions [Unconfirmed].

| Customer group | Accepts Premium Spring Water | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Upper Level customers | Not excluded; the patch only rules the others out | Update 049 wording |
| Non-Upper Level customers | No | Update 049, stated |
| Tourists | No | Update 049, stated |
| T3 buyers | [Unconfirmed]; only their budget scaling is mentioned | Update 049 |

The commercial reading is blunt. A premium water line needs customers who accept the tag, and tourists are not them. If traffic is mostly walk-ins, premium stock is inventory you cannot move.

## Wine, Wine Extract and liquid containers

The wine rule is a container rule. Update 049 states: "Pouring wine into a liquid container now produces a new Wine Extract liquid, which changes the color of the liquid."

What that confirms: wine is handled as a pourable liquid rather than only as a sealed bottle, pouring creates a distinct Wine Extract, and the contents change colour so a container shows what it holds. It does not confirm whether Wine Extract is worth more than the wine it came from, whether it carries tags, or whether it shares a container with water. Treat value, stacking and mixing rules as [Unconfirmed].

For the wine route, containers become dual-use. A container that held water can now hold Wine Extract, and colour is the only signal the game gives you at a glance. Keep the two lines separate until mixing rules are published.

## "Liability is yours" is a system, not a slogan

That sentence does real work, because the game tracks the machinery behind it.

- **Evidence exists.** Update 049 raises a perk's evidence-generation reduction from 25% to 33% and drops its cost from 3 points to 2; a reduction perk implies evidence is generated.
- **Criminal customers can be reported.** The same patch raises the credit bonus for reporting criminal customers from 20% to 50%.
- **Inspections are real.** A playtest patch fixes a reputation exploit involving contraband shown in the display case and then hidden in the smuggler bay to pass inspection. Both the bay and the inspection exist.

So a mislabelled product is not cosmetic. The scenario puts liability on the player because the station has mechanisms — evidence, reporting, inspection — that can turn a label into a problem. Which one triggers on a bad label is [Unconfirmed]; only the systems are documented.

## Water versus the other routes

The official one-liners are the fairest comparison available, because they are the developers' own summaries and contain no income claims.

The right-hand column is our reading, not the developers' words. Water's difficulty sits in judgement rather than equipment: no station to buy, no recipe to unlock, just what you write on a container.

## A first-week routine for the water line

This order is ours; each step is anchored above.

1. **Buy the water trading guide from Dr. Jackson** as soon as his stock allows.
2. **Test before you commit stock.** Unidentified water is a liability you are carrying, not inventory.
3. **Purify only what you intend to sell as premium.** The "or don't" makes purification a choice; whether un-purified stock sells cheaper is [Unconfirmed].
4. **Match the label to the customer.** Premium Spring Water is for Upper Level buyers; tourists refuse it.
5. **Keep wine containers away from water containers.** Wine Extract is generated on pour and recolours the liquid, so a shared container conceals both products.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do you make pure water in Probably Stolen?**
Testing and purification are listed as separate steps, so purification is an action performed on tested water. The tool, station and purity scale are [Unconfirmed].

**Can tourists buy Premium Spring Water?**
No. The Update 049 note says non-Upper Level customers and tourists will not accept it.

**What is Wine Extract?**
A separate liquid produced when wine is poured into a liquid container, which also changes the liquid's colour. Its value and tags are [Unconfirmed].

**Is relabelling water illegal in the game?**
The description does not say illegal, it says the liability is yours. The station has evidence, reporting and inspection systems; how they respond to a bad label is [Unconfirmed].

**Where do I get the water trading guide?**
From Dr. Jackson, per the 48.3.10 playtest patch note.

**Is the water route the best way to make money?**
No official income data exists for any scenario, so this is [Unconfirmed]. Officially, Apprentice is "Best for beginners" and Rock Bottom is "Maximum difficulty".
