Your first Neon Abyss 2 runs go better if you treat them as resource problems, not shooting problems. Learn the loop, spend Faith on purpose, stack items instead of swapping them, and change four settings before your second run — that is the whole beginner curriculum, and it fits in this page.


## 1. What a Run Actually Is

Neon Abyss 2 is a run-based roguelike. You start in the Black Dog Bar, drop into the Abyss, clear rooms, and leave each floor through a boss portal. Everything you earn inside a run is temporary; what persists is your Agent roster, Hatchmon collection, unlocks, and records.

| Phase | What you do | What carries over |
|---|---|---|
| Bar | Pick an Agent, review profiles, start a run | Agent choice, unlocks, DLC content |
| Floors | Clear rooms, take items, spend Faith, recruit Hatchmon | Nothing until the run ends |
| Boss portal | Kill the floor boss, choose to continue deeper | Floor progress for the run |
| Safehouse | Pause, recover, exit the run if portals are locked | Run state only |
| End of run | Die or take the exit portal back to the bar | Meta progression, records, achievements |

The safehouse rule is worth memorising because it prevents a wasted night: as of the October 10–16 update, "the safehouse now provides an exit to end the run when all boss portals are locked," and the in-run timer **pauses** while you are in the safehouse. Before that patch, waiting in a safehouse could time out a portal you still needed.


## 2. Four Settings to Change Before Your Second Run

The 1.0 and October updates added quality-of-life options that most guides skip. Change these first; they save minutes per run.

1. **Dismantle hold time is now 1 second.** The October update cut it from 2 seconds. You do not need to configure it — but you do need to stop treating dismantling as a slow, risky action. Breaking down a bad weapon mid-run is now cheap.
2. **Turn on tutorial skip for extra saves.** Once one save has cleared the tutorial, other saves can skip it. That matters because the achievement for clearing the game now asks for **3 different Agents**, not 10 (changed in the same update), so you will be running more than one save.
3. **Separate graphics quality from frame rate.** 1.0 lets you set them independently: on PC, Medium/High quality with 60, 120, or uncapped frame rate, with VSync handled by your display. Ultrawide users get 21:9 support including 2560×1080, 3440×1440 and 3840×1600.
4. **Leave Infinite Energy off.** It exists, and Veewo's own warning is blunt: it "will significantly reduce difficulty" and could "easily reduce enjoyment." Use it as an accessibility tool, not as a first-run crutch.

Two smaller ones: controller vibration and screen shake now have separate sliders, and 16:10 resolutions were restored in the October patch.


## 3. Faith: The Most Misread Resource

Faith is the in-run currency that decides whether a floor goes well. Veewo describes three sinks, and they are genuinely different decisions:

| Use | What you get | When it is right |
|---|---|---|
| Buy from the shop | Powerful items | You already have a build direction and one item closes a gap |
| Recruit Hatchmon | A companion that fights with you | Early floor, or when a boss challenge needs a Hatchmon count |
| Gamble | A chance-based payout | You are overstocked on Faith and cannot reach a shop |

The trap is treating Faith as a score. It does not carry between runs, and hoarding it to the end of a floor usually means leaving value behind. Spend down to near-zero before a boss portal unless you know a shop is next.

Hatchmon are not decorative. They hatch, evolve, and fight, and one boss challenge explicitly requires holding **at least 10 Hatchmons** before entering the next floor (the Dark Aphrodite challenge, via Hermes). If you plan to chase manager bosses, recruit early and do not skip egg rooms.


## 4. Items: Stack, Do Not Swap

The store description says it plainly: "Random item drops in the Abyss grant you powers that combine in surprising and powerful ways. Stack as many as you can… there's no limit to how many items you can wield!"

That single rule changes how you should read every pickup prompt:

- **Take the item even if it looks weak.** Items are multipliers on each other, not a loadout of four slots. The cost of a mediocre item is small; the cost of missing a synergy is a run.
- **Do not dismantle items to "clean up."** Dismantle weapons you cannot use, not passives that might combine later.
- **Read your weapon's affixes before swapping.** Since the Weapon Reforge update, each weapon carries static and dynamic affixes, and both weapons and affixes have rarities: Common, Rare, Epic, Legendary. A lower-damage weapon with a Legendary affix can beat a higher-damage one with nothing.
- **Use Tempering to add, not replace.** Weapon upgrades offer the usual two direct choices, plus Temper — which adds a specific affix instead. If your weapon's base damage is fine and its affix is the problem, Temper solves it.

Weapon XP comes from souls. The 1.0 update changed the Weapon XP Hexjar to release souls in set amounts for you to pick up, so souls are a deliberate resource: collect them when the floor is safe, not while a boss is charging.


## 5. The Blackdog Patch Is a Safety Net, Not a Cheat

The Blackdog Patch system was rebuilt in the March update, and the rules are unusually clear:

- Every boss you defeat earns **one** Blackdog Patch charge.
- A patch costs **three** charges.
- If you fail a run outright, you are given the three charges immediately.
- You **cannot earn charges** in a run where a patch is already active.

Read that as designed intent: the patch is there to stop a bad streak, not to be farmed. If you are sitting on three charges and the current run is going well, spending them is usually a waste. If you have just lost twice in a row, the game will hand you the charges anyway.


## 6. Economy: Where Coins Should Go

Coins, hearts, bombs, keys and eggs are all currencies in this game, and 1.0 made them more interchangeable. The Smiley Merchant change is the important one: if the merchant wants a resource you do not want to give, **hold the interaction button and spend coins to reroll the payment type** across coins, hearts, bombs, keys or eggs.

| Resource | Best early use | Wasteful early use |
|---|---|---|
| Coins | Reroll merchant payment types; shops | Buying items that break your synergy |
| Keys | Doors, locked rooms | Unlocking rooms you will not clear |
| Bombs | Destructible walls, escape routes | Clearing rooms you already cleared |
| Hearts | Health buffer before a boss | Topping off when a heal room is next |
| Eggs | Hatchmon recruitment | Hoarding for a Hatchmon you cannot evolve yet |

1.0 also added **Great Wonder Blocks** to some shops and Athena reward rooms, which means rerolling a shop is now a build decision rather than a coin sink. If a Great Wonder Block appears and your build has a hole, take it.


## 7. Pick the Right Mode

Storm Breach left beta with 1.0. Veewo describes it as "a faster, more streamlined game mode perfect for our 1-4 player co-op" with no dungeon exploration — you go from battle to battle, gaining upgrades between them.

- **Storm Breach** if you want to practise combat, test a build quickly, or play with friends who have 30 minutes rather than 90.
- **Classic mode** if you want exploration, secrets, and the boss-route challenges described in our [boss list](/guides/neon-abyss-2-boss-list).

New players should spend their first hour in Storm Breach. It compresses the learning loop: more fights per hour, fewer navigation mistakes, faster feedback on which weapons and affixes actually work.


## 8. Eight Mistakes That Cost New Agents Runs

1. **Hoarding Faith** until the floor ends.
2. **Skipping items** because they look minor — stacking is the whole design.
3. **Ignoring affix rarity** and judging weapons on damage numbers alone.
4. **Spending Blackdog charges** on a run that is going fine.
5. **Fighting a manager boss** before its prerequisite: Dark Aphrodite needs regular Aphrodite dead first.
6. **Updating mid-run.** Both the 1.0 notes and the October roundup say to finish your current run first, because updating can break an in-progress run.
7. **Mixing branches.** If a friend is on the Beta branch, you cannot play online together.
8. **Waiting in the safehouse** for a portal that is timing out — that was fixed, but the habit still wastes the timer you do have.


## 9. A Reasonable First Week

| Day | Goal | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Two Storm Breach runs, no shopping | Learn enemy patterns and weapon feel without economy pressure |
| 2 | First Classic run, tutorial skip on later saves | See the exploration loop and boss portals |
| 3 | Clear one floor with a deliberate item stack | Test the stacking rule instead of swapping |
| 4 | Spend a full floor's Faith on Hatchmon | Learn the companion system and hit 10 Hatchmon once |
| 5 | Temper a weapon instead of upgrading it | Understand affix-driven damage |
| 6 | Co-op session with three Agents | Separate rooms, shared bosses |
| 7 | Push for the 3-Agent clear achievement | The requirement is 3, not 10, since October |

That schedule is editorial, but every rule it exercises comes from the official notes cited on this page.
