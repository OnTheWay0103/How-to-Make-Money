Veewo has officially named four Agents — R-7, Cassie, Mira and Tino — and says the roster is bigger than that. Each Agent keeps its own profile after 1.0, and random selection is back. This page lists what official posts confirm, and marks everything else as unconfirmed.

## 1. What an Agent Is in Neon Abyss 2

An Agent is the character you take into the Abyss. You pick one at the Black Dog Bar before a run. Everything else about the run — items, weapons, Faith, Hatchmon — is earned inside it, so the Agent is the one decision that carries across runs.

The 1.0 notes changed two things about that decision:

| Change in 1.0 | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| **Random Agent selection returns** | You can ask the bar to pick for you, which Veewo framed as "allowing players to get a little more variety" |
| **Per-Agent profiles** | Each Agent tracks "their abilities as well as boss kill record and highest Fate record" |

That second change is the practically useful one. Before 1.0, choosing an Agent was a per-run pick with no memory. After 1.0, the game shows you how far each Agent has gone, which makes "which Agent have I actually cleared with" a question the UI can answer instead of you.

Profiles also matter for the boss-route challenges. The 1.0 notes say the profile data "makes it easier to plan your next boss route challenge" — the Dark Aphrodite challenge, for example, asks you to reach the next floor through Hermes while holding at least 10 Hatchmons, and knowing your kill history per Agent tells you whether that line is already open for you.

## 3. Tino: The Only Agent With a Documented Power

Tino is a street-dwelling rapper whose mechanic is duplication: he can copy Wonder Jars at the cost of some health. In practice that makes him a build-repetition Agent — if you find a Wonder Jar that fixes a problem in your run, Tino is the only character who can make a second one.

The health cost is the whole balance. Duplicating is a trade, not a free reroll, so the useful habit is to duplicate once, early, when the Jar is good, rather than repeatedly in a room full of enemies. A run where Tino is at low health and still holding a duplicate button is a run that ends to a stray projectile, not to the boss.

Everything past that — how much health a duplication costs, whether the cost scales, whether cheap healing changes the calculus — is not in the notes. `[Unconfirmed]`.

## 4. R-7: What the Fixes Tell You

R-7 has the second-most-documented kit, and it is documented sideways: through patches. The October roundup fixed two things about it, and both describe how the character is supposed to work.

- R-7 **revives**. The bug was that it revived "with no shields when it had no health containers," which left it "unable to attack or take damage." That is a description of a revive state with shields but zero health containers — a build state you only reach by stacking shield sources without health upgrades.
- The 1.0 notes fixed an "incorrect health after reviving with the R7 and Trauma VIP combination," which confirms R-7's revive interacts with items rather than resetting health to a fixed number.

Read together, R-7 rewards shield-stacking builds and punishes ignoring health containers. If you play R-7 and your revive leaves you a passenger, update — that specific failure was patched in October.

## 5. Random Selection, Profiles, and What They Do Not Do

Random selection is a variety tool, not a difficulty setting. It does not adjust the run to the Agent you rolled, and the notes do not describe any weighting. If you are chasing a specific clear — the 3-Agent achievement, a boss route challenge, or a personal best Fate record — pick deliberately, because rolling is a way to see more of the roster, not a way to progress faster.

Profiles are per Agent and per save. In co-op the client's own purchases and Fate settings stay with the client; the 1.0 notes fixed "incorrect ownership of clients' evolution purchases and Fate settings," which is the same rule applied to progression. Your profile numbers are yours.

## 6. The 3-Agent Achievement, and Why It Matters More Than It Sounds

The October roundup cut the clear-the-game achievement from **10 different Agents to 3**, and backfilled progress for players who had already cleared with several. That single number change reframes the Agent roster: the game no longer asks you to learn ten characters to finish its achievement list.

Practically, three clears with three different Agents is a weekend, not a month. The efficient path is to clear with whichever Agent you understand best, then take the two with documented mechanics (Tino, R-7) so you are not learning a kit blind on a final-floor run. `[Unconfirmed]`: whether the achievement counts co-op clears for a client, since the notes only describe the count.

## 8. FAQ

**How many characters are in Neon Abyss 2?**
At least ten. Four are named in official posts, and the achievement that once required ten different Agents confirms the roster reached double digits.

**Who is the best Agent in Neon Abyss 2?**
`[Unconfirmed]` in the sense players mean it. No official ranking exists, and any tier list of characters you find is community inference. The honest answer is that the Agent matters less than the items, weapons and Faith you collect in the run.

**Is Tino free?**
Yes — he shipped in the June 2026 Wonder Flow update, which was a default-branch content update rather than paid DLC. The only paid add-on referenced in 2026 posts is Archenemy Returns.

**Why does my Agent have no shields after reviving?**
That was an R-7 bug fixed in the October 10–16 roundup. Update to the current default branch.

**Do Agent profiles carry into co-op?**
Your profile is yours; the 1.0 notes fixed clients' evolution purchases and Fate settings being credited to the wrong player, which is the same separation.

**Can I play the same Agent as a teammate?**
`[Unconfirmed]` — the notes do not describe duplicate-Agent rules in co-op.
