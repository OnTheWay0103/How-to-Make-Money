# Delverium NPCs, Recruitment & Trading Guide

Recruiting NPCs and trading with them is one of Delverium's confirmed core-loop pillars: **gather resources → build shelters → farm → recruit and trade with NPCs → craft items → descend into dangerous dungeons**. If you have played Stardew Valley's village or Core Keeper's merchant NPCs, you already know the shape of it — here is everything confirmed about how recruitment and trading work in Delverium.

## How NPC Recruitment Works (Confirmed)

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| **NPCs wander the map** | NPCs are found wandering the world rather than living in fixed shops |
| **They move in if you have room** | An NPC moves into your settlement when your structures have sufficient space |
| **Recruitment is settlement-based** | Build room, and wandering NPCs can become residents — trading follows residence |
| **More NPCs, more wares** | Different NPCs offer different wares; recruiting multiple NPCs is the way to unlock variety |

**Demo example (community-documented):** the reviewer-verified path to livestock was recruitment-based — it took **three or four tenants** before an NPC appeared offering **chickens and sheep**.

## Trading (Confirmed)

- NPCs **trade with you** and **buy your excess resources**.
- The trading currency is **copper coins** — confirmed in demo coverage.
- Selling surplus farm produce and resources for copper coins is an intended loop — see the [Farming Guide](/guides/farming-guide).
- **Specific prices and wares lists are TBD** — no official shop tables have been published.

## The Starter Tools NPC

- The player's **first tools — axe, pickaxe, hammer — are given by an NPC** (confirmed in demo preview coverage).
- This establishes the pattern: early progression leans on NPCs, not just crafting.

## Practical Tips (Based on Confirmed Systems)

1. **Build room early.** Since NPCs only move in when structures have space, expanding your settlement is the prerequisite for trade access.
2. **Recruit several NPCs.** The livestock seller reportedly appears only after 3–4 tenants — one trader is not the whole market.
3. **Farm to feed trade.** Excess crops and food sell for copper coins, and copper coins are the confirmed currency — farming and trading are the same loop.
4. **Take the starter tools.** The NPC-given axe, pickaxe and hammer are your first progression; use them before crafting replacements.
5. **Watch for NPCs while exploring.** NPCs wander the map — exploration is how you find new potential residents.
