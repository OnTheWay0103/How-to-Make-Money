# Delverium Co-op & Multiplayer Guide

**Co-op is Delverium's whole identity.** It's a **1-8 player co-op survival sandbox RPG**: up to **8 players online via Steam**, up to **4 players in local split-screen**, and solo play for everyone else. The project began around 2016 when lead developer Scott Lowther played split-screen Terraria with his family — local co-op isn't an afterthought, it's the game's origin. This guide explains exactly how co-op works today (demo build), how you'll host and join games, how to divide party roles, and how to make an 8-player party run smoothly.

> **Pre-release guide — will be updated for EA launch (Sept 22, 2026).** Hosting details, dedicated servers, and crossplay are not fully announced; anything unconfirmed is marked unverified.

## How online co-op works

Online co-op runs **through Steam**. The February 20, 2026 demo update added **8-player online co-op** ahead of Steam Next Fest, and that same model will carry into Early Access — though full details on hosting, invites, and world persistence are expected to be finalized for the EA launch.

What's confirmed today:

- **Up to 8 players** in one world over the internet, joined via Steam (friends and invites).
- The demo already supports it — you can test 8-player online co-op right now, free.
- Steam Deck is a confirmed platform, so PC players can mix desktop and Deck sessions. (Crossplay with any non-PC platform remains unverified — nothing else is announced.)

What's not confirmed yet:

- **Hosting model** — whether games run peer-to-peer with a player hosting, or on dedicated servers, has **not been announced (unverified)**. The demo works via Steam sessions; expect specifics to be locked in for EA.
- **Matchmaking** — no public-server browser or matchmaking system has been announced. Joining is through Steam friends/invites as far as we know (unverified for full details).
- **World hosting for large parties** — how a world persists when the host leaves is unconfirmed (unverified).

## How split-screen works

**4-player local split-screen** has been in the demo since day one (February 7, 2025) — it's the game's founding feature. Key points:

- Up to **4 players on one screen**, sharing the same world.
- **Gamepads** are the way to play on a shared screen; **full controller support is planned** for the release.
- It stacks with online co-op conceptually: the game supports 1-8 players, so local + online mixes are the natural direction — though exact combination rules (e.g., 2 local + 6 remote) are **unverified** for EA.

Why split-screen matters for this game: it's the *reason Delverium exists*. If you have family or housemates, the 4-player couch setup is the canonical Delverium experience — the developers built it for exactly that.

## How to play Delverium with friends

Based on what's confirmed in the current demo build (full EA UI may differ slightly):

### Hosting a game

1. Launch **Delverium Demo** (or the full game after Sept 22, 2026) on Steam.
2. Start a new world — you can customize **world, character, and difficulty** (added in the Feb 2026 update).
3. Invite friends **via Steam** (friends list / invite system). Up to 8 players total.
4. The demo supports **drop-in/drop-out** play, confirmed in the February 2026 update — friends can join and leave a session without restarting the world.

### Joining a game

1. Accept a **Steam invite** from the host, or join through the Steam friends list.
2. Drop into the world and pick up where the party is — whether that's farming, building, or gearing up for a dungeon run.

That's genuinely most of what's officially confirmed. Session persistence details (what happens to your character/world between sessions), dedicated server options, and password-protected private sessions are all **unverified** until EA.

## Party roles: no classes, all playstyles

Delverium has **no character classes** — roles are defined by how you play, not a class picker. That's a strength for co-op: your party can specialize naturally, and everyone can do a bit of everything while leaning into their favorite loop.

| Role | Core loop | What you keep the party supplied with |
|------|-----------|---------------------------------------|
| **Gatherer / Explorer** | Mine ores, collect rare materials, hunt loot, scout hidden caves | Ores and materials for crafting upgrades |
| **Builder** | Build and expand the shelter, craft building materials | A safe, well-equipped base |
| **Farmer** | Till soil, plant seeds, water crops, breed animals, fish, cook | A steady food supply for every dungeon run |
| **Craft** | Craft food, tools, weapons, armor, building materials | The gear that makes runs possible |
| **Trader / Diplomat** | Recruit and trade with NPCs | NPCs, quests, and story progress |
| **Delver** | Descend into dangerous dungeons, fight bosses | Loot, secrets, and progress on "the Faults" mystery |

A classic 4-8 player spread: one or two **delvers** pushing the dungeon while **farmers and crafters** sustain the base and **gatherers** feed materials into the crafting pipeline. The demo update added a **world boss** (name not announced) — a party that reaches it together will want dedicated roles.

## Co-op tips for Delverium

### 1. Divide roles before you divide resources

Agree on who does what in session one: a farmer, a builder, a gatherer, a crafter, and delvers. No classes needed — just don't have four people planting seeds while nobody mines.

### 2. Farm to sustain dungeon runs

The confirmed loop is explicit: **gather → build → farm → craft → descend into dungeons**. Farming isn't the filler part — it's the fuel. Crops, animals, fish, and cooked food keep the delvers alive underground. A party that skips farming starves at the dungeon entrance.

### 3. Shelter first, dungeon second

Set up the base and crafting area before the first deep dive — in co-op the base is the party's resupply depot; build it before you need it.

### 4. Keep the gatherers ahead of the crafters

Crafting is only as good as its material input. Designate at least one explorer whose job is to find **ores, rare materials, hidden caves, and loot** — crafters can't make gear from nothing.

### 5. Coordinate 8-player dungeon runs

At 8 players the dungeon gets crowded, so pre-run chat matters: who tanks, who deals damage, who carries food and healing, who watches the exits. The world boss added in the Feb 2026 update is a party boss — a coordinated party will handle it far more easily than eight individuals.

### 6. Use Journey Shrines for fast travel

The Feb 2026 update added **fast travel via hidden "Journey Shrines."** In a large party, they save everyone from long marches across the procedurally generated map — find them early and use them.

### 7. Don't ignore NPCs and quests

**Recruiting and trading with NPCs** plus the **lore and quest systems** give the party shared objectives beyond building. The story — "the Faults" threatening the land, secrets hidden in the dungeons — is a co-op hook: progress it as a party.

### 8. Customize the run to your group

World, character, and difficulty customization (added in the demo's Feb 2026 update) means you can tune the experience for mixed-skill groups — great for playing with kids or newcomers.

### 9. Test the demo now

Hosting specifics for EA aren't finalized (unverified), so test **8-player online co-op in the free demo today** — it's free, and you'll know exactly how the Steam flow feels before buying.
