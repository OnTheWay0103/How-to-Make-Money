# Delverium Beginner Guide — First Day Survival & Getting Started

**Delverium** is an open-world survival sandbox RPG from **Sagestone Games** (Brisbane, Australia) — a pixel-art world of gathering, building, farming, crafting, NPC trading, and dangerous dungeon diving, for **1–8 players** (4-player split-screen or up to 8 online via Steam). It enters **Steam Early Access on September 22, 2026**, with a free demo already on Steam. If you're new, this guide covers the core loop, your first-day survival priorities, and how co-op changes the start.

> **Pre-release guide — will be updated for EA launch (Sept 22, 2026).** Specific recipes, biome names, and system details are not yet announced; anything unconfirmed is marked unverified.

## What is Delverium?

Delverium is a **co-op survival sandbox** in the tradition of Terraria and Core Keeper: a vast **procedurally generated world** full of **exotic biomes, hidden caves, and dangerous dungeons**. You gather resources, build shelters, tend farms, recruit and trade with NPCs, craft food, tools, weapons, and armor — and finally **descend into dangerous dungeons** to loot unique items and uncover the story mystery called **"the Faults."**

The studio describes the core loop explicitly:

> **Gather resources → build shelters → farm → recruit and trade with NPCs → craft items → descend into dangerous dungeons.**

Everything you do day one is one step in that loop. And because the game supports **solo, 4-player split-screen, and 8-player online co-op**, the loop scales from a quiet one-person farm to a full party operation.

## The Core Loop, Explained Simply

| Loop step | What you do | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Gather** | Chop, mine ores, collect rare materials | Every other system runs on materials |
| **Build** | Construct shelters and base structures | Storage, crafting, and a safe hub |
| **Farm** | Till soil, plant seeds, water crops, breed animals, fish, cook | A steady food supply beats foraging |
| **Recruit & trade** | Find NPCs, trade goods, take quests | Gear, resources, and story progress |
| **Craft** | Make food, tools, weapons, armor, building materials | Turns gathered materials into power |
| **Descend** | Enter dangerous dungeons, fight, loot unique items | The risk/reward heart of the game |

## First-Day Survival Priorities

If you only remember one thing: **tools → shelter → food → weapons → dungeon**. That order follows the confirmed loop and gets you through day one alive.

### 1. Tools first (first 30 minutes)

Your opening minutes go into **gathering resources and crafting basic tools**. You can't farm, mine, or fight efficiently without them. Better tools mean faster gathering, and faster gathering feeds every other craft. A good rhythm: chop and mine enough to craft a basic tool set, then use it to gather faster and further.

### 2. Shelter before nightfall (first hour)

A **shelter** isn't decoration — it's your base: storage for materials, a place for crafting, and a safe resupply point between expeditions. In co-op it's the party's depot, so build it early and expand as materials allow. The world is dangerous; the shelter is the line between a bad night and a reset.

### 3. Farm for food (hours 1–2)

The farming system is a core pillar — **till soil, plant seeds, water crops**, and later **breed animals, fish, and cook**. Cooked food sustains long sessions and dungeon runs, and it's the loop step the demo's February 2026 update expanded the most. Don't live on foraging: a farm is the difference between exploring and constantly hunting for your next meal.

### 4. Weapons and armor before the dungeon (hour 2+)

Dungeons are **dangerous** — that's their confirmed design. The loop explicitly places crafting weapons and armor *before* descending. Don't dive ungeared. Craft the best weapons and armor you can, pack food, and if you can, bring friends.

### 5. Descend when ready, not when desperate

The dungeon is the climax of the loop and where the story's secrets (**the Faults**) surface. Enter with full supplies, a full crafting pipeline behind you, and a plan for resupply — **hidden Journey Shrines** (fast travel, confirmed in the Feb 2026 update) make returning to base cheap if you find them early.

## First-Day Do's and Don'ts

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Craft tools before exploring deep | Wander far from base without materials |
| Build shelter early | Sleep in the open your first night |
| Start a small farm quickly | Rely on foraging alone |
| Check quests and lore from the start | Skip NPCs — they trade and give direction |
| Mark or remember Journey Shrine locations | Ignore fast travel once found |
| Try co-op for the real experience | Assume solo and co-op play the same |

## Solo vs Co-op on Day One

- **Solo:** everything is on you — gathering, farming, fighting. Slower, but the loop is small enough to manage alone.
- **Co-op (2–4, split-screen or online):** divide roles — one farms, one builds, one gathers, one preps gear. The classic family/friends setup.
- **Co-op (5–8 online):** full specialization is possible — dedicated delvers, farmers, crafters, and gatherers. Needs coordination, but day one becomes much faster.

## Common First-Day Mistakes

1. **Skipping tools** — grinding slow gathering wastes your whole first day
2. **Dungeon too early** — ungeared dives end at the entrance; craft weapons and armor first
3. **No farm** — foraging-only runs stall the moment food runs low mid-expedition
4. **Ignoring NPCs and quests** — they're a confirmed source of trading and story direction
5. **Solo-only tunnel vision** — even one co-op partner halves the workload; the game is built for it
