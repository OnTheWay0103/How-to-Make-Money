# Hidden Locations & City Secrets — The Secret Side of Nivalis

Every city has two maps: the one the guidebooks show, and the one the locals know. Nivalis is a city built on secrets — a cyberpunk metropolis pushed to the brink by CorpSec crackdowns and a mysterious serial killer, under which the sea hides the ruins of older ages. The [City Guide](/guides/city-districts-guide) maps the official districts; this guide covers the city that isn't on any map: the waterways beneath the pillars, the lower-city ruins, the ocean's deep mysteries, and the doors that only open to players who earn them.

**A note on sourcing:** Nivalis Nights is seven weeks from launch as of this guide's writing (August 9, 2026). What follows combines officially confirmed systems with pre-release coverage from community sources — every claim that goes beyond confirmation is marked. Exact locations will be mapped and verified after launch.

## The Confirmed Secret Layer — The Waterways

The single most significant confirmed discovery about Nivalis' hidden geography is the water layer itself:

- **The city is built on pillars above the sea.** The waterways run beneath and between the massive support structures that hold Nivalis up.
- **Your boat is the key.** Early in the game you gain a personal boat, docked near the city's lower levels — small enough to navigate the cramped passages between and beneath the pillars.
- **The lower city is explorable by design.** The waterways branch, loop, and descend. Following them to their edges reveals what the surface residents never see: rusted infrastructure, makeshift docks, graffiti on the support columns, and occasional passing boats.
- **The boat stays where you leave it.** Your boat persists at its last docked spot, so it doubles as a staging point — explore the lower city on foot, then return to the boat and push on.

This makes the waterways the game's primary hidden-layer highway. The [Transportation Guide](/guides/transportation-guide) covers the boat as transport; this guide covers what the boat finds.

### What Lives Beneath — Community Coverage

Pre-release community coverage (collated by early wiki efforts) has begun naming what's down there:

- **Salt Pete and the ocean mysteries.** At the Calypso Island port area, a character known as **Salt Pete** — a name straight out of the city's seafaring underbelly — tells stories of what the ocean holds: *"sentient slime, junk HOVAs and ancient, God-like AIs dreaming dark dreams."* Whether Salt Pete is a storyteller, a quest-giver, or a merchant is not yet confirmed — but the lore he carries connects Nivalis to the deeper history of the Cloudpunk universe, where AI entities like CORA shaped the city's fate.

> **Expectation, not confirmed:** Salt Pete's exact role, and whether the "ancient, God-like AIs" are lore flavor or actual content, are unconfirmed pre-release claims. They're consistent with the established Cloudpunk universe — but they haven't been officially verified.

- **The deep water holds more than fish.** The ocean beneath Nivalis is hinted to contain remnants of older technology and possibly older forms of intelligence. In a universe where AIs dream in the city's foundations, the darkest waters are where the story lives.

## Achievement Points — The Confirmed Unlock System

The most important confirmed rule of exploration in Nivalis Nights: **the map grows with your achievements.** The game's Achievement Points system — earned through business milestones — is explicitly confirmed to **unlock access to new city areas and business opportunities.**

This changes the exploration calculus in two ways:

1. **Some secrets are locked behind progress, not discovery.** You can wander into a dead end all day; the door opens when your business empire reaches a milestone. The [Money-Making Guide](/guides/money-making-guide) treats Achievement Points as a progression currency — treat exploration the same way.
2. **Business success and exploration are the same loop.** Hitting business milestones opens areas; new areas contain new business opportunities; those opportunities generate new milestones. The city rewards the full-loop player.

> **What we don't know:** which specific areas are Achievement-gated, what the milestones are, and whether any areas require *non-business* achievements (relationships, chess, fishing). Expect a mix — and expect the most interesting areas to demand more than money.

## Fog Days — The Explorer's Weather

The [Weather Guide](/guides/weather-guide) documents fog as a reduced-traffic weather type. Its exploration angle deserves emphasis: **fog days are the game's own invitation to explore.**

- Pre-release coverage explicitly notes that players use fog days for aimless travel and uncovering hidden areas — the city rewards wandering, and fog is the wandering weather.
- Reduced visibility works both ways: you may miss landmarks, but the city also reveals a different version of itself — silhouettes, lights, and routes that only make sense in low visibility.
- Fog + boat is the deep-water combination. If the waterways have secrets, fog is when they'll be most atmospheric — and possibly most accessible.

**Explorer's rule:** keep a mental (or written) weather log. The [Fishing Guide](/guides/fishing-guide) recommends one for catches; do the same for exploration — note what you found and what the weather was. Patterns will emerge after launch.

## The Cloudpunk Precedent — 122 Hidden Locations

Here's what the universe's history tells us to expect. *Cloudpunk*, ION LANDS' first game in the city of Nivalis, shipped with **122 hidden locations** scattered across its version of the city — plus a "Nivalis Tourist" achievement dedicated to finding them.

The precedent matters for two reasons:

1. **ION LANDS builds discovery into the city's DNA.** A studio that hid 122 locations in a delivery game will not ship a life-sim — a genre built on inhabiting a place — with fewer secrets. The design appetite is proven.
2. **The same city, denser.** Nivalis Nights returns to the same city with more systems, more verticality (boats under the pillars), and a longer play loop. The hidden-location count in Nivalis Nights could plausibly exceed Cloudpunk's — but that's an expectation, not a fact.

### How Cloudpunk Hides Things (For Reference)
Cloudpunk's hidden spots ran the gamut: off-map corners reachable by daring HOVA flight, easter-egg rooms, lore-rich dead ends, and views that were the reward themselves. The universal rules:
- **The map lies at the edges.** Hidden spots cluster at boundaries — the map edge, the ceiling, the waterline.
- **The unusual is a signpost.** A wall that shouldn't be there, a light where no business runs, a door that opens for no reason.
- **The journey is the content.** Many hidden locations were simply *worth seeing* — the reward was the place, not a chest.

## Exploration Strategy — Pre-Launch Playbook

### 1. Build the Boat Early
The waterways are the confirmed hidden layer, and the boat is the confirmed key. The [Fishing Guide](/guides/fishing-guide) makes the same point for income — the boat is your fastest route to both premium seafood and the city's secret geography. Unlock it, upgrade its range, and treat every canal as an unfinished map.

### 2. Earn Your Achievement Points
The confirmed unlock system. Push business milestones steadily — the [Beginner Guide](/guides/beginner-guide)'s venue-level-3-first advice is exploration advice too: the Manager unlock frees the time you'll need for wandering.

### 3. Explore in the Right Weather
Clear days for fishing and outdoor routes; fog days for the atmospheric deep exploration; stormy days — carefully — for anything that only appears in storms. The weather guide's systems are all exploration tools in disguise.

### 4. Walk the Unusual Route
Take the stairs, not the elevator. Dock where you don't need to. Enter shops without signs. The city guide's advice to walk regular routes for NPC schedules doubles as secret-hunting: **consistency is how you notice what changed.**

### 5. Talk to the Weird NPCs
Salt Pete is the documented example, but the principle is universal: characters with stories attached are walking treasure maps. The [NPC Relationships Guide](/guides/npc-relationships-guide) explains how to get characters talking — the seafaring ones know the water, the old ones know the city before the Corps, and the scared ones know what the Corps hides.

### 6. Log Everything
A location notebook — district, access method, weather, time of day, what you found — turns random discovery into systematic mapping. Post-launch, this wiki's hidden-locations coverage will be community-built; you can be part of the survey.

## Common Questions

### Are there hidden locations in Nivalis Nights?
The water layer — the city's under-pillar waterways — is confirmed as explorable hidden geography. The precedent of Cloudpunk's 122 hidden locations and the game's Achievement-gated area unlocks make extensive secrets near-certain, though the full inventory will be mapped after launch.

### How do I access the hidden waterways?
By boat. Your personal boat, docked near the city's lower levels, is small enough to navigate the passages beneath the support pillars. The boat stays where you leave it, doubling as a staging point for on-foot exploration of the lower city.

### Who is Salt Pete?
A character associated with the Calypso Island port area, documented in pre-release community coverage, who tells of the ocean holding "sentient slime, junk HOVAs and ancient, God-like AIs dreaming dark dreams." His exact in-game role is unconfirmed.

### Do I need to pay to unlock hidden areas?
Some areas are confirmed to unlock via Achievement Points — business milestones that open new city areas and opportunities. Expect a mix of free discovery and progression-gated content.

### Are there secrets from Cloudpunk in Nivalis Nights?
Almost certainly in spirit — the games share the same city and universe. Specific returning locations or callbacks are unconfirmed, but the City of Ghosts DLC already demonstrated ION LANDS' willingness to weave continuity between its Nivalis stories.

### Is exploration worth it for business players?
Yes — the game explicitly ties Achievement Points (earned through business) to area unlocks, and new areas contain new business opportunities. In Nivalis Nights, the explorer's map and the tycoon's ledger are the same document.
