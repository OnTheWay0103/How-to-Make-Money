# Transportation Guide — Getting Around Nivalis

Nivalis is a city built in the sky and on the water — a vertical, multi-layered sprawl where the distance between two points is rarely a straight line. How you travel in Nivalis Nights is a real decision with real costs: time, money, and access. Some districts can only be reached one way. Some activities require a specific vehicle. And in a game where "every day is a choice," the transport you choose decides how many choices you can fit into that day.

This guide covers every confirmed way to move around Nivalis — walking, the subway, flying taxis, the personal HOVA, and the boat — plus the travel economics that make the choice matter.

## The Five Ways to Travel — Quick Reference

| Method | Speed | Cost | District Access | Best For |
|--------|-------|------|-----------------|----------|
| **Walking** | Slow | Free | All street-level areas | Short trips, NPC schedules, atmosphere |
| **Subway (Metro Hub)** | Fast | Small fee | Network-connected districts | Commuting across the city |
| **Flying taxi** | Fastest | Moderate | All districts, including Skyhigh Gardens | Long hauls, time-critical errands |
| **Personal HOVA** | Fast (yours) | Fuel + maintenance | All districts once owned | Freedom, exploration, status |
| **Boat** | Medium | Free (yours) | Canals, lower city, Calypso Island | Fishing, hidden areas, goods transport |

The [City Guide](/guides/city-districts-guide) covers the district map in detail; this guide goes deep on the vehicles and the trade-offs between them.

## Walking — The Underrated Mode

Never dismiss walking in a game where the world is the content. Walking is free, and in Nivalis Nights it's a gameplay choice rather than a punishment:

- **NPC schedules are best harvested on foot.** The [NPC Relationships Guide](/guides/npc-relationships-guide) is built on catching citizens at the right time and place — and the city's own guidance recommends regular walks past the same spots to catch friendships, mini-games, and stories you'd miss from a taxi.
- **Hidden areas are found on foot.** The city rewards wandering — fog days are explicitly recommended for aimless travel and uncovering hidden locations (see the [Hidden Locations Guide](/guides/hidden-locations-guide)).
- **The cost is time.** Every minute walking is a minute not running a rush-hour venue. In the early game especially, budget your day's walking against your business hours.

**When to walk:** short trips, relationship-building routes, exploration, and any time you're not pressed for time. **When not to:** rush-hour coverage, cross-city errands, curfew pressure.

## The Subway — The Commuter Backbone

The confirmed Metro Hub is the city's subway interchange — the transit spine of Nivalis. What we know:

- **Fast cross-city travel** at (presumably) a small fee — the commuter option for players who run venues in different districts.
- **Hub-and-spoke structure.** If the Metro Hub is the interchange, expect lines radiating to the main districts. Players living and working in different neighborhoods will plan around the network.
- **A time and money trade-off vs. the HOVA.** A subway pass might be cheaper than fuel and repairs — but it doesn't go everywhere, and it doesn't come when you call it.

**When to take the subway:** daily commutes between districts, budget-conscious travel, and trips where the network covers both endpoints. **When not to:** time-critical runs, off-network destinations, atmosphere.

## Flying Taxis — Money for Time

Flying taxis are the city's on-demand premium option:

- **Fastest practical travel** for a fee — the classic life-sim trade of cash for time.
- **The only confirmed way to reach Skyhigh Gardens.** The eastern-inspired district above the clouds is confirmed as **flying-taxi access only** — no subway line, no footpath, no boat ramp. If your business or social life takes you to Skyhigh Gardens, taxi fare is a recurring business expense.
- **Late-night availability is a question.** With the 2 AM curfew, expect taxi service to behave differently after hours — whether that means premium fares, reduced availability, or curfew-exempt operation is unknown.

**When to take a taxi:** Skyhigh Gardens access, time-critical errands, weather days when walking is miserable. **When not to:** short hops, budget stretches — the fare economy adds up fast when taxis become a habit.

## The Personal HOVA — Freedom, for a Price

The HOVA — the rocket-propelled hovercar of the Cloudpunk universe — is the game's aspirational personal vehicle. In *Cloudpunk*, the HOVA was the entire game: a delivery rig that carried Rania through the neon canyons of Nivalis, governed by skylanes, fuel stops, and repair bills. In Nivalis Nights, a personal HOVA is your own flying car — a purchase goal and a new way to own the city.

### What Cloudpunk Teaches Us About HOVAs

The HOVA's behavior in the shared universe is documented from *Cloudpunk*, and Nivalis Nights is the same city — so expect strong continuity:

- **Skylanes:** the city's glowing virtual roads carry organized traffic. In Cloudpunk, drivers were not restricted to them — cutting off-lane through open air was often faster, since AI traffic never deviates and will happily collide with you.
- **Fuel and repairs:** HOVAs run on fuel and take damage from collisions. Fuel stations and garages are a real cost center — Cloudpunk players paid for every refuel and every dent.
- **Parking discipline:** in Cloudpunk, the HOVA could only be exited at designated parking spots. On-foot exploration branched from those points. Expect the same logic — your HOVA is freedom, but it docks where the city says.
- **Upgrades and cosmetics:** speed boosts, durability upgrades, and cosmetic options (colored contrail exhaust in Cloudpunk) were all part of the ownership game — and a new model was a later-game goal.

> **Expectation, not confirmed:** Nivalis Nights has not yet detailed its HOVA implementation — how you obtain one, whether the full Cloudpunk fuel/repair/parking loop carries over, and what the upgrade tree looks like. What's certain is the HOVA exists as a personal vehicle in the same city, and the established universe mechanics are the best available guide.

### Why a HOVA Is Worth Planning For

- **District access:** a personal HOVA presumably opens the same vertical freedom Cloudpunk players enjoyed — including access that taxis would otherwise gate behind fares.
- **Time efficiency:** owning your vehicle ends the taxi-fare math for time-critical runs.
- **Status and story:** in the city guide's own framing, a personal HOVA is "a purchase goal; personal freedom." In a game where reputation and identity matter, the car you fly is part of the signal.

**The budget question:** a HOVA is presumably a major mid-game purchase competing with venue expansion, apartment upgrades, and greenhouse investment. The disciplined play: buy it after your income is passive — Manager-run venues first, per the [Best Business Guide](/guides/best-business-guide).

## The Boat — The Water Is a Highway

Your private boat is confirmed as both a vehicle and a gameplay system — see the [Fishing Guide](/guides/fishing-guide) for the fishing layer. As transport, the boat matters more than most players expect:

- **The canals are roads.** Nivalis is built above the sea; the water between the city's support structures is an entire navigable layer, largely out of sight of the street-level city.
- **Access to Calypso Island** is confirmed by boat — the upscale island district is reachable by boat (and taxi), and the boat is the atmospheric way.
- **The lower city is boat territory.** The waterways run beneath the massive pillars that hold Nivalis up — cramped passages, makeshift docks, and hidden spots the surface never sees. The [Hidden Locations Guide](/guides/hidden-locations-guide) details what lives down there.
- **Goods transport:** the [Best Business Guide](/guides/best-business-guide) explicitly recommends the boat for neighborhood arbitrage — moving goods between districts where prices differ. Your boat is a cargo carrier as much as a fishing platform.
- **Boat upgrades:** expect upgrade paths for speed, range, and fishing gear (per the [Fishing Guide](/guides/fishing-guide)).

**When to use the boat:** fishing, Calypso Island, lower-city exploration, cargo movement, and any route where water beats streets.

## Travel Economics — The Real Cost of Getting Around

Transport in Nivalis Nights is a time-money system with three levers:

### 1. Time vs. Money
Every trip has a time cost and a money cost. Walking is free but slow; taxis are fast but billed. The optimization problem: **your time is worth more at some hours than others.** A taxi during dinner rush — when an understaffed venue bleeds money — is cheap. A taxi on a quiet afternoon, when you could walk and harvest NPC encounters, is expensive.

### 2. Access Gating
Some destinations are mode-locked:

| Destination | Walking | Subway | Taxi | HOVA | Boat |
|------------|---------|--------|------|------|------|
| Meridian Market | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | Canal access |
| Metro Hub | ✅ | — | ✅ | ✅ | ❌ |
| Seaside Boardwalk | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| Calypso Island | ❌ | ❌ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| Skyhigh Gardens | ❌ | ❌ | **✅ only** | Unconfirmed | ❌ |
| Lower city waterways | Partial | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | **✅ only** |

Access gating means your transport portfolio is a progression: walking and subway early, taxis when cash flows, boat for the water layer, HOVA for the full vertical city.

### 3. The Curfew Factor
The 2 AM curfew (see the [Day & Night Cycle & Weather Guide](/guides/day-night-cycle-guide)) changes transport math at night: street options shrink, security patrols matter, and the boat's relative discretion may become valuable. If you plan curfew-era activities, your vehicle choice is part of the risk calculation.

## Choosing Your Transport — Decision Framework

| Situation | Best Choice | Why |
|-----------|-------------|-----|
| Rush hour starts in 10 minutes | Taxi (or HOVA) | Time beats money during revenue windows |
| Friendly NPC spotted on your usual walk | Walking | Relationships are harvested on foot |
| Skyhigh Gardens business meeting | Taxi | The only confirmed access |
| Calypso Island visit | Boat | Free, atmospheric, and fishing en route |
| Cargo arbitrage between districts | Boat | Best goods capacity |
| Fog day exploration | Walking / Boat | The city rewards slow wandering |
| Curfew sabotage planning | HOVA / Boat | Discretion matters after 2 AM |
| Daily commute, both ends on the network | Subway | Cheapest fast option |

## Common Questions

### Can I own a flying car in Nivalis Nights?
The personal HOVA — the hovercar of the Cloudpunk universe — is confirmed as a personal vehicle in the game's travel options, described in the city's own materials as a purchase goal. Implementation details are pending final confirmation.

### How do I get to Skyhigh Gardens?
The confirmed answer is the flying taxi — it's described as the only way to reach the above-the-clouds district. A personal HOVA may add a second option at launch.

### Is walking actually viable?
Yes — and it's the recommended mode for relationship-building and exploration. It's free, it's where NPC encounters live, and the game explicitly rewards wandering. Its only cost is time.

### Does the boat go everywhere?
No — but it goes places nothing else does: the waterways beneath the city, hidden lower-city areas, and Calypso Island's dock approaches. It's the only vehicle for the water layer.

### Are there fuel and repair costs?
For the HOVA, almost certainly — fuel stations and repair garages were core to the HOVA in Cloudpunk, and the same city's vehicles should behave the same way. Taxis bill per trip; the subway bills per ride. Exact fares and fuel economics are unconfirmed and will be documented at launch.

### Can I travel after curfew?
The curfew changes travel rather than banning it: security patrols become a factor, and the street layer is effectively off-limits legally. Vehicles that keep you off the streets — boat, HOVA — are the natural curfew-era transport.

## Transport Progression Plan

1. **Early game:** walking everywhere, subway for commutes. Harvest every encounter and hidden spot on foot — free value.
2. **First profits:** taxis for time-critical errands. Treat fare as an investment when it protects rush-hour revenue.
3. **First expansion:** unlock the boat early — it's confirmed as your fastest route to premium fishing income and the water layer (see the [Fishing Guide](/guides/fishing-guide)).
4. **Mid-game:** boat upgrades, then the HOVA purchase once Manager-run venues make income passive.
5. **Late game:** full portfolio — walking for social routes, subway for commutes, HOVA for freedom, boat for the deep city, taxis for the convenience tax.

## Bottom Line

Transportation in Nivalis Nights is a strategy layer, not a loading screen. Every mode has a job: walking harvests the city's life, the subway commutes, taxis buy time, the HOVA buys freedom, and the boat unlocks an entire hidden layer of the city. Build your transport portfolio in the same order you build your business — one solid mode mastered, then the next — and the whole city opens up.
