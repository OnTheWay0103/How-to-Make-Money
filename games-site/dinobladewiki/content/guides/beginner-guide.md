# Dinoblade Beginner Guide — Survive Your First Hunt

Welcome to Dinoblade, a dinosaur-themed "Soulslite" — a Soulslike with a kinder death penalty — developed by solo developer Jean Nguyen (a former Sucker Punch senior gameplay animator) under **Team Spino LLC**, published with Boltray Games. You play a young **Spinosaurus gripping a colossal greatsword in its jaws**, fighting through a world where a cataclysm armed every creature with a blade. The game launched **July 23, 2026 on Steam (PC) at $19.99** and is **single-player only**.

This guide covers the confirmed core mechanics: how the parry-and-Resolve loop works, what to do in your first hour, and the mistakes every new player makes.

---

## The Core Loop: Parry → Resolve → Finish

Dinoblade's combat is built on **posture and deflection, not raw damage** (the parry system is frequently compared to Sekiro). The loop:

1. **Block** to survive attacks you don't want to parry (holding block costs stamina).
2. **Perfect parry** — release block just before an incoming strike — to deflect the attack. A perfect parry **restores stamina and Resonance** (the blue bar under your health that powers special attacks).
3. Every enemy has a **Resolve bar** that fills with successful parries. When it's full, land a **heavy finishing blow**.
4. Against bosses, a full Resolve bar plus a perfectly timed parry triggers an **Instinct Counter** — a powerful counterattack "clash" that deals massive damage. This is the single most important boss mechanic, and the game teaches it before your first real boss, Kira the Exile.

The tutorial covers **blocking and parrying, light and heavy attack combos, sprinting, dodging, lock-on, healing charges, and charge attacks** (you pick up the game's flaming sword at the very start).

### Resources to Manage

- **Stamina** — spent on attacking, blocking, dodging, and sprinting. If you exhaust it, you're a sitting duck.
- **Resonance** — the blue bar that powers special attacks. Perfect parries restore it, which is why the game rewards defense.
- **Healing charges** — your heal. You refill them at **Save Stones**, the game's rest points.

### Key Combat Facts (Confirmed)

- **Parry input:** hold the block input and release it right before the incoming strike.
- **Unblockable attacks exist** — they must be dodged with roll i-frames. Don't try to parry everything.
- **Charged heavy attacks** release a **ranged fire projectile** that costs 5 Resonance plus stamina — useful against enemies holding scythes, tridents, and hammers.
- **Lock-on** exists, and you can switch targets with the scroll wheel.
- **Death is forgiving:** you do **not** drop currency when you die — a major departure from Dark Souls, and the reason the game calls itself a Soulslite.
- **Leveling is partly automatic**, and you can adjust your character's attributes before boss fights.

## Your First Expedition

### Where You Start: The Cavern (Part 1)

The game opens in **the cavern** — a tutorial corridor that teaches you the toolkit above. Don't rush it: the groups of small dinosaurs and the larger enemy you meet are there to drill parry timing. Use them as practice before you ever see a boss.

### Save Stones

Rest points are called **Save Stones**, not campfires or bonfires. Resting restores your healing charges and respawns enemies. Light every Save Stone you find — they're your respawn anchors.

### First Priorities

1. **Learn to parry before you fight Kira.** Kira the Exile, the first main boss, is designed around the Instinct Counter — the game explicitly teaches it right before this fight. His telegraphed **three-hit combo and charging attacks** are your practice.
2. **Level up at Save Stones / adjust attributes before bosses.** The game lets you tune your character's attributes before major fights, so don't hoard — spend what makes the next boss survivable.
3. **Use your healing charges deliberately.** Don't heal when you're at full safety; learn the boss pattern first, then heal to survive the second attempt at the pattern.

### Enemy Priority (Confirmed Types)

| Enemy | Threat | Strategy |
|-------|--------|----------|
| Small raptor packs | Low | Parry practice. Perfect parries restore stamina + Resonance. |
| Larger Part 1 enemy | Medium | Parry its telegraphed attacks to fill its Resolve bar. |
| Scythe/hammer-wielding dinos | Medium-High | Reach weapons beat you at range — use charged fire projectiles or dodge through. |
| Kira the Exile (first boss) | High | Instinct Counter on a full Resolve bar is your biggest damage window. |

## Common Beginner Mistakes

1. **Never parrying.** Parrying is the game's identity. It restores resources and fills Resolve bars. Playing this game like a dodge-roll-only Soulslike wastes its core mechanic.
2. **Spamming attacks.** Every attack costs stamina. Exhausting yourself mid-combo is how packs of small dinosaurs kill you.
3. **Trading hits with reach weapons.** Scythes and hammers out-range your greatsword in neutral. Charge a fire projectile or close with a well-timed roll instead.
4. **Dying fearfully.** You don't drop currency on death. Experiment, die, learn the pattern, try again — the game is built for that loop.
5. **Ignoring Boss SP and Soul Arts.** After beating Kira you earn Boss SP from boss victories to buy **Soul Arts** (Meteor Slice, Meteor Dash, Alpha Roar, Meteor Zone, Survival Instinct) and unlock Summoning. See the [Soul Arts Guide](/guides/soul-arts-guide).

## What to Expect Next

- **Part 2** takes you to the **northern paths** (several waterfalls, a giant skull landmark, hidden SP pickups between rocks and along the main path) and the boss **Axe** — a melee fighter with spinning combos, diagonal slashes, and jumping attacks. His final swing is the easiest attack to parry.
- **Part 3** is the **mountain caves**, ending with **Kasei the Tyrant**, a two-phase final boss who switches to a massive greatsword below one-third health.
- After the final boss, **Boss Rush mode** unlocks.
