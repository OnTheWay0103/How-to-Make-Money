## Enemies of Dinoblade

Dinoblade's world is populated by a diverse range of creatures — from small raptors that hunt in packs to massive sauropods that shake the earth with every step. Each enemy type requires a different approach, and knowing their attack patterns is essential for survival.

This guide covers every enemy type in the game, their behaviors, weaknesses, and the best strategies for taking them down.

## Understanding Enemy Behavior

Enemies in Dinoblade follow specific behavior patterns based on their type. Understanding these patterns is more important than raw stats.

### Enemy Behavior Categories

- **Aggressive** — Charge at the player immediately. High damage, low defense. Best handled with parries and counterattacks.
- **Territorial** — Patrol a set area. Attack only when the player enters their range. Can be bypassed or pulled individually.
- **Pack Hunters** — Move in groups. Coordinated attacks from multiple directions. Requires crowd control and positioning.
- **Ambush Predators** — Hidden until triggered. Burst damage from stealth. Keep your weapon drawn in suspicious areas.
- **Defensive** — Slow but heavily armored. Require elemental damage or backstabs to damage effectively.

## Attack Pattern Analysis

### Velociblade

These small, fast predators hunt in packs of 3-5. They are the first enemy you encounter and remain dangerous throughout the game.

**Attack Patterns:**
- **Leap Strike** — The Velociblade crouches for 0.5 seconds, then leaps at the player. Parry window: the moment it leaves the ground.
- **Claw Swipe** — A quick 2-hit combo. The second hit has a slight delay — roll after the first hit, not before.
- **Pack Call** — A screech that summons nearby Velociblades. Interruptible with a strike to the head during the screech animation.

**Strategy**: Use wide-swinging weapons to hit multiple at once. A single parry against the leap attack can turn the tide of a pack fight. Lightning damage causes them to flinch, interrupting their attack chains.

### Stego Defender

Heavily armored and slow, but dangerous if you get careless.

**Attack Patterns:**
- **Tail Slam** — Lifts tail, pauses for 1 second, then slams down. Deals massive damage in a cone behind the Stego. Never stand behind it.
- **Side Swing** — Rotates body and swings tail horizontally. Has a wider range than it looks — dodge twice.
- **Leg Stomp** — Raises one leg and stomps. Close-range only, easy to avoid.

**Strategy**: The hardest part of fighting Stego is getting through its armor. Fire damage gradually burns through its defensive plating. Focus on one front leg — after enough damage, the Stego staggers, giving you 3 seconds to attack the unarmored underbelly for critical damage.

### Carno Stalker

A mid-game ambush predator that appears in forest and canyon biomes.

**Attack Patterns:**
- **Bite and Hold** — Lunges forward and bites. If it connects, it holds you in its jaws for 2 seconds while dealing damage. The parry window is generous — right as its head reaches you.
- **Head Swipe** — A quick horizontal head movement. Deals less damage but has very little wind-up.
- **Stalk** — If the Carno is not aggroed, it will follow the player at a distance, hiding behind terrain. It attacks when your back is turned.

**Strategy**: The Carno Stalker is all about patience. Watch for the stalk behavior — if you notice it following you, back toward a wall so it cannot attack from behind. The bite attack is the easiest to parry and leaves the longest recovery window.

### Spino Tyrant

A late-game boss-class enemy found in the Swamp and River biomes.

**Attack Patterns:**
- **Sweeping Bite** — Wide horizontal bite. Dodge into the attack (toward the Spino's neck) to avoid it.
- **Tail Whip** — 270-degree tail sweep. Jump to avoid it, or dodge through the tail.
- **Rising Slam** — Rears up and slams down with its full body weight. The shockwave covers a large area. Roll toward the Spino right as it lands.
- **Roar Stun** — A roar that stuns the player for 1.5 seconds. The Spino follows with a charge attack. The only way to avoid the stun is to break its Super Armor during the roar wind-up.

**Strategy**: The Spino Tyrant requires learning a 4-attack combo cycle. Once you learn the rhythm, you can reliably dodge every attack. Use Fire or Lightning damage to deal with its high health pool. Never get greedy — it has several attacks with very short wind-ups.

## Elemental Damage Guide

Matching elemental damage to enemy weaknesses can double your effective damage output.

| Element | Effect | Best Against | Weapons Available |
|---------|--------|-------------|-------------------|
| Fire | Burn (DoT), Armor Break | Stego Defender, Ankylo Smasher, Spino Tyrant | Fire Greatsword, Ember Dagger |
| Ice | Slow, Stamina Drain | Raptor Claw, Carno Stalker, Trike Charger | Frost Blade, Ice Lance |

| Wind | Knockback, Speed Debuff | Ptera Screamer, Compy Swarm | Wind Spear, Gale Claws |
| Dark | Weaken, Life Steal Block | Elite variants, Boss enemies | Obsidian Greatsword, Void Dagger |
| Physical | No weakness, no resistance | All enemies (baseline) | All starting weapons |

### Enemy Weakness Strategy Tips

- Keep at least two weapon types equipped. If an enemy resists your primary, switch to your secondary.
- Status effects from elemental damage stack. Burn + Slow on the same enemy significantly reduces their threat level.
- Elite enemies sometimes have shifted weaknesses. Check the enemy's color tint — red-tinted elites resist Fire, blue-tinted resist Ice, and so on.
- The "Elemental Sight" skill (unlocked in the Skill Tree) reveals enemy weaknesses when you lock on.
