## The Short Answer

**The best mining depth in Dwarf Delve is the deepest you can safely extract from.** That sounds like a non-answer, but it's the game's actual design: the official premise is that "the deeper you dig, the richer the haul but the farther you are from safety." Depth is a risk/reward dial, not a fixed destination — and no official depth numbers (meters, floors, or levels) have been published. Any guide that tells you "mine at 150m for mithril" is inventing data.

What this guide gives you instead: a honest depth strategy that follows from the confirmed mechanics — how deep to go at each stage of progression, how to decide in the moment, and how to manage the risks that depth adds.

## What's Confirmed About Depth

### 1. Depth Increases Reward
"The deeper you dig, the richer the haul" — official premise. Rare finds (treasures, "long-lost relics") are the confirmed high-value rewards, and depth is the confirmed route to them.

### 2. Depth Increases Risk
"...but the farther you are from safety" — official premise. Two confirmed risks scale with depth:
- **Distance from the elevator** — the longer the return trip, the more time in the danger zone
- **Collapse exposure** — the mine collapses progressively from the moment your run starts; deeper runs accumulate more collapse

### 3. Depth Is Procedural, Not Fixed
Mines are procedurally generated ("No two mines will ever be the same!"), so there are no fixed "depth zones" with named loot. Every run's depth curve is its own.

### 4. No Official Depth Units
No meters, floors, or levels are published. Community depth tables with specific numbers are speculation.

## Depth Strategy by Progression Stage

Since depth is a gradient, strategy is about matching depth to your current power. The confirmed power sources: gear upgrades, perks/skills (move faster, hit harder, carry more loot), and the minecart system for hauling.

### Stage 1: Learning the Loop (First ~10 Runs)
**Depth target: shallow — barely below the elevator's reach.**

- Learn the collapse rhythm, trap tells, and your tools
- Extract with whatever you bank — even small hauls fund upgrades
- Goal: consistent extraction, not depth

**Why:** reviews (Mostly Negative, small sample) say onboarding is rough. The shallow game is where you learn the confirmed loop without paying deep-run tuition.

### Stage 2: Building Power (Runs 10-30)
**Depth target: push one stage deeper than comfortable, then extract.**

- Upgrade Battlemallet (mining + combat) and lantern (darkness) first
- Each run, descend a little further than the last — until the first instability warning, then extract
- Start using flares to mark deeper routes

**Why:** the confirmed meta-progression compounds: each successful extraction funds the upgrades that make the next depth stage survivable.

### Stage 3: The Deep Game (Runs 30+)
**Depth target: as deep as your extraction discipline allows.**

- Max carry capacity (confirmed "carry more loot" theme)
- Rail the heavy ores — confirmed to "shift and store those heavy ores for you"
- Hunt relics — the confirmed endgame prize — and extract the moment one is found

**Why:** at this stage the loop is mastered; depth becomes the only remaining dial, and the confirmed reward for it ("the richer the haul") is real.

## How to Decide Depth in the Moment

Every run asks the same question: how deep now? Use the confirmed inputs:

### Push Deeper When:
- Inventory has clear room
- Tunnels are stable (no creaking, no falling dust)
- You haven't found a relic or treasure yet
- You know the exact return route and its length
- You have beams and light in reserve

### Extract Now When:
- Inventory is meaningfully full of valuables
- A relic or treasure is in hand — the run's objective is complete
- Tunnels are unstable
- Beams or light are running low
- The return trip is long, unmarked, or unbraced

### The Honest Rule of Thumb
Community practice (not official data): **the first instability warning is the signal to start the return.** Deeper runs multiply the cost of ignoring warnings — a deep death loses the biggest haul of the day.

## Risk Management at Depth

### The Depth Budget
Before descending, set a mental budget: "I'm going to X depth, and I extract at the first warning." The game's own slogan — "One wrong boot could cost you your life — or worse, your loot!" — is the design telling you to budget.

### The Return-Trip Rule
Depth is only as good as the return trip. Every meter down is a meter back — through the same hazards, with a heavier inventory, on a worse clock. Ask "can I get back from here?" before you go deeper, not after.

### The Cart Factor
The minecart system is the confirmed depth enabler: rails turn deep heavy-ore hauls into fast, safe extracts. When your runs involve heavy ore far from the elevator, rails convert depth from a liability into profit.

### The Relic Trigger
Relics are the confirmed endgame prize (unique perks + quarters decorations). Set the trigger before descending: "a relic in hand = extraction now." It's the single best depth-management rule in the game.

## Depth and "Best Ores"

What's confirmed is the shape: **rarer, higher-value finds scale with depth.** Hunt value by going deeper; don't hunt specific names that don't exist yet.

## Reading the Mine: Depth Signals

Since no official depth meter or zone names exist, every run is a judgment call. These signals — grounded in confirmed mechanics and safe extraction practice — help you read a run's depth potential:

### Instability Cadence
The confirmed collapse means every run degrades. The practical read: **the sooner instability starts, the shallower your depth budget should be.** A run that's creaking at the second floor is telling you its ceiling; a quiet, stable run is an invitation to push.

### Route Geometry
Procedural mines ("No two mines will ever be the same!") vary in layout. Some runs have a natural spiral of short, connected levels; others drop deep fast. **Read the geometry before committing:** if the return trip is already long and winding at modest depth, that's a shallow-budget run.

### Find Quality Early
Found a relic or treasure early? The run's objective is achieved — depth becomes optional. Found nothing but common ore by the time you're deep? You're accumulating risk for marginal reward; extract and reroll. The best depth decision is often "this run isn't worth it."

### Light and Beam Reserves
Your confirmed survival tools (lantern, flares, support beams) are a depth budget in themselves. **When reserves run low, depth budget runs out** — deep runs without light and beams are how loot dies.

## A Sample Staged Run

Concrete plans make the abstract depth question actionable. Here are three run templates by stage:

### Template 1: The Shallow Learner (First 10 Runs)
1. Descend one stop; sweep the immediate floor
2. Mine until the first instability warning
3. Extract with whatever you have
4. Refine, upgrade the Battlemallet, repeat

**Depth decision:** shallow, always. **Trigger:** first warning OR full inventory.

### Template 2: The Mid Push (Runs 10-30)
1. Descend; sweep shallow for quick ore
2. Push one level deeper than last run, marking the route with flares
3. Continue until the second instability warning
4. Return via the marked route; extract

**Depth decision:** one stage deeper per run, retreating at the first signs of trouble. **Trigger:** second warning OR relic.

### Template 3: The Deep Hauler (Runs 30+)
1. Descend fast with maxed Battlemallet, lantern, and capacity
2. Find a rich deposit; build rail toward the elevator (confirmed: carts "shift and store those heavy ores")
3. Fill the cart; ride back; extract; refine; reinvest
4. On relic runs: push depth with a hard trigger — relic in hand = extraction now

**Depth decision:** as deep as the rail line and your discipline allow. **Trigger:** full cart OR relic OR first warning.

## Common Depth Mistakes

- **Going deep before upgrading** — upgrades are permanent; depth is not. Shallow-and-safe funds deep-and-rich
- **Ignoring the return trip** — every meter down is a meter back with a heavier load and a worse clock
- **No extraction trigger** — deciding to leave "when it feels right" turns into deciding to leave at 1 HP in a sealed tunnel
- **Hauling heavy ore on foot** — that's the confirmed job of rails and carts; hand-carrying deep ore doubles the risk
- **Deep runs without beams or light** — the two confirmed survival tools; a deep, dark, unbraced run is a donation to the mine
- **Ignoring warning signs** — the first creak is a suggestion; the second is a deadline

## Depth FAQ

**What's the maximum depth in Dwarf Delve?** Unpublished. No official maximum exists in the materials.

**Is there anything at the bottom of the mine?** Unconfirmed. The game's premise ("the deeper you dig, the richer the haul") suggests reward at depth, but no bottom content is documented.

**Should beginners go deep?** No. Learn the loop shallow, build upgrades, then push depth. The confirmed economy rewards successful extraction far more than heroic death.

The best mining depth in Dwarf Delve is a moving target: shallow while learning, one stage deeper each time you outgrow the last, and as deep as your extraction discipline allows once the loop is mastered.
