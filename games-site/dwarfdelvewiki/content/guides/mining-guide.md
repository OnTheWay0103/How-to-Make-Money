## Mining in Dwarf Delve

Mining is the foundation of everything in Dwarf Delve. Every upgrade, perk, and piece of gear begins with ore pulled from the earth. You mine with your **Battlemallet** — there is no pickaxe in this game — and the deeper you dig, the richer the haul but the farther you are from the safety of the elevator.

This guide covers everything about mining that is officially confirmed, plus community-tested techniques. Dwarf Delve is a new game, so anything not confirmed by the developer is marked honestly as unconfirmed.

## Core Mining Mechanics (Confirmed)

- **The Battlemallet is your mining tool.** The official description calls it your "trusty Battlemallet" and it serves as both tool and weapon. Any guide that tells you to craft a pickaxe is describing a different game.
- **Mines are procedurally generated.** "No two mines will ever be the same!" — you cannot memorize a layout from a previous run.
- **The mine collapses over time.** The elevator leaves on a schedule, and anything not extracted is lost. Mining time is limited time.
- **Rails and minecarts are your heavy-lifting partners.** The official feature list confirms you can "place rail and construct networks of minecarts to shift and store those heavy ores for you" — worth building for big hauls.
- **Support beams and ladders are craftable supports.** The official description mentions crafting "support items like sturdy beams and platforms" to stabilize tunnels and reach new areas.
- **Gear hooks exist.** The official feature list confirms you can "attach tools to gear hooks to free up your hands whilst you strike the earth" — a quality-of-life mechanic unique to this game.

## Ore Types and Depth (Partially Unconfirmed)

The official materials confirm that you gather **ores, treasures, and long-lost relics** and that they are refined back at your dwarven home. They do **not** name specific ore types. The keyword "mithril" appears in Google Suggest data, but as of August 2026 no official source confirms mithril or any other named ore in the game.

What is reasonable to say, and confirmed by the game's own pitch:

| Depth | Confirmed Behavior | Risk |
|-------|-------------------|------|
| Shallow | Easy access, modest finds, close to the elevator | Low |
| Deep | "The deeper you dig, the richer the haul" (official premise) | High — farther from safety, higher collapse exposure |

Specific depth numbers (meters, floors, or levels) have **not** been published. Community depth tables you see elsewhere are speculation.

Upgrade tiers, costs, and exact effects for these tools have not been published. The official line — "every piece of gear can be upgraded and customized to your playstyle" — is the only confirmed guarantee.

## Mining Techniques That Work in Any Extraction Game

Until the community documents the game's hidden math, these techniques are safe general practice:

### 1. Mine Toward a Route Back

Never tunnel so deep that you can't find the elevator. Leave flares or markers on your path, and lay rail early if your haul is heavy.

### 2. Prioritize by Value Per Slot

Carry capacity is limited, so bank the most valuable finds first. Relics and treasures outrank common ore in value; heavy ores are exactly what minecarts are for. If you must choose between a full inventory near the elevator and an empty one far below, extract.

### 3. Let the Minecart Do the Heavy Lifting

"Shift and store those heavy ores" is the confirmed purpose of rails and minecarts. A rail line from a rich deposit back toward the elevator turns one heavy haul into a safe extraction.

### 4. Stabilize as You Go

Place support beams before the tunnel creaks, not after. The official warning is blunt: "One wrong boot could cost you your life — or worse, your loot!"

### 5. Upgrade the Battlemallet Early

Mining speed is extraction speed. Since the Battlemallet does double duty in combat, its upgrade is the best early investment.

## Efficient Route Planning

### The Circuit Route

Plan a loop: descend → sweep a floor in a spiral around the elevator → return. Because mines are procedural, treat every run as a fresh survey: find a rich wall, mine it, mark the route home, and repeat on the next run.

### The One-Direction Rule

Explore outward from the elevator in one direction at a time, and always keep a lit, stable path behind you. Getting lost in a collapsing mine is the most common confirmed cause of losing your loot.

### Depth Budgeting

Treat depth as a budget. Every meter down is a meter back up, plus more collapse time accumulated. The confirmed trade-off is explicit in the game's own marketing: richer haul, farther from safety.

## Honest Answers About Mining

**Do I need a special tool to mine rare ores?** Unconfirmed. The official materials describe only the Battlemallet as a mining tool, and no ore tiers have been published.

**Are ores found at fixed depths?** Unconfirmed. The game confirms depth increases reward and risk, but no official depth tables exist.

**Can I build a permanent base underground?** No. Dwarf Delve is a run-based extraction roguelite — the mine resets every run. Base-building lives in your quarters on the surface (decoration and customization), not underground.

**What happens if I die with a full inventory?** Anything not extracted is lost. That's the core of the genre and the game's official pitch.
