# Why Is Your Ore Selling for Nothing? Dwarf Delve Material Exchange & Money Guide

If you played Dwarf Delve in its first few days and felt like the game was paying you nothing for a full backpack of ore, you were not imagining it. The launch build had a **zero-profit ore sale bug**, and the developer fixed it in **QoL Hotfix 1 on July 31, 2026**. The same hotfix fixed the **Material Exchange** listing negative market stocks and repaired the **chest opening UI** that sometimes refused to appear.

This guide explains how the money loop is supposed to work now, what the Material Exchange is confirmed to do, what to sell versus keep, and how to spend coin so the next run actually feels stronger. It is an economy guide, not a mining guide — for route optimization, see the [Mining Strategies Guide](/guides/mining-strategies-guide).

## The Short Answer

Your ore should not be selling for nothing anymore. Update the game.

- **Launch-era bug:** ore sales could return zero profit. Fixed July 31, 2026.
- **Material Exchange bug:** market stocks could display as negative. Fixed in the same hotfix.
- **Chest UI bug:** the chest opening interface could fail to appear. Fixed in the same hotfix.
- **Lockpicks:** price increased slightly, but they are now sold **in quantities of five**, which is a real quality-of-life change for anyone opening chests.

If you are on the current build and a sale still returns zero, that is worth reporting rather than assuming the economy is designed that way. The official patch note says the zero-profit issue was fixed, so a repeat is a bug, not a feature.

## The Confirmed Economy Loop

The store page describes the loop in plain language:

1. **Descend** into a procedurally generated mine.
2. **Mine ore**, collect treasure, and find relics.
3. **Extract** before the elevator leaves or the tunnels collapse.
4. **Return to your Dwarven home** and **refine the ore**.
5. **Sell resources for coin.**
6. **Buy better equipment, tools, perks, and skills.**

The important part is step 4. Ore is not necessarily the thing you sell. The confirmed wording is that you **refine ores back at your Dwarven home** and **trade resources for coin**. Refining is the conversion step that turns raw rock into something the economy values. If you are trying to sell unrefined ore and wondering why the number is small, check whether the game expects you to refine first.

The second important part is step 6. Coin is not a score. It only matters if it becomes **mining speed, combat damage, carry capacity, light, or survival**. A big coin stack with an unupgraded Battlemallet is a worse state than a small stack with a stronger tool.

## What the Material Exchange Is Confirmed to Do

The Material Exchange is named in the official QoL Hotfix 1 notes, which fixed an issue where it **could list market stocks as being in the negative**. That confirms three things:

- It is a **market-style vendor or exchange** with stock.
- Stock is tracked as a quantity, and that quantity could go below zero in the launch build.
- The developer treats negative stock as a bug, not a mechanic.

What the patch notes do **not** confirm: the full inventory, restock timing, buy/sell ratios, or whether the exchange is the vendor that sells lockpicks. Those details are **[Unconfirmed]**. Do not trust a guide that prints an exact ore-to-coin table; no official table exists.

What you can rely on:

- **Sell surplus resources there or through the normal sell flow.** The confirmed loop is "trade resources for coin."
- **Check stock before planning a purchase.** If an item is missing, it may be sold out rather than bugged.
- **Five-packs of lockpicks are the intended purchase size** after the hotfix. If you see singles, you may be on an older build.

## What to Sell and What to Keep

Dwarf Delve does not publish per-item values, so the safe rule is category-based:

| Category | Sell? | Why |
|----------|-------|-----|
| **Surplus common ore** | Yes, after refining | The backbone of coin income; you only need so much for upgrades |
| **Ore needed for your next upgrade** | No | Selling upgrade materials then buying them back is a losing loop |
| **Treasures** | Usually yes, after checking | High-value extraction loot; the store page treats treasure as a reward category |
| **Relics** | No | Relics unlock unique perks and quarters decorations; they are a collection, not vendor trash |
| **Lockpicks** | No | They open chests; a chest you cannot open is lost value |
| **Gear you still use** | No | The economy is tight enough that re-buying is a setback |

The honest caveat: individual treasure and relic sale values are **not documented**, and whether relics can be sold at all is **[Unconfirmed]**. When in doubt, keep the unique item and sell the stackable rock.

### The "Keep a Buffer" Rule

Before you sell a full load, set aside enough ore for the next upgrade you actually plan to buy. The launch economy was criticized for a **"gold drought"** and for item costs that did not feel proportional to earnings. The fix improved the zero-profit bug, but it did not turn the game into a loot fountain. A buffer prevents the classic mistake of selling everything, then discovering the next Battlemallet tier needs the ore you just vendored.

## Why You Still Feel Broke

Three separate things can create a "no money" feeling, and they have different fixes:

1. **You are on an old build.** The zero-profit sale bug is the first thing to rule out. Update and test one sale.
2. **You are selling the wrong stage of the resource.** Refine first, then sell. Raw ore may not be the intended sale item.
3. **You are spending coin on the wrong things.** The confirmed perk themes are **move faster, hit harder, carry more loot**. If you are buying cosmetics or spreading coin across every category, your runs will not feel stronger even if your balance is technically fine.

There is also a pacing issue that the developer acknowledged. The **Vitality Hotfix (July 28)** reduced the punishing Vitality drain and mining grind after launch feedback. That is a direct admission that the first build was too tight. If you bounced off in the first week, the current build is a different experience — not a generous one, but a less hostile one.

## The Money Route That Works

A practical loop for players who feel stuck:

1. **Run shallow and extract consistently.** A small haul that banks is worth more than a deep haul that dies. The elevator is the win condition.
2. **Refine everything you extracted.** Do not let ore sit in the vault as potential.
3. **Sell surplus, keep a buffer.** Sell the rock you do not need for the next upgrade.
4. **Buy capacity first, then damage, then speed.** Capacity multiplies every future run; damage speeds mining and combat; speed shortens exposure.
5. **Upgrade the Battlemallet before cosmetics.** It is both your mining tool and your weapon, so one upgrade improves every minute of every run.
6. **Use rails for heavy ore.** Hand-carrying heavy ore is the most common way to lose value to a collapse. The minecart system exists to move bulk cargo.
7. **Open chests when you have lockpicks.** Five-packs make this less painful, and the chest UI fix means the interaction should work.

This route does not require a specific ore type or a secret vendor. It requires finishing runs and converting loot into permanent power.

## Spending Priorities, in Order

The store page confirms what perks and skills improve, so the spending order follows from the design:

| Priority | What to buy | Why it compounds |
|----------|-------------|------------------|
| 1 | **Carry capacity** | More loot per successful extraction |
| 2 | **Battlemallet upgrades** | Faster mining and combat in the same purchase |
| 3 | **Lantern / light upgrades** | Safer navigation and fewer missed secrets |
| 4 | **Movement speed** | Shorter exposure and faster extraction |
| 5 | **Quarters decoration** | Cosmetic and possibly mechanical; last |

Exact prices are **not published**, and any guide quoting "Trophy Wall — 100 coins" is inventing numbers. Treat the table as an order of operations, not a shopping list.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Did they fix ore selling for zero?**
Yes. QoL Hotfix 1 on July 31, 2026 fixed zero-profit ore sales.

**What is the Material Exchange?**
A market-style exchange named in the official patch notes. The hotfix fixed it listing negative stock. Its full inventory and restock rules are not documented.

**Are Gild and coin the same currency?**
The launch bonus awarded **1,000 Gild**, while the store description says **coin**. Whether they are the same currency is **[Unconfirmed]**; treat Gild as the named currency and coin as the generic term until the game says otherwise.

**Should I sell relics?**
No. Relics unlock unique perks and quarters decorations. Whether they can be sold is **[Unconfirmed]**, but keeping them is the safe play.

**Why do lockpicks matter to money?**
They open chests, and chests contain value. The hotfix made lockpicks purchasable in quantities of five and fixed the chest UI, so the intended flow is to carry lockpicks and open what you find.

**Is there a best ore to farm?**
No verified ore-value table exists. The general rule is deeper runs carry rarer finds, but specific ore names and values are **[Unconfirmed]**.
