## Mining: The Heart of Dwarf Delve

Mining is the foundation of everything in Dwarf Delve. Every upgrade, perk, and piece of gear begins with ore pulled from the earth, and every successful run ends with your haul extracted before the mine collapses. You gather **ores, treasures, and long-lost relics** with your Battlemallet — there is no pickaxe in this game — and convert them into permanent power at the surface.

This guide is the complete strategy layer for mining: the confirmed mechanics, the honest answer to "how deep should I go?", how rarity works, resource priorities, and run management for maximum profit. Dwarf Delve is a new game, so anything not confirmed by the developer is marked honestly as unconfirmed.

## Core Mining Mechanics (Confirmed)

- **The Battlemallet is your mining tool.** The official description calls it your "trusty Battlemallet," and it serves as both tool and weapon. Any guide that tells you to craft a pickaxe is describing a different game.
- **Mines are procedurally generated.** "No two mines will ever be the same!" — you cannot memorize a layout from a previous run.
- **The mine collapses over time.** The elevator leaves on a schedule, and anything not extracted is lost. Mining time is limited time.
- **Depth equals reward and risk.** The game's own pitch: "the deeper you dig, the richer the haul but the farther you are from safety."
- **Rails and minecarts are your heavy-lifting partners.** The official feature list confirms you can "place rail and construct networks of minecarts to shift and store those heavy ores for you."
- **Support beams and ladders are craftable supports.** The official description mentions crafting "support items like sturdy beams and platforms" to stabilize tunnels and reach new areas.
- **Gear hooks exist.** "Attach tools to gear hooks to free up your hands whilst you strike the earth" — a confirmed quality-of-life mechanic.

## The Best Mining Depth: The Honest Answer

**The best mining depth in Dwarf Delve is the deepest you can safely extract from.** That sounds like a non-answer, but it's the game's actual design: depth is a risk/reward dial, not a fixed destination — and no official depth numbers have been published. Any guide that tells you "mine at 150m for mithril" is inventing data.

Depth strategy breaks down into two questions, answered below: how deep to go at each stage of progression, and how to decide in the moment.

### Depth Strategy by Progression Stage

**Stage 1: Learning the Loop (First ~10 Runs) — depth target: shallow, barely below the elevator's reach.**
Learn the collapse rhythm, trap tells, and your tools. Extract with whatever you bank — even small hauls fund upgrades. Goal: consistent extraction, not depth.

**Stage 2: Building Power (Runs 10-30) — depth target: one stage deeper than comfortable, then extract.**
Upgrade Battlemallet (mining + combat) and lantern (darkness) first. Each run, descend a little further than the last — until the first instability warning, then extract. Start using flares to mark deeper routes.

**Stage 3: The Deep Game (Runs 30+) — depth target: as deep as your extraction discipline allows.**
Max carry capacity (confirmed "carry more loot" theme), rail the heavy ores, and hunt relics — the confirmed endgame prize. Extract the moment one is found.

## Mining Efficiency Techniques (Safe Practice)

Without published game math, these are general voxel-mining practices that hold up in any similar game — use them, but don't treat them as official mechanics:

1. **Sweep, don't tunnel** — mine in expanding sweeps around your position rather than one long tunnel. Sweeps reveal veins on multiple walls and keep you oriented. Coverage beats depth-per-minute.
2. **Listen and look** — in voxel-style games, veins often reveal themselves through texture or color differences in the rock. When you find a vein, follow it — veins frequently connect to larger deposits.
3. **Mine toward a route back** — never tunnel so deep that you can't find the elevator. Leave flares or markers on your path, and lay rail early if your haul is heavy.
4. **Plan the circuit** — descend, sweep a floor in a spiral around the elevator, return. Because mines are procedural, treat every run as a fresh survey.
5. **The one-direction rule** — explore outward from the elevator in one direction at a time, keeping a lit, stable path behind you. Getting lost in a collapsing mine is the most common confirmed cause of losing your loot.
6. **Pre-craft for the return** — beams and flares cost nothing to carry in terms of inventory pressure (they're tools, not loot). Always keep enough for the return trip — the way home is where runs are lost.
7. **Upgrade the Battlemallet early** — mining speed is extraction speed, and the Battlemallet does double duty in combat.

## When to Push Deeper vs. Extract

The confirmed decision inputs:

| Push Deeper When | Extract Now When |
|------------------|------------------|
| Inventory has room | Inventory meaningfully full of valuables |
| Tunnels stable (no creaking/dust) | Tunnels unstable |
| No relic found yet | Relic found (bank it) |
| Beams and light in reserve | Beams/light running low |
| Elevator route known and short | Route long or unmarked |

The community rule (honestly labeled, not official): the first instability warning is the extraction signal. Greed is the game's designed killer — "One wrong boot could cost you your life — or worse, your loot!"

### Reading the Mine: Depth Signals

Since no official depth meter or zone names exist, every run is a judgment call:

- **Instability cadence** — the sooner instability starts, the shallower your depth budget should be. A run that's creaking at the second floor is telling you its ceiling; a quiet, stable run is an invitation to push.
- **Route geometry** — some runs have a natural spiral of short, connected levels; others drop deep fast. If the return trip is already long and winding at modest depth, that's a shallow-budget run.
- **Find quality early** — found a relic or treasure early? The run's objective is achieved; depth becomes optional. Found nothing but common ore by the time you're deep? Extract and reroll.
- **Light and beam reserves** — your confirmed survival tools (lantern, flares, support beams) are a depth budget in themselves. When reserves run low, depth budget runs out.

## Risk Management at Depth

The golden rule of Dwarf Delve mining: **your most valuable resource is a successful extraction.** A dead Delveling brings home nothing.

- **The depth budget** — before descending, set a mental budget: "I'm going to X depth, and I extract at the first warning."
- **The return-trip rule** — depth is only as good as the return trip. Every meter down is a meter back — through the same hazards, with a heavier inventory, on a worse clock. Ask "can I get back from here?" before you go deeper, not after.
- **The cart factor** — rails turn deep heavy-ore hauls into fast, safe extracts. When your runs involve heavy ore far from the elevator, depth converts from liability into profit.
- **The relic trigger** — a relic in hand = extraction now. It's the single best depth-management rule in the game.
- **Shallow** — learn the loop, push your luck safely
- **Mid** — start the return earlier than feels necessary
- **Deep** — set a hard trigger (relic, full cart, first warning) before descending — and honor it

## A Sample Staged Run

Concrete plans make the depth question actionable. Three run templates by stage:

### Template 1: The Shallow Learner (First 10 Runs)
1. Descend one stop; sweep the immediate floor
2. Mine until the first instability warning
3. Extract with whatever you have
4. Refine, upgrade the Battlemallet, repeat

**Depth decision:** shallow, always. **Trigger:** first warning OR full inventory.

### Template 2: The Mid Push (Runs 10-30)
1. Descend; sweep shallow for quick ore
2. Push one level deeper than last run, marking the route with flares
3. Continue until the second instability warning
4. Return via the marked route; extract

**Depth decision:** one stage deeper per run, retreating at the first signs of trouble. **Trigger:** second warning OR relic.

### Template 3: The Deep Hauler (Runs 30+)
1. Descend fast with maxed Battlemallet, lantern, and capacity
2. Find a rich deposit; build rail toward the elevator (confirmed: carts "shift and store those heavy ores")
3. Fill the cart; ride back; extract; refine; reinvest
4. On relic runs: push depth with a hard trigger — relic in hand = extraction now

**Depth decision:** as deep as the rail line and your discipline allow. **Trigger:** full cart OR relic OR first warning.

## Common Mining & Depth Mistakes

- **Going deep before upgrading** — upgrades are permanent; depth is not. Shallow-and-safe funds deep-and-rich
- **Ignoring the return trip** — every meter down is a meter back with a heavier load and a worse clock
- **No extraction trigger** — deciding to leave "when it feels right" turns into deciding to leave at 1 HP in a sealed tunnel
- **Hauling heavy ore on foot** — that's the confirmed job of rails and carts; hand-carrying deep ore doubles the risk
- **Deep runs without beams or light** — the two confirmed survival tools; a deep, dark, unbraced run is a donation to the mine
- **Tunneling without a route back** — getting lost in a collapsing mine is how full inventories die
- **Building a permanent base underground** — the mine resets every run; base-building lives in your quarters on the surface
