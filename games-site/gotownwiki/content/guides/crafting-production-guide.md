# Go-Go Town! Production & Crafting Guide — Every Chain Explained

Go-Go Town! is a production-chain game wearing a cozy disguise. Every shop menu, every machine, and every tourist purchase traces back to one of four production zones: **forestry, mining, farming, and fishing**. Understand the chains and you can keep every shop stocked automatically; ignore them and your shops sit empty while warehouses overflow.

## The Four Production Zones

### Forestry

The forest supplies wood products:

- **Chainsaw** trees → **wood**
- **Sawbench** → wood into **planks** or **paper**
- **Woodworking machine** → planks into **frames** or **fiberboard**
- **Table saw** → **paper reams** (for the bookstore)
- **Loom** → **fluff into cloth**

### Mining

The mine supplies construction materials and gems:

- **Jackhammer** → **stone**, **metal**, and **gems**
- **Brick kiln** → stone into **bricks**, metal into **ingots**
- **Assembly machine** → bricks into **concrete**, ingots into **metal beams**
- **Magic extractor** (place on purple-smoking holes) → **raw magic**, refined into **mana** (barbershop) 
- **Gem crusher** → gems into **gem powder**, which becomes **glass**

### Farming

The farm feeds the food economy:

- **Wheat** → **flour** → **bread**
- **Milk** → **cheese**, **butter** (milk/soy machine)
- **Honeycomb** → **honey**
- **Sugarcane** → **sugar**
- **Coffee beans** → **roasted coffee** (bean roaster)
- **Cocoa** → **chocolate**
- **Rice paddies** → **rice**
- **Animals** → **milk** and **eggs**

### Fishing

The fishery handles seafood:

- **Fish** → **sausages** and **protein** (seafood converter)
- **Octopus** → **octopus sausage**
- **Ribeye steak** → **protein**
- **Crab pots** → crabs
- **Rice paddies** also live near the water

## Shop Menus and Supply Chains

Every shop needs a menu that your production chain can actually feed. When setting up a shop:

1. **Pick a menu** that matches what your zones produce (or build production to match the menu you want)
2. **Assign staff** — shops don't sell without a shopkeeper
3. **Supply raw materials** — manually at first, then via couriers from the Logistics building

**Stalls and food trucks** don't need staff — stock them manually (food trucks get auto-restocked by couriers). They're the ideal early-game sales point for overproduced items.

## The Overproduction Problem

Farming and fishing overproduce fast — rice, fish, and honeycomb pile up until workers' backpacks fill and the line stops. Solutions, in order of effectiveness:

1. **Add sales points** — vending machines are the perfect sink for fast-accumulating items
2. **Plant only what you need** — one rice paddy is usually plenty; you will drown in rice otherwise
3. **Lock storage bins** — press I on a storage box, select a product, press L; workers deposit that item there automatically, keeping products separate

## Setting Up Automated Chains

The goal is a chain where you never touch a machine again:

1. Build the zone's machines **inside the zone boundary**
2. Add **storage inside the zone** (workers won't fetch tools outside it)
3. Hire workers at the **zone clock**
4. Build the **Logistics building** and hire couriers to move goods to shops
5. Add **vending machines or stalls** to absorb surplus

The chain fails silently at any broken link — if shops sit empty, check storage locks first, then courier paths, then worker tools.

## Recipe Price Guide

Most items sell in the 10–15 coin range, so **volume matters more than margin**. Popcorn sells for 15 coins at market stands and has one of the fastest chains (corn → roaster), which is why endgame players convert farms into popcorn factories. Check the community recipe lists for exact prices, but plan for volume: a stocked stall with cheap goods usually beats an empty shop with fancy ones.
