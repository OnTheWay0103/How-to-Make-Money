# Go-Go Town! Townie Guide — How to Recruit Residents

Townies are the residents who live in and work for your town — and they are your **only workforce**. Every shopkeeper, miner, farmer, courier, and sanitation worker you'll ever hire was once a tourist who decided to stay. Understanding how recruitment works is the difference between a town that runs itself and a town where you do every job personally.

This guide covers the full Townie loop: how tourists become residents, the quests each one brings, how housing works, and how to turn your residents into the workers who automate your economy.

## What Is a Townie?

A Townie is any character who has moved into your town permanently. Go-Go Town! currently has **83 townies** in total (per the community wiki), and every one of them starts out as a visiting tourist.

The core loop of the game depends on them:

**attract tourists → convert some into Townies → house them → assign jobs → use their production to attract more tourists**

The game has no timer, no rent, and no wages — the only real cost of having a Townie is the housing you build for them. There's no downside to a big population except the space they take up, so growing your resident count is almost always correct.

## Step 1: Find Tourists Ready to Move In

Tourists arrive by **train every day** — which is why building the train station early is so important (see our [beginner guide](/guides/beginner-guide)). Not every visitor wants to stay, but the ones who do are easy to spot:

- **Look for the "+" icon floating above a tourist's head.** That means they're considering moving in.
- **Talk to them** to learn what they need. Tourists have a **specific request** — pay a sum of money, build a particular building or decoration, or complete some other objective in town.
- **Complete the request**, then talk to them again and **invite them to stay**.

Until their request is fulfilled, they'll keep visiting as a tourist. Fulfilled requests turn them into a resident — but only if you have somewhere to put them.

## Step 2: Build Housing First

A tourist will only move in if you have a **house or apartment** ready with space for them. This is the #1 reason players think recruitment is broken: they invite a "+" tourist and nothing happens, because there's no home available.

Housing basics:

- **Small house** — the first housing you can build; houses a small number of residents.
- **Apartments** — larger-capacity housing that lets you grow your population past what a few houses can hold.

Once a resident has a home, they are formally a Townie: they live in your town permanently, and you can assign them to work anywhere in it.

**Housing is your population cap.** If you want more workers, build more housing. Early on, keep a spare room available so that when a "+" tourist shows up you can convert them immediately instead of waiting to build.

## Step 3: Each Townie Has a Unique Quest

This is the part most guides miss: the 83 Townies aren't interchangeable. Each one has a **unique recruitment quest and unique dialogue**, and the requirements vary wildly:

- Some ask for a **sum of money**
- Some ask you to **build a specific building, prop, or decoration**
- Some ask for specific items or in-town milestones

For example, the community wiki documents that townie **Louie** requires a **garbage bin** built in town as part of his quest — a reminder that even "simple" townies can force you into the build menu. Plan for quests by keeping some spare materials and coins on hand rather than spending every dollar the moment you earn it.

### Recruitment Rewards

Many Townies **reward you when they move in** — new **clothing or items** are common rewards. It's worth working through quests even for residents you don't strictly need a job for, just to collect the rewards and expand your customization options.

### The One Disappointment to Expect

After recruitment, Townie interaction is **limited**. Once moved in, they become generic workers you can reassign freely; there's no ongoing dialogue system. The community has repeatedly asked the developers for more resident interaction, and it may come in a future update — for now, treat recruitment as the fun part and post-recruitment as workforce management.

## Step 4: Assign Your Workforce

Now that you have residents, put them to work. The roles you can hire for include:

- **Miners** (mine), **lumberjacks** (forest), **fishermen** (fishery), and **farmers** (farm) — the four production jobs
- **Shopkeepers, artisans, and hot dog sellers** — they run your shops and food carts
- **Couriers** — hired at the Courier Building (the "logistics building"), they deliver materials to shops, food trucks, and construction sites automatically
- **Sanitation workers** — trash collection and cleaning

Hiring works through **timeclocks** in each production zone (community guides call them "zone clocks") plus the various service buildings, and the automation options unlock gradually through the **economy tier of the tech tree** — see our [automation guide](/guides/automation-guide) for the full worker setup.

### Choose Your Couriers Carefully

Ghosts, robots, and mummies — the special tourist types from the Tourist Trap update — are **slow movers**. If you recruit them, they make poor couriers and errand runners. Put them to work in stationary jobs (shops, stalls, machines) instead.

## Bonus: The Secret Townie

Treasure hunters have already found one hidden resident: the **secret Townie Dusty**, who joins your town after a questline that starts deep in the mine tunnel network. Look for a **cracked wall** that looks slightly different from the others, use an **upgraded drill** on it to reveal the hidden **Tomb**, and complete the associated questline — see our [treasure hunting guide](/guides/treasure-hunting-guide) for how to get there.

## More Townies = More Space: Unlocking Land

Population growth eventually needs room. After the "Build and Bustle" update, the map is much larger, with **new areas to unlock through the Real Estate Agent (Stanley S. Trollson)** — you buy land parcels with EGO and unlock new construction areas across the map. If you've run out of space for houses, check what land is still locked before giving up on growth.

## Common Recruitment Problems

**"The "+" tourist won't move in."** Check, in order: (1) do you have a house or apartment with a free room? (2) did you complete their request? (3) did you invite them *after* completing the request? Most "broken" recruitments are one of these three.

**"I can't find any "+" tourists."** Recruitment volume tracks tourist volume. If the town isn't growing, work on the basics first: train station built, tourist slider tuned up, decor and cleanliness keeping satisfaction high, and town rank rising. See our [tourist attraction guide](/guides/tourist-attraction-guide) — more and happier tourists means more candidates.

**"I recruited a Townie but they won't work."** Residents only become workers once they're **housed** and you **assign them** through the appropriate hiring point (timeclock or service building). A resident without an assignment just lives in town — they don't self-organize.

**"Why does everyone want money?"** It's normal. Many Townie quests cost coins, so budget for recruitment just like you budget for buildings. Early on, that's a reason to keep a small coin reserve instead of spending everything on decor.

## Summary

The Townie loop is the heart of Go-Go Town!: **attract tourists → find "+" visitors → complete their quests → build housing → invite them in → assign jobs → profit**. Build housing before you invite, budget coins for quests, keep courier jobs for fast residents, and keep an eye out for hidden residents like Dusty. With enough Townies and the right assignments, your town will genuinely run itself — and you can spend your time decorating, treasure hunting, or just watching the chaos.

### Quick Checklist

1. Train station built and tourists arriving
2. Houses/apartments with free rooms available
3. "+" tourists' requests completed before inviting
4. Coin reserve for money-based quests
5. Residents assigned at timeclocks and service buildings
6. Slow Townies (ghosts, robots, mummies) kept off courier duty
7. Land unlocked when housing space runs out
