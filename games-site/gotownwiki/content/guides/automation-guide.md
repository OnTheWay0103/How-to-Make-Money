# Go-Go Town! Automation Guide — Workers, Couriers & Logistics

Go-Go Town! is less of a pure city builder and more of an **automation-focused building game**. The pitch is simple: hire Townies to do the work, build logistics to move the goods, and let your town run itself while you decorate. This guide covers every worker type, how to unlock automation, and how to fix the town when it stops running.

## Step 1: Hire Residents

Automation starts with **Townies**. Tourists with a "+" above their head move in if you have a house or apartment for them. Once they're residents, you can assign them jobs — this is the only workforce you'll ever get, so grow your population deliberately.

## Step 2: Zone Clocks — Production Automation

The four production zones each have a **zone clock** (farm, fishery, forest, mine). Unlock the relevant tech, hire workers at the clock, and they will automate resource production inside their zone:

- **Farm** — plant, water, and harvest crops; feed animals for milk and eggs
- **Fishery** — fish at marked spots and run crab pots
- **Forest** — chainsaw trees, run the sawbench, table saw, and woodworking machines
- **Mine** — jackhammer stone, metal, and gems; run the brick kiln and assembler

Each zone needs its own **storage and tools inside the zone boundary** — workers won't fetch tools dropped outside, and full storage stops production.

## Step 3: The Logistics Building — Courier Automation

The **Logistics building** is the backbone of a self-running town. Assign residents as **couriers**, and they deliver materials to shops, food trucks, and construction sites automatically. Build it as soon as it unlocks — without couriers you are the courier.

Courier tips:

- **Keep roads clear.** If couriers can't drive, they run on foot and the supply chain slows to a crawl.
- **Don't fight your couriers for work.** If you place materials first, the AI may drop them on the ground — task conflict. Let them do the job.
- **Avoid slow couriers.** Ghosts, robots, and mummies make poor couriers — they're too slow.

## Step 4: Commerce and Sanitation

- **Shopkeepers, artisans, and hot dog sellers** run your shops so you never touch a counter again.
- **Sanitation Bureau workers** empty trash bins, clean purple stains, and repair damaged equipment.
- **Stalls and vending machines** need no staff at all — stock them manually (or let couriers auto-restock food trucks) and tourists buy on their own.

## Common Automation Problems and Fixes

**"My workers are idle and nothing is happening."** Working through this checklist fixes 90% of cases:

1. **Tools outside the zone** — move tools inside the zone boundary.
2. **No seeds** — farm workers stop replanting when seed inventory hits zero. Collect seeds from the purple flowers left of the farm (they refresh daily) or buy seed packs from the Import app.
3. **Full storage** — workers stop when storage trays are full. Add storage or more sales points (vending machines are the best sink).
4. **Storage not locked** — press I on a storage box, select a product, press L to lock it, and workers will deposit that item there automatically.
5. **Stuck equipment** — if a machine or worker is stuck, delete and rebuild the building (or the courier station if the courier's vehicle is stuck).
6. **Task conflict** — stop doing the worker's job yourself; the AI gets confused by interference.

**"Workers only catch crabs and don't fish"** — delete crab cages (or clear storage), and delete fishing rods if you want the opposite. **"Stuck maintenance worker"** — unhire/rehire, or give them a gentle nudge with a vehicle.

## What Still Requires Manual Work

Even a fully automated town leaves a few tasks to you: collecting seeds, repositioning magic extractors on purple spots, and unlocking new recipes. Players have asked the developers to automate these too — for now, treat them as quick daily chores.

## The Automation Progression

Automation unlocks gradually with town rank and EGO Tech: **all-manual → semi-auto → near-full automation**. By mid-game your role shifts from worker to manager — tweaking menus, expanding production, and landscaping. The early game is the only part where you must do everything yourself, so push through it; the self-running town is worth the wait.
