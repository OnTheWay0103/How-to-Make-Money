# Go-Go Town! Photo Mode Guide — How to Open It and Take Better Screenshots

Photo Mode is one of the quietest additions to Go-Go Town!. It arrived in **hotfix 1.0.26502 on September 14, 2026**, tucked into a patch mostly about terrain brushes and Garbird sliders. There is no tutorial pop-up for it, and the game does not advertise it on the main menu. If you have been playing since 1.0 and never noticed it, you are not alone.

This guide covers how to open Photo Mode, what each camera control does, how filters and time of day work, and how to use it in co-op without ruining someone else's shot.

**Version note:** Photo Mode came to PC first in 1.0.26502. Community reporting places the Switch and Switch 2 version at **1.0.26511 (September 28, 2026)**. If the option is missing, check your build number before assuming the feature is broken.

## How to Open Photo Mode

The entry point is the **Emote Wheel**:

1. Open the Emote Wheel with its normal input.
2. Select **Photo Mode** from the wheel.
3. The camera detaches and the Photo Mode interface appears.

That is the entire unlock process. There is no quest, no Tech Tree node, and no purchase. If you can emote, you can use Photo Mode.

**Why the Emote Wheel?** It is the same wheel you use for character expressions, which matters later: Photo Mode does not replace the wheel. You can still trigger a smile or frown while the camera is active.

## Camera Controls

The official patch notes confirm that Photo Mode has **different camera modes, filters, and time-of-day selection**. The community guide fills in the control set:

| Control | What it does | Best use |
|---------|--------------|----------|
| **Free camera** | Move the camera independently of the player | Wide town shots, rooftops, street layouts |
| **Orbit** | Rotate around the player | Character portraits, Townie close-ups |
| **FOV** | Changes how much of the scene is in frame | Tight portraits vs. sweeping vistas |
| **Roll** | Tilts the camera | Dramatic angles, stylized screenshots |

These are **community-reported labels**, not official button bindings. The patch notes do not publish a control map, and the exact buttons may differ by platform or controller. Treat the table as a description of what the camera can do, not a guarantee of which key does it.

**Pro tip:** start with **orbit** for people and **free camera** for places. Orbit keeps your mayor in frame and makes it easy to adjust height; free camera is better for showing a neighborhood or a production line without your character blocking the view.

## Filters and Time of Day

Photo Mode includes filters and a **time-of-day selector**. The community guide reports **14 filters**, but does not list their names, and the official patch notes do not enumerate them either. That means:

- The **count (14)** is community-reported, not officially confirmed.
- The **names and exact looks** are **[Unconfirmed]**.
- The **time-of-day options** are not named in any official source.

What is confirmed is the workflow: you can change the time of day for the photo, and you can apply a filter. The practical advice is to **set the light first, then the filter**. A filter that looks flat at noon can look dramatic at dusk, and changing the time of day often does more for a screenshot than cycling through every filter.

**A good default workflow:**

1. Pick the camera angle and FOV.
2. Set the time of day to the light you want (golden hour for warm town shots, night for neon/industrial areas).
3. Apply one filter, not three. Filters stack visually; subtlety reads better at thumbnail size.
4. Hide the UI.
5. Take the shot.

## Hide the UI

The community guide reports a **Hide UI** option. Use it. A screenshot with quest markers, the build toolbar, and the money counter is a screenshot of the interface, not the town.

Hide UI is also useful for **before/after documentation**: capture a run-down block before you rezone it, then capture the same angle after the shops and decorations go in. The comparison is more convincing than a description.

## NPCs, Emotes, and "Say Cheese!"

Photo Mode has two features that make character shots much easier:

- **"Say Cheese!"** triggers all nearby NPCs to **wave or cheer**. It is a one-button crowd reaction, and it works on Townies and tourists.
- **The Emote Wheel stays available** while you are in Photo Mode, so you can trigger a smile or frown on your own character.

The order matters. Trigger **Say Cheese** first, then use the Emote Wheel for your own expression, then take the shot. If you emote first and then trigger Say Cheese, the NPC reaction may pull focus while your character is mid-expression.

**Pro tip:** Say Cheese is also a good way to check whether an area is populated enough to photograph. If nobody nearby reacts, your "busy town center" shot is going to look empty.

## Co-op: Camera Drones and Photobombing

In multiplayer, a player in Photo Mode is represented by a **small camera drone**. Other players can see where the drone is aiming, which makes coordinated shots possible — and makes photobombing very easy.

The patch notes explicitly frame this as a feature: other players can see how you are lining up your shot, "meaning you can photobomb at all the right moments."

**Co-op etiquette that keeps the session fun:**

- **Call your shot.** Say "I'm taking a wide shot of the station" before someone walks into frame.
- **Photobomb on purpose, not by accident.** A deliberate photobomb is funny; a player who does not know Photo Mode exists will just be confused.
- **Use the drone as a marker.** If a friend is lining up a shot, the drone shows you where to stand to be in it.
- **Take turns.** A full lobby all entering Photo Mode at once turns the town into a drone convention.

## Composition Tips for Go-Go Town!

Go-Go Town! is a dense, colorful builder, which makes it easy to take busy screenshots that lose the subject. A few rules help:

### 1. Pick one subject per shot

A shop, a train station, a farm plot, a Townie portrait. If everything is in frame, nothing is.

### 2. Use roads and paths as leading lines

The town's road grid is a natural composition tool. Put a road along the bottom third of the frame and let it pull the eye toward the building you want to show.

### 3. Shoot at the edges of the day

Midday light flattens terrain and shadows. Dusk and night add contrast, and the game's lighting changes are free to use.

### 4. Get low for buildings, high for layouts

A low camera makes a shop look bigger and more inviting. A high free-camera shot shows how zones connect — useful for layout guides and before/after posts.

### 5. Hide the UI before every shot

It is one toggle and it is the difference between a screenshot and a marketing image.

## Using Photo Mode for Town Planning

Photo Mode is not only for sharing. It is a planning tool:

- **Audit your layout from above.** A high free-camera shot reveals gaps, dead ends, and zones that are technically connected but visually confusing.
- **Document worker routes.** Photograph a production chain from input to output and check whether the path is actually short.
- **Compare decoration passes.** Take the same angle before and after a decoration push; the difference is easier to judge than in motion.
- **Check tourist sightlines.** Stand where tourists arrive and photograph what they see first. If the answer is "a blank wall," your first impression needs work.

The [best layout guide](/guides/best-layout-guide) covers zone placement; Photo Mode is how you verify it in your own town.

## Platform and Patch Caveats

- **PC:** Photo Mode was added in **1.0.26502** (September 14, 2026).
- **Switch / Switch 2:** community reporting places it in **1.0.26511** (September 28, 2026).
- **Button bindings, filter names, and exact time-of-day options** are not officially documented. If a guide lists them as fact, it is guessing.
- **Photo Mode is not a pause button.** In co-op, the world keeps running while you frame a shot. Do not enter Photo Mode during a tourist rush or a worker emergency.

## FAQ

**How do I open Photo Mode?** Open the Emote Wheel and select Photo Mode.

**Is Photo Mode free?** Yes. It is a base-game feature added in a hotfix, not DLC.

**Can I use it in co-op?** Yes. Other players see you as a camera drone and can photobomb your shot.

**How many filters are there?** A community guide reports 14. The official patch notes do not list a number or names, so treat the count as community-reported.

**Can I hide the UI?** Yes, the community guide reports a Hide UI option.

**Why is Photo Mode missing on my Switch?** Check your build. Community reporting places Switch/Switch 2 support at 1.0.26511; older builds do not have it.

## Final Verdict

Photo Mode is a small feature with surprising utility. Open it from the Emote Wheel, set the light before the filter, hide the UI, and use Say Cheese to wake up the crowd. In co-op, the camera drone turns screenshots into a group activity; in single-player, the free camera is the best layout-audit tool the game has. It will not change how your town runs, but it will change how well you see it.
