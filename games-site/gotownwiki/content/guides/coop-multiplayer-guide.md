# Go-Go Town! Co-op Multiplayer Guide — Playing with Friends

Go-Go Town! markets its multiplayer as **"Chaotic Co-Op"** — team up with another mayor and build together, manage together, panic together. A carefully planned town can quickly go sideways when everyone has different ideas of helping. Here's how multiplayer works and how to make it productive instead of chaotic.

## Player Counts by Platform

- **PC (Steam)** — up to **4 players** in online co-op and local split-screen, plus Steam Remote Play Together
- **Nintendo Switch / Switch 2** — **1–2 players** locally and online (the Switch 2 Edition adds 1080p/60fps and improved visuals)

PC split-screen layout details vary by version and display — some players report a vertical (top-and-bottom) split.

## How Co-op Works

In co-op, multiple mayors share the same town. Everyone can build, terraform, gather, explore, and manage — there are no separate economies, so any structure either player places belongs to the shared town. Character customization is per-player, so each mayor looks distinct.

Co-op gameplay includes timed challenges plus the relaxed activities (fishing, treasure hunting) you'd expect from a cozy game.

## Setting Up a Session

- **Online co-op** — the host opens the in-game phone's **Friends app** and invites Steam friends, or shares an **invite code** so friends can jump straight in. Any combination of up to 4 online or local split-screen players can share one town.
- **Local split-screen** — Player 1 opens the phone's Friends app and presses Invite; Player 2 presses a button on a connected controller to join. The Steam version supports full controller play for 2–4 players on one machine.
- **Steam Remote Play Together** — the host streams the game, so invited friends don't need to own it. Note: only one keyboard and mouse is supported — extra players need controllers.

The host assigns invited players roles (**Vice-Mayor, Townie, or Tourist**) with customizable permissions, and co-op towns are saved on the host's account. If a player leaves mid-session, they drop a duffle bag with their gear that anyone can collect or move.

## Division of Labor: The Best Co-op Strategies

Because zones are independent bubbles, splitting responsibilities by zone is the cleanest strategy:

- **One mayor runs production** — farming, fishing, forestry, and mining chains
- **One mayor runs commerce** — stocking shops, stalls, and vending machines, setting menus
- **One mayor runs logistics** — couriers, roads, and keeping delivery paths clear
- **One mayor runs tourism** — decor, cleanliness, and Tourist Tracker complaints

This prevents the classic co-op failure mode where both players try to stock the same shop and confuse the workers.

## Co-op Tips

1. **Agree on a layout first.** Since you can't rotate buildings and zones are per-player-managed, decide the road grid and zone placement before building begins. Our [layout guide](/guides/best-layout-guide) covers the principles.
2. **Don't double-do tasks.** If your teammate is stocking a shop, work elsewhere — task conflicts confuse couriers and workers.
3. **Use the town's automation to cover each other.** Hire Townies and couriers so the town runs even while one mayor is off treasure hunting.
4. **Designate one mayor for challenges.** Boss challenges are shared — assigning one player to track them avoids double-completing objectives.

## Crossplay Considerations

Steam co-op covers online, local split-screen, and Remote Play Together — PC players can include friends without the game via Remote Play. Cross-platform co-op between PC and consoles isn't currently featured; the Switch versions support 2-player local and online co-op within the Nintendo ecosystem. Check your platform store page for the latest details.

## Is Co-op Worth It?

Yes — if anything, co-op makes the game more fun than solo because two mayors can work the early game in parallel: one gets the economy running while the other decorates and recruits. The game is fully playable solo (the same loop, just more manual work early), but the "chaos" of shared building is a genuinely different experience worth trying.
