# How Do Difficulty Modes Work in Beast of Reincarnation? Very Hard & Ruthless Explained

Beast of Reincarnation launched with three difficulty settings. **Patch v1.1.0 (October 1, 2026)** added two more — **Very Hard** and **Ruthless** — and extended the difficulty options into New Game+. If you last checked a guide in August, the list you saw is out of date.

This guide explains what each mode is, what the patch actually confirms, how to change difficulty mid-run, and what Ruthless demands from a build. It sticks to published facts; where the developers have not stated a multiplier or a reward, the guide says so instead of inventing one.

## The difficulty modes at a glance

| Mode | Where it sits | What is confirmed | What is not confirmed |
|------|---------------|-------------------|-----------------------|
| **Story** | Below Normal | The most forgiving launch mode; intended for players who want the story | Exact damage, enemy HP or parry-window changes |
| **Normal** | Baseline | The default combat balance; Reincarnation+ starts here | Exact multipliers |
| **Hard** | Above Normal | A launch mode; Very Hard was added above it | Exact multipliers |
| **Very Hard** | Above Hard | Added in v1.1.0 for players who found Hard too forgiving | Exact enemy damage, HP or reward changes |
| **Ruthless** | Challenge mode | Emma dies instantly from a single hit | Any reward, achievement or completion requirement |

The official patch notes describe **Ruthless** as "a bonus challenge mode for fans," not as a replacement for Hard. That wording matters: it is a self-imposed challenge, not a fourth tier of the normal progression.

**Reincarnation+** is the New Game+ difficulty layer, not a sixth row in the table. At launch it was the hardest setting. From v1.1.0, Reincarnation+ lets you choose **Hard, Very Hard or Ruthless**, and its default difficulty corresponds to **Normal**. If you want the old Reincarnation+ experience, select the hardest option available on your build rather than assuming the default is still maximum.

## How to change difficulty

The official v1.1.0 notes state that you can **switch between difficulty modes at any time during gameplay**. You do not need to start a new run, and the patch notes do not mention a penalty for changing.

The exact menu path is **[Unconfirmed]** and may vary slightly between PC and console builds. Look for the difficulty option in the settings menu, usually alongside gameplay or combat options. Save before experimenting so you can return to the same spot if a mode is not what you expected.

This freedom is the most useful part of the update. You can test Ruthless on a boss you already know, drop back to Very Hard when a new attack pattern catches you, and keep the story moving on Normal. It also means a difficulty wall is now a settings problem, not a restart problem.

## What patch v1.1.0 changed besides the modes

The same patch adjusted combat balance, so an old tier list or boss guide may no longer be exact:

- **Nushi variants** had some attack patterns overhauled.
- **Shidou** received updated attack patterns, with more changes planned.
- Bosses and enemies that were easier than intended were adjusted upward.
- **Ranged weapon damage against bosses** was adjusted.
- The power of select **Blade Arts Finishers and Bloom Arts** was adjusted.

Patch v1.1.01 followed on October 9 with a **Field of View setting**, an option to show or hide cutscene skip prompts, and a story-skip option after the Great Wall Terraces zone. That skip option lets you bypass the Pilgrim's Zone, Matsukawa Colony Zone and Fuji Frontier Zone. The patch notes are explicit about the cost: achievements obtainable in those zones during a **New Game** run cannot be earned if you skip them, but they remain available on a **New Game+** run that takes the normal Mountain Route. That is an achievement rule, not a difficulty rule, but it belongs in the same planning conversation.

## What Ruthless actually asks of you

Ruthless changes one rule: a single hit kills Emma. The patch notes do not add a damage multiplier, an enemy HP increase or a new move set on top of that. The challenge is the existing combat with the safety net removed.

That has build consequences, and these are our recommendations rather than developer statements:

- **Defensive stats lose much of their purpose.** If any hit ends the run, stacking health or damage reduction does not buy the extra mistake it normally buys. Prioritise offence, posture pressure and utility instead.
- **Parry and evasion become the whole game.** The launch combat already rewards parrying; on Ruthless, a missed parry is not a bad trade, it is the end of the attempt.
- **Koo's commands matter more.** A companion command that interrupts, staggers or redirects a boss can create the window you need without exposing Emma. Treat Koo as a defensive tool, not only a damage rotation.
- **Learn one boss at a time.** The mode is best treated as a boss-rush challenge. Practise a single fight on Very Hard until the tells are automatic, then switch to Ruthless for the clean attempt.

Do not assume Ruthless grants better loot, more XP or an exclusive ending. The patch notes announce no such reward, and the community guide we checked explicitly warns against assuming one. If an achievement is tied to Ruthless, it is not documented in the official notes as of v1.1.01 — treat any claim otherwise as **[Unconfirmed]**.

## Which mode should you pick?

A practical order for most players:

1. **First run — Story or Normal.** Use the mode that keeps the story moving. Beast of Reincarnation's combat has a wide parry window on the forgiving end, but the campaign is long and the bosses are pattern-heavy.
2. **Second run — Hard.** This is where the launch balance was aimed at experienced action players. You already know the Nushi tells, so the step up is readable.
3. **Very Hard — after a completed build.** The mode is designed for players who found Hard too soft in the endgame. Bring a finished skill tree, upgraded weapons and a spirit-stone setup rather than starting from scratch.
4. **Ruthless — as a challenge, not a campaign.** Pick individual bosses or a short segment. A full Ruthless run is possible, but the mode's one-hit rule punishes every unfamiliar encounter, and the story sections between bosses add little to the challenge.

For New Game+, start at **Normal** if you want to enjoy the carry-over power fantasy, then raise the difficulty once you have re-acquired the abilities that matter. The default Reincarnation+ difficulty is Normal, so the game will not force the hardest setting on you.

## Common questions

**Can I change difficulty in the middle of a boss fight?** The patch notes say difficulty can be changed at any time during gameplay. Whether the menu is available during an active boss encounter is **[Unconfirmed]**; the safe assumption is that you can change it outside combat.

**Does difficulty affect achievements?** The v1.1.0 and v1.1.01 notes do not state that any achievement is difficulty-locked. The only documented achievement restriction in this cycle is the zone-skip warning in v1.1.01. Do not rely on an unverified claim that Ruthless is required for completion.

**Is Reincarnation+ harder than Very Hard?** Reincarnation+ is a New Game+ mode with selectable difficulty. Its default is Normal, and you can choose Hard, Very Hard or Ruthless within it. The label alone no longer tells you the difficulty; the selected option does.

**Did the patch make bosses harder across the board?** No. The notes say some under-tuned bosses and enemies were adjusted, some Nushi attack patterns were overhauled, and specific Arts and ranged damage values changed. That is a set of targeted adjustments, not a universal increase.
