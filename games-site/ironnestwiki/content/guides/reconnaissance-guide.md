# Reconnaissance & Intelligence — Turning Intel into Firepower

IRON NEST is won on the map, not at the trigger — and the map is only as good as your intelligence. Every engagement starts with pieces of information from different sources: spotters, star shells, recon planes, and teleprinter channels. This guide covers each intelligence asset, how to combine them, and how the weather station complicates everything from Chapter 3 on.

## Intelligence Assets — Quick Reference

| Asset | What It Gives You | Best Use |
|-------|-------------------|----------|
| **Spotter reports** | Direction-only bearings with a margin of error | Triangulation — combine two or more spotters to fix a position |
| **S.T.A.R. star shell** | Illuminates a 12.74 km area, revealing hidden positions and accurate range/bearing | Night and fog missions; confirming suspected target clusters |
| **Recon plane** | Topography and map blanks filled in; lets you arc shells over ridges | Before a long-range engagement; fly along the target corridor, not broad unrelated terrain |
| **Teleprinters** | Written intel — orders, threat warnings, frontline developments | Every mission start; check *every* channel |
| **Second-spotter confirmation** | A confirmed second bearing to the same target | Before spending shells — dramatically improves accuracy |

## Spotter Reports — The Foundation

A single spotter report gives you a **direction, not a position**. The true target appears where **multiple observer lines intersect** — which is why map discipline matters. The workflow:

1. Place your known tokens first: the Iron Nest token, blue spotter tokens, green reference-point tokens.
2. Draw bearing lines from each spotter toward the reported direction.
3. Where the lines intersect is the estimated target location.
4. Mark it — yellow for spotting lines, red for the final firing solution.

Spotter data carries a margin of error. Treat intersections as strong estimates; confirm with a star shell or recon plane when the target matters.

## Star Shells — Your Own Eyes at Night

The S.T.A.R. illumination shell is the most important recon asset in the game. When it detonates it lights up a **12.74 km area**, revealing enemy positions and providing accurate distance and bearing data you can feed directly into the ballistic calculator. In night missions, star shells are effectively mandatory: they illuminate the grid square so spotters can give highly accurate updated vectors.

**Skill tip:** time the illumination shell so it's in the air while you're already loading the follow-up — the shell reveals, you solve, the kill shell lands while the area is still lit.

## Recon Planes — Topography and Coverage

Recon plane runs fill in map blanks and reveal terrain, letting you adjust elevation to arc shells over ridges. The common mistake is photographing broad unrelated terrain — instead, **aim the recon flight along the corridor you'll be shooting** (e.g., ~90° east along a gorge) so the imagery matches your actual firing lane.

## The Weather Station Module — Chapter 3 Onward

From Chapter 3, a **Weather Station Module** forces you to factor in environmental ballistics:

- **Crosswinds** push shells off course — the effect varies by shell type: heavy projectiles cut through wind but drop aggressively (needing higher elevation), while light illumination rounds get blown significantly off target if you ignore wind.
- **Barometric pressure** shifts the ballistic solution from what the calculator predicts.

This is where the **MOA (Minute of Angle) micro-adjustment** becomes essential. In mid-game missions, spotters also make errors and radio corrections like "Impact 100 meters short, 50 meters left" — instead of recalculating from scratch, apply rapid MOA dial adjustments to shift the pattern onto target. The arrow keys are your friend for precise corrections.

## Intelligence Checklist Per Mission

- [ ] Read every teleprinter channel (secondary channels carry threat warnings)
- [ ] Plot the Iron Nest token position first — don't bump it; everything measures from it
- [ ] Draw all spotter bearings before placing any firing solution
- [ ] Check the weather — wind direction and strength change shell selection
- [ ] Confirm with a second source before firing (spotter, star shell, or recon imagery)
- [ ] Update the map as impacts land — "Impact 50 m north, 20 m west" corrections are faster than new solutions
