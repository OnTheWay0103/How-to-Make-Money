# Story & Lore — The Civil War Explained

> Launch Version / Updated: August 11, 2026

IRON NEST is more than a ballistics sim — it drops you inside a civil war that never happened, in a decade where the machine you command should not exist. This guide covers the setting, the machine, the factions, the moral choices the campaign hands you, and the story of the game itself.

## The Setting: A Spain That Never Was

The game takes place in an **alternate-history Spain in the late 1920s, on the brink of civil war**. The dieselpunk twist: the industrial age has produced walking artillery — colossal self-propelled fortress guns — and the machine you operate is one of them. The civil war that grips the country is fought with trench warfare, convoys, fortified ridges and counter-battery duels, all coordinated by a central command that never leaves the desk.

Everything about the fiction is filtered through the operator's one-window view: you never see the war, only its paperwork — teleprinter orders, spotter reports, newspaper clippings and the red-and-blue skull tokens that mark what your shells did to the people the reports were about.

## The Machine: The Iron Nest

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Class | Heavy walking artillery fortress |
| Mass | 5,000 tons |
| Armament | Two 800 mm barrels, twin-gun configuration |
| Propulsion | Dieselpunk hydraulics and a diesel generator |
| Crew | One — you (see the [stations guide](/guides/crew-stations-guide)) |
| Amenities | Coffee machine, clipboard, and a fully operational warhorn |

The machine is the third character in every mission. Its hydraulic lines, generator temperature, barrel heat and recoil buffer all degrade under fire — the lore and the gameplay are the same system: you are not piloting a war machine, you are keeping one alive long enough to fire it (see [systems & failures](/guides/systems-failure-guide)).

## High Command and the Moral Choice System

The campaign is framed by **High Command** — the teleprinter voice that dictates objectives, ordnance and patience. Launch reviewers and community reports describe a genuinely unusual mechanic: **you can obey or defy High Command's orders**, and the choices branch the story.

- **Obey** — follow the dictated ordnance and targets; the campaign proceeds along High Command's plan.
- **Defy** — refuse an objective or use your own judgment on the target selection; expect consequences in the story and the mission structure.

The newspaper reports that arrive between missions track what you did — the story is handcrafted, even though individual objectives are procedurally generated. The final battle is fixed narratively: a **massive, multi-stage siege defense** against an entire armored division, reached however your choices played out.

## The Factions

The community has not yet fully mapped the war's factions — the game deliberately keeps the larger picture in the background. What is confirmed:

- **High Command** — your side's headquarters; issues orders, tracks your patience, and court-martials slow operators.
- **The enemy** — a full military apparatus of infantry, artillery batteries, Fire Direction Centers, armored convoys and fortified bunkers, plus the civil unrest of a country collapsing into war (see [enemy types](/guides/enemy-types-guide)).
- **Friendlies** — spotters, observers and frontline infantry whose positions the map marks in blue. They are the reason the "0 ally kills" medals exist.

## The Story of the Game

IRON NEST was built by a **two-person team: Nick Nieuwoudt and Dominik Latos**, and launched on **Steam on August 6, 2026**.

| Milestone | Detail |
|---|---|

| **The launch** | Over 150,000 copies sold within the first day, a peak of over 17,000 concurrent players on launch weekend, and an Overwhelmingly Positive review rating (per press coverage) |
| **The lineage** | The developers credit **PVKK: Planetenverteidigungskanonenkommandant** as a major inspiration — Iron Nest takes the manual-cannon concept into a ground-based, dieselpunk battlefield with a far larger map-plotting and counter-battery layer |
| **The content** | 15 regions, 100+ medals, 33 Steam achievements, 30 advertised ammo types/abilities, two leaderboard challenge modes, newspaper reports and a **customizable cat companion** living in the turret |

## Why the Lore Matters to Your Gameplay

The fiction is load-bearing: every mechanic is justified by it, and reading the fiction is a strategy.

- The **teleprinters** are the only channel to the war — check every channel, because the fiction of a disorganized army includes warnings buried in secondary channels.
- The **newspapers** foreshadow what comes: a report of a new enemy formation is a hint at next mission's target mix.
- **Moral choices** are, mechanically, choices about which objectives to take and which ordnance to use — defiance is a playstyle, not just a story toggle.
- The **cat** is non-negotiable. Pet it between salvos. It is the only crew member who never complains about the hydraulic pressure.
