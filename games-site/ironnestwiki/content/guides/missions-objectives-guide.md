# Missions & Objectives Guide — Iron Nest Story Mode

IRON NEST: Heavy Turret Simulator's campaign isn't a fixed list of named levels — it's a series of **campaigns ordered by High Command with procedural elements**, so no two playthroughs are identical. What is consistent is the structure: three difficulty phases, a handful of objective types that repeat with escalating complexity, and a definitive final battle. This guide maps the whole campaign so you know what's coming and what to bring.

## Campaign Phases at a Glance

| Phase | Missions | What's Introduced | Failure Condition |
|-------|----------|-------------------|-------------------|
| **Phase 1 — Training Wheels** | Missions 1–5 | Clear weather, perfectly accurate bearings, stationary targets | Almost none — pure muscle memory |
| **Phase 2 — Deviations** | Missions 6–15 | Spotter errors, radio corrections ("Impact 100 m short, 50 m left"), rapid MOA dial adjustments | Missed targets accumulate |
| **Phase 3 — Multi-Target Salvos** | Missions 16+ | Three-target vectors, mixed shell loads, speed before enemy triangulation | Enemy counter-battery triangulates your position |
| **Final Battle** | Campaign end | Massive multi-stage siege defense against a full armored division | Any stage failed |

The phase names and mission counts are based on the community walkthrough record — the game itself does not number missions on screen. What matters is the skill ramp, which is consistent.

## The Core Mission Flow

Every mission follows the same sequence:

1. **Read the teleprinters.** High Command sends the day's orders plus spotter reports and frontline intel — check *every* channel; secondary channels carry threat warnings.
2. **Plot targets on the tactical map.** Mark known positions (your Iron Nest token, spotters, reference points) and triangulate.
3. **Calculate the firing solution.** Range into the ballistic calculator → elevation; bearing into the turret controls.
4. **Load and fire.** Correct shell, correct powder charge count, correct gun.
5. **Confirm the kill.** Second-spotter confirmation (or a star shell) before firing again saves ammunition and time.

## Objective Types — The Four Confirmed Classes

Community-verified targets are classified by armor rating, and each class has an optimal shell:

| Objective Class | Target | Optimal Shell | Notes |
|-----------------|--------|---------------|-------|
| **Class 1** | Light Infantry Trench | Airburst / shrapnel | Softest target; wide blast radius preferred |
| **Class 4** | Enemy Artillery Battery | Counter-battery HE | **Zero tolerance** — if you don't silence it fast, it silences you |
| **Class 3** | Armored Convoy | Armor-piercing (AP) | Moving targets — lead your bearing and confirm with a second spotter |
| **Class 5** | Reinforced Bunker | Bunker buster / tandem | Heaviest armor; expect multiple hits and follow-up ranging shots |

Target class isn't always announced — reading the spotter report's language ("trench system", "convoy moving east", "hardened position") tells you which shell family to stage on the loading platform.

## Special Mission Types

Beyond the standard objectives, the campaign throws in four distinctive mission formats:

### 1. Recon Mission
The campaign's training for intelligence warfare: **fire star shells to illuminate grid squares**, then use the revealed positions to build firing solutions. Timing the illumination shell with your loading sequence is the skill being tested — light up the area, plot fast, engage before the illumination fades.

### 2. FDC Counter-Battery Mission
A countdown timer runs while hostile artillery hunts your turret. The key mechanic: **destroying the enemy's Fire Direction Center (FDC) units pauses the countdown**, buying you breathing room. Locate FDCs by drawing bearings from known positions — where the lines intersect is the likely FDC location. Remember spotter data carries a margin of error, so treat intersections as estimates.

### 3. Emergency Reposition — Reverse Triangulation
The mission where you must figure out **where your own turret is**. You know the exact bearing, distance, and charges used for a calibration star shell, so you work backward from the detonation point to draw a line to your position. Failure here almost always comes from rushing the earlier coordinate entries — double-check every spotter coordinate before placing map markers.

### 4. The Gorge
A community-named mission fought in a narrow river gorge. The walkthrough trick: you can complete it **without the ballistic calculator** by using a well-aimed ranging shell first and correcting from impact — a valuable fallback when a system is damaged or time is short.

## The Final Siege Defense

The campaign ends with a **massive, multi-stage siege defense**: you must hold off an entire armored division single-handedly. Expect waves of Class 3 and Class 5 targets coming from multiple bearings with little time between salvos. Preparation tips:

- **Stock mixed shells.** You will need AP for convoys and bunker busters for the hard targets in the same engagement.
- **Protect your systems.** A hydraulic rupture or barrel overheat mid-siege is fatal — see the [Systems & Failure Guide](/guides/systems-failure-guide) for safe operating zones.
- **Counter-battery first.** Enemy artillery batteries (Class 4) should be silenced the moment they appear — you cannot survive sustained counter-battery fire while fighting the siege.

## High Command's Patience Meter

High Command tracks your efficiency: miss targets or take too long on vectors and their patience drops — enough failures can end in **court-martial**. Practical rules that keep patience stable:

- **Don't fire without a solution.** A confirmed second-spotter intersection beats a guess every time.
- **Use the right shell class.** Hitting a bunker with HE wastes shots and time.
- **Speed comes from map discipline.** Keep the map clean — yellow for spotting lines, red for firing solutions — so you never re-derive a vector you already solved.
