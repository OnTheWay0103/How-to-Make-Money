# Advanced Guide — Late-Game Survival & Base Defense

Late-game Ardent Wilds is defined by one pressure: the **Eldran**. The hivemind's corruption spreads across the land, enemies grow more dangerous, and your base becomes a target. Surviving the late game requires defensive design, prepared resource stockpiles, and a team that knows its role.

**A note on honesty:** Ardent Wilds is pre-release (demo: July 30, 2026; full game TBA). This guide covers confirmed systems and sound strategy. Specific wave schedules, enemy rosters, and numbers from any source are unconfirmed and should be treated as provisional — the game is changing rapidly with each playtest build.

## The Eldran Threat Escalates

Spellware Studios has confirmed that the Eldran — an "encroaching eldritch hivemind" — are the core threat, and that the game features **formidable bosses and difficult PvE challenges**. What is not confirmed:

- Exact raid/wave schedules (some playtest reports describe periodic base assaults, but the cadence is unconfirmed)
- The precise enemy roster and named "elite" types
- Whether assaults scale with Spires restored, time played, or boss progress

**What you should do anyway:**

1. **Assume attacks will come.** Build defense into your base from day one rather than retrofitting after a loss.
2. **Stockpile before exploring.** Keep repair materials, food, and potions on hand at all times — a well-supplied base survives whatever the current build throws at it.
3. **Watch the patrol patterns.** Enemies commonly approach from the direction of the nearest corrupted zone. Fortify that side first — but keep every side minimally defensible in case the AI changes.

## Base Defense Layouts That Work

Good defense is about geometry more than raw materials. These layout archetypes are general survival-game wisdom that applies well to Ardent Wilds' voxel world:

| Layout | Strength | Weakness | Best For |
|--------|----------|----------|----------|
| **Ring Fort** | All-round defense, no weak angles | Expensive in materials | Solo bases |
| **Choke Point** | Cheap, funnels enemies into one kill zone | Vulnerable flanks | Co-op bases |
| **Elevated Core** | Ground enemies struggle to reach it | Ranged and flying threats | Late-game bases |
| **Carved Base** | Terrain itself is the wall (voxel digging) | Hard to expand, dark inside | Endgame |

**Wall priorities:**

- **Upgrade the approach side first.** Whatever direction threats usually come from gets the best walls.
- **Don't leave gaps.** In a voxel world, a hole is an open door — seal mining tunnels that lead into your base.
- **Layer defenses.** A wall that slows enemies down is still useful even if it breaks; build a second line.

**Anti-breach tip:** if you mine tunnels for storage or farming underground, add a door or a sealed section between the tunnel and your main base. Eldran-corrupted enemies following a tunnel into your base is the classic late-game death.

## Ancient Spire Strategy

The **world-built skill tree** is the heart of late-game progression:

- Restoring **ancient Spires and forgotten facilities** unlocks powers — every node grants a **passive bonus** and serves as a **fast-travel location** (travel via Aether Rays)
- In the full game the goal is to **rebuild four ancient magical spires** to push back the Eldran
- Forgotten facilities are tied to rare materials and often sit in dangerous territory

**Restoration strategy:**

- **Restore the travel nodes first.** Fast travel pays off every single session — a second fast-travel point near your base or a resource region saves hours.
- **Do not hoard rare materials.** Materials that could restore a facility are worth more as progression than sitting in a chest.
- **Plan restoration around boss readiness.** Some facilities are deep in high-difficulty territory; time your trips between boss attempts.
- **In co-op, split the map.** Divide restoration targets between players, then share the unlocked fast-travel points.

## Advanced Crafting

Confirmed: you craft **armor and weapons at specialized crafting stations**, and you **farm, cook, and brew elixirs** to support combat. Also confirmed: **some of the best gear is looted from defeated enemies** — do not neglect boss and elite loot.

**Advanced crafting principles:**

1. **Build parallel stations.** Smelting and cooking are long-pole tasks; running multiple stations shortens downtime dramatically.
2. **Cook and brew in bulk.** Meals and elixirs are combat multipliers. Consumables you made before a fight are better than the ones you wish you had made during it.
3. **Loot beats craft for top tier.** If a challenging enemy or boss is farmable, the gear it drops is often the fastest path to power — check loot before investing in expensive crafting chains.
4. **Stock consumables between expeditions.** Every late-game expedition should start with a full inventory of food, potions, and repair materials.

## Co-op Role Strategy

With up to 6 players (confirmed; the game is not balanced beyond 6), specialization wins. These roles are conceptual — the underlying mechanics (taunts, dedicated support skills) are not yet confirmed, so adapt to whatever tools the current build gives you:

| Role | Duties |
|------|--------|
| **Defender** | Walls, perimeter, aggro control, repairs |
| **Hunter** | Elite and boss damage, kiting dangerous enemies |
| **Gatherer** | Resource runs between expeditions |
| **Quartermaster** | Farming, cooking, brewing, crafting, storage |
| **Scout** | Exploration, Spire hunting, map marking |

**The one role rule:** in a team of 3+, one player dedicated to base logistics (farming, cooking, brewing, crafting) roughly doubles the team's effective progression speed. This is the most consistent finding across playtest reports.
