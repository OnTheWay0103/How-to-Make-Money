# Farming & Cooking Guide — Grow, Cook & Brew Your Way to Survival

Every survival game has a hidden boss that kills more players than any monster: **an empty stomach**. Ardent Wilds is no exception. The developers have confirmed three food-chain systems as core pillars of the game — **growing crops, cooking delicious meals, and brewing powerful elixirs** — and all three exist to keep you alive and fighting.

This guide covers how the food chain works, how to set up your first farm, and how to turn crops into the meals and elixirs that carry you through the game. It also covers what we do and do not know about the current build.

**Honesty note:** Ardent Wilds is pre-release (demo July 30, 2026; full game TBA). The systems here are confirmed to exist, but exact crop names, growth times, recipe ingredients, and buff numbers have not been officially published. This guide gives you the confirmed framework and honest strategy — not invented numbers.

## Why Food and Elixirs Matter

The official description confirms the food chain's role: cooking produces "delicious meals" and brewing produces "powerful elixirs" — and both **aid in battle**. What that means in practice:

1. **Food sustains you.** Survival games run on hunger, and cooking turns raw ingredients into food that is safer and better for you
2. **Meals provide buffs.** "Cooking delicious meals" as a combat-support system means prepared food does more than fill the bar — it makes you stronger, faster, or tougher for a window of time (exact effects unconfirmed)
3. **Elixirs are combat consumables.** "Brewing powerful elixirs" that aid in battle means healing and buffing are something you prepare, not something you find
4. **Farming feeds both.** Crops are the raw material for the whole chain — food, cooking ingredients, and likely brewing reagents

In a game where combat is fast and skill-driven, the player who walks into a fight full, buffed, and carrying elixirs is playing a different game than the player who walks in hungry.

## Setting Up Your First Farm

Farming is confirmed, and the fundamentals are the same across survival games. Here is how to start:

### Choose the Spot

- **Near water.** Crops need water in almost every survival game. A riverbank, lake edge, or pond-adjacent flat area is ideal
- **Near your base.** You will visit the farm constantly. Keep it inside your base's perimeter or a short walk from your crafting hub
- **Flat and sunny.** The voxel world can be flattened with your pickaxe, so "flat" is always achievable — but open ground beats the shadow of a cliff or forest canopy
- **Fenced or enclosed.** Wildlife — including the Eldran-corrupted variety — is the enemy of crops in this genre. Whether corrupted creatures specifically eat crops in Ardent Wilds is unconfirmed, but a fence is cheap insurance

### Prepare the Ground

1. **Clear the plot.** Remove rocks, stumps, and debris with your tools
2. **Flatten the terrain** with your pickaxe — the voxel system makes this easy
3. **Plan rows.** Rows by crop type make planting, watering, and harvesting fast
4. **Start small.** A modest first plot that you can actually maintain beats a huge field you cannot

### Get Seeds

In most survival games, seeds come from harvesting wild plants — forage the countryside around your base, and whatever you gather is usually plantable. Whether Ardent Wilds works exactly this way is **unconfirmed**, but the pattern is universal enough that it is the first thing to try: harvest wild vegetation and look for seed drops, or check your crafting menu for seed recipes.

## Growing Crops — What to Expect

Expect a mix of crop types with different purposes. The exact roster is unconfirmed, but the genre-standard pattern is:

| Crop Type | Expected Use |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Staple crops** | Bulk food — the base of every meal |
| **Vegetables** | Better meals, higher satiety |
| **Herbs and flowers** | Brewing reagents — the elixir chain |
| **Fruits and berries** | Eat raw, cook, or juice |

### Growth Essentials

- **Water is everything.** A farm within reach of water, or a watering routine you actually keep, is the difference between a farm and a decoration
- **Rhythm beats quantity.** Harvest on a schedule — a small plot harvested reliably out-produces a big plot harvested once a month
- **Replant immediately.** The golden rule: for every crop you harvest, plant one. Food security is a habit, not a project
- **Check for growth requirements.** Some crops need specific conditions (light, moisture, season). If a crop stalls, the game usually hints why

### Growth Times — Unconfirmed

Specific growth timers have not been published. Do not trust numbers from any guide — including this one. The honest version: expect staple crops to be fast, advanced crops to be slow, and plan your food reserves around the slow ones.

## Cooking Meals

Cooking is confirmed as a system — "cooking delicious meals" — and it is your second food chain. The pattern:

### Raw vs Cooked

- **Raw food sustains** — in a pinch, eating what you gather works
- **Cooked food is better** — cooked meals restore more, and the confirmed combat-support design means meals come with buffs
- **Cooked food lasts longer** — cooked meals spoil slower (spoilage mechanics unconfirmed, but batch cooking is genre standard)

### Cooking Station

Meals are made at a cooking station (exact name/build cost unconfirmed). Expect a simple start — cook raw ingredients into basic meals — and a recipe tree that opens up as you gather rarer ingredients and restore progression nodes.

### Buff Meals

The confirmed design — meals "aid in battle" — means the best cooking recipes are combat preparation:

- **Damage buffs** — eat before boss fights
- **Survival buffs** — max health, damage resistance, stamina
- **Utility buffs** — movement speed, gathering speed

**The habit:** never start a major expedition or boss attempt without a fresh meal buff active. In a game where the developers emphasize preparation, the player who eats wins.

## Brewing Elixirs

"Brewing powerful elixirs" is the third confirmed food-chain system, and it is your combat consumable pipeline.

### What Elixirs Are For

The official description ties elixirs to combat directly. Genre-standard uses, likely to appear:

| Elixir Type | Expected Use |
|-------------|-------------|
| **Healing elixirs** | Restore health mid-fight — the most important consumable in the game |
| **Stamina elixirs** | Recover the resource behind dodging and sprinting |
| **Elemental protection** | Resist hazards — fire, cold, corruption |
| **Offensive tonics** | Temporary damage or effect boosts |

Exact names, ingredients, and effects are **unconfirmed** — the developer's word is "powerful elixirs," and that is all we know for certain.

### Brewing Tips

1. **Brew before you need it.** An elixir brewed at base is worth ten elixirs wished-for mid-fight
2. **Stock a potion shelf.** A dedicated chest of elixirs, always topped up, turns every boss attempt from a gamble into a plan
3. **Save ingredients.** Brewing reagents are usually the herbs and rare finds that beginners throw away. Hoard them
4. **Brew in batches.** Like cooking, brewing is a long-pole task — queue batches and pick them up later

## Building the Food Loop

The three systems combine into one loop:

**Farm → harvest → cook meals → eat (buff) + brew elixirs → fight/explore → gather more seeds and ingredients → farm again**

Make the loop explicit in your routine:

1. **Morning routine:** harvest the farm, replant, check ingredient stocks
2. **Before any expedition:** cook a batch of meals, brew a batch of elixirs, fill your inventory
3. **Return routine:** process everything gathered — seeds go to the farm, ingredients to the stations, loot to storage
4. **Weekly (in-game) stock check:** if your potion shelf is below two expeditions' worth, refill before anything else

This routine turns food from a crisis into background infrastructure. The farms and stations stop being chores and start being the reason you can take risks.

## Co-op Food Division

With co-op up to 6 players (confirmed), the food chain is the perfect specialization:

- **One Quartermaster** — the player who owns farming, cooking, and brewing feeds the whole team. This single role is the highest-leverage job in the game
- **Everyone forages** — gathering wild plants, seeds, and herbs is a zero-cost habit for explorers
- **Stock the shared kitchen** — all food and elixirs go into the team's storage, everyone draws from it
- **Call supply status** — "out of healing elixirs, brewing now — wait before the boss" is the most important sentence in co-op

Teams that feed themselves properly out-survive teams that don't by a wide margin — no numbers required to know why.

## Common Food Mistakes

1. **Eating raw when you could cook** — cooked food is strictly better; a cooking station pays for itself in a day
2. **Ignoring buffs before bosses** — the game's own description says meals and elixirs aid in battle; skipping them is fighting nerfed
3. **Selling or discarding ingredients** — everything in the food chain is an ingredient for something. Keep it all
4. **Over-farming early** — a huge field you cannot maintain is a time sink. Start small, scale with your routine
5. **No potion shelf** — if healing elixirs only exist in your inventory during emergencies, you are one bad fight from a long walk back
6. **Trusting recipe lists from the internet** — in a pre-release game, published recipe tables are almost certainly fabricated. Learn recipes in-game
