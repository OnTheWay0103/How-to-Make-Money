# Multiplayer & Co-op Guide — Play With Up to 6 Players

Ardent Wilds supports **online co-op for up to 6 players, plus LAN play with no internet required** — both confirmed by the developers. This guide covers the confirmed multiplayer facts, how to organize your group, and team strategies that will keep your whole party alive against the Eldran.

**Honesty note:** the game is pre-release (demo July 30, 2026; full game TBA). Menu labels, server settings, and matchmaking specifics differ between builds and are not officially documented. This guide covers confirmed capabilities and durable strategy; it deliberately avoids pretending to know exact menu paths or a "known bugs" list.

## Confirmed Multiplayer Facts

1. **Online co-op: up to 6 players** via Steam
2. **LAN co-op: no internet required** — play over a local network
3. **The player cap can be increased beyond 6**, but the game is not balanced for larger groups — the developers said so themselves
4. **An experimental dedicated server executable** was added in the April 2026 playtest — server hosting outside a host's game session is being worked on
5. **The full game will include single-player** — co-op is optional, not mandatory

## Setting Up Multiplayer

The exact setup flow depends on the build, but the shape is standard for Steam co-op games:

### Online Co-op (Steam)

1. **Host:** start a world and make it joinable from the session menu (typical labels: "Open to Friends" / "Invite")
2. **Friends:** join through the Steam friends list or an invite
3. The **host's world is the world** — host progress persists, everyone else plays in it

### LAN / Direct Connection

1. **Host:** enable LAN mode (wherever network settings live in the current build)
2. **Players:** join from the multiplayer menu using the host's local IP
3. All players must be on the same local network — no internet required, which makes this the choice for offline play sessions

### Notes on Hosting

- **Host stability matters.** The host's connection and machine carry the session. If lag is a problem, look at the host first
- **Dedicated servers are experimental.** The included server executable exists (playtest, April 2026), but expect rough edges until it matures
- **Whoever hosts controls the world.** If you want a persistent shared world, agree on who hosts before starting

## Shared Base Strategies

### The Communal Base Model

All players contribute to one large base. Pros: efficient resource use, easier to defend, everything in one place. Cons: crowded, resource competition, and one attack threatens everyone.

### The Hub-and-Spoke Model (Recommended for 4+)

One central "Hub Base" with shared workstations, storage, and farm. Each player (or pair) also keeps a small outpost near a distant region for specialized gathering. **Fast travel between restored Spires** (the world-built skill tree's confirmed feature) is what makes this viable — spokes connect to the hub via fast travel.

**Hub Base contains:** all workstations, bulk storage, main farm, best defenses
**Outpost contains:** bed, chests, campfire, regional gathering tools

### Resource Management in Co-op

The build determines whether resources are shared or individual. Regardless of the setting, the same principles apply:

- **Set expectations early.** Agree on who farms what, who crafts what, and who owns the rare materials — before someone hoards them
- **Rare materials flow to need.** Assign crystals, ores, and artifact drops by who needs them most, not who found them
- **One person manages bulk logistics.** The Quartermaster organizes storage, tracks stock, and announces shortages

## Combat Synergies in Co-op

Teamwork multiplies the confirmed combat design:

- **Freeze + fire:** one player immobilizes (ice/utility artifacts), another unloads damage. The **Tome of Ice**'s frost bridges and the fire wizard's pyroblasts are a natural pairing
- **Aggro rotation:** when the boss targets a player, that player plays safe while everyone else deals damage. The target swaps every few seconds — the fight practically plays itself with good rotation
- **Protect the support:** the buff/heal player stays out of the action; everyone else keeps pressure off them
- **Terrain teamwork:** one player reshapes the battlefield (digging, blasting) while others fight. A funnel dug mid-fight is worth a thousand DPS

### Boss Fight Coordination

Bosses have **unique patterns and multiple phases** (confirmed design). Team rhythm:

1. **First phase:** everyone learns the pattern; the tank figures out aggro timing
2. **Phase transitions:** when the boss changes behavior, call it out and pull back. Re-assess before re-engaging
3. **Death protocol:** if a teammate dies, the group's job is to **survive** while they return, not to greed the boss — a wiped team gains nothing (respawn specifics unconfirmed; play it safe)

## Communication Tips

Voice chat availability depends on platform (Steam voice or Discord are the common choices; built-in voice is unconfirmed). Communicate:

- **Discoveries** — Spires, caves, resource veins: "marking a fast-travel point" is the single most valuable call in the game
- **Resource calls** — "rich ore vein here, bring picks"
- **Threat warnings** — "big Eldran group coming from the east, rally to base"
- **Loot calls** — "got a spare artifact, who wants it"
- **Supply status** — "out of potions, cooking now — wait before the boss"

## Troubleshooting Common Co-op Issues

General advice — these are patterns, not a confirmed bug list:

| Issue | Try |
|-------|-----|
| **Can't join friend's game** | Restart Steam on both ends; re-invite; confirm the world is set to joinable |
| **Lag / desync** | Check the host's connection; reduce player count; move away from heavily built-up areas |
| **Missing items or progress** | Reload the session; report on the Steam community hub if it recurs — pre-release builds have real bugs, and the developers benefit from reports |
| **Gear gap between players** | The Quartermaster should prioritize bringing the weakest player up — a team is as strong as its weakest member |
