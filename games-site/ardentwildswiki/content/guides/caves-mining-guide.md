# Caves & Underground Mining Guide — Ores, Mines & Secrets

In Ardent Wilds, everything worth having is underground. The official description puts it bluntly: you will "venture deep into ancient caves and underground mines, where danger lurks around every corner." That is the game's own promise — **dark, labyrinthine caverns** packed with **deadly traps, fierce enemies, and hidden secrets**, all guarding the three things your progression runs on: **valuable loot, rare materials, and powerful artifacts**.

This guide covers what is confirmed about the underground layer, how mining works, how to survive an expedition, and what the latest playtest patches added below the surface.

**Honesty note:** Ardent Wilds is pre-release (demo July 30, 2026; full game TBA). The underground systems below are confirmed to exist, but exact ore names, tool tiers, recipe costs, and trap types have not been officially published. Where we do not know something, we say so instead of inventing a table.

## Why the Underground Matters

The developers have confirmed the reward structure outright: the caves and mines hold **valuable loot, rare materials, and powerful artifacts** — and the corruption has a pattern to it:

1. **Rare materials are found, not grown.** The surface gives you the basics (wood, stone, fibers). The rare stuff — the materials behind upgraded tools, advanced crafting, and better gear — lives underground
2. **Artifacts hide in dangerous places.** Powerful rare artifacts are found as loot in exactly the kind of locations nobody wants to explore: caves, mines, and guarded spots (see the [Artifacts & Loot Guide](/guides/artifacts-loot-guide))
3. **Corruption concentrates below.** Playtest reports and the game's own framing describe the deepest caves and mines carrying the worst of the Eldran corruption — which is precisely where the best rewards are (the [Enemies Guide](/guides/enemies-guide) covers what lives down there)
4. **Mining is a core gameplay loop.** The April 2026 playtest introduced a fully reworked digging and mining system — the developers treat mining as a system worth iterating on, not a background chore

If the surface is where you survive, the underground is where you progress.

## What Is Confirmed About Caves and Mines

These facts come from the official Steam description and Spellware Studios' own patch notes:

- **"Dark, labyrinthine caverns"** — the developer's own description. Bring light; going in blind is a death sentence
- **Deadly traps** — confirmed environmental threat. Traps damage you independent of enemies
- **Fierce enemies** — underground enemies are tougher and more varied than surface wildlife (see the [Enemies Guide](/guides/enemies-guide))
- **Hidden secrets** — the developers explicitly promise secrets beyond loot and materials. Watch for odd walls, out-of-place terrain, and unexplainable structures
- **New underground mine points of interest** were added in the April 2026 playtest build — mine POIs are an actively expanding content category
- **Mines and minecarts were improved** in the v0.5.9 patch (June 10, 2026) — minecart transport exists in the game and the developers are actively developing it
- **Mining and digging were reworked** in the same patch — expect a deliberate, tool-driven mining loop rather than simple block-breaking

Note what is *not* confirmed: the exact ore roster, tool tiers, and minecart mechanics. Any guide naming specific ores with "mining level required" tables is inventing them.

## The Mining Loop — How Digging Works

The confirmed facts: the world is **fully destructible voxels**, and the v0.5.9 patch reworked digging and mining. That means:

- **You dig with tools.** A pickaxe is your primary mining tool (recipe and tiers unconfirmed — the [Crafting Guide](/guides/crafting-guide) has the confirmed crafting framework)
- **Better tools mine faster and better materials.** This is confirmed design, though the exact tier list is unconfirmed. The usual pattern — wood → stone → metal → rare ore — matches the game's confirmed resource progression, but do not treat it as gospel
- **Terrain is yours to reshape.** The voxel system means you can tunnel, carve rooms, and mine vertically. There is no "correct" way to enter a cave — you can dig your own door
- **Spells are mining tools.** Confirmed: bombs and pyroblasts leave craters in terrain. In dense rock, a well-placed blast is faster than pickaxe swings — at the cost of being loud, destructive, and likely to collapse exactly what you wanted to keep. Use blasts for bulk removal and pickaxes for precision
- **Laser Logging exists as an unlockable.** A patch note fix references the "Laser Logging" unlock — an upgraded, technology-flavored gathering tool is confirmed to exist in the current builds, though how it is unlocked is unconfirmed

### Digging Tips That Work

1. **Dig defensively.** When you break into a new cavern, do not jump in — break a small hole, listen, and look before committing. Enemies and traps do not care about your curiosity
2. **Create shortcuts and escape routes.** A backdoor tunnel out of a cave can save a run that goes wrong. Terrain manipulation is your best panic button
3. **Watch what you mine.** In voxel games, removing support terrain can cause collapses or simply open paths you did not intend. Mine deliberately
4. **Mark your route.** Cave layouts are labyrinthine by design. Leave landmarks — light sources where you can place them, dug markers, or memorable terrain features — because losing your way in the dark is a classic way to lose a character. (Placeable lighting specifics are unconfirmed, but the light source itself is confirmed necessary)
5. **Do not mine straight down.** Genre wisdom that applies in any voxel world — you may drop into a pit, a trap, or an elite enemy's lap

## Planning an Expedition

Underground runs fail from lack of preparation, not lack of skill. Your checklist:

- **Light** — torches or a light source. "Dark, labyrinthine" is the developer's description, not a suggestion
- **Food and elixirs** — hunger does not pause underground, and combat buffs are combat support (see the [Farming & Cooking Guide](/guides/farming-cooking-guide))
- **A good pickaxe, and a spare** — tools break at the worst moments. A backup is cheap insurance
- **Repair or replacement materials** — if tools degrade, you need the means to fix or replace them
- **An escape plan** — know your route back, or dig one. Co-op groups should designate the route-finder
- **A combat set you trust** — you will fight. Underground enemies are the fierce kind

### Trap Survival

The environment itself is a confirmed threat. Practical trap discipline:

1. **Move slowly in unfamiliar sections** — fast movement and floor plates do not mix
2. **Watch the ground** — pressure plates, tripwires, and suspicious gaps are genre-standard trap signifiers; specifics are unconfirmed but caution is free
3. **Listen** — audio cues are your early warning system; the v0.5.9 patch added area-based audio reverb, so sound now tells you about the space around you
4. **Send one player ahead** — in co-op, one scout triggers traps and takes hits so the group does not
5. **Use terrain as armor** — dig a protective wall, blast a pit between you and a trap, or kite enemies into the traps yourself (they do not respect the environment either)

## The Aetherium Conflux — What We Know

The **Aetherium Conflux** is one of the most interesting confirmed underground systems, and it has grown through patch notes:

- A new **Aetherium Conflux** was added in a recent playtest patch that **unlocks farming** — the Conflux is tied to the farming system, though the exact mechanism is unconfirmed
- The v0.5.9 patch notes describe a **wave-based Aetherium Conflux** — it is a combat event that spawns enemies in waves, and a desync fix in that patch specifically mentioned creatures spawned by it

Put together: the Aetherium Conflux appears to be a **wave-defense style event location** that ties into your farming progression. Expect to fight for what it offers. How you find it (exploration? story?), how many waves it has, and what it drops are all unconfirmed — treat any detailed Conflux guide you see elsewhere as speculation.

The name itself is a hint about the setting: **Aetherium** is the game's magic-defense substance — the **Aetherium Nexus** is the planetary defense core you awaken near (see the [Story & Quests Guide](/guides/story-quests-guide)). The Conflux is part of that Aetherium infrastructure.

## Minecarts and Transport

The v0.5.9 patch notes confirm **improved mines and minecarts**. What that means concretely:

- **Minecarts exist** in the current builds — this is confirmed
- They were improved in June 2026, so they are an active development area
- How you get them (crafted? found?), where they run (player-built rails? fixed mine tracks?), and whether they carry materials or players are **unconfirmed**

**Community guidance:** treat minecarts as a quality-of-life system to look forward to rather than plan around. If a mine has rails, they were probably put there for a reason — follow them to content.

## Mining Strategy for Progression

Order your underground priorities by what you need:

1. **Early game: ores for tools** — your first metal-tier tools and better crafting materials come from shallow caves. Stay near the entrance, mine what you need, retreat
2. **Mid game: rare materials for gear** — once you can fight, push deeper for the materials behind advanced armor and weapons (see the [Armor Guide](/guides/armor-guide) and [Weapons Guide](/guides/weapons-guide))
3. **Always: artifacts** — any expedition that comes home with a new artifact is a good expedition, and caves are a confirmed artifact source
4. **Late game: the Conflux and secrets** — wave events and hidden secrets are where the best sustained rewards live

## Co-op Underground

A cave run is where a 6-player group earns its keep (see the [Co-op Guide](/guides/coop-guide)):

- **Divide roles: scout, fighter, miner, carrier.** One player lights the way and reads traps; one or two hold the line; the miner gathers; the carrier protects the haul
- **Guard the exit.** A player watching the escape route turns a retreat into a fighting withdrawal
- **Share the loot drops.** Artifacts one player cannot use may define another player's build
- **Never split up in unknown caves.** Strength in numbers is the entire point of co-op
