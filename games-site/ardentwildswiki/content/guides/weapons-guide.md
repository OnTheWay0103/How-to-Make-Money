# Weapons Guide — Every Weapon Type in Ardent Wilds and How to Get Them

Weapons in Ardent Wilds are your primary tool for everything dangerous: clearing Eldran corruption, holding your base against raids, and eventually challenging Yorgoroth, the Devourer of Worlds. The game is **classless** — you are not locked into a weapon type at character creation, and the design actively rewards carrying several weapons and swapping them based on the situation.

**Honesty first:** Ardent Wilds is in Early Access development, and Spellware Studios has not published a weapon catalog. No official weapon list, stat tables, or damage numbers exist yet — and any "full weapon list" you find online is invented or from an unconfirmed playtest build. This guide covers what is confirmed (the weapon families, how weapons are obtained, how progression works) and gives you strategy that survives balance patches.

## The Three Confirmed Weapon Families

The developers describe three broad combat directions, which map directly to weapon families:

| Family | Weapon Types | Playstyle |
|--------|-------------|-----------|
| **Warrior** | Melee weapons (swords, axes, hammers) | Up close, aggressive, dodge-timed |
| **Marksman** | Ranged weapons (bows, crossbows, firearms) | Distance, precision, kiting |
| **Wizard** | Magic weapons (staffs, wands, tomes) | Spells, area damage, terrain destruction |

Combat is described as **fast-paced and arcade-style** — you will be dodging, timing attacks, and chaining abilities rather than tanking hits. Every family fits this style, but each handles it differently:

- **Melee** is about positioning and timing. You are in the danger zone, so your dodges and pattern knowledge matter most. In co-op, melee users are the natural frontline while the boss is occupied.
- **Ranged** is about distance control and kiting. You trade some burst for safety and can keep dealing damage while repositioning. Ranged weapons shine against slow melee enemies and Eldran swarms.
- **Magic** is the utility-plus-damage family. Spells have **unlimited combinations** (confirmed) and many of them **alter the terrain itself** — bombs and pyroblasts leave craters, and the **Tome of Ice** artifact creates frost bridges. A wizard reshapes the battlefield as they fight.

The exact weapon catalog — names, tiers, specific stats — is **unconfirmed**. Treat any stat table from an unverified source as fiction until the full release.

## Spells Are Weapons Too

Magic in Ardent Wilds is not a single weapon with one attack. The confirmed design is a **combinatorial spell system**: you combine spells and effects in ways that are "unlimited" per the official description. Practical implications:

- Your "wizard loadout" can be built around a theme — fire, ice, earth — and combined with artifacts for unique effects
- Terrain-destroying spells (bombs, pyroblasts) are both offensive tools and utility: destroying terrain can expose caves, create cover, or open shortcuts
- The **Tome of Ice** artifact (confirmed feature) creates frost bridges — a mobility tool and a crowd-control option in one

This means weapon choice and spell choice blur together for magic users: your staff may be a delivery system, but the spell loadout is the real weapon.

## How to Get Weapons

There are three confirmed ways to obtain weapons:

### 1. Crafting at Specialized Stations

Weapons and armor are crafted at **specialized crafting stations** using materials gathered on your journey. The loop is:

1. Gather raw materials — mining, chopping, foraging, and **looting enemies** (confirmed)
2. Process materials at your base
3. Craft weapons and armor at the appropriate station
4. Use crafted gear to push into harder zones for better materials

Your base's crafting stations are the backbone of your loadout. See the [Crafting Guide](crafting-guide.md) for the full pipeline.

### 2. Looting Enemies and Dungeons

Some of the best gear is **looted from defeated enemies** rather than crafted (confirmed). Underground caves and mines — the game's dungeon content — hold traps, enemies, hidden secrets, loot, and rare materials. Expect:

- Gear drops from elite and corrupted enemies
- **Rare materials** from dungeon exploration that unlock higher-tier crafting
- Hidden chests and secrets worth hunting in the underground

### 3. Legendary Loot from Bosses

Defeating **epic and legendary bosses** grants access to **legendary loot, rare items, and powerful weapons** that significantly enhance your abilities (confirmed design). If a fight is brutally hard, the reward at the end is the point — boss loot is the top of the gear pyramid. See the [Boss Guide](boss-guide.md) for fight preparation.

## The Gear Loop: Craft vs. Loot

The confirmed progression pattern is a two-track loop:

- **Craft** for reliability: predictable upgrades at every material tier
- **Loot** for spikes: enemy drops, dungeon finds, and boss legendaries can skip you ahead

In practice, strong players use both — craft to fill gaps and survive the next tier, loot to chase the outliers. The [Artifacts & Loot Guide](artifacts-loot-guide.md) covers this loop in depth.

## Artifacts: The Build-Defining Layer

Rare artifacts unlock **unique abilities** (confirmed) and are the highest-leverage items you own. Artifacts interact with weapons in two ways:

- They can define a whole build (for example, a frost-based loadout built around the Tome of Ice)
- They can be **swapped between fights** — bring the artifact that counters the boss you are about to face

Weapons give you damage; artifacts give you new ways to use it. Character builds in Ardent Wilds are really *weapon + artifact + spell* combinations — see the [Character Builds](character-builds.md) guide for build directions.

## Weapon Management Tips

These follow directly from confirmed design:

- **Carry a spare weapon.** Durability behavior is build-dependent, but a backup weapon is free insurance — the [Boss Guide](boss-guide.md) recommends it for every major fight
- **Swap by situation, not by preference.** The classless system and fast-paced combat reward adapting: melee for single tough targets, ranged for swarms, magic when you need terrain control
- **Upgrade before you need it.** If you can afford the next material tier, craft it before a boss attempt rather than after a failed one
- **Dungeons first, bosses later.** Caves and mines are the confirmed source of rare materials — a dungeon run is usually the fastest path to your next weapon tier

## Weapons in Co-op

With up to 6 players (confirmed), weapon families map naturally to team roles:

- **Frontline melee** — occupies the boss, holds aggro where possible
- **Ranged DPS** — consistent damage from safety during aggro rotation
- **Magic support** — terrain control, crowd control, and utility; a wizard's frost bridges can extract a pinned team

Coordination beats raw gear — see the [Co-op Guide](coop-guide.md) for role strategy.
