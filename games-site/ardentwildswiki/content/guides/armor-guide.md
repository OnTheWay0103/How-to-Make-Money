# Armor Guide — Every Armor Set & How to Get It

Armor in Ardent Wilds is your second line of defense — and in a game built on dodging, timing, and positioning, understanding where armor fits into the fight is half the battle. This guide covers what is confirmed about armor, how to get it, and how to think about defense in a combat system where skill beats stats.

**Honesty note:** Ardent Wilds is pre-release (demo July 30, 2026; full game TBA). The full armor catalog, stat values, slot counts, and set bonus mechanics are unconfirmed — any "complete armor stat table" you find online is invented. What is below is confirmed, clearly marked, and honestly framed.

## Confirmed Armor Facts

These come from the official Steam description, developer statements, and official patch notes:

1. **Armor is crafted at specialized crafting stations** — the official description confirms you "craft powerful armor and weapons at specialized crafting stations." Armor is a first-class crafting category, not an afterthought
2. **Some of the best armor is looted, not crafted** — Spellware has confirmed that powerful armor and weapons can be found on defeated enemies. Crafting is the baseline; looting is the upgrade path
3. **Armor sets exist** — two sets have been confirmed by name in playtest patch notes: the **Frayed Apprentice set** and the **Krell War set**. Both were added as new armor in a recent playtest patch
4. **Armor is part of the confirmed build layers** — the classless build system runs on weapons, artifacts, spells, Spire bonuses, and armor & gear (see the [Character Builds Guide](/guides/character-builds))
5. **Cosmetics are separate from armor** — character creation options (hair styles, beards, colors) are appearance, not gear. Fashion does not mix with function here

What is *not* confirmed: how many armor slots exist (head, chest, legs?), whether sets have bonus effects for wearing multiple pieces, stat ranges, and the armor recipe list. Treat those as open questions.

## The Confirmed Armor Sets

### Frayed Apprentice Set

Added in a recent playtest patch as a new armor set. From the name and context, it reads as starter-tier mage-style gear — apprentice robes for the spell-focused direction. What it actually does is **unconfirmed**; the name is the only confirmed fact.

**Practical take:** expect to see this set early in progression. If the game hands you an "Apprentice" set, it is telling you what tier of content you are at.

### Krell War Set

Also added in the same patch. The name is the only confirmed fact — what "Krell" refers to (a faction, a creature, a region) and what the set does are both **unconfirmed**. The "War" in the name points to combat-focused armor, but that is reading the name, not the stats.

**Practical take:** a "War" set almost certainly favors aggressive, front-line play — warrior and tank directions (see the [Combat Guide](/guides/combat-guide) for how those playstyles work).

**The honest bottom line:** both sets are confirmed to exist by name in the current builds. Everything else about them — stats, sources, set bonuses — is speculation until Spellware publishes details. Do not trust tier lists ranking them.

## How to Get Armor

There are two confirmed acquisition paths, plus one community-reported one:

1. **Craft it.** Specialized crafting stations produce armor. Which station, which materials, and which recipes: the crafting framework is confirmed, the specifics are not (see the [Crafting Guide](/guides/crafting-guide))
2. **Loot it.** Defeated enemies drop powerful armor — confirmed design. Elite enemies and bosses are the natural suspects for the best pieces, though drop tables are unconfirmed
3. **Boss drops (community reported).** The alpha playtest focuses on reaching and defeating the boss **Zyrrhak** (see the [Boss Guide](/guides/boss-guide)), and community playtest reports describe boss rewards including gear. This is consistently reported but **unverified** — treat it as likely, not certain

**Progression rule of thumb:** craft to fill gaps, loot to upgrade. If you are wearing something you crafted early, an enemy drop should replace it within a few hours of play. When drops stop improving your defense, it is time to find tougher enemies — or a crafting upgrade.

## Defense Philosophy — Armor in a Dodge-Based Game

Here is the confirmed context that shapes every armor decision: Ardent Wilds combat is **fast-paced, arcade-style, and skill-driven**. Dodging, timing, and positioning are the intended defense loop — the developers' own words. What that means for armor:

1. **Armor is your safety net, not your shield.** The best defense is not being hit. Armor exists for the hits you fail to dodge — and in this game, those mistakes are your main damage source
2. **Never trade mobility for armor without knowing what you lose.** Exact movement penalties are unconfirmed, but heavy armor in a dodge-based game is a real trade-off. If you cannot dodge, armor is a tax on your only reliable defense
3. **Dodge first, armor second.** The ideal build is *dodge everything, armor the mistakes*. Optimize for that order — practice the timing, and let armor cover the rest
4. **Armor does not win boss fights; preparation does.** Bosses have unique patterns and multiple phases (confirmed). Armor helps you survive pattern-learning attempts — bring the best you have — but the fight is won with knowledge (see the [Boss Guide](/guides/boss-guide))

### The Three Defense Layers

Think of defense as three layers that stack:

| Layer | What It Is | Status |
|-------|-----------|--------|
| **Dodge/positioning** | The primary defense — skill-driven, always available | Confirmed core combat pillar |
| **Armor** | Stats and survivability for the hits you take | Confirmed; numbers unconfirmed |
| **Consumables** | Food buffs and elixirs that aid in battle | Confirmed; see [Farming & Cooking Guide](/guides/farming-cooking-guide) |

Wearing armor without dodging is wasting layer one. Dodging without armor means one mistake ends the run. Stack all three.

## Armor and Co-op Roles

In a 6-player group, armor becomes a team resource question (see the [Co-op Guide](/guides/coop-guide)):

- **The front-liner.** Whoever holds enemy attention needs the best armor in the group. Tanking is a confirmed-adjacent role — community playtest reports describe players absorbing attention while teammates deal damage
- **The ranged damage dealer.** The marksman direction survives on distance, not armor — but a single bad pull ends a squishy build. Give your ranged players survivability options, not necessarily the heaviest sets
- **Rotation discipline.** If your armored front-liner falls, the rest of the group is suddenly the front line. Keep spare armor pieces and repair materials at base

**Co-op armor rule:** the player who gets hit most should wear the best set. That is rarely the player who wants it most.

## Armor Progression Plan

Concrete plan, honest about what we do not know:

1. **Early: craft your first set.** Starter armor from the workbench is better than no armor. Expect it to be replaced quickly
2. **Mid: loot up.** Run caves and mines (see the [Caves & Mining Guide](/guides/caves-mining-guide)) and fight elite enemies — the confirmed loot sources for powerful gear
3. **Track the patch notes.** The confirmed set additions (Frayed Apprentice, Krell War) show the armor catalog is actively growing. New sets will keep landing — watch official channels rather than tier lists
4. **Prepare for bosses.** Enter boss fights in your best set, with consumables topped up. Armor is a preparation item
5. **Keep multiple sets.** The classless system rewards swapping gear per situation. A fast set for exploring, a heavy set for boss attempts, a spare for when one breaks
