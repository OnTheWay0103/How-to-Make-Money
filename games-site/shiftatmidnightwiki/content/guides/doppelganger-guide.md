## The Art of Detection

In Shift At Midnight, a doppelganger can walk through your gas station doors at any moment. These creatures mimic human appearance — but they cannot perfectly mimic human behavior. Your job as a night-shift clerk is to find the cracks in their disguise before they complete their purchase. Let one escape and it returns later as a monster, and the store locks down for a 60-second alert.

This guide covers the identification system community guides and walkthroughs have documented: the question pool, the NET database cross-reference, the ID check routine, and the visual tells that give fakes away.

---

## The Golden Rules of Detection

Community guides converge on three rules:

1. **Trust the computer.** The NET Database profile is treated as verified ground truth. Your job is hunting for **contradictions** between three sources: what the database says, what the customer says, and what your own eyes and tools show. A real human matches all three; a doppelganger always slips somewhere.
2. **Never act on a single clue.** One odd answer can be a misread — players have shot innocent people over a single misread birth date. **Two or three stacked clues** (a wrong job plus a suspicious scan plus a mismatched ID) confirm a doppelganger.
3. **Don't complete the sale until you're sure.** The moment the transaction closes, your window is gone. Even shooting after payment counts as a miss. Kill confirmed doppelgangers **before** the sale completes.

## The Question System

Every customer can be asked questions at the register — a limited pool per customer (community sources report **up to 5 questions**). Choose wisely: every question spent on small talk is a question you can't use on something verifiable.

### The Three-Layer Questioning Method

A widely recommended pattern:

1. **Warm-up (1 question)** — "Where are you going?" / "Why are you out this late?" — gauges natural tone and reaction.
2. **ID-correlated (1-2 questions)** — ask about their address, job, or a specific detail on the ID; see if the answers match.
3. **Pressure test (1 question)** — re-ask something they already said in a different way. Humans repeat consistently; fabricated stories collapse under re-asking.

Focus questions on anything that can be cross-verified with the ID or database — that's what forces a fake to improvise.

## The ID Check Routine

Community guides document a repeatable routine for every customer:

**Step 1: Inspect the physical ID**
- Compare the photo to the person: eyes, hair, expression, age
- Check name spelling, expiration date, address, and printed details — the game hides tiny inconsistencies
- Note whether the ID has a barcode

**Step 2: Scan or enter the ID**
- Scan the barcode. If it won't scan, don't jump to conclusions — type the name into the computer manually
- First check whether the person is even **listed as alive** — this single check catches many obvious fakes
- Some drivers refuse to hand over IDs; run passports and licenses through the computer instead

**Step 3: Cross-check four database fields**
- **Occupation** — does their stated job match the database and their story?
- **Appearance** — does the description (bald, glasses, scars) match the person? A scar on the wrong side of the face is a classic tell
- **Date of birth** — does the ID match the database, and does the customer hesitate when asked?
- **Expiration date** — an expired or invalid ID is suspicious

**Step 4: Ask the targeted questions** (see the three-layer method above). Don't waste questions; prioritize anything cross-verifiable.

### The Verify Menu

During screening you can inspect a customer across categories — community guides document the four as **Head, Clothes, Limbs, Movements**. Spend your first checks on whatever the database description mentions, then use the rest on anything that looked wrong.

## The Emotoscope

Partway through the campaign you unlock the **Emotoscope**, a tablet that reads a customer's emotional state. Two honest notes from community guides:

- Its readings are **accurate** — but some doppelgangers replicate human emotions, so a clean reading clears nobody.
- Treat it as **one clue among several**, never as a verdict on its own.

## Visual Tells to Watch For

Community guides document these tell categories:

| Category | Tells |
|----------|-------|
| **Face** | Eyes pointing in slightly different directions, uneven ears, distorted smiles, strange jaw proportions, expressions that never change |
| **Hands** | Missing fingernails, too many fingers, fingers bending unnaturally, oddly stretched joints |
| **Body language** | Standing perfectly still (humans rarely do), long periods without blinking, sudden head jerks, robotic posture |
| **Behavior** | Emotions that don't match words, hesitation on simple questions, refusals to show ID, contradictions when re-asked, unblinking stares |
| **Audio** | Repeating words, strange answers, instant emotional tone shifts, asking for products the store doesn't sell, awkward pauses |

One red flag is coincidence. **Three or more is a strong signal.**

## Environmental Tricks

- **Noticeboard / newspaper** — read it each shift. It can disprove customer stories (a customer claims to work at a diner the paper says burned down).
- **Reflective surfaces** — give viewing angles you can't get from the counter; details sometimes look wrong in a reflection before they look wrong head-on.
- **The lore note** — doppelgangers are born from people's negative emotions and are bad liars. Use the truth as your weapon.

## Pump Duty and End-of-Shift Checks

Once gas pumps unlock (around Shift 4), inspect vehicles before serving:

- Ask a service question and question their clothes against what you see through the window
- Run the license plate through the lookup
- Sweep the car with your flashlight for suspicious items

And before clocking out, **check the bus**: an empty driver's seat means it's safe; someone sitting behind the wheel is a doppelganger, and boarding introduces one of the fastest monsters in the game.

## The Norbert Exception

One famous false positive breaks every rule above: **Norbert the Gnome**. His ID scan fails with "Fake ID. Scan Unsuccessful." — but he is a harmless prankster. Shooting him escalates his pranks (including a lemonade stand that poisons you). If a "customer" fails to scan but stacks no other tells, consider whether you're looking at Norbert. See the [Norbert guide](/guides/norbert-the-gnome-guide) for the full story.

## If You Miss One: The Lock-Up Phase

If a doppelganger completes its purchase, the store enters a **60-second lock-up alert** and the creature returns in monster form. The response routine:

1. **Barricade the three doors** with planks
2. **Lay traps** in its likely path
3. **Hide** — ventilation shafts work but only temporarily (you'll be dragged out if you stay too long)
4. **Stay quiet** — the entity's patience bar doubles as its health and timer, and community reports say it tracks audio

Weapons come from the **Emergency Arsenal** purchased beforehand on the store computer — see the [Weapons Guide](/guides/weapons-guide) for what's confirmed.

## Common Screening Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It Hurts | The Fix |
|---------|-------------|---------|
| Acting on one clue | Innocent people get shot over misreads | Stack two or three contradictions first |
| Wasting questions on small talk | You run out of verifiable checks | Ask only what the ID/database can confirm |
| Trusting the customer over the computer | Fakes lie; the database doesn't | Database first, always |
| Completing the sale "to be safe" | That's exactly what loses the shift | Hold the sale until the call is made |
| Forgetting the bus check | One of the fastest monsters boards | Empty driver seat = safe, driver in seat = fake |
