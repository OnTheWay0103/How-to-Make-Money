# Shift At Midnight Dentist Guide — Identify and Escape

Most of what hunts you in Shift At Midnight you can *handle*: screen the doppelganger at the register, shoot it before payment, barricade the doors when the entity comes. Then there is the **Dentist** — a threat that ignores every rule the game teaches you. You cannot scan him, you cannot question him, and community guides agree you **cannot kill him**. The only winning move is to see him early and run.

This guide covers who the Dentist is, how to recognize him in the seconds you have, why your usual tactics fail, and the escape routine that keeps you alive.

## Who Is the Dentist?

The Dentist is a **tall, severely emaciated serial killer** whose calling card is removing his victims' jaws. According to the game's background lore, he was declared dead roughly **40 years before the game's 1993 setting** — and yet he is apparently back in Midnight Town. The game deliberately leaves it ambiguous whether he is the original killer, a supernatural return, or something wearing his story.

### He Is Not a Doppelganger

This is the most important thing to understand about him: **the Dentist is completely separate from the doppelganger system.**

- He is **not in the NET database**. Looking him up does nothing.
- His ID cannot be scanned at the register.
- Asking him questions about occupation or destination is useless.
- Screening every other customer perfectly will not protect you from him.

The game runs two independent threat systems: the doppelgangers you handle at the register, and the Dentist, who operates outside it. Mastering the first gives you zero protection against the second — which is exactly the point.

## How to Recognize Him

You will rarely get a long look at the Dentist. He typically appears on the **roof and around the store's exterior**, and once he realizes he's been spotted, he tends to slip away quickly. Your observation window can be very short, so memorize his features:

| Feature | What to Look For |
|---------|------------------|
| Build | Extremely tall and severely emaciated |
| Skin | Cracked, damaged, or deteriorated |
| Jaw | **Loose, broken, or hanging jaw** — his signature feature |
| Hands | White, wrapped in bandages, and bloodied |

The **bandaged hands and loose jaw** are the two clearest tells. Community guides are blunt about it: if you see a tall figure with a broken jaw, that's enough confirmation. **Do not get closer to be sure** — the closer you get, the less time you have to run.

### How He's Introduced

The Dentist is not tutorialized. Instead, the game introduces him through **in-game background news reports or computer notices** you can read before a shift begins. Read the threat updates at the start of every shift — if a news item mentions the jawless killer, assume he may be around that night.

## When He Appears

- He is encountered **after hours** — i.e., outside the normal customer-flow of the shift — per community reporting.
- He becomes an active factor from around **Shift 7** of the campaign, where the story walkthroughs describe him arriving and being driven off with upgraded weapons.
- Some encounters appear to be scripted; others seem tied to random night events. Since shifts are randomly generated, his exact schedule is not fully documented.

## Why Normal Tactics Fail

Here is what community guides consistently report about fighting or preparing for the Dentist:

- **Barricades, traps, weapons, and supplies are meaningless against him.** When a Dentist encounter starts, the game strips away its own resource-management core: the only thing that matters is your reaction speed and how long you can run.
- **He cannot be killed** — there is no confirmed kill method in the current version. Attempting to fight him is described as gambling with your life.
- **Surviving one encounter does not remove the threat.** He can return on later nights.

The lore adds one more layer of dread: witnesses who survive an encounter are reportedly dead within a week. There is no visible countdown, no follow-up objective, and no known way to prevent it in-game — treat it as atmosphere rather than a mechanic to solve.

## The 6-Step Escape Plan

When you confirm a Dentist sighting, follow this routine:

1. **Read all threat updates before the shift** — early knowledge is your biggest advantage.
2. **Confirm with one glance, then commit.** Don't second-guess what you saw. A tall, jaw-broken figure is the Dentist.
3. **Co-op: make one short callout and stop talking.** Something like "Dentist, run" — no explanations, no back-and-forth.
4. **Run immediately and keep moving.** Don't stop until the encounter ends.
5. **Stay quiet.** Mute your microphone or switch to push-to-talk; open-mic noise is flagged as a problem and can work against you, just as with the entity.
6. **Do not look back.** Turning around wastes distance and works against your survival — in a chase, looking back neither slows him nor helps you.

### The Anti-Demented Rule

Shift At Midnight veterans like to point out the symmetry: the **Demented** enemy freezes while you keep it in your line of sight — you survive by *watching* it. The Dentist is the exact opposite: once the chase begins, you survive by *never looking back*. If you have internalized the Demented rule, unlearn it when the Dentist arrives.

## Co-Op Rules for Dentist Encounters

The Dentist punishes normal team instincts. A few rules community guides converge on:

- **One callout, then silence.** After the first "run" call, everyone goes quiet.
- **Don't crowd in narrow corridors.** If multiple players try to run down the same hallway, someone gets caught.
- **Do not stop for a caught teammate.** There is no reliable rescue — when the Dentist catches a player, the remaining players should keep running rather than attempt a save.

It sounds cold, but the escape mechanics leave no room for heroics. Surviving players can continue the run; nobody survives a rescue attempt.

## The Dentist in the Finale (Shift 13)

The Dentist bookends the campaign. On **Shift 13**, the final shift, you face a swarm of doppelgangers of all types — and the finale plays out in cutscenes: shooting continues until the Dentist cutscene triggers, at which point you **run in the opposite direction** until a second cutscene catches you. In that second cutscene, a stranger kills the Dentist — and then himself.

Because the finale is cinematic, don't treat Shift 13 as a boss fight with a health bar. There is no confirmed HP pool or kill strategy; the correct play is to survive the swarm, follow the cutscene triggers, and let the story play out.

## What to Do If You're Caught

Community guides are blunt about the worst case: **there is no confirmed rescue from the Dentist.** When he catches a player — solo or in co-op — the encounter effectively ends for that player. That's not a bug; it's the encounter design. The Dentist is the one threat in Shift At Midnight where the game explicitly removes the safety nets it gives you everywhere else.

The honest playbook:

- **Solo**: if he catches you, the run is over. Don't waste time on last-second escapes — the guides that test this report no reliable counter.
- **Co-op**: if he catches a teammate, **keep running**. Stopping to attempt a rescue costs you the escape window, and no community-documented rescue method exists.
- **After a catch**: treat the loss as information. Which part of the routine failed — the noticeboard read, the one-glance confirmation, the escape route, or the silence? Most catches trace back to one of those four.

This is also why the "one glance, then run" rule matters so much: the encounter is decided in the first few seconds. The players who survive are the ones who commit to the escape before the chase even properly starts.

## How the Dentist Compares to Other Threats

It helps to see him in context. Every other major threat in the game has a mechanical answer; the Dentist's answer is *avoidance*.

| Threat | Can You Fight It? | Can You Hide From It? | Can You Screen It? | The Real Answer |
|--------|-------------------|-----------------------|--------------------|-----------------|
| Doppelganger | Yes — kill before payment | Yes — the 60-second alert phase | Yes — NET Database | Screening routine |
| The Entity | Yes — gun and Arsenal weapons | Yes — vents (temporary) | No | Barricades, traps, audio discipline |
| Demented | Unconfirmed — community treats it as watch-and-freeze | No | No | Keep it in view |
| **The Dentist** | **No — no confirmed kill method** | **No — he finds you** | **No — not in the database** | **See him first, run, never look back** |

The pattern is deliberate: each major threat strips away one layer of your toolkit. The doppelganger tests your eyes and your database work. The entity tests your preparation and your discipline. The Dentist tests your awareness and your legs — everything else is stripped away.

## Skills That Transfer to a Dentist Encounter

You can't practice the Dentist on demand, but the skills he punishes are the same ones the rest of the game rewards:

1. **Audio discipline** — the entity already punishes open-mic noise; the Dentist does too. If you've internalized push-to-talk for hunts, the Dentist's silence rule costs you nothing.
2. **One-glance confirmation** — the ID check routine trains you to commit quickly to a call with limited information. The Dentist version just has higher stakes.
3. **Escape-route planning** — the bus check, the barricade routine, and the vent hiding all teach you to know your exits before you need them. Know the store's layout before a Dentist night.
4. **Reading the noticeboard** — the Dentist is introduced through in-game news and computer notices. The players who read them know he's active; the players who skip them find out the hard way.
