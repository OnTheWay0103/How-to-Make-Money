# Shift At Midnight Marionette Guide — Rewind the Box or Face the Music

Shift At Midnight's late-game shifts add a new kind of tension: not just what's at the register, but what's playing in the dark. From **Shift 9 onward**, a jack-in-the-box can begin winding up somewhere in your store. If you treat it as background noise, you meet the **Marionette** — a creature that joins the Entity's hunt and turns a bad night worse. This guide covers when it appears, how the music box mechanic actually works, how to kill the Marionette for the **Last Performance** achievement, and how to juggle the box during the busiest shifts of the campaign.

---

## What Is the Marionette and When Does It Appear

The Marionette is a **jack-in-the-box-style creature** that can appear during Entity hunts **from Shift 9 onward**. It is not a customer, it is not a doppelganger — it is an ambush that triggers mid-hunt if you let its music box finish its melody.

In **Shift 9** you receive an **email from N.E.T.** warning you about the Jack-in-the-Box. That email is the game telling you, out loud: from this point on, listen. The Marionette does not announce itself with a scream or a knock — it announces itself with a **music box melody**, and that melody is the entire mechanic.

---

## The Jack-in-the-Box Mechanic: Rewind Before the Third Play

During a hunt, you may hear a music box start playing somewhere in the store. That is your only warning. The rules are simple:

1. **A melody starts playing.** The box is winding up somewhere nearby — usually on a shelf, counter, or aisle you have walked past a dozen times.
2. **You have until the melody completes three times** to find the box and interact with it.
3. **Rewinding the box** stops the wind-up and **prevents the Marionette from appearing entirely**. Fully rewound, the box is harmless for the rest of the hunt.

### The Rewind Tradeoff

There is a catch, and it is important: **rewinding the box draws the Entity and other creatures to your location.** The winding mechanism is loud, and everything in the store is listening for it. Community guides are unanimous on this point — do not rewind blind.

- Time the rewind when you have a **clear escape route** to a vent, locker, or barricade
- If you are mid-hunt and the Entity is already closing in, let the melody play once more rather than rewinding into a corner
- A quiet stretch of the store is the best moment to act — you will hear the box long before it finishes

---

## What Happens If You Fail

Let the melody complete three times and the box pops. **The Marionette spawns and attacks.**

- It is **tougher than the regular Entity** — community guides consistently recommend bringing real firepower rather than trying to outrun it
- It does not replace the Entity; it **joins** the hunt, so you are now dealing with both
- It is beatable, but it is not a free kill — solo players in particular need real firepower or precise rewind timing

Failing the rewind is not the end of the night, but it is the hardest part of it. Treat the third melody as a hard deadline, not a suggestion.

## How to Defeat the Marionette

Here is the good news: **the Marionette is killable** — unlike the Dentist, who cannot be harmed at all, this creature dies when you bring enough firepower.

- **Bring real weapons.** Whatever the Emergency Arsenal has — the gun, and anything heavier you have stocked from the store computer — is what you use. See the [Weapons Guide](/guides/weapons-guide) for what to stock before late-game shifts.
- **Fight rather than hide.** A stocked Arsenal can put the Marionette down in one focused exchange. Play it like the Entity speedrun tactic: commit to the fight once you have committed.
- **In co-op, split the job.** One player keeps the box rewound while another engages the Marionette — the fight is dramatically easier when the box never finishes winding again.
- **Killing the Marionette unlocks the Last Performance achievement.** It is one of the campaign's milestone trophies, and it only exists because this monster can be beaten.

## Managing the Music Box During Busy Shifts

The reason the Marionette is dangerous is not the monster itself — it is everything happening at the same time. From Shift 9, your plate is full: **customers to serve, doppelgangers to screen, and now a music box winding up mid-hunt.** The shift does not pause while you find the box.

Priority order that works:

1. **Doppelganger at the register first.** A fake completing a purchase triggers a 60-second lock-up alert on top of everything else. Kill before payment — it skips the alert entirely (see the [Doppelganger Guide](/guides/doppelganger-guide)).
2. **Rewind during natural lulls.** Between customers, after a fake is dealt with, or while a co-op partner holds the register.
3. **Never rewind with the Entity adjacent.** The rewind noise pulls it to you; rewind with an escape route mapped, or not at all.
4. **Use the melody count.** Rewinding on the **first or second play** is the difference between a minor detour and a full Marionette fight. The third play is the deadline — budget your tasks so you never reach it.

## Co-op Coordination for Marionette Shifts

The Marionette is where good co-op habits pay off. The community standard is simple: **assign one player as the designated "music box checker."**

- **The checker's only job** during a hunt is audio duty — listen for the melody, locate the box, rewind it. Everyone else runs the store and the fight.
- **Call it out twice:** once when the melody starts ("box started"), once when the rewind is done ("box clear"). If the checker dies or is cornered, the next player takes over — say the handoff out loud.
- **If the Marionette spawns anyway**, the checker keeps the box rewound while the fighters engage. A box that never finishes winding means the fight stays one-on-one with the monster instead of monster-plus-ambush.
- **In a full 3-player lobby**, the split is clean: one on register, one on the hunt, one on the box. Screen-clearing co-op is the intended endgame.

## Marionette vs. Other Special Monsters

The Marionette is not the only special threat in late shifts. Here is how it compares to the monsters the community actually documents — and where the gaps are.

| Monster | When It Appears | What It Does | Counter | Killable? | Achievement |
|---------|-----------------|--------------|---------|-----------|-------------|
| **Marionette** | Shift 9+ | Jack-in-the-box ambush, joins Entity hunts | Rewind box before 3rd melody; fight if it escapes | Yes | Last Performance |
| **Demented** | Late campaign (rare) | Weeping Angel-style, moves only when unobserved | Bear trap + deliberate look-away | Yes (via trap) | Freed |
| **Shrieking Doll** | Mid-campaign+ | Crawling swarm, better sight than the Entity | Shoot from range; dodge sideways | Yes | Silenced |
| **Norbert the Gnome** | Throughout campaign | Pranks, poisoned lemonade stand | Never shoot him; handle his antics | Never — don't shoot | — |
| **Dentist** | Late game | Jawless killer that ignores every rule | Spot him early and run | No | — |

Two names worth knowing: **Phantom and Demon appear in some third-party guides, but have no reliable public confirmation** — our own [Enemy Bestiary](/guides/enemy-bestiary) flags them as unconfirmed, and we recommend treating any stat table for them elsewhere as speculation.

The pattern across the confirmed roster: everything is beatable except the **Dentist**. The Marionette is the one threat that asks you to manage a **timer** instead of a weapon — the music box is a clock, and the achievement is your reward for beating it.

## Final Checklist for Marionette Shifts

- [ ] Know it starts in **Shift 9** — expect the N.E.T. email, expect the melody
- [ ] Rewind the box before the **third play** of the melody
- [ ] Never rewind without an **escape route** — the noise pulls the Entity in
- [ ] Stock **real firepower** from the store computer before late shifts
- [ ] Kill it for the **Last Performance** achievement — it is tougher than the Entity, but beatable
- [ ] In co-op, name a **music box checker** and keep the handoff rules clear

The Marionette is Shift At Midnight's way of teaching you that the worst threats announce themselves — if you listen.
