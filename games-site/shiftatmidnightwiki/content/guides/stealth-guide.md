# Shift at Midnight Stealth Guide — The Night Nobody Notices

**Shift At Midnight / Updated: August 4, 2026**

Shift at Midnight is a stealth game wearing a customer-service uniform. The perfect shift — the one the speedrun community measures everything against — has **zero alerts**: every doppelganger dies before payment, the entity phase ends quietly, and the only sound is the bus pulling up. This guide covers the stealth fundamentals: sound discipline, screening without escalation, entity-phase avoidance, and the trap placement that wins the night.

---

## 1. Why Stealth Is the Win Condition

| Outcome | Cost |
|---------|------|
| Doppelganger completes a purchase | **60-second lock-up alert** — the store locks down, the fake returns in monster form |
| Alert fires | ~60 seconds lost plus a monster-form fight with the entity |
| Fake killed **before** payment | No alert at all — the pro move |
| Perfect shift (zero alerts) | Fastest campaign time, cleanest entity phases |

The alert is the campaign's most expensive mistake. Speedrun knowledge (see the [Advanced Tactics Guide](/guides/advanced-tactics)) treats it as a metric: perfect shifts have zero alerts, and every alert costs roughly a minute plus a fight.

---

## 2. Sound Discipline (The Cleanup Phase)

The shift starts with cleanup, and cleanup is where noise discipline begins:

1. **Mop, don't splash.** The blood cleanup in the next room should take one pass, not a scrubbing session.
2. **Bag limbs quietly.** Trash bags make noise when overstuffed — bag in twos, not fives.
3. **The garage door is a decision.** Leaving it open lets the delivery truck in faster; it also leaves the building open. Close it before the entity phase, open it when you need the escape (see the [Escape Routes Guide](/guides/escape-routes)).
4. **Restock between sales, not during.** Every shelf restock is a noise window; customers and entities both react to activity.

---

## 3. Screening Without Escalation

Stealth doesn't mean avoiding customers — it means screening them without tipping off the fake ones.

| Step | Stealth Behavior |
|------|------------------|
| Trust the computer | The NET Database is ground truth; your job is finding contradictions |
| Two-clue minimum | Never act on a single clue — one odd answer is a misread |
| Warm-up questions | Gauge natural tone before ID-correlated questions (see the [Doppelganger Guide](/guides/doppelganger-guide) for the full method) |
| Kill before payment | Confirmed fakes die before the transaction closes — this is the entire alert-avoidance skill |
| No hesitation | Once confirmed, act. A fake that senses hesitation escalates |

**The golden rule:** the moment the transaction closes, your window is gone — even shooting after payment counts as a miss. Stealth here is timing, not hiding.

---

## 4. The Entity Phase — Avoidance Routes

When the entity attack begins (after the last customer every night), the stealth playbook changes:

1. **Know the patience bar.** The entity's patience bar is its health *and* its timer — it depletes as you survive and as you damage it. Surviving quietly drains it too.
2. **Move in loops, not lines.** The entity tracks position; a continuous loop through the station beats standing in any one spot.
3. **Use the counters as cover.** Line of sight breaks around the register counter and shelving — peek, move, hide.
4. **Never sprint past the garage.** The open garage door is a sightline the entity uses; close it before the phase starts.
5. **Blood Moon nights are louder.** Community guides report guaranteed entity activity plus more doppelganger customers on Blood Moon nights — expect escalation and pre-place traps.

---

## 5. Trap Placement (The Alert Window Playbook)

Even perfect players get alerts. When one fires, the ~60 seconds are a resource, not a loss:

| Location | Trap | Why |
|----------|------|-----|
| Garage | Bear trap / barricade | The creature's most common entry point |
| Near the desk | Tripwire | The entity's patrol passes the register last |
| The back-room doorway | Barricade | Blocks the fastest re-positioning route |

**Timing:** lay traps *during* the alert window (you know where it's coming), then get into position with the gun. The [Entity Survival Guide](/guides/entity-survival-guide) covers the full monster-form fight, but the stealth version is simpler: traps do the damage, you do the aiming.

---

## 6. Stealth Mistakes That End Nights

1. **Screening with single clues.** One misread birth date, one wrong shot — the classic run-ender. Two or three stacked clues or nothing.
2. **Restocking during the entity phase.** Any activity draws the entity; the shelves can wait.
3. **Killing after payment.** The "post-payment kill" is a miss that costs the alert anyway — the timing is the whole mechanic.
4. **Ignoring the Driver.** From Shift 4 onward the bus Driver is a recurring fake — expecting it is the difference between a clean bus ride and a monster in the driver's seat (see the [Escape Routes Guide](/guides/escape-routes)).
5. **Trap hoarding.** Traps in your inventory are dead weight; traps in the garage are wins. Place them before the phase, not during.

---

## 7. The Perfect Shift Checklist

1. Noticeboard read → cleanup done quietly → garage door set
2. Stock once, restock only after sales
3. Every customer screened with the two-clue rule; fakes killed before payment
4. Entity phase: loops, cover, pre-placed traps
5. Correct bus, driver checked

Every step is quiet, deliberate, and on schedule — that's what "stealth" means in a gas station.
