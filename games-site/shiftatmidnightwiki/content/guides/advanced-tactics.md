## Advanced Tactics Guide — Pro Night Shift Strategies

Once you've survived a few shifts, the game stops being about staying alive and starts being about optimization: shaving time off the routine, avoiding alerts entirely, and routing your money toward the True Ending. This guide covers the advanced tactics that separate veterans from survivors — all grounded in the community's documented strategies.

## The Speedrun Routine

The community's fastest campaign loop is confirmed in speedrun walkthroughs:

1. **Read the noticeboard** — objectives and warnings
2. **Clean and prep fast** — mop the blood, bag the limbs, dump them outside, leave the garage door open
3. **Stock once** — get the shelves to quota, restock only after sales
4. **Screen every customer** — knowing exactly who's fake and who's human is the whole speedrun skill
5. **Kill fakes before payment** — the walkthroughs state it plainly: *"Killing a suspected doppelganger before payment is faster because it skips the alert entirely"*
6. **Survive the entity phase** — gun down the entity and everything it spawns
7. **Catch the correct bus** — from Shift 5, check the driver's seat

A tight loop beats a fast player. The run is won on routine discipline, not reflexes.

## The Per-Shift Fake List (Speedrun Knowledge)

The speedrun community has mapped each shift's fakes (full details in the [Story Walkthrough](/guides/story-walkthrough)). The advanced lessons from that list:

- **The Driver is a recurring fake** from Shift 4 onward — expect it and screen accordingly
- **Name traps punish pattern-matching**: Shift 7's *first* Louisa Mari is fake (the second is real); Shift 11's *first* Andal Khan is real (the second is fake). Always verify repeat identities
- **Shift 9's fake Clyde** is the campaign's cruelty: the game reuses a name you trusted on Shift 1
- **Shift 12's self-doppelgangers** attack your own face near shift end — same rules, no hesitation
- **Shift 13 is a swarm** of all types — the finale tests everything at once

Memorize the lists for speed; keep the tell categories for safety, because shifts are randomly generated and the names can vary between runs.

## Alert Management

The 60-second lock-up alert is the campaign's most expensive mistake. Advanced players treat it as a metric:

- **Perfect shifts have zero alerts** — every fake dies before payment
- **One alert costs ~60 seconds plus the monster form** — that's the difference between a clean run and a scrambling one
- **If an alert fires anyway**, use the window: lay traps (walkthroughs suggest the garage and near the desk), then finish the entity with the gun

## Arsenal Timing

Weapons come from the Emergency Arsenal, bought on the store computer before the night begins. The confirmed timing:

| Milestone | Why It Matters |
|-----------|----------------|
| **Before Shift 7** | The Dentist arrives — upgraded weapons drive him and the entity off |
| **Before Shift 9** | The campaign escalates; walkthroughs recommend upgraded weapons again |
| **Blood-rain nights** (Shift 5, later again) | The Arsenal is unreliable — the pistol must suffice |

**Advanced rule**: on blood-rain nights, plan the whole shift around the pistol. Buy traps and planks instead of gambling on Arsenal weapons mid-fight.

## Fund Routing for the True Ending

The True Ending is a routing problem: **$250+ in personal cash by the finale**, with **store money and personal funds kept separate**.

The advanced habits:

1. **Track the personal balance from Shift 1** — never let a slow stretch go uncorrected
2. **Store expenses come from store money** — traps, planks, and Arsenal purchases never touch personal cash
3. **Do the confirmed bonus tasks** — Shift 4's rats, Shift 9's roaches — free income with no account risk
4. **Shift 10 is the checkpoint** — the $250 surgery requirement is revealed; audit your personal balance the moment it is
5. **Decline the Shift 12 email** — the sheriff's surgery offer is a bluff; calling Clyde costs the True Ending

## Advanced Screening

1. **Question budget**: use all your questions on customers early in a shift; late shifts, the flow is heavier and the walkthroughs recommend exhausting the pool on every customer in the finale
2. **Double cross-reference**: pass every ID through the NET Database once — the second pass catches altered digits on IDs you rushed
3. **The environment is evidence**: the noticeboard's news can disprove customer stories (a diner the paper says burned down); reflective surfaces show flaws you miss head-on
4. **Norbert is the exception**: a failed scan alone is not a kill order — the gnome is harmless (see the [Norbert guide](/guides/norbert-the-gnome-guide))

## Set-Piece Preparedness

The scripted events are all beatable with foreknowledge:

- **Shift 2 generator sabotage**: check the generator armed, then shoot the doppelganger waiting back inside
- **Shift 7 thieves**: shooting beats chasing
- **Shift 7 Dentist**: upgraded weapons drive him and the entity off
- **Shift 12 self-doppelgangers**: same screening rules; don't freeze on your own face
- **Shift 13 finale**: shoot until the Dentist cutscene triggers, run the opposite direction, and let the second cutscene resolve it

## Pro Tips

- **Zero alerts is the speedrun goal** — kill-before-payment is the single highest-value habit in the game
- **Blood rain changes the loadout** — pistol-centric shifts, trap-heavy spending
- **The bus check is non-negotiable from Shift 5** — empty driver's seat = safe; driver in the seat = the fastest death in the game
- **Personal cash is the ending currency** — routing beats grinding
- **The routine is the strategy** — the community's fastest runs are just the standard loop executed without mistakes
