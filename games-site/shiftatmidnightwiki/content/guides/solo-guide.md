## Shift At Midnight Solo Guide: Tips & Strategy for Playing Alone

Shift At Midnight supports **1-3 player co-op**, and playing solo is a completely different experience from running with a team. You shoulder every responsibility — stocking shelves, checking IDs, cleaning messes, and surviving the entity — with no backup. But solo play also means **all the income is yours**, there's zero coordination risk, and no teammate can accidentally serve a doppelganger you've flagged.

This guide covers the triage mindset, solo screening, entity survival, and the money discipline that makes solo runs work.

## Solo Difficulty Overview

What changes when you play alone:

- **No role specialization** — you must do every job yourself
- **Every doppelganger is your problem** — you are the only ID checker
- **The end-of-shift entity phase is all yours** — no teammate to draw it off
- **Task management is relentless** — customers, stock, cleaning, all on you
- **Death ends the shift** — there's no revival

The advantage: **every dollar earned is yours** — and the [True Ending](/guides/endings-guide) runs on personal cash, so solo players who master the systems below control their own money picture completely.

## The Solo Prioritization System

Community guides converge on a triage order for solo play:

1. **The counter comes first** — a customer at the register is priority; screening is the core of the game
2. **Identify doppelgangers** — use the NET Database and the question system on every ID
3. **Stock shelves in the gaps** — use empty-counter moments for stocking and cleaning
4. **Prep the defenses** — traps and planks before the end-of-shift phase, not during it

### Why the Counter Comes First

Every customer is a potential doppelganger and a sale. Ignoring the queue lets fakes slip through — and every fake that completes a purchase triggers the **60-second lock-up alert** on top of the shift's normal entity attack. Solo, there's no one to watch the line while you work the stockroom.

**The rhythm**: serve in batches when the queue allows, then use the lull to restock. The goal is a calm counter with full shelves — not perfect multitasking.

## Solo Doppelganger Screening

In co-op, the team divides the work: one on the counter, one on stock, one floating. Solo, **you are the whole team** — and that's a screening advantage in one important way: every customer gets *your* full routine, with no teammate skipping a check.

The routine that works solo (full detail in the [Doppelganger Guide](/guides/doppelganger-guide)):

1. Scan the ID and inspect it (photo, name, expiration, address)
2. Cross-reference the NET Database (alive status, occupation, appearance, birth date)
3. Ask verifiable questions (up to 5 per customer — spend them on things the ID/database can confirm)
4. Stack the clues — two or three contradictions confirm a fake; one odd answer doesn't
5. Kill confirmed fakes **before payment** — it skips the 60-second alert entirely

**Solo-specific tip**: doppelgangers escalate in later shifts (Shift 7+ brings heavier fake lists, Shift 12 even throws your own face at you). Be extra careful with repeat customers — the campaign's name traps (Shift 7's Louisa Mari, Shift 11's Andal Khan) are designed to catch pattern-matching.

## Entity Survival Solo

The end-of-shift entity attack is the solo player's most dangerous moment — there's no teammate to draw it away or rescue you. Survival depends on preparation made *before* the hunt:

### Entity Countermeasures (Confirmed)

- **Kill fakes before payment all shift** — every fake that pays adds a 60-second alert to the shift's finale
- **Barricade the three doors** with planks
- **Lay traps** (bought from the store computer) in the entity's path
- **Stay silent** — the entity tracks audio; mute your mic or use push-to-talk
- **Hide when needed** — ventilation shafts work but only temporarily; you get dragged out if you stay too long

### Fighting vs Hiding, Solo

With a stocked Arsenal you can gun the entity and its spawns down — the community's speedrun tactic. Early on, with an empty Arsenal, hide and survive. **Never count on the Arsenal during blood-rain nights** — community walkthroughs report it's unreliable then (first around Shift 5), and the pistol must carry you.

## Task Management Alone

With no team to split the work, triage constantly:

- **Cleaning** — do the cleanup routine (mop blood, bag limbs, dump outside) at shift start, when the store is empty
- **Rats and roaches** — Shift 4's rat task and Shift 9's roach bonus are confirmed income; do them when the counter is clear
- **Outdoor tasks** — the forest cleanup (Shift 6) and grave task (Shift 10) need the flashlight; prepare before heading out
- **Urgent events** — generator sabotage (Shift 2) and thieves (Shift 7) are scripted; handle them immediately

### When to Call It Quits

Some nights are not worth saving. If the shift has spiraled — a failed money goal, multiple escaped fakes, and a broken defense line — accepting the restart is often better than limping into the finale short on personal cash. The True Ending runs on discipline; a clean restart beats a ruined run.

## Solo Advantages

Playing alone is not all bad. What you gain:

- **All income is yours** — no splits, no coordination overhead
- **No miscommunication** — you know exactly what you checked
- **Perfect information** — no teammate accidentally serving a flagged customer
- **Consistent pacing** — you control the speed of every interaction
- **No voice chat needed** — the entity can't hear what you don't say

## Best Solo Loadout

Without teammates to cover your back, loadout matters more. The confirmed essentials:

1. **The gun** — picked up on Shift 1, the campaign's workhorse; kill-before-payment depends on it
2. **Traps** — bought from the store computer, scattered in advance
3. **Planks** — for the three-door barricade
4. **Flashlight** — for outdoor tasks and dark areas

Exact weapon stats and prices are **not publicly documented** — don't trust third-party tier lists with precise numbers.

## No AI Companions

Shift At Midnight does **not** provide AI companions for solo players — no bots, no NPC assistants, no automated clerks. Every task is yours alone. It's a deliberate design choice: the game wants you to feel the weight of running a gas station by yourself at midnight.

If solo gets too stressful, the game supports **1-3 player co-op**, and even one additional player dramatically reduces the workload — the [Co-Op Strategy Guide](/guides/coop-strategy-guide) covers team play.

## Final Solo Checklist

- [ ] Serve customers in batches; stock in the gaps
- [ ] Run the ID + NET Database routine on every customer
- [ ] Kill fakes before payment (no 60-second alerts)
- [ ] Buy traps and planks before the shift's threats arrive
- [ ] Stay silent during entity phases (push-to-talk)
- [ ] Keep store and personal money separate (True Ending discipline)
- [ ] Check the bus before boarding from Shift 5 (empty seat = safe)
- [ ] Track personal cash against the $250 target from Shift 1

Master these habits, and solo mode becomes not just survivable but profitable — and the [True Ending](/guides/endings-guide) is very achievable alone.
