## Shift At Midnight Characters Guide

Midnight Town's gas station has a small cast of recurring faces — and a long list of creatures wearing their names. This guide covers the **named characters** of *Shift At Midnight*: who they are, where they appear, and the questions players ask most (how to call the sheriff, how to save Clyde, who the Dentist is, whether you can find Guillermo).

For the full doppelganger roster — every fake and its tell — see the [All Doppelgangers list](/guides/all-doppelgangers-list). This page is about the people (and animals) behind the names.

## The Main Characters

| Character | Who They Are | Where They Appear | Status |
|-----------|--------------|-------------------|--------|
| **Clyde Dawson** | The town sheriff and the campaign's key supporting character | Shift 1 (human), Shift 4 (human per walkthroughs), **Shift 9 (doppelganger)**, finale | Central to all three endings |
| **Bonnie McCree** | A regular customer | Shift 1 (confirmed human) | Minor role |
| **The Dentist** | A tall, emaciated serial killer who removes victims' jaws — separate from the doppelganger system | Factor from Shift 7, anchors the Shift 13 finale | Identity unconfirmed (see below) |
| **Norbert the Gnome** | A harmless prankster whose ID scan fails | From Shift 2 onward (Shift 6, Shift 9 reappearances) | Do not shoot him |
| **Louisa Mari** | A customer with a name trap: the *first* Louisa Mari on Shift 7 is a fake | Shift 7 | Verified through the database |
| **Tofu** | A service dog who visits as a customer | Random shifts | **Do not shoot** — he's an innocent customer |
| **Guillermo** | The missing dog from the Night 10 poster | Poster only — no in-game model exists | Cannot be found (see below) |
| **Your pet** | The companion you adopt at the start — a cat, dog, or goose | The whole campaign | The $250 surgery subplot |

## How to Call the Sheriff in Shift At Midnight

The only confirmed "call the sheriff" mechanic is the **end-of-Night 12 decision**: after completing Shift 12, you're offered the choice to call Sheriff Clyde about the pet's surgery.

- **Call him** → Grave Decision ending: Clyde covers the surgery and dies on the final night
- **Decline** → the ending depends on your personal cash ($250+ = True Ending, under $250 = Empty Home)

There is no other documented way to call the sheriff mid-shift — no in-game phone for reporting events is documented in public sources. If you see a guide describing a "call the sheriff" button during regular shifts, treat it as unconfirmed.

## How to Save Clyde (And What Kills Him)

Clyde dies in exactly two ways — both avoidable:

1. **Call him after Night 12** (Grave Decision). He comes, he dies. This is a *choice* — skip the call to keep him alive.
2. **Shoot the real Clyde earlier in the campaign.** This is the disaster case: Clyde visits as a human on earlier shifts, and **on Shift 9 a doppelganger wearing his name** walks in. If you shoot the *real* Clyde, he's permanently dead, the Night 12 call option disappears, and **all three ending achievements are locked out** — there's no ending left to earn.

**The rule**: never shoot on name recognition. Check the NET Database every visit. On Shift 9 specifically, the Clyde at your register *is* the fake — verify the tell before you draw, because the game deliberately mixes real and fake versions of the same name (see the [Doppelganger Guide](/guides/doppelganger-guide) for the verification routine).

## How to Save Both Clyde and Your Pet

Saving both is the **True Ending**, and it requires two things at once:

1. **Don't call Clyde** at the end of Night 12 (so he never comes to the store)
2. **Have $250+ in personal cash** at the vet check after Night 13 (so you pay for the surgery yourself)

That's the whole recipe — and it's why the True Ending is described as a money-discipline test rather than a combat test. Full details and the checklist: [Endings Guide](/guides/endings-guide).

## Who Is the Dentist?

The Dentist is the campaign's stalker: a tall, severely emaciated figure who removes his victims' jaws. He's **not** a doppelganger — he isn't in the NET Database, can't be scanned or questioned, and no confirmed kill method is documented. Surviving him is about reaction speed: one glance to confirm, run, don't look back (see the [Dentist Guide](/guides/dentist-guide) and [Enemy Bestiary](/guides/enemy-bestiary)).

His identity is the game's biggest open question:

- **The ID card detail**: in the Shift 13 finale, the Dentist is shown carrying **Clyde's identification card** — widely reported across community guides
- **The theory**: combined with in-game hints that the town's former mayor was secretly behind a string of killings years ago, the leading theory is that the mayor's guilt became the most powerful doppelganger — one that targets his own bloodline. That reading explains both the fake Clyde on Shift 9 and the Dentist's fixation on Clyde in the finale

**"Is the mayor the dentist?"** — the most common way players ask this. The honest answer: **unconfirmed**. The ID card is documented; the mayor connection is community inference. Neither the game nor the developers have stated the Dentist's identity. More in the [Endings Explained guide](/guides/endings-explained-guide).

## Can You Find the Missing Dog Guillermo?

**No — and stop burning shift time trying.** The "missing dog" poster for **Guillermo** appears on the noticeboard around Shift 10 with a phone number and a reward. Community investigation (including the official Discord) confirmed:

- No in-game model for Guillermo exists anywhere on the map
- The poster's number cannot be called — there's no dialing mechanic
- Reading the poster triggers no dialogue, no quest, no objective
- No player has ever reported collecting the reward

It's a deliberate **red herring** — environmental storytelling about the game's protection theme, not a solvable side quest.

**Don't confuse the three dogs**: Guillermo (the poster dog — can't be found), **Tofu** (the service dog customer — *don't shoot him*, he's innocent), and **your pet** (the cat/dog/goose you adopt at the start — the $250 surgery subplot). Shooting Tofu is a false positive: it docks your pay and can sink a True Ending run.

## How to Question a Customer's Occupation

The "question occupation" mechanic is part of the standard screening routine: ask the customer up to **5 questions** and cross-reference answers against the **NET Database** at the store computer (occupation, appearance, birth date, expiration). A customer whose story contradicts the database — a job they couldn't hold, an age that doesn't match, a "deceased" status — is a doppelganger. The occupation question is one of the fastest filters because the database entries are usually detailed. Full routine: [Doppelganger Guide](/guides/doppelganger-guide).

## Characters With No Public Record (Honest Note)

Search queries turn up more names — **Baron Dean, Bartholomew, Esteban Morales, Judith Calhoun, Karen Whitfield, Kevin Larson, Pan Yu, Ryan Holt, Tyler Brackett, Victor Hale** and others — but no reliable public source documents their in-game roles yet. Treat any guide that gives these characters detailed biographies as unverified. In-game, the NET Database is the authoritative source for every customer you actually meet.
