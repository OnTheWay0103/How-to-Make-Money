# Deadly Days: Roadtrip Beginner Guide — First 5 Days

Deadly Days: Roadtrip is a bullet-heaven roguelite where your backpack matters as much as your aim. You drive a bus through a zombie apocalypse, stop for fuel and loot, clear escalating hordes, and fight a final boss. This guide covers the first five days: priorities, item placement, and the meta upgrades worth buying first.

## What Kind of Game Is This?

Deadly Days: Roadtrip mixes three genres that usually live apart:

- **Bullet heaven / survivor-like:** hundreds of zombies converge on you, and your build — not your twitch aim — decides whether you survive.
- **Inventory management:** weapons and items sit in a grid backpack. Where you place them changes the result, because adjacent items create synergies.
- **Roguelite structure:** each trip is a run. You pick a route, spend fuel and days, and return to a base camp where permanent upgrades wait.

The 1.0 version launched on **October 29, 2026** for PC via Steam, Xbox Series X|S, and PlayStation 5. It followed roughly a year of Early Access. The Steam page describes it as an inventory-management bullet heaven with local co-op, and the store tags list Roguelite, Zombies, Loot, and Top-Down Shooter.

Two things separate it from Vampire Survivors-style games. First, you choose where to go: routes and stops are part of the strategy, not a fixed arena. Second, the backpack is a puzzle. A strong weapon in the wrong slot can underperform a weaker weapon surrounded by the right support items.

## The Run Loop in 60 Seconds

Every trip follows the same skeleton:

1. **Pick a destination on the road map.** Each stop shows a mission type and a threat level.
2. **Drive, then loot.** You need fuel to keep moving. Crates, shops, airdrops, and loot zombies supply weapons, items, and apples.
3. **Fight the horde.** Enemies spawn in waves; level-ups arrive during combat and let you take new items or upgrades.
4. **Manage the backpack.** Rotate items, combine duplicates, sell junk, and make room for better gear.
5. **Reach the boss.** A run ends with a final boss. Beat it to unlock the next biome and meta progress.
6. **Return to base.** Spend apples on permanent upgrades, unlock the workbench, and start the next trip.

Days are a resource. The 1.0 update lengthened days slightly and added a coffee stand at the base: brewing a coffee lets your next run start with increased movement and loot speed. That is a small buff, but it compounds over many runs.

## First Three Runs: What to Prioritise

### Run 1 — Learn the map, not the meta

Your first trip is a scouting run. Do not chase a perfect build. Instead:

- Take fights in open ground so you have room to dash.
- Open every crate you pass; early items matter more than a clean route.
- Note which mission types you can clear comfortably.
- Spend apples only on upgrades that help every survivor.

If you die, that is expected. The base camp is where the real progression happens, and your first unlock — the workbench — changes everything.

### Run 2 — Unlock the workbench

The workbench lets you craft and combine gear. Community unlock guides report that it requires tape, which appears as loot. Once it is open, you can turn duplicate or low-rarity items into stronger versions, and legendary crafting becomes possible. Treat the workbench as your second priority after basic survivability.

### Run 3 — Commit to one damage plan

By the third run you should know whether you prefer melee, guns, or thrown/projectile items. Pick one lane and feed it:

- Melee builds want attack speed, area, and on-hit effects.
- Gun builds want damage, pierce, and reload or fire-rate support.
- Throwable builds want projectile count and explosion modifiers.

Splitting your backpack between three plans is the most common reason new players stall around the first boss. Our [weapon tier list](/guides/weapon-tier-list) explains which 1.0 weapons are easiest to build around.

## Backpack Rules That Decide Runs

The backpack is the heart of the game. These rules matter more than raw item rarity:

**Adjacency matters.** Items placed next to each other can stack effects or improve a weapon. When you get a new item, test it beside your main weapon before you sell it.

**Rotation matters.** Items have shapes. Rotating an item can free a block or create a better adjacency. Do not auto-place; drag and rotate deliberately.

**Rarity is a hint, not a verdict.** A blue item that fits your build can beat a red item that does nothing for it. The 0.22 update made loot crates glow in the colour of their highest-rarity contents, and an unlockable radar shows crate and gas-canister rarity. Use those colours to decide which crates are worth a detour.

**Sell with a purpose.** Selling junk funds better gear. The 0.19 update added item banishing at the workbench, which removes up to 10 unwanted items from your loot pool at the highest tier. Banishing is a long-term fix for a cluttered pool, not an early-game priority.

**Craft, do not hoard.** Combining items into legendary versions is usually better than carrying two mediocre copies. The Chicken survivor's unlock even asks you to craft three legendary items.

For a deeper breakdown of rarities, crafting, and the workbench, see the [backpack and loot guide](/guides/backpack-and-loot-guide).

## Which Survivor Should You Start With?

The starting Survivor is intentionally plain, which makes it a good tutorial character. Once you have a few runs finished, switch to a survivor whose signature item matches your preferred plan:

- **Bushy** plants Trap Seeds that root enemies, and gains bonus crit chance against rooted targets. Strong if you like crowd control.
- **Penny the Clown** releases homing balloons that explode on contact. Strong if you like passive damage while you reposition.
- **Vampire** starts without a weapon but with a swarm of life-stealing bats that grows on every level-up. Strong if you can survive the first few minutes.
- **Astronaut** and **Death** are community-reported unlocks that reward specific play patterns; both are covered in the [character unlock guide](/guides/unlock-all-characters).

If you want the short answer: start with Survivor to learn the loop, then move to Bushy or Penny for your first serious boss attempt. The full ranking is in our [character tier list](/guides/character-tier-list).

## Where to Spend Apples First

Apples are the base-camp currency. Prioritise upgrades in this order:

1. **Survivability.** Extra health or armor keeps runs alive long enough to reach the boss.
2. **Loot and movement speed.** Faster looting means more items per day; movement speed keeps you out of bad spawns.
3. **Damage.** Take damage upgrades once you can reliably reach the boss.
4. **Utility.** Radar, extra rerolls, and banishing slots come later.

The 0.19 update lowered the price of several existing incremental upgrades and added four new ones with row-based unlocks. Check the upgrade rows before spending: a cheap upgrade in a new row may unlock a better tier than an expensive upgrade in an old one.

## Co-op Notes for New Players

Couch co-op arrived in the 0.22 update and is finished in 1.0. Two players share a screen, and Steam Remote Play lets a friend join online without owning the game. One player needs a controller. The inventory has a turn notification in co-op, so watch for the prompt before you drag items — the other player may be mid-menu.

Co-op is more forgiving for learning bosses because one player can kite while the other loots. It is not easier in raw enemy count; expect more chaos and more projectiles.

## Five Mistakes That Cost the Most Time

1. **Ignoring fuel.** A great build does not matter if you cannot reach the next stop. Treat fuel as a route constraint.
2. **Fighting in tight corridors.** Dash is your main defensive tool; walls remove your escape route.
3. **Overvaluing rarity.** Build fit beats colour. Check adjacency before you swap.
4. **Skipping the workbench.** Crafting is a core power spike, not a side activity.
5. **Spreading upgrades thin.** Commit to one damage lane until the first boss is comfortable.

## What Changed in 1.0

The 1.0 update added the Vampire survivor, the boss Laz, more than 110 total weapons and items, new maps, a revamped base camp with a coffee stand, 30+ achievements, reworked tutorials, and hundreds of balance changes. Early Access save games carry over. If you are returning after a break, your old build may still work, but item values and boss behaviour have changed enough that a quick test run is worth it.

The endgame is the **Headquarters Run**, added in the 0.21 update: build a bomb, assault MKing Corp's headquarters across multiple stages, and beat its CEO. You do not need to think about it during your first five days, but it explains why boss practice matters — the final run assumes you can handle the earlier final bosses.
