# Deadly Days: Roadtrip Backpack and Loot Guide — 1.0

The backpack is the real build screen in Deadly Days: Roadtrip. Weapons and items occupy grid blocks, and placement changes performance because adjacent items stack effects. This guide covers the loot loop: grid rules, rarity colours, crafting and banishing, and which stops are worth the fuel in 1.0.

## The Backpack Is a Grid, Not a List

Every item has a shape and occupies blocks. That single design choice creates most of the game's decisions:

- **Space is a resource.** A large weapon may be stronger than a small one, but it also blocks support items.
- **Rotation is free power.** Before you sell an item, rotate it. A different orientation can free a block or create an adjacency you did not see.
- **Adjacency is the multiplier.** Items placed next to each other can stack effects or improve a weapon. A common item next to the right partner can outperform a rare item sitting alone.
- **You can drag during combat.** The inventory is not pause-safe in the way a turn-based game is; in co-op there is even a turn notification. Practise quick swaps before a boss, not during one.

The official store page calls the genre an "inventory management bullet heaven," and that is accurate: the backpack is not a menu, it is the build.

## How Rarity Works

The 0.22 update made loot crates light up in the colour of their highest-rarity contents. That is the single most useful quality-of-life change for looting: you can see, from a distance, whether a crate is worth crossing the map for.

- **Crate colour = highest rarity inside.** A crate with one high-rarity item glows even if the rest is junk.
- **Radar shows rarity.** A one-time unlock enables an improved radar that displays the rarity of loot crates and gas canisters. Buy it as soon as it appears; it turns route planning from guesswork into triage.
- **Legendary is the top craftable tier.** Combining items is how you reach legendary gear. The exact colour-to-tier mapping for every rarity is `[Unconfirmed]` — the game shows colours, but the developer has not published a full legend. Do not trust a list that claims exact drop rates.

Players often search for "red items." Red is used by the community for high-rarity loot, but because the official patch notes do not define a colour chart, treat any specific "red = best" claim as `[Unconfirmed]` and judge by the item's effect instead.

## Where Loot Comes From

There are five practical loot sources in a run:

1. **Crates and containers** on each stop. The baseline source; colour tells you which are worth opening.
2. **Shops.** You can buy items with the run's currency. Prices vary by rarity, and the 0.18 hotfix notes mention a Copy Shop price adjustment, so shop pricing has been tuned over time.
3. **Airdrops.** Level-ups can trigger airdrops. These are a mid-fight power spike and a reason to keep leveling even when you are comfortable.
4. **Loot zombies.** Special enemies that flee when damaged and drop items. The 0.18 hotfix changed them to flee only after taking damage, which makes them easier to kill than before.
5. **Boss rewards and unlocks.** Bosses and run completion feed the meta layer, not just the current backpack.

Item packs are a common search term. The only extra we could confirm on the store side is a **supporter pack**, which is a purchase for supporting the developers rather than a gameplay item bundle. There is no confirmed paid "item pack" that gives in-run power. If you see a site selling one, treat it as `[Unconfirmed]`.

## The Workbench: Crafting and Combining

The workbench is the base-camp station that turns loot into better loot. Community guides report it unlocks with **tape**, which appears as loot. Once open, it supports:

- **Combining duplicates** into stronger versions.
- **Crafting legendary items**, including the craftable 1.0 weapons.
- **Item banishing**, added in 0.19, which removes unwanted items from your loot pool (up to 10 items at the highest tier).
- **Extra rerolls**, via items such as the dice, which is unlocked with any reroll at the workbench.

Crafting is not a side activity. Three legendary crafts unlock the Chicken survivor, and several of the strongest 1.0 weapons — the Grinder, Flamethrower, Mega Shotgun, Desert Eagle, Crossbow — are craftable. If you ignore the workbench, you are playing with a smaller item pool and a weaker ceiling.

### Crafting Priority

Craft in this order:

1. **A crowd-clear weapon** if your current build struggles with swarms.
2. **A boss weapon** if you consistently reach the final fight but cannot finish it.
3. **A synergy item** that triggers your existing on-hit or Burn effects.
4. **Utility** (Crowbar, radar, extra rerolls) once damage is solved.

Do not craft a second copy of a weapon you already have unless the combination upgrades it. Materials are better spent covering a weakness.

## Item Banishing Explained

Banishing is the answer to a cluttered loot pool. At the workbench you select items you never want to see again; at the highest tier you can banish up to 10. Banished items stop appearing in airdrops, shops, and loot zombie drops.

Use it on items that are **never** correct for your playstyle, not on items that are merely situational. Banishing a decent support item because you had one bad run narrows your future options. Good early bans are items whose shape never fits your backpack or whose effect you consistently ignore.

The 0.20.3 hotfix notes mention that banish had to be manually re-activated for older banish sets, so if you are returning after a break, check that your ban list is still active.

## Luck, Rerolls, and Why They Matter

The 0.19 update added a **Luck** stat. Higher luck improves your chances by rerolling chance checks. The Lucky Clover item boosts luck.

Luck is a multiplier on every probabilistic system in the run: drops, rerolls, and chance-based item effects. It is not a substitute for damage, but it is an excellent third-stat investment once your build has a core. If you find a Lucky Clover early and your build is stable, keep it. If your build is not stable, the slot is better spent on damage or defense.

## The Loot Route That Pays

Route choice is where the backpack meets the map. A practical 1.0 route:

1. **Prioritise fuel first.** A great item you cannot carry to the next stop is worth nothing.
2. **Take the glowing crate detour** when the colour is high and the threat is manageable.
3. **Fight in open ground.** Loot density is worthless if you die in a corridor.
4. **Clear loot zombies** when they appear; they are a dense item source.
5. **Leave one stop of slack** before the boss. You want time to craft and rearrange, not a forced fight with a half-built backpack.

The 1.0 coffee stand helps here: brewing a coffee gives your next run increased movement and loot speed. It is a small edge, but on loot-heavy routes it compounds.

## Common Backpack Mistakes

- **Auto-placing items.** Rotation and adjacency are free stats. Use them.
- **Hoarding duplicates.** Combine or sell; two mediocre copies are worse than one upgraded item plus a support slot.
- **Ignoring shapes.** A strong item that breaks your adjacency web can be a net loss.
- **Banishing too early.** You do not know the full item pool in your first few runs.
- **Skipping the radar.** Crate rarity visibility changes which detours are worth taking.

## Bottom Line

The backpack rewards planning more than reflexes. Build a shape that supports your main weapon, use crate colours to choose detours, open the workbench early, and banish only the items you truly never use. Rarity helps, but adjacency and synergy decide runs.
