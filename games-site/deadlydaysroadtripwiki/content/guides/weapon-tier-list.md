# Deadly Days: Roadtrip Weapon Tier List — Best 1.0 Weapons

Deadly Days: Roadtrip does not publish damage numbers, so this weapon tier list is built on behaviour, not spreadsheets. It ranks the named 1.0 weapons by four questions: fire uptime, crowd clear, boss damage, and craft cost. Everything below is editorial, checked against the official 1.0 weapon descriptions.

## How We Ranked These Weapons

Four criteria, in order of importance:

1. **Uptime.** A weapon you can fire constantly beats a stronger weapon that leaves you reloading while a horde closes in.
2. **Crowd clear.** Most runs end to a screen full of enemies, not to one elite.
3. **Boss damage.** The final boss is a single target, so pure AoE needs a partner weapon.
4. **Craft cost.** A craftable weapon that eats three rare items is not automatically better than a common drop.

We do **not** rank by hypothetical DPS. The developer has not published per-weapon damage, fire rate, or ammo values, so any exact number you see elsewhere is either datamined or invented. When a value matters and is not public, this page writes `[Unconfirmed]`.

## Quick Tier Table

| Tier | Weapons | Role |
|------|---------|------|
| S | The Grinder, Flamethrower | Screen-wide clear with strong uptime |
| A | Mega Shotgun, Desert Eagle, Crossbow, Rifle | Reliable damage at every range |
| B | Machete, Tommy Gun, LMG, Crowbar | Good tools with a clear trade-off |
| C | Brass Knuckles, Hammer, Lottery Ticket | Niche, unconfirmed, or outclassed |

## S Tier

### The Grinder

The Grinder is a craftable 1.0 weapon: a spiked spinning wheel that, in the official wording, "destroys everything that gets in its way." That description is doing a lot of work. A persistent hitbox that follows your movement solves the core bullet-heaven problem — enemies that path into you from every direction — without asking you to aim.

The Grinder earns S because it is both offence and space control. Its weakness is single-target damage: a spinning wheel that shreds a crowd is usually less efficient against a boss. Pair it with a piercing or high-damage gun and the weakness disappears. Its craft cost is `[Unconfirmed]` beyond being craftable.

### Flamethrower

The Flamethrower is also craftable. It shoots a stream of flames and leaves fires that deal a share of that damage every second, and the fires can apply Burn. That makes it the best "fire and forget" weapon in the game: you spray a lane, retreat, and let the ground do the work.

It ranks beside the Grinder rather than below it because Burn scales with time, which suits the game's day-based run structure. The trade-off is that you need to keep enemies inside the fire. On open maps that is easy; in tight corridors it is less reliable. Against a boss that moves out of the flames, bring a second damage source.

## A Tier

### Mega Shotgun

A craftable 1.0 weapon described as "very high damage and lots of pellets." Shotguns are the natural answer to a boss that closes distance, and the pellet count also gives it respectable crowd clear at short range. It sits in A because the range falloff is real: if you are kiting, you are losing damage.

### Desert Eagle

The craftable hand cannon is described as "high damage, pierces one extra enemy." Piercing turns a single-target weapon into a line clearer, which is exactly what you want in a corridor. It is an A-tier pick for players who like precise, ammo-efficient shots rather than spray.

### Crossbow

The craftable Crossbow "shoots bolts that stick in enemies" and repeats on-hit effects when triggered. That makes it a synergy weapon rather than a raw-damage weapon. If your backpack is built around on-hit effects, the Crossbow can outperform its apparent tier; if it is not, it is a slow single-target tool. We place it in A because the ceiling is high and the floor is still usable.

### Rifle

The Rifle is not a 1.0 addition, but it remains the benchmark starter gun. It is tied to the Sharp-Shooter achievement (1,000 rifle kills), which unlocks Bushy, so every new player will spend time with it. The Rifle earns A for consistency: steady damage, manageable recoil, and no crafting requirement. It is the weapon you measure every other gun against.

## B Tier

### Machete

A 1.0 melee weapon with a "small sweeping attack." Sweeping melee is excellent against trash and dangerous against elites, because you have to be close enough to trade hits. It is a solid early pick before your backpack has defensive items.

### Tommy Gun

"Fast, but inaccurate." The Tommy Gun is a crowd weapon that suffers against bosses and at range. It pairs well with anything that tightens spread or rewards volume of fire, but it is not a weapon you build a run around.

### LMG

"Fast, but heavy." The LMG's trade-off is movement or handling rather than accuracy. In a game where dash timing keeps you alive, a heavy weapon can be a real cost. Use it when your build already solves mobility.

### Crowbar

The 1.0 Crowbar is a melee weapon that "increases looting speed." That is a utility weapon, not a damage weapon. It is genuinely useful on loot-heavy routes and genuinely bad as your only answer to a boss. Keep it as a swap-in, not a main.

## C Tier

### Brass Knuckles

"Fast, short-range punches." Short range plus fast attacks is a high-risk profile in a game where standing still is how you die. It can work with a tanky build, but it asks more than it gives.

### Hammer

The 1.0 announcement lists a Hammer among the new weapons, but its exact behaviour is not described in the patch notes we checked. Treat it as `[Unconfirmed]` until you test it. It sits in C by default, not because it is bad, but because we cannot defend a higher placement without data.

### Lottery Ticket

The Lottery Ticket is named in the 1.0 announcement as one of the new items, but its effect is not described in the source we used. It may be a utility item rather than a weapon. `[Unconfirmed]` — do not build around it until the behaviour is verified.

## The Three Build Archetypes That Work

### Melee and Aura

Core: The Grinder, Machete, Brass Knuckles. Support: attack speed, area, on-hit effects, armor. This build wants to be close, so it needs a defensive item and a dash plan. It clears trash faster than any gun build and struggles against bosses that punish melee range.

### Guns and Pierce

Core: Rifle, Desert Eagle, Mega Shotgun, LMG. Support: damage, pierce, reload or fire-rate, projectile count. This is the safest build for new players because it works at every range. It is also the most item-hungry: a gun with no support items feels weak.

### Burn and Ground Control

Core: Flamethrower, Crossbow, any fire-spreading item. Support: Burn duration, area, on-hit triggers. This build trades burst for zone control. It is the best answer to the game's swarm phases and the worst answer to a boss that leaves the fire.

Your backpack decides which archetype is real. A perfect S-tier weapon with no support is worse than an A-tier weapon surrounded by synergy. The [backpack and loot guide](/guides/backpack-and-loot-guide) explains adjacency and crafting in detail.

## Crafting Priority

If you have limited materials, craft in this order:

1. **A crowd-clear weapon** (Grinder or Flamethrower). Most deaths are swarm deaths.
2. **A boss weapon** (Mega Shotgun or Desert Eagle). The final boss is a single target.
3. **A synergy item** (Crossbow if your build triggers on-hit effects).
4. **Utility** (Crowbar) only when your route is loot-heavy.

Do not craft a second crowd weapon before you have a boss answer. Doubling down on trash clear is the most common crafting mistake.

## Co-op Weapon Notes

In couch co-op, split roles instead of stacking the same build. One player takes a crowd weapon (Grinder or Flamethrower) and the other takes a boss weapon (Mega Shotgun or Desert Eagle). The crowd player holds the lane; the boss player saves cooldowns for the final fight. Two identical builds look strong in the first ten minutes and fall apart when a boss absorbs the same damage twice.

## Bottom Line

The Grinder and Flamethrower are the most impactful 1.0 weapons because they solve the problem the game throws at you most often: too many enemies. The A-tier guns are the reliable backbone. The B and C tiers are not unusable — they are situational, and the game rewards matching a weapon to your backpack more than chasing a ranking.
