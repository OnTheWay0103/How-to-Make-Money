# Deadly Days: Roadtrip Character Tier List — 1.0 Ranking

This tier list ranks Deadly Days: Roadtrip 1.0 survivors by three things: how reliably they keep a run alive, how little they depend on a lucky backpack, and their unlock cost. Placements are editorial; the game publishes no official balance ranking, so each tier is an argument you can check against the listed mechanics.

## How This Tier List Works

Three criteria decide each placement:

1. **Survivability.** Does the character's signature item protect you when the screen fills with projectiles?
2. **Build independence.** Can the character function with common items, or does it need a specific rare drop?
3. **Unlock cost.** A strong character locked behind a long achievement is less useful to a new player than a slightly weaker one you can get in two runs.

The tier labels mean:

- **S — carries runs.** Strong from the first minute and flexible in the late game.
- **A — reliable.** Good with almost any build, but has a clear weakness.
- **B — situational.** Strong in the right build, mediocre outside it.
- **C — challenge picks.** Fun or meme characters that ask more than they give.

This list is about the 1.0 build. Balance changes have been frequent during Early Access, and the developers shipped hundreds of changes in 1.0 alone, so revisit the list after major patches.

## Quick Tier Table

| Tier | Survivors | One-line reason |
|------|-----------|-----------------|
| S | Vampire, Penny the Clown | Damage engine that works without a perfect weapon |
| A | Bushy, Astronaut, Death | Strong tools with a clear condition or learning curve |
| B | Chicken, Sheriff, Survivor | Dependable but build-hungry or deliberately plain |
| C | Banana Person | Joke kit; low ceiling for serious runs |

## S Tier

### Vampire

The Vampire was added in the 1.0 update and starts **without a weapon**, but with a handful of bats. Every level-up adds another bat. The bats deal melee damage and have a chance to life-steal.

That kit is why the Vampire sits at the top. It turns the level-up curve itself into your damage source, so you are not praying for a specific gun drop. The life-steal also patches the biggest beginner problem: chip damage between fights. The trade-off is the opening minutes. With no weapon, your first fight is the hardest part of the run, and a bad first mission can end it before the bats snowball.

Official unlock hint: the 1.0 announcement says "a meeting with the shady merchant might be mandatory." The exact trigger is not spelled out, so treat any step-by-step route as community-reported.

### Penny the Clown

Penny arrives with the **Funny Balloon**, which periodically releases a balloon that homes in on enemies and explodes on contact. Damage scales as Penny levels up, and balloons pop on their own after a few seconds.

Penny is the most forgiving survivor in the game. The balloon keeps dealing damage while you loot, reposition, or drag items in the backpack. It does not need a weapon to function, and it clears trash mobs that would otherwise chip you down. The weakness is boss damage: homing balloons are great against spread-out hordes and less impressive against a single large target. Pair her with a focused single-target weapon and the weakness mostly disappears.

Official unlock: complete the Juggler achievement by having **3 Rubber Balls in your backpack and active**.

## A Tier

### Bushy

Bushy's mechanic is **Trap Seeds**. You plant seeds that sprout into traps when enemies get close, and the traps can root enemies with the Rooting status effect. Bushy also gains bonus crit chance against rooted enemies.

This is a strong, active kit. Rooting solves the bullet-heaven problem of being surrounded, and the crit bonus gives you a clear damage plan: root first, then burst. Bushy drops to A rather than S because the kit asks you to think about placement. If you plant seeds behind you while retreating, you get far more value than if you panic-plant into a crowd.

Official unlock: complete the Sharp-Shooter achievement by killing **1,000 zombies with the rifle**.

### Astronaut

The Astronaut is a community-reported unlock: find and defend the spaceship event in The Canyon, The Mountains, or The Snowy Peaks, then take the spacesuit. The event is random, so it can take several attempts.

The Astronaut earns A because the spacesuit is a defensive item that fits almost any build. Official patch notes also reference an Astronaut spacesuit armor upgrade, which confirms the item is part of the real kit rather than a rumour. The randomness of the event is the main cost: you cannot reliably target the unlock in a short session.

### Death (Grim Reaper)

Death is the high-risk pick. Community guides report the unlock by staying in one location for **12 minutes**, after which Death appears on the radar as a skull icon. Touching it — or killing it — unlocks the character.

Death belongs in A because the character item is a genuine safety net, but the unlock is a test of patience and build quality. Staying in one place for 12 minutes forces you to fight continuously, which is the opposite of the hit-and-run style most beginners use. If you can survive the unlock, you have already learned the skills the character rewards.

## B Tier

### Chicken

The Chicken is unlocked by crafting **three legendary items**, which requires the workbench and a fair amount of loot luck. Its identity is a mechanical egg that spawns chicks, according to community documentation.

The Chicken is not weak, but it is build-hungry. Getting three legendaries means committing to crafting, and crafting means giving up inventory space you could spend on immediate power. Put it in B: a good character for players who already enjoy the workbench loop, not a first pick.

### Sheriff

The Sheriff unlocks by **winning one run**, which makes it one of the most accessible alternates. Community reports describe a straightforward kit without a dramatic gimmick. That is fine, but "no gimmick" also means no problem-solving tool. The Sheriff is a fair pick once you understand the loop; it will not rescue a bad build the way the Vampire or Penny can.

### Survivor

The default Survivor is deliberately plain. That is a feature for your first two runs: you learn the map, the fuel economy, and the backpack without a character mechanic distracting you. It is a B pick for anything past that. Once you have unlocked Bushy or Penny, the Survivor has little reason to come back except for a clean baseline.

## C Tier

### Banana Person

The Banana Person is unlocked by running over a banana costume in the base camp. It is a joke character, and that is the point. Treat it as a challenge run or a co-op gag, not a progression pick. If you are trying to beat the Headquarters Run for the first time, the Banana Person is working against you.

## Unlock Priority for New Players

If you want the shortest path to a stronger roster:

1. **Finish one run** to unlock the Sheriff and confirm you understand the loop.
2. **Unlock the workbench**, then craft toward the Chicken if you enjoy crafting.
3. **Chase Penny** — three Rubber Balls is a concrete, achievable target.
4. **Chase Bushy** with a rifle-focused run; 1,000 rifle kills is a grind but a predictable one.
5. **Attempt Vampire** once you can reach the shady merchant reliably.
6. **Leave Death and Astronaut** for later; both depend on survival or random events.

The full step-by-step methods, including the community-reported President password, are in the [character unlock guide](/guides/unlock-all-characters).

## Co-op Tier Notes

Couch co-op changes the ranking slightly. Penny and Vampire remain excellent because their damage is passive, which leaves you free to manage the shared screen. Bushy is better in co-op than solo because one player can hold a chokepoint while the other plants traps. The Astronaut's defensive item is more valuable when a partner is learning the boss patterns.

The weakest co-op picks are the characters that need careful inventory management, because the co-op inventory uses a turn notification and two players dragging items can slow the run down. That is a coordination problem, not a character problem, but it matters when you are choosing who plays what.

## What Would Change This List

Three things could move characters between tiers:

- **A balance patch** that changes bat scaling, balloon damage, or Trap Seed duration.
- **New items** that make a previously build-hungry character self-sufficient.
- **A new endgame** that rewards single-target damage over trash clearing.

The 1.0 update already added more than 110 weapons and items, so the item pool is large enough that most characters have at least one strong build. If a future patch adds a boss with heavy anti-summon mechanics, Penny and Vampire would fall; if it adds more swarm phases, they would rise.

## Bottom Line

For most players, the best first investment is **Penny the Clown** because her unlock is concrete and her damage is passive. **Vampire** has the highest ceiling once you survive the opening. **Bushy** is the most interactive and rewards good positioning. The Survivor is a fine tutorial and a forgettable main.
