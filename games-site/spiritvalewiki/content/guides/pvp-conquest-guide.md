# SpiritVale PvP Conquest Guide — How 5v5 Capture Points, Valor, and the PvP Channel Work

> **Last verified: October 23, 2026 — SpiritVale 0.34.2.**

> **Patch-detail note (October 24, 2026):** the 0.34.1 and 0.34.2 announcements could not be reopened during the October 23 QA pass. The 0.34.0 Conquest mode itself is confirmed by multiple sources; the specific 0.34.1/0.34.2 details below (level scaling 100→150, max party 2, consolidated queues) are taken from those patch notes and should be treated as **[Unconfirmed]** until you see them in-game.

SpiritVale's 0.34 update added a real competitive mode, and it is not the Colosseum. **PvP Conquest** is a **5v5 fight over three capture points**, with its own lobby, its own currency, and a rule set that deliberately flattens burst damage.

It also has a restriction that surprises people on their first queue: **you cannot enter as a five-stack.** Since 0.34.2, the maximum PvP party size is **two**. The mode is five versus five, but you queue as a solo or a duo and the rest of the team is filled from the consolidated queue.

That single rule changes how you should approach the mode. This guide covers the queue, the lobby, the capture loop, the current balance numbers, Valor rewards, and the PvP channel that now sits on every map.

---

## 1. What Conquest Actually Is

The official 0.34.0 notes describe it in three lines: **5v5, three capture points, and a stats screen** available by clicking the PvP button during and after a match.

That is the whole confirmed ruleset. There is no official description of match length, score threshold, respawn timer, or tiebreaker. Those details are learnable in-game but are not published. **[Unconfirmed]**

What we can say with confidence is the shape of the mode:

- Two teams of five.
- Three contested points on the map.
- Holding points is the objective; the stats screen exists because individual performance matters to the mode's evaluation.
- Matches are entered through the **PvP lobby**, not the open-world arena.

---

## 2. Who Can Queue

Three requirements apply as of 0.34.2:

1. **Level 100 or higher.**
2. **An advanced class.** Base classes cannot queue.
3. **A party of no more than two.** Solo or duo only.

The level requirement was tightened over two patches. 0.34.1 raised **PvP level scaling from 100 to 150**, mainly affecting HP and MP. 0.34.2 then restricted queue access to **Lv.100+ advanced classes**. Conquest is endgame content: you need both the level and the advanced class.

The party cap is the more interesting design choice. A five-stack cannot queue together, so the mode is structurally solo/duo. That reduces premade stomps, but it also means communication is limited to your one partner and whatever the match's ping system supports. If you want coordinated five-player play, the game's **Guild Wars / GvG** system is the mode for that, not Conquest.

### Region queues

0.34.2 also consolidated the queues:

| Your region | Queue pool | Match starts in |
|-------------|-----------|-----------------|
| NA / EU / SA | Shared pool | NA |
| OCE / SEA / ASIA | Shared pool | ASIA |

This reduces queue times at the cost of higher ping for some players. If you are in SA and get placed in NA, treat latency as part of the mode and favour defensive, reaction-based play over frame-perfect combos.

## 3. The Lobby and the Combat Master

Conquest has a dedicated **PvP lobby**. The official notes list four things you can do there:

- **Queue** for a match
- **Spar** with other players
- **Use services** (the same town services you would expect in a hub)
- **Purchase gear with Valor**

Entry is through the **Combat Master**. The lobby is also where the **stats screen** becomes relevant: the PvP button shows your performance during a match and at the end, which makes the lobby the natural place to review before requeueing.

---

## 4. The Three Capture Points

The objective is simple: **hold more points than the other team.** The strategy is not, because three points create a constant trade-off between spreading thin and stacking one lane.

Editorial advice, based on the three-point structure rather than any official tactical guidance:

**Do not send all five to one point.** With three points, a five-stack on one node concedes the other two for free. A common shape is 2-2-1: two on each outer point, one flexible player rotating to whichever fight is losing.

**The solo queue is your roamer.** If you queued alone, you are the most expendable piece of the formation. Rotate, reinforce, and avoid committing to a fight you cannot win. A duo should stay together; two players who can combo are worth more than two players on separate points.

**Trade points, not kills.** A kill gives you a temporary numbers advantage. A capture gives you the actual win condition. Chasing a low-HP enemy away from a point is usually a mistake unless the kill secures the point.

**Use the stats screen between matches.** It is the only official performance feedback the mode provides. If your damage is high but your point contribution is low, you are playing team deathmatch in a domination mode.

For class pairing, the existing [Party Composition Guide](/guides/party-composition) covers the tank/healer/DPS roles. In Conquest, a duo of one durable initiator and one ranged damage dealer is the most self-sufficient combination, because you cannot rely on a third player to peel for you.

## 5. The Balance Rules That Define the Mode

Conquest does not use open-world damage numbers. The official 0.34.0 notes apply three modifiers:

| Rule | Effect |
|------|--------|
| **Damage reduced by 82.5%** | Effective damage in PvP is a fraction of PvE damage |
| **Healing reduced by 65%** | Sustained healing is cut, so burst and crowd control matter more |
| **Second Wind Grimoire disabled** | The Berserker's emergency sustain is removed |

The intent is obvious: fights should last long enough for capture points to matter, and one-shot burst should not decide a match. The healing cut is proportionally smaller than the damage cut, so a dedicated healer is valuable — but not enough to out-sustain coordinated focus.

Subsequent patches refined the rule set:

- **0.34.1:** Swift Intervention Grimoire disabled in PvP; level scaling raised from 100 to 150 (mainly HP/MP).
- **0.34.2:** Perfect Cloak fixed against Shadow Monsters; Seer Gem no longer allows targeting cloaked units without a ground reveal; Survivor status from Gladys weapons no longer lacks a cooldown.
- **October 12 balance:** Paladin **High Guard** no longer grants Ground Resist in PvP only; **element damage mods no longer work on players or summons** (this also affects Faith); Berserker **Dusk Scythe** and **Trident of Tides** cooldown reduction reduced from 5% to 3%; Unyielding cooldown fixed.

The element-mod change is the sleeper. If your build relied on an element modifier to punch through a player's resistance, that interaction is gone in PvP. Re-test your damage before blaming the queue.

## 6. Valor and Rewards

Conquest pays **Valor**, the PvP currency. The official notes list what it buys:

- **Gladys gear**, restricted to PvP and GvG modes and maps
- **Materials and goods**
- **EXP tomes** that increase a character's level by one (account-bound)
- **Titles**
- A **new offhand item type** that can be used with a weapon while keeping a two-handed stance

The community database SpiritValers reports the payout as **10 Valor for a Conquest win and 4 for a loss**, compared with 5/2 for Arena 3v3. It also states that **forfeited matches pay nothing**, and that you must contribute — deal damage, heal an ally, or contest a point — to qualify for the reward. Those figures come from a community database, not the official patch notes, so treat the exact numbers as **[Unconfirmed]** until you see them on your own end-of-match screen.

Two reward details are official and easy to miss:

- **Gladys gear is PvP/GvG-restricted.** Do not expect to wear it into a raid. It is a separate loadout, not an upgrade path for PvE.
- **EXP tomes are account-bound.** They are a way to level an alt, not a tradeable commodity.

The new offhand is the most build-relevant reward. An offhand that preserves a two-handed stance effectively adds a stat slot to two-handed builds. Whether it is competitive with a dedicated two-hander setup is **[Unconfirmed]**.

## 7. The PvP Channel — the Risk Outside the Lobby

0.34.0 changed the open world too: **the last channel of every map is now PvP and Boss enabled.**

That means the highest-numbered channel on each map is no longer a safe farming space. It is a contested channel where players can fight each other and bosses are active. If you are farming materials or hunting a world boss, check your channel before you pull.

The practical rules:

- Treat the last channel as a **contested zone**, not a normal map.
- Do not bring gear you cannot afford to lose if the game's death rules apply there. The patch notes do not specify the penalty, so **[Unconfirmed]**.
- Boss timers plus PvP means third parties. Expect to be interrupted, and clear the surrounding players before committing to a boss.

This is also where Conquest's balance rules do **not** apply. The 82.5% damage reduction is a Conquest modifier, not a world-PvP modifier. Open-world fights use open-world numbers.

## 9. FAQ

**Is Conquest ranked?** The mode has ranks, revised in 0.34.2, but new thresholds are unpublished. **[Unconfirmed]**

**Can I queue with five friends?** No. Maximum PvP party size is two.

**What level do I need?** Level 100 and an advanced class.

**Does PvE gear work?** It works, but Gladys gear is the PvP-specific set, and element mods no longer function against players. Do not assume a PvE burst build transfers intact.

**Is the PvP channel the same as Conquest?** No. The channel is open-world PvP; Conquest is the instanced 5v5 mode.

**Do I lose Valor on a loss?** No. A loss pays less, and a forfeit pays nothing.
