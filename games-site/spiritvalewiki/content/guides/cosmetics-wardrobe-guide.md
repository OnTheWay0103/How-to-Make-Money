# How to Get Cosmetics in SpiritVale — Wardrobe Unlocks, Gear Conversion, and What the Shop Sells

**SpiritVale EA / Updated: September 16, 2026**

SpiritVale separates what your character **looks like** from what your character **wears**. Your stats come from equipment, cards and artifacts; your appearance comes from a cosmetic layer that the developers added in the **March 15, 2026 cosmetics update** and have been expanding in every patch since — the August 25, 2026 patch alone added eight named cosmetic sets plus a batch of backer-designed weapons, shields, pets and mounts.

That layer is called the **wardrobe**, and it is where all three ways to change your look — drops, conversion, and the shop — end up. This guide covers each of them, plus the binding rules that decide which cosmetics you can trade.

---

## The Short Answer: Three Routes Into the Wardrobe

| Route | Cost | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| **World drops** | Free (grind) | Boss-exclusive cosmetics; monster pet and mount cosmetics; Mystery Pet Box and Mystery Mount Box |
| **Convert your own gear** | Shop currency per item | A cosmetic copy of a piece of equipment you already own |
| **Cosmetic shop** | Real-money shop currency | Directly purchasable cosmetics, added permanently to the wardrobe |

## 1. What the Wardrobe Is (and Why It Is Account-Wide)

The developer's own description of the wardrobe menu is short and specific: cosmetic items "can be purchased from here, adding it permanently to your wardrobe **across all characters**."

That is the key property. A cosmetic you unlock is not attached to the character who unlocked it. Buy a set, unlock a drop, or convert a piece of gear, and every character on the account can wear it — useful in a game with 7 base classes and 8 advanced specializations, where most players run more than one.

Cosmetics are worn in a dedicated cosmetic slot, separate from your real gear slots. Nothing about equipping a cosmetic changes your stats.

## 2. Free Cosmetics: What Actually Drops

You do not have to spend anything to look different. The March 2026 update notes list three free sources:

- **Boss-exclusive cosmetics.** Cosmetics that drop from bosses only. These are the prestige items — they cannot be bought.
- **Monster-exclusive pets and mounts.** Monsters can drop their own pet version, and a few drop a mount version of themselves. Killing a monster enough times is the only way to get its cosmetic.
- **Mystery Pet Box / Mystery Mount Box.** World drops that each give one random monster pet or one random monster mount. Because the contents are random, these are the two items most worth watching on the player market.

The notes explicitly say there are "a lot of new cosmetic things you can hunt without premium currency" — the free route is a designed part of the system, not an afterthought.

## 3. Converting Your Own Gear Into a Cosmetic

This is the mechanic that surprises people. Instead of hunting a cosmetic drop, you can turn **a piece of equipment you already own** into a cosmetic version of itself — keeping the look of a weapon or armour piece whose stats you have outgrown.

The March 15, 2026 notes list the price as **"Equip conversion to cosmetic (100p)"**, where *p* is the shop currency the notes call premium currency. Two cautions:

- **Treat the 100p figure as the March 2026 number.** The notes were written during the public playtest, before Early Access launch, and the same entry says that currency was bought through Ko-fi donations at the time, with a move to proper Steam purchases planned after launch. Whether the live build prices conversion identically is **[Unconfirmed]** — check the in-game wardrobe menu for the current figure rather than budgeting against this one.
- **A player report describes a much lower in-game cost.** A review from August 30, 2026 (156 hours played) describes "every item ingame can become cosmetic that cost a low amount of ingame currency, this includes pet and mount." The two accounts of the cost do not match, so treat neither number as exact. The direction — conversion costs something, and it is not free — is consistent.

Conversion is the route to reach for when you have a favourite weapon skin and a better weapon, or when you want to keep a low-level armour look at endgame.

## 4. The Cosmetic Shop: Bought, Not Earned

SpiritVale has an in-game **cosmetic shop**, and the August 25, 2026 patch notes prove it is live: three of that patch's bug fixes are shop fixes — a Dark Fortress mask and gear set that was broken in the cosmetic shop, a Solaris Blade that was missing from it, and a Cyber Rogue Eyewear cosmetic that was rendering backwards.

Everything sold there is appearance only. The store page states it in the developer's own words: "**All Microtransactions in SpiritVale are purely cosmetic and do not affect gameplay**," published under the heading "Fair-To-Play. Never Pay-To-Win."

> **One caveat worth knowing.** The same store page's Early Access Q&A says the developers "plan to introduce cosmetic purchases **and optional bonuses (e.g. inventory space)**", and the Early Access launch notes list "Character Slot purchases" and a "Storage Expansion +500 (500p)". So the shop is not literally cosmetics-only: it also sells the premium currency "p" and a small number of **non-power convenience** items. Nothing in the official notes, the store page, or the Steam review corpus describes a stat, XP or economy advantage for sale — but if you want to be strict about it, the accurate phrasing is "no pay-to-win items", not "cosmetics only".

## 5. Bound vs Tradable — the Rule That Decides Market Price

This is the part most players get wrong, and it is worth memorising:

> "All premium cosmetics are account bound, world droppable monster pets and mounts are tradable."

In plain terms:

| Cosmetic type | Bound? | Tradable? |
|---|---|---|
| Anything bought from the cosmetic shop | Account bound, permanently | ❌ No |
| Monster pet and mount cosmetics from world drops | Not bound to the character | ✅ Yes — sell it, trade it, gift it |

So the free route is also the *liquid* route. A duplicate Mystery Box mount is not a dead item; it is stock. That puts cosmetic drops squarely into the economy covered in the [Trading & Market Guide](/guides/trading-market-guide), where cosmetics are already listed as a high-margin, low-volume category — the flip is slow but the multiplier is large.

## 6. Is SpiritVale Pay-to-Win?

No, and the sources agree from three directions.

**The developer:** "Fair-To-Play. Never Pay-To-Win… All Microtransactions in SpiritVale are purely cosmetic." The shop sells looks, not power.

**Players:** a September 7, 2026 review with 304 hours played: "The only things you can buy from the cash shop are cosmetics. They don't give you any stats or hidden stats." A September 13, 2026 review puts it more bluntly: "0 and I mean 0 P2W."

**Independent write-ups:** 2UpSkill's dedicated monetization review (2026) states plainly that SpiritVale "is not pay to win," that "Once you've bought the game, that's it," that there is no subscription, and that "There isn't a shortcut waiting behind a payment screen." JeuxOnline describes the announced model the same way — "fair-to-play, avec des microtransactions présentées comme purement cosmétiques et non pay-to-win" — with the caveat that this is the developer's *stated* model, not an audited storefront breakdown.

The one honest asterisk: the paid route exists, and it is the fastest way to a specific look. It just does not move your character's numbers.

## 7. Gotchas

- **Grimoires can no longer be converted into cosmetics.** This was fixed in the August 25, 2026 patch. If you were planning to convert spare Grimoires into wardrobe fodder, that route is closed — see the [Artifact Sets Guide](/guides/artifact-sets-guide) for how Grimoires actually work.
- **Expect a long first login after a cosmetics patch.** The March 2026 update rebuilt inventory and storage structures, and the notes warn the first relog may take a while.
- **The pet and mount cosmetic slots are not purely decorative.** The same update describes pets as periodically collecting items for you and mounts as granting a bonus to movement speed while out of combat. Both are cosmetic items in the wardrobe, but both carry a small quality-of-life effect — see the [Pets and Mounts Guide](/guides/pets-mounts-guide) for the companion side of that system.
- **Cosmetic drops are random, so do not farm for one specific look.** Boss and monster cosmetics come from specific kills, but the Mystery Boxes hand out a random pet or mount. If you want one particular cosmetic, the market is usually the faster route than the drop.
