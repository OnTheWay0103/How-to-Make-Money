# Why Was My SpiritVale Account Banned? — Ban Reasons & Appeal Guide

**SpiritVale EA / Updated: September 9, 2026**

You press Play and instead of your character list you get a block — or the client simply refuses to let you in. For a game you have poured hours (and possibly money) into, that screen stings. This guide covers what actually gets SpiritVale accounts banned (using the developer's own August 2026 numbers), what false-ban reports look like, and the one channel that gets you a straight answer.

> **Honesty note:** most SpiritVale enforcement happens behind the scenes. The developer's enforcement *numbers* are public (reported by MassivelyOP on August 17, 2026); everything about ban *tiers*, the appeal *process*, and specific false-ban *stories* is community-reported and marked **[Unconfirmed]** below. Nothing here guarantees an appeal outcome.

---

## 1. What the Developer's Own Ban List Shows

On **August 17, 2026** the SpiritVale team published an enforcement update that put actual numbers on its ban waves. MassivelyOP [reported the announcement](https://massivelyop.com/2026/08/17/spiritvale-shares-a-ban-list-updates-on-dev-team-size-and-a-list-of-features-for-its-next-update/), which is the independent record we cite:

| Category | Accounts removed | What it covers |
|----------|------------------|----------------|
| Unauthorized programs / macros | **3,821** | Automation — anything that plays the game for you or sends inputs on a script. MassivelyOP characterizes these as permanent bans. |
| Real-money trading (RMT) | **1,106** | Selling in-game gold, items, or services for real-world money, or buying from those sellers. |
| Code of conduct | **65** | Harassment, chat abuse, and rule-breaking that is not about cheating software. |

Two things stand out. First, the largest category is **automation**, not RMT — this is the team fighting the botting problem that threatened the economy (see the [Connection & Server Guide](/guides/connection-server-guide) and [Update & Patch Notes](/guides/updates-patch-notes)). Second, the developer published the numbers openly, and the same update previewed the next content push.

The push did not start in August. On **July 21, 2026** MassivelyOP [reported](https://massivelyop.com/2026/07/21/indie-mmorpg-spiritvale-sees-20000-concurrent-players-as-the-dev-fights-economy-wrecking-bots/) that SpiritVale hit **20,000 concurrent players** in launch week while the studio fought "economy-wrecking bots" and opened a **Bot Hunter Program** that takes reports through official Discord tickets.

## 2. The Three Ban Reasons, Explained

If you are trying to work out why your account was hit, the categories above are the frame.

**Unauthorized programs and macros (the 3,821).** Any third-party tool that automates gameplay — a script that casts on a timer, a program that keeps you "online" overnight, an input repeater for gathering. The line catches *benign-sounding* tools too. If anything pressed keys or moved the mouse for you, this is the most likely reason, and the hardest to argue against.

**Real-money trading (the 1,106).** Selling gold, gear, or carries for real currency — or buying from such sellers. The Auction Hall's 5% tax and 30-listing cap (see the [Trading & Market Guide](/guides/trading-market-guide)) partly exist to make RMT harder to launder. Reports in this bucket often dispute the call: players say they only traded with friends/family, or that a gift was misread as a sale.

**Code of conduct (the 65).** A small category — harassment, slurs, or exploiting chat. It rarely surprises the person banned, but it is enforced separately from cheating.

> **What the numbers do NOT tell you:** whether *your* ban was correct. The list is an aggregate; it does not name accounts. The only way to learn your own reason is the official support ticket (Section 4).

## 3. False-Ban Reports — What Players Actually Say

Steam discussions and reviews contain a cluster of "I did nothing wrong" threads. Treat them as real *reports* — and read them critically as *facts*:

- **A handheld console flagged as a bot.** The thread "[Wrongfully accused as a Bot](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3767850/discussions/0/586182635679706563/) describes a player banned for "botting" while playing manually on a **ROG Ally** — no automation installed. The claim is that anti-cheat misread the handheld's input pattern. **[Unconfirmed — single player's account of events]**
- **RMT bans where the player denies selling.** Reviews and threads include RMT bans where the player says they only traded items with friends/family or received a "gift." **[Unconfirmed — seller intent is exactly what the evidence is about]**
- **A child's account.** At least one review describes a son's account banned for botting while playing normally. **[Unconfirmed — same single-source caveat]**

The counterweight: the same pages include players noting **"~90% of the bots disappeared"** after the wave **[Unconfirmed]** — enforcement largely hit real bots. False-positive complaints are anecdotal and establish no false-ban *rate*; the developer has not published one. So do not assume bans are mostly wrong from a few threads — or that yours was right from the aggregate numbers.

If your case genuinely fits "normal setup, no automation, no real-money deals," follow the appeal path below. If not, be honest with yourself — the ticket will ask.

## 4. How to Appeal: The Official Support Ticket

Across the threads, the consistent advice — including from players who got answers — is:

1. **Open an official support ticket.** In "[I haven't done anything wrong, but my account got banned for no reason](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3767850/discussions/0/586182361974100027/), the top reply is blunt: *"Open a ticket and then you will find out what happened. Writing here won't help you."* Steam discussions are other players, not the team.
2. **Put evidence in the first message.** Character name, server, last-play date/time, and anything proving your setup was clean — screenshots of your software list, your purchase receipt, a clip of your session. Front-loading evidence shortens the back-and-forth.
3. **Use the official Discord for context.** The **Bot Hunter Program** and known-issue threads live there, and the team has responded to individual reports (see the [Connection & Server Guide](/guides/connection-server-guide)). Tickets are the enforcement channel; Discord confirms you are using the right form.

**What players report about tickets — [Unconfirmed].** The same threads describe **slow responses, tickets auto-closed without a real review, and players unable to log back into the support site to read the final reply.** Treat that as the current community baseline: the ticket is the *only* official channel, so expect patience — and a closed ticket is not necessarily final; a follow-up citing your ticket number is a reasonable next step. **[Unconfirmed — community-reported, not developer-published]**

## 5. What NOT to Do While You Appeal

These mistakes can make a reversible ban permanent:

- **Do not make a new account to keep playing.** Ban evasion is its own offense and links trivially to the old account — it turns "maybe a false positive" into "definitely circumventing enforcement."
- **Do not spam tickets or tag staff.** Players report the team deprioritizes noise **[Unconfirmed]**; one clear, evidence-backed ticket beats ten angry ones.
- **Do not re-buy the game on a second account.** Same problem as evasion, and you lose your character either way.
- **Do not post account details in Steam discussions.** Public threads attract "unban service" scammers — only the developer can unban you.

---

## 6. How to Never Need This Guide

- **No macros, scripts, or "AFK" tools** — even for gathering or overnight farming. The 3,821-account category is the largest, and "everyone does it" is not a defense the anti-cheat recognizes.
- **No real-money trades** — and be visible about legitimate gifts. A gold gift that looks like a sale can read as RMT; the [Trading & Market Guide](/guides/trading-market-guide) covers safer in-game transfer routes.
- **Keep the account to yourself.** Sharing muddies the ownership evidence an appeal needs, and a friend running a macro on your account bans *you*.
- **Report botters instead of joining them.** The **Bot Hunter Program** accepts Discord-ticket reports; reporting keeps the economy healthy and your account clearly on the right side.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: Will SpiritVale tell me why I was banned?**
A: Through the official support ticket — the channel the community points to for learning the actual reason. **[Unconfirmed]**

**Q: Are SpiritVale bans permanent?**
A: The August list characterizes the macro/unauthorized-program removals as permanent. Community members describe a leniency ladder for first offenses (about 7 days, then 14, then permanent for repeat botting; about a week for a first macro offense) — **[Unconfirmed]** player descriptions, not developer policy.

**Q: How many accounts did the developer ban?**
A: In the August 17, 2026 enforcement update: **3,821** for unauthorized programs/macros, **1,106** for RMT, and **65** for code-of-conduct violations.

**Q: My friend bought gold and I got banned — can that happen?**
A: Receiving RMT-sourced gold can look like participation. If a legitimate trade was misread, explain it in the ticket — include the friend's character name and trade date. **[Unconfirmed — outcome varies by case]**

**Q: Does the Bot Hunter Program report me if I play on a handheld?**
A: No — that program reports *other* players. The ROG Ally claim concerns automated anti-cheat, not player reports. **[Unconfirmed]**
