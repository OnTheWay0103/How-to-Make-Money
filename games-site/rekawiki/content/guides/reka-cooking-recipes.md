Cooking in REKA is a three-slot puzzle: put exactly three ingredients into the oven, fuel it with wood, and a valid combination becomes a dish. The oven sits in the centre of your cottage and unlocks once the starting house is restored. This page lists every recipe we could verify across two independent recipe tables, plus what each dish sells for.

## How the oven actually works

The operation is simple, but the order matters:

1. **Fuel the back.** Open the compartment at the rear of the oven and add wood. Critical Hits and NineWiki both warn about this; a loaded recipe will not cook without fuel.
2. **Interact with the front.** Upcomer describes clicking ingredients from your inventory into the three slots.
3. **Use exactly three ingredients.** Two or four will not start a recipe.
4. **Wait for the timer.** A valid combination turns into a bowl and begins cooking; finished food appears on the shelf or the right-hand side.
5. **Collect or decorate.** You can take the dish, queue another, or place it on a table as decoration. 2UpSkill notes that fresh food can show visible steam.

Invalid combinations simply do nothing. That is the discovery mechanic: experiment, and when a combination is valid it is recorded in the recipe book, which Critical Hits places in the **final tab of your journal**. Once a recipe is known, you can repeat it without guessing.

One naming note: some guides call the station a **stove**, others an **oven**. They describe the same central fixture. The store page does not name it, so either word is fine in search, but the location is consistent across sources.

## Where the ingredients come from

This is the part that decides whether cooking is profitable. The ingredient pool splits cleanly into foraged goods and merchant goods.

| Ingredient | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Berries / raspberries | Forage | Bushes across the map; also used to feed deer |
| Mushrooms (edible) | Forage and merchant | The merchant stock is the reliable supply |
| Grain | Forage | Used for porridge; not the same item as flour |
| Honey / honeycomb | Forage | Sweetener for porridge, pancakes and cakes |
| Eggs | `[Unconfirmed]` | Used in several recipes; the sources we checked do not document a reliable farm |
| Flour | Merchant | Sold beside the covered cart in each town |
| Salt | Merchant | Same merchant; also the base of Black Salt |
| Cabbage, pumpkin, garlic | Forage / quest unlock | Garlic is tied to the scarecrow favour in the Leshy questline |

The practical consequence: **flour and salt are the bottleneck**. You can forage the rest on a normal walk, but every bread, pierogi and cake consumes merchant stock. Our [flour guide](/guides/reka-how-to-get-flour) covers that economy in detail.

The witchcraft recipes are the highest-value use of a slot, but they are not food. NineWiki is explicit that Black Salt cannot be fed to villagers and that Mild Poison poisons them instead of earning a reward. Treat both as crafting inputs, not order fulfilment.

## What villagers actually want

Villager requests are the main reason to cook at all. Cozy Gaming Panda describes rewards as **coins, ingredients or furniture**, which means an order can pay you in something other than cash. That is usually better than selling the same dish for 6 to 12 coins.

Two rules matter when you are choosing what to cook for an order:

- **Porridge is neutral.** NineWiki notes that plain porridge is neither sweet nor savoury, so it needs a second dish to match a villager's preference.
- **Jam is only half a small sweet meal.** Two jams are needed to make one small sweet meal, so a single jar is rarely enough on its own.

The sources do not publish a full list of villager preferences or the exact payout per request. Anything claiming a fixed "30 coins per order" or "60 coins for a perfect match" is `[Unconfirmed]` on this site unless a 1.0-era source appears.

## Is cooking profitable?

Sometimes, and not automatically. Rutab's cooking guide makes a point that surprises new players: **raw ingredients can outsell the finished dish**. The example given is that three bags of flour may be worth more than the bread you bake from them.

So the decision tree is:

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| A villager has an active request | Cook the requested dish; the reward is usually better than the sale price |
| No active request, and flour is scarce | Sell or keep the raw ingredient |
| You have surplus foraged goods (grain, mushrooms, pumpkin) | Cook and sell; the merchant cost is zero |
| You need Black Salt for witchcraft | Spend salt on 3-salt batches, not on soup |

That table is editorial judgement built from the sourced prices, not a hidden game formula. The numeric sell values are sourced; the priority order is ours.

## Common cooking questions

**Why did nothing happen when I added ingredients?**
The combination is invalid. Upcomer notes that invalid combinations simply do nothing, and only valid ones start cooking.

**Why is the oven not heating?**
Check the rear fuel compartment. Critical Hits and NineWiki both say wood goes in the back, and the recipe will wait until the oven has fuel.

**Can I cook more than one dish at a time?**
You can queue dishes. The first cooks, and the next bowl enters the oven when it is free. Critical Hits describes this queue behaviour.

**Do recipes disappear if I forget them?**
No. A valid combination is recorded in the recipe journal tab once discovered, so you can repeat it later without re-guessing.

**Are there more than 24 recipes?**
The two tables we cross-checked list 24, and we have not found an official complete list. Treat the table above as the verified set, not a claim that nothing else exists: additional combinations remain `[Unconfirmed]`.

## A practical cooking routine

1. Buy flour and salt **every** time you pass a town merchant; they are the only reliable stock.
2. Forage grain, berries, mushrooms and honey on the way to quest markers.
3. Keep three ingredient types in stock so you can fill any villager request without a shopping trip.
4. Cook Black Salt only when you actually need witchcraft supplies; it consumes three salt and cannot be eaten.
5. Before selling a dish, check whether a villager is asking for it.
