Flour in REKA comes from the **town merchant**, the NPC standing beside a covered cart, not from a mill. Two independent guides describe flour, salt and mushrooms as merchant stock, while grain is something you forage in the wild. That single distinction prevents the most common early cooking mistake: hunting for a flour node that does not exist.

## The short answer

| Question | Answer | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Where do I buy flour? | From the merchant beside the covered cart in each town | Verified by two guides |
| Can I make flour from grain? | No documented milling or crafting step | No source describes one; any mill claim is `[Unconfirmed]` |
| Where do I get grain? | Forage it while exploring | Verified |
| What else does the merchant sell? | Salt and mushrooms | Verified |
| Does stock refill? | Not documented | `[Unconfirmed]` |

If you only remember one line: **buy flour, forage grain**.

## Who the merchant is and where to find them

Critical Hits describes the seller as standing next to a **covered cart** in every town you visit. Rutab's Russian-language cooking guide independently describes the same NPC in the same position and lists the same three goods: flour, salt and mushrooms.

That consistency matters because the merchant is easy to walk past. The cart is not a shop building with a sign; it is a wagon in the town area. If you arrive in a new settlement and immediately chase the quest marker, you can finish the whole visit without noticing that the one reliable flour source was behind you.

A practical habit: on every town visit, find the cart first, buy flour and salt, then do the quests. The walk is short and the stock is the backbone of every bread, pierogi and cake recipe.

## Flour and grain are not the same item

This is the confusion that produces most bad advice. The two items look related and are used in similar-sounding dishes, but they are separate:

- **Grain** is foraged. It goes into porridge: three grain for plain porridge, or combinations with berries and honeycomb.
- **Flour** is bought. It goes into bread, garlic bread, pancakes, cakes and pierogies.
- **No source describes converting one into the other.** Rutab explicitly notes that the article describes no ingredient-crafting method at all.

Community threads sometimes assume a mill exists because other life sims have one. That assumption is not supported by the guides we checked, and the game's own store page separates "forage wild flora" from "cook traditional recipes" without mentioning processing chains. Until an official 1.0 source documents milling, this site treats it as `[Unconfirmed]` rather than fact.

### What grain is actually for

Grain is not a dead-end item even without milling. It is the base of the cheapest reliable food in the game:

| Dish | Grain used | Other ingredients | Sells for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Porridge | 3 | None | 8 |
| Porridge with Fruit | 2 | 1 raspberry | 8 |
| Porridge with Honey | 2 | 1 honeycomb | 8 |
| Porridge with Fruit and Honey | 1 | 1 raspberry + 1 honeycomb | 12 |
| Mushroom Stuffed Pumpkin | 1 | 1 pumpkin + 1 mushroom | 6 |
| Cabbage Stuffed Pumpkin | 1 | 1 pumpkin + 1 cabbage | 6 |

It also has a quest use: the optional graveyard-keeper favour in the forest spirit area asks for **one fruity porridge and three normal porridges**, which is a pure grain sink. Details are on our [Leshy trial page](/guides/reka-leshy-trial).

## Every recipe that consumes flour

Because flour is the scarce input, it helps to know exactly where it goes. These are the verified flour recipes:

| Dish | Flour | Other ingredients | Sells for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bread | 3, **or 2 + 1 salt** | Salt optional | 8 |
| Garlic Bread | 2 | 1 garlic | 6 |
| Pancake | 1 | 1 raspberry + 1 honeycomb | 12 |
| Honey Cake | 1 | 1 egg + 1 honeycomb | 6 |
| Berry Cake | 1 | 1 egg + 1 raspberry | 6 |
| Pumpkin Cake | 1 | 1 egg + 1 pumpkin | 6 |
| Pierogies (pumpkin / mushroom / cabbage) | 1 | 1 salt + 1 vegetable | 6 |

The efficiency standouts are obvious once the table is laid out:

- **Bread with salt** uses two flour instead of three for the same 8-coin sale value. If salt is in stock, the salted version is the better use of flour.
- **Pancake** uses a single flour and sells for 12, the highest value in the flour family. It needs a raspberry and a honeycomb, both foraged.
- **Pierogies** use one flour but also one salt and one vegetable, and sell for only 6. They are request food, not profit food.

## How to budget flour without a spreadsheet

Flour has no documented respawn timer, so the safe assumption is that every bag is a one-time purchase. That leads to a simple budgeting rule:

1. **Count the flour recipes you owe.** If a villager wants bread, reserve three flour (or two plus salt).
2. **Do not spend the last flour on low-value dishes.** Pierogies at 6 coins are a poor use when a pancake at 12 is available.
3. **Buy salt with every flour purchase.** Salt converts three-flour bread into two-flour bread and enables pierogies.
4. **Check the raw sale price before baking.** Rutab notes that selling three bags of flour can beat selling the bread made from them.

That last point is not a trick; it is a warning about opportunity cost. Cooking adds value only when the dish is worth more than its inputs, and the flour supply is limited by merchant visits rather than by foraging time.

One more budgeting detail: salt is not just a bread filler. It is the base of Black Salt (three salt), it enables pierogies, and it turns a two-flour bread into a valid recipe. Because the same cart sells both, the efficient purchase is always flour plus salt in the same trip. Buying flour alone and then discovering a pierogi request is the most common way to force a second town run.

## What the merchant does not solve

Buying flour does not remove the need to forage. Three ingredient families still come from the map:

- **Berries**, for jam, pancakes, fruit porridge and cakes.
- **Mushrooms**, for soups, deviled eggs, pierogies and stuffed pumpkin.
- **Honey**, for pancakes, cakes and honey porridge.

So the real loop is hybrid: buy the two goods you cannot gather (flour, salt), gather the three you can (berries, mushrooms, honey), and use grain as the cheap filler. The full recipe list is on our [cooking recipes page](/guides/reka-cooking-recipes).

## Common flour questions

**Can I farm flour or grow wheat?**
No farming or milling system is described in the sources we checked. That does not prove one is absent, but it means any "wheat farm" guide is `[Unconfirmed]`.

**Is flour respawning at the merchant?**
Not documented. Plan as if each visit has finite stock and buy ahead of quests.

**Why can't I cook bread with two flour?**
You can: two flour plus one salt. Three flour also works. Two flour alone is not a valid combination.

**Does bread need salt?**
No. Three flour is a valid bread recipe. Salt is the substitute that saves one flour.

**Where do eggs come from?**
The recipe table uses eggs in cakes, scrambled eggs and deviled eggs, but the sources we checked do not document a reliable egg source. That stays `[Unconfirmed]` rather than guessed.

**Is flour used in witchcraft?**
No verified witchcraft recipe uses flour. Black Salt is three salt, and Mild Poison is three fly agaric mushrooms.
