REKA is a combat-free Slavic-folklore life sim: you play Baba Jaga's apprentice, rebuild a walking chicken-legged cottage, forage, cook, tame animals and run errands for villagers. Your first two hours are mostly about settling the house, learning the food loop and avoiding three mistakes that quietly cost you progress. This guide covers exactly that.

## What kind of game you are actually playing

The Steam page is unusually clear about the pitch. REKA is a "cozy, witchy world inspired by Slavic folklore", and the advertised pillars are building without limits, "Cozy, Combat-Free Gameplay", a "Rich Slavic Atmosphere", "Woodland Crafts & Cooking" and "Animal Companionship". It is listed as **single-player**, with Steam Cloud and Family Sharing.

That matters for how you plan a session. There is no combat rotation to learn and no fail state waiting to punish you. The pressure comes from **resource routing** instead: which ingredients you can buy, which you must forage, and whether the walk to a quest marker is worth the trip. If you play it like an action game and sprint at every marker, you will spend a lot of time walking back.

The other structural fact is that 1.0 launched on **26 October 2026**, after Early Access began on **12 September 2024**. Guides written during Early Access may describe older UI or older quest flow. Where a detail changed, this page follows the 1.0 store page or labels the older community claim as `[Unconfirmed]`.

## The first ten minutes: the mirror you must not skip

Character creation is not the last word on your appearance. After you finish the creator, you enter a foggy transition area with a **large mirror**. According to 2UpSkill's beginner guide, this is your **last chance** to change how the witch looks before the game locks the appearance in.

Walk past it and you are committed until you find the craftable magic mirror later. That is not a disaster, but it is a detour. Use the starting mirror, check the face, hair and outfit under the scene lighting, and only then walk on. The exact list of options the starting creator exposes is not documented on the store page, so treat any "you can change X later" claim as `[Unconfirmed]` unless it comes from the in-game menu.

The other thing to do before leaving the opening area is **look at the ground**. Loot is not a side activity in REKA; it is one of your main sources of furniture, clothes and coins. Ruined houses, broken wagons, abandoned carts and graveyards are the places 2UpSkill tells players to check, and sacks and hidden chests can contain money and furniture.

## Settle the house before you go exploring

Your first real milestone is getting the cottage into a usable state. Critical Hits' cooking guide puts it plainly: after restoring the starting house, the **oven in the centre of the house** becomes usable. That oven is the centre of the entire food economy, so treat "restore the house" as the end of the tutorial and the start of the actual game.

Once the oven works, the loop is:

1. **Forage** berries, mushrooms, grain and honey while you travel.
2. **Buy** flour, salt and mushrooms from the merchant beside the covered cart in each town.
3. **Cook** exactly three ingredients at the oven.
4. **Use** the dish for a villager request, sell it for coins, or place it as decoration.

Step 2 is the one new players miss. Flour, salt and mushrooms are merchant goods, not forage. If you are hunting for a flour node in the woods, you are looking for something the guides do not describe. The full breakdown is on our [flour and grain guide](/guides/reka-how-to-get-flour).

## Stop trusting the map

Only the opening area around Baba Jaga is fixed. 2UpSkill describes the rest of the world as **procedurally generated**: terrain, landmarks and points of interest move between runs, so a guide that says "the mirror is southwest of the N" is describing one seed, not your map.

The practical rule is to learn **what a location type gives you**, not where a specific object sits:

| Location type | What to check it for |
|---|---|
| Ruined houses and sheds | Furniture, clothes, chests, occasionally a quest item |
| Broken wagons and carts | Coins, building materials, loose loot |
| Graveyards | Coins and furniture; a graveyard keeper also gives optional quests |
| Berry bushes and mushroom patches | Cooking ingredients for the three-slot oven |
| Animal traps | A rescued animal you can release or bring home |

This is also why "follow the marker" is not always the fastest route. If a ruin is on the way, clear it. The detour is usually shorter than a dedicated loot run later.

## Money in the first hours: requests, not grinding

There is no combat loot explosion to fund you. Early coins come from three places:

- **Villager requests.** NPCs ask for specific dishes or small favours and pay in coins, ingredients or furniture. 2UpSkill notes that an early pumpkin soup request rewards a **new window design**, which is a building blueprint rather than cash.
- **Selling cooked food.** NineWiki's recipe table lists sell values of **6 to 12 coins** per dish, with pancakes and the three-ingredient fruit-and-honey porridge at the top end.
- **Selling raw ingredients.** Rutab's cooking guide warns that raw goods can beat the cooked version on price — three bags of flour may sell for more than the bread you make from them.

That last point creates a real decision. If a villager is paying for bread, cook the bread. If nobody is asking, check the raw sale price before burning fuel and time. Do not assume "cooked" automatically means "worth more".

## Get a bed, and use the day/night choice

You do **not** start with a bed. 2UpSkill says the bed comes from an early food-related fetch quest or, occasionally, from chests in ruined landmarks. Place it in the cottage as soon as you have one.

Sleeping lets you choose to wake during the **day or night**. The guide is explicit that this mainly changes lighting and atmosphere rather than quest progression. So do not treat it as a time-management exploit; treat it as a comfort and screenshot setting. If you are hunting a specific NPC schedule, the sources we checked do not document one, and that stays `[Unconfirmed]`.

## Don't rush the trees

Wood looks like the obvious first upgrade, and that is the trap. Free tree cutting is gated behind the **Trial of the Forest Spirit**, the Leshy questline in the second area. Before that, the sustainable early building materials come from scavenging ruins and loose debris, not from clearing a forest.

Rutab's walkthrough describes the gate clearly: after the Big Larch step, "wood can be gathered freely". Going earlier mostly wastes time on a resource the game is not ready to give you. The full quest is on our [Leshy trial walkthrough](/guides/reka-leshy-trial).

## A sane first-session order

If you want one route to follow, use this:

| Step | Action | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Finish the creator, then use the misty-area mirror | Last free appearance change |
| 2 | Loot the opening ruins and wagons | Furniture, coins, early clothes |
| 3 | Restore the house and light the oven | Unlocks cooking, requests and selling |
| 4 | Meet the town merchant | Flour, salt and mushrooms are buyable |
| 5 | Learn to cook three-ingredient dishes | Requests and income |
| 6 | Progress the early story in Kopnik | Recipe books, merchant's wagon wheel, Trial of the Chicken |
| 7 | Start the forest spirit trial | Unlocks free wood and the next map |
| 8 | Only then build ambitiously | Materials and blueprints are in place |

The Kopnik step comes from 2UpSkill's cottage guide, which lists the opening tasks as recipe books, a merchant's wagon wheel and the Trial of the Chicken before the recall talisman becomes available. That sequence is also what unlocks convenient travel, covered in our [cottage moving guide](/guides/reka-cottage-moving).

## Five mistakes that cost the most time

1. **Skipping the starting mirror.** You will spend a later session hunting a magic mirror instead.
2. **Looking for flour in the wild.** Buy it from the town merchant; the guides describe no milling step.
3. **Cutting trees before the Leshy trial.** Free wood is quest-gated.
4. **Cooking without fuel.** The oven needs wood in the back compartment; a prepared recipe will sit there.
5. **Treating maps as fixed.** Check location types, not coordinates.

## Quick FAQ

**Is REKA multiplayer?**
No. The Steam page lists single-player only and does not mention co-op. See the [FAQ page](/guides/reka-faq) for the full platform answer.

**Can I fail?**
The store page advertises combat-free play, and none of the guides we checked describe a death or fail loop. What you can lose is time.

**Should I follow an Early Access guide?**
Only for systems that clearly still exist, such as the oven and the merchant. Quest flow and UI changed between Early Access and 1.0; prefer 1.0-era sources.

**What should I do with my first 500 coins?**
The sources do not publish a vendor price list, so there is no defensible "best purchase" ranking. Spend on recipes and furniture you need for requests, and keep a buffer for flour and salt.
