# How to Survive Grain Rot Expedition Mode — Permadeath, Peter the Heater and Frost Damage

Grain Rot's **1.1 update on September 24, 2026** added **Expedition Mode**, and it is not a difficulty slider. It is a second progression track with its own outpost, currency, death rule and reward pool. If you load it expecting Story Mode with tougher enemies, the first cold snap will end your run. [Official]

This guide covers what the mode actually changes, how Peter the Heater works, what survives a failed run, and the patch changes since launch that alter how you should play it.

## What Expedition Mode actually is

Expedition is a **hardcore mode with permadeath**. The official announcement is blunt: "if your run fails, it's game over." That is the whole premise. Every choice about depth, loot and furniture is made under a rule that Story Mode does not have. [Official]

It also starts somewhere else. Expedition begins at a **new Expedition Outpost on the dark side of the planet**, where "the heat you're used to is replaced by bitter cold, and frost damage is in place of scorch damage." You are not descending from the sunny surface you know from Story Mode. [Official]

Three structural differences follow. **The outpost is separate** — Expedition progression does not run through Story Mode room upgrades, so you are building a different character with a different currency. **The environment is the antagonist** — cold is not a status you cure with a potion, it is the clock the mode is built around. And **failure is final for the run** — a failed expedition ends, and you return to the Expedition Outpost to assess the damage. [Official]

The mode is selected from the game's mode menu. Patch 1.13 fixed gamepad navigation "when selecting game mode," which confirms a mode selector exists but does not indicate any unlock gate. [Official]

## Peter the Heater is the only timer that matters

Peter the Heater is the furnace that keeps the Expedition Outpost warm, and the official description makes the loop explicit:

1. **Feed Peter scrap and specific constructs** — furniture counts as fuel.
2. **Meet the quota** and the furnace keeps you warm.
3. **Let the heat drop** and you take "punishing debuffs."
4. **Let it reach zero** and your run is over. [Official]

That is a resource system disguised as a base upgrade. Furniture is no longer only decoration or a tactical object for the Deep Layers; in Expedition it is also the thing standing between you and a run-ending debuff spiral. The announcement frames the tension directly: furniture becomes "a precious resource here, whether you burn it to stay alive or save it for tactical use down in the Deep Layers." [Official]

The practical consequence is that **you should not treat every construct as fuel**. Burn the low-value pieces first and keep the pieces that change a fight. [Editorial]

One detail is easy to misread. The official wording says frost damage replaces scorch damage, which sounds like a second health bar. An independent review of the 1.1 build reads the cold pressure as running through the Peter the Heater meter instead — miss the quota, take the debuffs, hit zero, lose the run. Treat the separate-frost-health reading as unconfirmed and watch your heat meter rather than a hypothetical frost bar. The same review reports there is no sunlight on the dark side to heal or clear Rot, which fits the mode's design: the safety valve you rely on in Story Mode is gone. [Community]

## Permadeath changes the risk math

Story Mode's core rule is that extracted loot is banked when you return alive; a failed run costs the haul, not the account. Expedition keeps the account but raises the stakes on the run itself. The official announcement calls it "an unforgiving twist" and says "every choice matters and every risk is yours to take." [Official]

In practice, the bail triggers from the [Extraction Guide](/guides/extraction-guide) still apply, but the cost of ignoring them is higher. A bad room is no longer a setback you can grind out on the next descent — it is the end of the expedition, so bias toward leaving earlier. [Editorial]

Co-op does not remove the rule. The store page confirms online co-op for up to four players, and the mode is described as playing much like Story Mode at its core. Whether one player's death ends the expedition for the whole party is not stated, so treat that as **[Unconfirmed]** and agree on a bail call before you descend. [Official]

## Progression: Gumballs, not rooms

Expedition replaces room upgrades with **Gumballs**. You collect them and spend them on **permanent stat upgrades** that persist across expeditions. That is the roguelite layer: a failed run still advances the account, even though it ends the current expedition. [Official]

The difficulty also ratchets. "Each successful run raises Peter's scrap requirements and scales up the dungeon's difficulty," so the mode gets harder as you get stronger. This is not a bug or a soft cap; it is the intended curve. The deeper you go, the more the furnace demands and the more the dungeon answers. [Official]

When a run ends, you return to the Expedition Outpost and see your **Deep Layers score**, which is the mode's progress readout. The announcement says a dedicated end-of-run screen showing unlocked cosmetics, run results and peak level was still in development at launch. If your build does not show that screen yet, that is why. [Official]

A sensible spending order for early Gumballs is survivability first, then heat efficiency, then damage. The mode's failure states are cold and attrition before they are damage checks. That ordering is our assessment, not a developer-published tier list. [Editorial]

## Rewards: 25 patterns and the Rainbow Ward

Expedition is the only source of **25 new pattern textures and pattern colours**. They are bound to your profile and carry back to the main Outpost, so the rewards are cosmetic but permanent and usable in Story Mode. [Official]

Update 1.1 also added the **Rainbow Ward**, a new biome whose walls include community-submitted art from the Inner Child Art Submissions. It is part of the 1.1 content drop, not an Expedition-only space. [Official]

The 1.1 announcement also teased a new Corrupted enemy. That enemy — the **Corrupted Builder** — arrived in **Patch 1.11** on September 28, along with item-slot hooks on the expedition elevator. [Official]

## What the 1.11–1.13 patches changed

Several fixes in the fast post-launch patch cadence matter to Expedition players specifically.

| Patch | Date | What changed for Expedition |
|---|---|---|
| 1.11 | 2026-09-28 | Added the Corrupted Builder; added item-slot hooks to the expedition elevator; added unstuck logic for chairs, beds and spark possession; fixed the furnace heat bonus being calculated incorrectly after loading a save; improved elevator stability during descent |
| 1.12 | 2026-09-29 | Added a timestamp to the dungeon run and current-run info in the build menu; fixed a crash in the Grain Rot infection system |
| 1.13 | 2026-09-30 | Fixed gamepad navigation in mode select and save deletion; made all room-upgrade cosmetics available in the Expedition wardrobe; small vessels can now change colour patterns; improved melee hitbox orientation |

The 1.11 furnace fix is the one to care about most. A heat bonus that recalculates incorrectly after a load can quietly change your quota math, which in a permadeath mode is the difference between a stable run and a lost one. Patch 1.13's wardrobe change also answers a common early complaint: Expedition now has access to cosmetics unlocked through room upgrades instead of forcing you to unlock the same look twice. [Official]

## A practical first-expedition plan

1. **Treat the first run as a scouting run.** Learn the Expedition Outpost layout, find where scrap and furniture spawn, and do not push depth for its own sake.
2. **Set a fuel reserve before you leave.** Decide how much scrap stays at the furnace and do not spend it on a single deep room.
3. **Burn cheap furniture first.** Keep the constructs that change a fight; burn the ones that only look good.
4. **Bank Gumballs into survival stats first.** Heat efficiency and damage reduction make later expeditions stable; raw damage does not save you from a cold snap.
5. **Leave earlier than you would in Story Mode**, and check your patch level — the 1.11 heat-bonus and elevator fixes are not cosmetic.

## What is still unconfirmed

The developer has not published exact scrap quotas, heat-drop debuff values, the difficulty multiplier per successful run, or the full end-of-run screen. Any guide that quotes those numbers is guessing. It is also not confirmed whether Expedition has its own achievement set or whether every co-op player must meet the heat quota individually. [Editorial]

## FAQ

**Is Expedition Mode permadeath?**
Yes. The announcement states that if your run fails, it is game over. [Official]

**Does Expedition replace my Story Mode save?**
No. It has a separate outpost and progression currency. [Official]

**How do I keep Peter the Heater running?**
Feed it scrap and specific constructs. Meeting the quota keeps the outpost warm; letting heat reach zero ends the run. [Official]

**What do Gumballs do?**
They buy permanent stat upgrades that persist across expeditions, replacing Story Mode's room-upgrade progression. [Official]

**Is the Rainbow Ward exclusive to Expedition?**
No. It was added as a new biome in Update 1.1, not as an Expedition-only space. [Official]
