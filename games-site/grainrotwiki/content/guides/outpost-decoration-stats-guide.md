# Grain Rot Outpost Decoration & Room Stats — How Decorating Your Base Raises Your Stats

You have extracted a dozen times. The Outpost has furniture in it. Your stats have not moved in three runs. Does decorating actually do anything, or is it clutter you have been hauling scrap for?

As of September 13, 2026, the player consensus is that **decorating is the progression system**: objects placed in an Outpost room raise that room's level, and each room's level raises a specific stat. It is why a crew that keeps wiping still gets stronger.

> **Scope note:** this page covers the **decoration and room-stat system** — what raises a room, what undoes it, and how to plan one. For upgrade order across constructs, stats, rooms and survivors, see the [Outpost Guide](/guides/outpost-upgrade-guide).

---

## The Short Answer

| Question | Reported answer |
|----------|-----------------|
| Does decorating do anything? | Yes — stat bonuses are reported "tied to how you decorate your home." **[Unconfirmed]** |
| How do rooms work? | Each room has its own level, and each level raises a specific stat. **[Unconfirmed]** |
| Do I lose it when I die? | No — players report stat progress survives a failed run. |
| Where are the stat tables? | Not published. No official breakdown of rooms, stats, costs or level caps exists as of September 13, 2026. |

Everything above except "it is persistent progression" is **player-reported** — the community reverse-engineered the system's shape; the studio has never published the numbers.

---

## What Is Actually Confirmed

1. **The Outpost has stat upgrades and interactive furniture.** Official unlock copy: "Unlock interactive furniture, stat upgrades, rescued survivors, and stranger ways to survive the next descent."
2. **Outpost strength gates depth.** The same copy says "The stronger the outpost becomes, the deeper you can afford to go."
3. **It is permanent.** "Persistent Outpost rebuilding and progression" is a listed feature, and 1.0 added a **second Outpost floor**.
4. **Rooms are showcased, not hidden.** The official demo announcement ran a **"Best Build"** contest asking players to "Share 2 to 3 screenshots of your best Outpost room."

What is **not** official: which rooms exist, which stat each raises, how many objects a level needs, the level cap, or whether two objects help or hurt each other.

---

## How the Decoration Loop Is Reported to Work

1. **You extract loot** and convert it into Outpost objects — the same scavenged furniture that funds the rest of the hub.
2. **You place objects in a room.** The room is the unit, not the object: it is the *room* that carries a level.
3. **The room levels up as it fills**, and each level raises that room's stat. One reviewer calls the result "genius"; another says the game "does get super easy when you level your rooms enough."
4. **Failure does not erase it.** "While we get set back on the floors, the game always feels like its progressing as we update our house by decorating," one September review puts it — and another states that "you don't lose progress on your stats if you die." Items can be lost; room levels are not. That is the design argument for Grain Rot over its neighbours: a failed run still fed the house.

*Cosmetics — vessel hats and paints — are a separate track with no gameplay effect. The Outpost furniture this page covers is what players report as carrying the bonuses.*

---

## Why Every Outpost Looks the Same

The most detailed review of the system, from **August 11, 2026**, describes what happens when bonuses become the point:

> "Since you always want the highest bonuses, you end up building the same pattern everywhere. Ten different rooms make no sense when the layout is basically forced."

The same reviewer counted the supply problem: **"roughly 40 objects"** of decor against **"12 rooms with unique effects to fill"** — some apparently never meant as decor. A September reviewer arrived from the other direction: "All we do is spam rooms with the same item." That is a complaint about the strategy, not proof it fails.

The practical read: **copy your best room.** The system rewards repetition far more than variety, and until the mapping is worked out, duplicating a room that already produces a visible bonus is the only reliable play available. A third player asked the developer to "add room item lim[it]," suggesting the stacking is real enough to want rules. Decorating purely for looks is legitimate — nothing in the reports says an attractive room is penalised, only that appearance and bonus strength are different goals.

## Three Things That Quietly Undo a Room

- **Physics and NPCs.** The most serious report: "Nothing you build stays put." Objects are not rooted and NPCs "spawn inside them or walk straight through them, throwing everything around," which "destroys both your room bonuses and the objects themselves." It happens at the Outpost, worst in the entrance and basement rooms. If a bonus vanished untouched, look here first. **[Unconfirmed]**
- **A co-op partner editing a room you levelled.** One September 7, 2026 review reports a room going from Level 2 to "level 1" on the next load after their partner placed an item in it. A single report, possibly a bug — but it argues for one player owning each room. **[Unconfirmed]**
- **Unpredictable object interactions.** Players cannot see whether an object helps or hurts before committing: "good luck figuring out how to setup a room without seeing if stuff Syncs for a boost or ruins each other." Change one thing at a time. **[Unconfirmed]**

## A Working Method for a Room

Since no numbers exist, work empirically:

1. **Target the stat you actually need.** Early crews need survivability; deeper crews need whatever gets them out. Confirm which rooms affect which stat *in your own game* before committing scrap.
2. **Fill one room at a time to a visible change.** Spreading objects across five rooms teaches you nothing.
3. **Record the level before and after.** Baseline, add, check, write it down — your notes are the only accurate table in existence.
4. **Replicate the pattern once it works.** The one place copying yourself beats creativity.
5. **Re-check after a session break.** Verify room levels when you next load in and repair anything scattered.

## What Nobody Has Confirmed

- **No official room list** — the 12-room figure is a player count.
- **No stat mapping** or item-to-bonus table, not even an agreed community one.
- **No level caps or costs.** Any guide quoting exact costs is guessing.
- **No confirmation that stacking identical objects is intended** rather than emergent.
- **No ruling on co-op ownership** — both the [Outpost Guide](/guides/outpost-upgrade-guide) and the [Multiplayer Guide](/guides/multiplayer-matchmaking-guide) flag whether a guest's room work counts toward their own Outpost as unverified.

**Will this change?** The August 12, 2026 roadmap schedules a **September "Deep Layers Update"** (one new biome, several new enemies) and an **October "Halloween Update"** that opens the **third Outpost floor**. A new floor means new rooms and a restarted mapping — check the [Patch Notes & Update History](/guides/patch-notes-guide) before trusting any room list, including this page.

## FAQ

**Q: Does decorating in Grain Rot actually give you stats?**
Players report yes — bonuses "tied to how you decorate your home," with each room carrying a level and a stat. Not officially documented. **[Unconfirmed]**

**Q: What stat does each room give?**
Nobody has published a verified mapping — no guide claiming a complete table has shown its evidence.

**Q: Do I lose room levels if the run wipes?**
No. Players report stat progression persists through death and failed runs. Items can be lost; the house keeps what you put into it.

**Q: Why did my room level drop?**
Two reported causes — objects knocked around by physics and NPCs at the Outpost, and a co-op partner placing an item. Check both before assuming a bug. **[Unconfirmed]**
