# Grain Rot Controller Guide — Gamepad Support & Settings

Grain Rot has **full gamepad support at 1.0** — a confirmed feature of the August 7, 2026 release. The demo was keyboard-first, which was one of its most-cited gaps; the launch announcement explicitly fixed it. This guide covers what's confirmed, what's expected, and what remains unverified about playing Grain Rot with a controller. **Important note up front: exact control mappings have not been published as of this writing — this page will be updated with the official layout as verified data emerges.**

## Confirmed: Gamepad Support Is New in 1.0

The publisher's launch announcement confirmed **gamepad support** as a 1.0 addition, listed explicitly as fixing "a notable demo gap" — the demo shipped keyboard-first. What that means:

If you skipped the demo because it was keyboard-only — or bounced off it — the 1.0 build is the version to try with your gamepad.

## Controller vs Keyboard & Mouse: The Honest Comparison

Grain Rot is not a twitch-shooter, which changes the usual equation:

**Where keyboard & mouse wins:**
- **Precision possession.** Flicking the camera to a distant vessel or target with a mouse is faster than thumbstick tracking. In combat against multiple Corrupted, that precision matters.
- **Speed of action.** Quick-access binds for tools and constructs beat d-pad cycling.
- **Familiarity.** If you learned the demo with a mouse, your muscle memory transfers.

**Where controller wins:**
- **Sound-based gameplay favors deliberate movement.** The Corrupted detect you through *sound and movement* — this game rewards slow, careful input over twitchy aim. A thumbstick's gradual input curve is arguably a better fit for stealth pacing than mouse flicks. See the [Stealth & Sound Guide](/guides/stealth-sound-guide) for why movement discipline matters more than aim.
- **Comfort for long sessions.** Extraction runs have downtime (looting, smashing furniture, elevator rides) — controllers are the better ergonomic fit for hours-long sessions.
- **Couch co-op.** If your crew gathers in one room, controllers are the only option.
- **The game is co-op-centric.** Team games with built-in voice distortion and emotes are friendlier from a couch position; precision isn't the skill that carries a four-person extraction.

**Verdict:** both are fully viable. Mouse-and-keyboard is the precision pick for solo deep runs and combat-heavy moments; controller is the comfort pick for stealth pacing, long sessions, and co-op. The confirmed 1.0 difficulty rebalancing for solo and small groups also suggests the game won't demand mouse-level precision from solo players.
