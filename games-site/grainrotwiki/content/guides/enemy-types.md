# Enemy Types Guide — The Corrupted, Detection & Combat Strategies

Grain Rot's enemies are the **Corrupted** — wooden vessels twisted by the Rot into hostile entities roaming the underground ruins. This guide covers everything confirmed about them and the combat strategy that follows, with an honest note up front: **the specific enemy roster is not officially documented before launch**, and this guide was rewritten to remove unverified enemy names and stats.

## The Confirmed Enemy Model

Everything about Grain Rot combat flows from one official sentence: the Deep Layers are "filled with Corrupted vessels, **reacting to sound, movement, and each other**."

That means:

- **Sound** — noise is the primary trigger. Loud actions (sprinting, smashing, fighting) draw attention.
- **Movement** — at close range, the Corrupted track motion.
- **Each other** — enemies react to each other's reactions. When one is alarmed, others investigate the same disturbance.

There is **no confirmed roster, HP list, or attack-pattern table** from the developers. The demo contains 9 enemy types — enough variety to practice the model, but not officially named or documented.

## What This Model Means for Combat

### 1. Every Fight Is a Recruitment Event

Because enemies react to each other, combat is expensive in the worst way: killing one enemy often pulls its friends. The meta-skill of Grain Rot is **managing which fights happen at all**.

### 2. The Corrupted Are Like You

They are vessels, like your own body. They can be dropkicked, they burn, and they exist on the same physics engine — official copy says "dropkick enemies" and the game is built on "physics-driven chaos." Environmental play (knocking enemies into hazards, using furniture) is a confirmed possibility.

### 3. Threat Is Depth-Dependent

"The deeper you go, the worse the Rot becomes" — deeper floors mean more and worse Corrupted. Enemy pressure is the price of loot.

## Combat Strategy Before the Roster Is Known

### The Decision Framework

Before any engagement, run the three questions:

1. **Do I need to fight?** — No → leave. The best fight is the one you never have.
2. **Can I win quietly?** — Fast, contained fights avoid recruitment events.
3. **Can I afford the noise?** — Assume every loud fight signals the floor. If you can't survive what arrives, you can't afford the fight.

### Tactics That Work on the Confirmed Model

- **Isolate targets.** Fight enemies away from corridors and adjacent rooms; every neighboring room is a potential reinforcement source.
- **Kill fast or don't commit.** Drawn-out fights are noise generators. Burst damage beats sustained brawling when possible.
- **Use the environment.** Physics-driven combat means furniture, dropkicks, and hazards are tools — not just scenery.
- **Bait and slip.** Since enemies path toward disturbances, a thrown object or Grinder noise can pull patrols away from your route.
- **Run is always an option.** Sprinting away and breaking line of sight is a legitimate strategy — the Corrupted track, but they aren't omnipresent.

## Enemy "Sound Cues" — What's Real

Older versions of this guide listed specific sound cues for named enemies (creaking wood, scuttling, groaning). The underlying principle is confirmed — the Corrupted react to sound, so **your ears are your early warning system**, and headphones are genuinely valuable. The specific cue-to-enemy mapping is unverified until the roster is documented.

## Co-op Combat Notes

- **Focus fire**: a team that damages one target kills it before it becomes a recruitment event.
- **One player calls the shots**: designate who decides fight vs. flee, so the team doesn't split on a sound call.
- **The Keeper never fights**: the loot carrier stays out of engagements. If a fight starts, the Keeper moves toward extraction, not toward the noise.
- **Sacrifice is strategic**: a vessel is replaceable ("death is just a change of scenery") — a Spark can possess a new vessel. Trading one body to save the haul is the game's intended math.
