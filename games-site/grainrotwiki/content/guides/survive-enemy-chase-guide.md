# Grain Rot Enemy Chase Guide — How to Survive When the Corrupted Won't Stop Chasing

You smashed one wardrobe too many, something in the dark turned its head, and now it is sprinting at you — and it does not stop. Negative Steam reviews of Grain Rot's 1.0 release keep returning the same complaint from different players: the Corrupted "never lose aggro," are faster than you, and always seem to know exactly where you are. If running is the escape, players ask, why is it the hardest thing to do?

This guide separates what is confirmed about the Corrupted from what players report, then gives you an escape playbook that works **whether or not** an enemy ever drops a chase.

> **Honesty note:** The developer's official wording confirms how the Corrupted *detect* you. It does **not** document whether they *stop* chasing. Everything below about permanent aggro is player-reported from 1.0 reviews and is marked **[Unconfirmed]**. Treat "they will not let go" as your working assumption and you will rarely be caught out.

---

## Why a Chase Feels Endless

The confirmed enemy model is one sentence from the official store description: the Deep Layers are "filled with Corrupted vessels, **reacting to sound, movement, and each other**." The [All Enemies Guide](/guides/all-enemies-guide) spells out what that means: enemies escalate each other, so a fight in one room can pull reinforcements from the rooms around it. Every fight is a **recruitment event**.

What players add on top of that model — and what no official source confirms or denies — is that once a Corrupted has fully committed to chasing you, it behaves as if it has **no leash**: it does not give up and wander back, it outpaces your walk (and often your sprint), and it keeps tracking you around corners as if it always knows your heading.

**[Unconfirmed — player reports from 1.0 Steam reviews; the developers have not published AI leash/aggro-drop behavior.]** If you have not felt this yourself, you will the first time a plague carrier decides you are dinner.

### Alarm vs. Committed Chase — the distinction that matters

The wiki's [Stealth & Sound Guide](/guides/stealth-sound-guide) and [All Enemies Guide](/guides/all-enemies-guide) correctly say that breaking line of sight and sprinting past works — and it does, **during an alarm**, when a Corrupted has merely *noticed a disturbance* and walks over to investigate.

A **committed chase** is different: the enemy has seen you directly or taken damage and is actively pursuing. Players report that hiding behind a wall at this stage just gives it time to cut you off. Practical rule: **escape during the alarm window, because you cannot count on escaping once the chase begins.**

---

## The Real Rule: Don't Let the Chase Start

The confirmed model makes one thing unambiguous: the cheapest escape is the one you never need. Before you enter any room, run the [All Enemies Guide](/guides/all-enemies-guide)'s three-question check — *Do I need to fight? Can I win quietly? Can I afford the noise?* If the answer to the last question is no, do not make the noise.

Chase-proof habits:

1. **Smash deliberately.** Furniture is your income, but smashing is loud and echoes. Only break what you need, in rooms you have confirmed are clear.
2. **Keep an exit in mind.** Before you commit to a deep loot room, know which way you will leave and whether the elevator route is clear (see the [Extraction Guide](/guides/extraction-guide)).
3. **Let the environment work.** Fans, pits, explosive objects, and traps kill Corrupted without a fight. A room you can turn into a hazard is a room you do not have to flee.
4. **If you are carrying the haul, you do not fight.** The [Co-op Guide](/guides/co-op-guide)'s "Keeper" rule applies solo too: once you have real loot, your job is extraction, not heroics.

## When You Are Spotted (Not Yet Committed)

You have a few seconds while the enemy is still in "what was that?" mode:

1. **Go quiet immediately.** Stop sprinting, stop smashing, stop talking. Sound is what pulls the rest of the room.
2. **Break line of sight early.** Put a wall, a stack of furniture, or a corner between you and it — *then* move away. Do not run in a straight line where it can watch you the whole way.
3. **Move perpendicular or back the way you came.** Running deeper into unexplored rooms trades one pursuer for whatever is waiting in the next room.

If you break contact here, you usually do not need to kill anything.

## When You Are in a Committed Chase

Once it is actually chasing you, change your goal from "lose it" to **"get to a hard exit or make the chase not worth it."** In rough order:

### 1. Lure it into the environment, not into a fight

The Corrupted share your physics engine — official copy lists "dropkick enemies," and furniture and hazards are real tools. Back toward a pit, a spike trap, a fan, or an explosive prop and sidestep at the last moment; a lunging enemy commits and takes the hazard for you. This kills the threat without a recruitment-event fight.

### 2. The elevator is the only guaranteed separation

If you have the elevator fee, the elevator is a hard exit: get in, pay, leave. A **pre-activated elevator** — paid before you explore — is the game's panic room. Community beginner guides (gamerblurb, 2UpSkill) are consistent on this: secure the fee early, and a chase near the shaft ends with you gone and your loot banked. The [Extraction Guide](/guides/extraction-guide) calls this the "elevator mindset."

### 3. Spend the vessel, not the run

Your vessel "splinters, collapses, and catches fire," and when it breaks your **Spark escapes** — you stay in the run and can possess another body "before you fade out completely." A committed chase is a good moment to use that: if a replacement vessel is nearby, let the chasing enemy break your current body on your terms, and flee as a Spark. Community Spark-survival guides report the Spark is smaller, faster, and harder for standard threats to detect, which makes it a genuine escape state — **[Unconfirmed, community-documented]**. The [Best Vessels Guide](/guides/best-vessels-guide) covers why a fallback body should always be part of your plan.

### 4. Do not run toward your team or into the unknown

Every room you enter mid-chase is a potential second pursuer, and running to your friends hands the Corrupted a recruitment event with your whole squad as the prize. If you must lead it somewhere, lead it to a hazard or the elevator — not to your teammates.

### 5. In co-op, someone creates space

If the squad is together, one player with a **durable vessel** can hold the corridor and absorb the chase while the Keeper extracts — "your job is to create space." One body traded to save the haul is the game's intended math. The [Co-op Guide](/guides/co-op-guide) covers the trade-offs of sacrificing a body to buy the squad extraction time.

## After the Chase: Reposition or Leave

If you escape, do not go back to looting as if nothing happened. The [Stealth & Sound Guide](/guides/stealth-sound-guide) is blunt: once an enemy has alerted the floor, that area's stealth is over. Wait the alert out somewhere quiet, move to a different section, or extract and come back fresh — a run you survive with less loot beats a run you lose with more.

## Quick FAQ

**Q: Do the Corrupted ever give up chasing?**
A: Players report that once one fully commits, it does not drop the chase and keeps tracking you. **[Unconfirmed — no official AI documentation]** Plan around that.

**Q: So is running useless?**
A: Running *alone* will not end a committed chase, but running **to a hard exit** — a hazard, the elevator, or a replacement vessel — will. Use the sprint to reach one of those, not to outdistance the enemy.

**Q: Doesn't breaking line of sight work?**
A: It works during an early alarm, before the enemy commits. Do not rely on it once the chase is on.

**Q: My vessel is about to break. What now?**
A: If a replacement body is nearby, let it break on your terms and escape as a Spark, then repossess. If no replacement is near, make for the elevator — a broken vessel with no fallback is how runs end.
