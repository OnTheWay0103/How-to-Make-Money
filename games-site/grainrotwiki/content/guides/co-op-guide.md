# Co-op Guide — Multiplayer Tips & Team Strategies

Grain Rot is designed around cooperative chaos. You descend into the ruins with friends, and the game explicitly builds its humor and horror around what happens when a crew falls apart — "Block them in a monster-infested corridor or get stuck in the crossfire." This guide covers everything confirmed about co-op, and the team strategy that follows from it.

## Confirmed Multiplayer Facts

- **Online co-op is confirmed** (Steam page lists Online Co-Op alongside Single-player).
- The official description says you "descend with your friends," and press materials describe teams of "you and up to four friends" — most coverage treats 4-player co-op as the party size, though an exact maximum isn't listed on the Steam page. See our [player count guide](/guides/player-count-guide) for the details.
- **Physics-driven chaos is an official feature** — vessels are fragile wooden bodies, and the physics engine is a character in the game.
- **Dropkicking is confirmed** — you can dropkick enemies, or "'accidentally' launch your ally's Spark out of their vessel."
- **Griefing is a feature**: "Block them in a monster-infested corridor or get stuck in the crossfire" is official copy. "Rage-bait your friends with cursed emotes" is a listed feature.
- **Loot is shared at the team level**: "If everyone breaks, your loot is lost" — the whole run's loot is at stake, not just yours.
- **The Outpost is a team project** — everything the crew hauls back rebuilds the shared Outpost.
- **If you break, you're not out**: your Spark escapes and can possess another vessel in the same run, "before you fade out completely."

## Why Teams Survive Better

The confirmed mechanics stack in favor of a coordinated crew:

1. **Numbers divide attention.** The Corrupted react to sound, movement, and each other. Four vessels make four noise sources — but also four sets of eyes and four bodies that can draw fire.
2. **One survivor saves the haul.** Because loot is lost only if *everyone* breaks, a team can designate an extraction runner: if the run goes sideways, one player heads for the elevator while others hold or sacrifice. Their Spark lives; the loot banks.
3. **Possession keeps the team alive.** A broken teammate isn't dead — they're hunting for a new vessel. A team that protects its Sparks keeps its player count high.
4. **The Outpost compounds teamwork.** The same extraction bankrolls everyone's unlocks.

## Team Strategy Without Fake Roles

Earlier versions of this guide recommended named roles tied to specific vessels (Vanguard, Scout, Striker, Hauler). Those vessel names were unverified, and the role system itself isn't officially documented. Here's the honest version — roles that follow from confirmed mechanics:

### The Confirmed-Role Framework

| Role | What It Actually Is | Why It Works |
|------|--------------------|--------------|
| **The Lead** | The player who enters rooms first and calls the shots | Someone has to eat the first encounter; better it's deliberate |
| **The Scout** | The fast player who checks the route and finds the elevator | Elevator location is survival-critical on every floor |
| **The Grinder** | The player who converts junk to gold efficiently | Gold is compact; converting mid-run maximizes value per run |
| **The Keeper** | The player who stays back with the best loot | If everyone else breaks, the Keeper extracts and banks the haul |

Players can rotate these on the fly — the game has no role-lock, and flexible teams outperform rigid ones.

## Communication That Actually Matters

- **Call out the elevator route.** Knowing where extraction is, and communicating it, saves more runs than anything else.
- **Announce your corruption.** Official copy confirms voices distort and bodies warp as the Rot builds — your team should know who's degrading before it becomes a liability.
- **Warn before loud actions.** Smashing, sprinting, and combat draw the Corrupted. A "smashing now" call prevents the whole crew from being ambushed.
- **Coordinate the extraction.** If the team decides to bail, everyone bails — "just one more room" is how hauls die.

## Co-op Chaos: The Feature, Not the Bug

Grain Rot deliberately lets teammates hurt each other: dropkicks can launch allies' Sparks out of their vessels, and you can block someone in a corridor full of Corrupted. A few ground rules for keeping chaos fun:

1. **Establish the griefing threshold before the run.** Some crews love chaos; some want clean extraction attempts. Say which one you are.
2. **Never grief the Keeper.** The player carrying the best loot is everyone's lifeline.
3. **Dropkicks save and kill.** Knocking an ally out of a grab or a fire hazard is a rescue; doing it at the extraction elevator is a rage-bait highlight. Know which you're aiming for.
4. **Cursed emotes are a communication tool.** Official copy: "Emotes become…worse" with corruption. A degraded emote is a signal everyone understands.

## Co-op Loadout Notes (Honest)

Specific construct names are unverified before launch, but the confirmed framework gives clear priorities:

- **Every player should carry a tool that helps the team**, not just themselves.
- **At least one player should be built for durability** — someone must hold doors while the rest work.
- **At least one player should be fast** — extraction runs live or die on speed.
- **Fire is everyone's problem** — vessels "catch fire"; a crew that respects fire hazards keeps its bodies.

## Common Co-op Mistakes

1. **Everyone splitting up** — divided teams die divided. The Corrupted react to noise; four solo players make four noise sources.
2. **No extraction plan** — agree before descending how deep you're going and when you bail.
3. **Greedy holdout** — one player's "one more room" can cost the whole haul.
4. **Ignoring the Keeper** — protect the loot carrier; they are the run's bank.
5. **Chaos at the wrong time** — griefing is fun until it wipes the team. Save the antics for extraction-safe moments.
