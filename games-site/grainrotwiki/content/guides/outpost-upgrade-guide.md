# Outpost Guide — Base Building, Progression & What to Upgrade First

Grain Rot is an extraction game, which means your "base" works differently than in an open-world survival game. You don't chop wood on the surface, raise walls, or defend a camp from nightly raids. Your base is the **Sanctuary Outpost** — a persistent hub you return to between runs, where everything you extracted is spent on permanent upgrades. It's a crumbling high-rise run by two brothers, **Biggie and Murch**, and it's where the game's meta-progression lives. This guide explains what base building actually means in Grain Rot, what is confirmed about Outpost progression, and gives honest upgrade priorities.

## The First Thing to Know: No Open-World Building

Correction to a common misconception: Grain Rot's confirmed feature set includes **no free-form base construction**. The official feature list says:

- "Persistent Outpost rebuilding and progression"

There is no confirmed mechanic for placing walls, doors, moats, or turrets in the world. The Steam page's "Building / Base Building" tags refer to the Sanctuary Outpost hub and the game's building-themed crafting (the "tactical carpentry with interactive furniture" feature). Any guide describing open-world base construction — wall placement, perimeter defense, base raids — is describing a different game.

## What the Outpost Actually Is (Confirmed)

From the official Steam page:

- The Outpost is **persistent** — it exists between runs and grows as you play.
- "Everything you haul back rebuilds your Outpost."
- It improves "your chances for the next run" — Outpost progress directly powers your in-run capabilities.
- It unlocks **interactive furniture** — the "tactical carpentry" loop extends to your hub.
- It provides **stat upgrades** — permanent character or vessel improvements.
- It houses **rescued survivors** — "rescue survivors trapped in the Deep Layers" is an official feature, and the Steam copy confirms survivors are an Outpost unlock.
- The 1.0 release adds a **second Outpost floor** and an **enchantment and repair machine** (confirmed additions from the publisher's launch announcement).

**Specific room names, upgrade costs, and unlock trees are not officially documented as of this writing.** Any guide with precise gold/wood costs and level tables is fabricating data.

## How Base Building Fits the Game Loop

The Outpost sits at the end of every extraction loop:

```
Descend → Scavenge → Fight or flee → Extract → Rebuild the Outpost → Go deeper
```

- **Every run funds the base.** Loot you extract converts into Outpost progression. Loot you lose to a full team wipe is gone.
- **The base makes future runs easier.** Upgrades, furniture, and survivors compound: better stats, better tools, more options.
- **The base is shared in co-op.** The Outpost is a team project — everyone's extractions contribute (exact sharing rules are unverified).

## What the Demo Confirmed

The demo (free on Steam since June 12, 2026) included **upgradeable Outpost rooms** — confirmed by the publisher's demo announcement. So the loop was already testable: extract loot, return to the Outpost, spend it on room upgrades, and see the effect on your next run. **Demo progression did not carry over to the full game** — the demo was a standalone build, so everyone started the Sanctuary Outpost fresh at 1.0.

## The Confirmed Upgrade Categories

### 1. Constructs (Weapons, Tools & Utilities)

The demo contains **20+ unlockable constructs** — the game's arsenal. Construct unlocks are the most direct power: new options for combat, resource conversion, and escape.

**Priority logic:** constructs solve in-run problems. Unlock broadly early (options), then deep (power).

### 2. Stat Upgrades

Confirmed to exist. Stat upgrades are permanent, run-agnostic power — they improve every descent from now on.

**Priority logic:** stats compound. Anything that improves survival (durability, speed, carry capacity) pays every single run.

### 3. Rescued Survivors

"Rescue survivors trapped in the Deep Layers" is official. Survivors are Outpost inhabitants — likely vendors or service providers (details unverified).

**Priority logic:** survivors are gated behind depth, so they're naturally mid-to-late game. Worth chasing when you can reach them.

### 4. Interactive Furniture & Rooms

"Tactical carpentry with interactive furniture" and "upgradeable Outpost rooms" are confirmed. The hub itself is a build space, not just a menu — and 1.0 adds a second floor to expand it.

**Priority logic:** room upgrades presumably gate other unlocks (constructs, stats, survivors). Expect to invest here constantly.

### 5. Cosmetics

"Questionable cosmetics" — hats, paints, and looks. Confirmed to exist, zero gameplay impact.

**Priority logic:** last. Fun, not functional.

## Honest Upgrade Priorities

Without verified costs, the confirmed mechanics still dictate a sensible order:

1. **Constructs that solve your biggest problems.** Early on, that's survivability and resource conversion.
2. **Stat upgrades.** Permanent, run-agnostic, compounding.
3. **Room upgrades.** They're the gate to everything else (per the demo's upgradeable rooms).
4. **Survivors.** Deep-layer rewards with likely service value.
5. **Cosmetics.** When the economy can afford it.

Older versions of some guides recommended specific sequences — "Workshop Lv.1 → Forge Lv.1 → Alchemy Lv.1" — based on unverified room names and costs. Ignore any such sequence until verified post-launch data exists.

## Co-op and the Outpost

The Outpost is a shared project in co-op: everyone's extractions feed the same hub (exact sharing rules unverified). Practical implications:

- **Coordinate spending.** A team that spends in sync (all funding the same unlocks) progresses faster than one that splits gold across every option.
- **Designate a plan.** Agree on the upgrade path before descending, so resources don't scatter.
- **The Keeper banks for everyone.** Loot extracted is loot shared — protect the player carrying it.

## Common Mistakes

- **Assuming demo progress carried over.** It didn't — the demo was a standalone build.
- **Spending everything on cosmetics.** Confirmed feature, but it doesn't improve runs.
- **Skipping the Outpost between runs.** Persistent progression is the whole point — a run you extract from is a run that made your team permanently stronger.
- **Wiping with loot still in your bags.** "If everyone breaks, your loot is lost" — extraction is the only way to bank progress.
