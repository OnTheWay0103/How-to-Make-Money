# How to Clear the Rot Debuff in Grain Rot — Why Am I Vomiting Green Liquid?

You are two floors down in the ruins, your vessel suddenly lurches, and a stream of green liquid comes out of your wooden body while your health ticks down. Search the community hubs and you will find this exact moment everywhere — the Rot debuff is one of the most asked-about mechanics in Grain Rot, and the game explains almost none of it. This guide collects what the community has worked out: what the Rot debuff is, how you catch it, what it does to you, how to clear it, and the one symbol you should read before you ride the elevator down.

One honest caveat before the details: the developers have not published an official breakdown of the Rot meter, and this wiki's [Game Mechanics & Systems Guide](/guides/game-mechanics-systems-guide) flags "specific corruption thresholds, cleansing items, and whether corruption can be reversed in-run" as unverified. What follows is the best current picture from player coverage and Steam Community discussion. Every specific number below is community-reported and marked **[Unconfirmed]** unless it is tied to official copy.

## What Is Confirmed About the Rot

Two things come straight from the official Steam page:

- **The Rot worsens with depth.** "The deeper you go, the worse the Rot becomes."
- **The Rot corrupts bodies.** "Bodies warp. Voices distort. Emotes become…worse." The Corrupted enemies you fight are vessels the Rot has already twisted into hostility.

The general principle is confirmed: the Rot is the game's soft timer, and it scales with how deep you go and how long you stay. Everything past this point — the visible meter, the stack count, the clearing method — is community-reported.

## What the Community Reports About the Debuff

Fan guide coverage of the released build (GamerBlurb's "GRAIN ROT: How to Get Rid of Grain Rot" and FinalBoss's Rot debuff guide) describes the debuff like this:

- **How you catch it.** Rot builds up "while you are exploring certain contaminated sections of the Deep Layers" — areas reported to be marked by puddles of dark green slime. Corrupted enemies are another source: "Allowing one to get too close can inflict Rot as well." **[Unconfirmed]**
- **What it does before the cap.** Even below the cap, the status "can already interfere with your vision and movement." **[Unconfirmed]**
- **What happens at the cap.** Community coverage reports that once the counter reaches 100, "your movement is slowed slightly, and your Vessel can randomly begin vomiting green liquid while also losing health." **[Unconfirmed]** This is the "why am I puking green" moment most players ask about — it is the Rot debuff at its worst.
- **How you clear it.** "Stand outside in the Sun" resets the accumulated Rot. One guide adds that you can "Clear it at the Outpost by using the Sun or the available cleanse function" before boarding the elevator again. **[Unconfirmed]**

**The sunlight tradeoff.** Standing in the Sun "also damages your Vessel. You are essentially exchanging some health to get rid of the Rot" — it is "not a free recovery mechanic." **[Unconfirmed]**

That tradeoff is why clearing Rot is a decision rather than a no-brainer. If you are low on health, a sunlight cleanse can drop you into a dangerous extraction window; if you are about to descend into more contaminated rooms, carrying a full meter in is arguably worse than the health cost of clearing it.

## How to Clear the Rot Debuff (Step by Step)

Putting the community-reported methods together, the practical routine is:

1. **Clear between runs, not mid-run.** Community coverage recommends clearing Rot "before committing to another Deep Layers run," so you do not carry a full meter into a fresh descent. **[Unconfirmed]**
2. **Use sunlight at the Outpost.** Return to the surface and stand in the Sun until the meter resets, accepting the vessel health cost. **[Unconfirmed]**
3. **Look for a cleanse or cure point at the Outpost.** One community guide reports an "available cleanse function" or "cure point" at the Outpost as an alternative to sunlight. Treat this as **[Unconfirmed]** — the exact location and cost are not documented in official materials.
4. **Never board the elevator with a high meter.** A fresh descent is the worst time to carry Rot, because the Deep Layers immediately re-contaminate you. (Editorial recommendation, not official guidance.)

If you are mid-run with a high meter and no safe way to the surface, the honest answer is that the community sources do not document a reliable in-run cleanse. Your options are to play cautiously back toward extraction or accept the debuff and shorten the run.

## How to Avoid Catching Rot in the First Place

Prevention beats cleansing, and most of it is depth and route discipline:

- **Respect the green slime.** Community reports tie Rot buildup to contaminated rooms with dark green slime puddles. Learn to recognize those rooms and limit how long you stand in them. **[Unconfirmed]**
- **Keep distance from the Corrupted.** Since close contact is reported to inflict Rot, fight at range when you can, and lean on the confirmed sound model to avoid contact altogether. **[Unconfirmed]**
- **Watch your depth.** This one is officially confirmed: "the deeper you go, the worse the Rot becomes." Shallower runs accumulate Rot slower; deep runs pay better but demand a faster exit.
- **Fire may clear the puddles.** One Steam Community discussion reports that the slime puddles "can be destroyed with fire" — smashing an ignited lantern or using a fire-based vessel some players call the Tinderbox/Firebug. This is secondhand and **[Unconfirmed]**, and the vessel name is community shorthand, not an official name. If you test it, do it in a cleared room so the fire does not become its own problem.

## The Skull Symbol Debate (Read This Before You Ride the Elevator)

Before each descent you may see symbol modifiers near the elevator. There is an active debate in the Steam Community thread "Symbols meaning" about what the **skull** symbol means:

- **Interpretation A:** the skull marks rooms "filled with Grain Rot" — the green-slime contaminated rooms described above.
- **Interpretation B:** a player in that thread argues this is wrong and that "Grain Rot appears regardless" of the modifier, believing instead that skulls signal **harder enemies** — they report a "double skull" run where enemies "basically one shot" the squad.

Both readings are community speculation, and the thread is a genuine dispute rather than a settled fact. The only safe takeaway: a skull symbol is a warning, and the two strongest candidate meanings are "extra Rot hazard" or "deadlier enemies." Treat either as a reason to be more conservative on that descent. [Unconfirmed]

## Why the Rot Debuff Matters for Your Runs

The Rot debuff is the extraction clock wearing a mask. Officially, the Rot is what makes depth dangerous; in community-reported practice, a high meter actively hurts you — slowed movement, vomiting, health loss — and makes the extraction itself riskier. The bottom line:

- Clear Rot at the Outpost between runs; accept the sunlight health cost or find the reported cleanse point.
- Do not enter a fresh descent with a full meter.
- Treat skull symbols as a warning, whichever reading is right.
- When in doubt, extract with something rather than die with everything — the [Extraction Guide](/guides/extraction-guide) covers that discipline.
