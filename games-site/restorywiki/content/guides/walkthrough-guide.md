## Overview

ReStory's story is **branching, non-linear and customer-driven**. There is no chapter list and no quest tracker — the plot unfolds through **walk-in customers, online orders, and license progression**. The exact number of endings has not been officially confirmed (待验证), and reviewers note the story is "anything but chill": mystery, espionage, betrayal, petty theft and conspiracy run beneath the cozy surface.

This walkthrough covers the confirmed campaign structure, the milestone sequence, every documented choice moment, and a concrete first-week plan. Story-critical spoilers are flagged.

---

## The Premise (No Spoilers)

You inherit a rundown repair shop from a previous master who **left without warning — and appears to have been in some kind of trouble**. You immediately face rent pressure from a pushy landlord and must build up the business. Beneath the cozy loop, the neighborhood is threatened by **real-estate developers planning a massive shopping center**, and the cast — a weird cop, rough locals, a conspiracy-chasing shut-in — orbit something darker.

## How the Story Actually Progresses

| Driver | How It Advances the Story |
|--------|---------------------------|
| **Walk-in customers** | Recurring regulars appear and reappear; their personal arcs advance with each repair |
| **Online orders** | Courier-delivered jobs introduce new devices and plot threads |
| **Licenses** | Buying certifications unlocks story beats that stay locked until you own them |
| **Choice moments** | Dialogue decisions permanently shape character outcomes and the ending |

**Golden rule:** if the story seems stalled, buy a license or keep accepting walk-ins — the game gates plot behind both.

## The Campaign Structure (Community-Mapped)

The developers have not published an official act structure; the following is a reconstruction from launch-week reviews and player reports (待验证 on specifics):

**Act 1 — First Days.** Meet the landlord, pay rent, complete Hashimoto's Pokia (your first repair), set up internet, take your first online orders. End: first license purchase, which unlocks new device types and the next wave of story.

**Act 2 — The Neighborhood.** The regular cast fills in — the post-it-note upstairs neighbor, the hapless romantic, the weird cop, the maid-cafe teenager. Choice moments arrive: the landlord's post-it number, the student's confession, the gangster's phone. License count grows toward 5 (**Golden Partner**).

**Act 3 — The Pressure.** The real-estate shopping-center plot tightens; protection fees and the cop's warnings escalate. The most expensive license becomes affordable — and with it, **"Are you serious? This is Absurd."** (spoiler-light: the story's twist lands here, deliberately left unspoiled by reviewers).

**Act 4 — The Competition & Endings.** The campaign builds toward a **major repairs competition**. Win it to claim the one license that cannot be bought — the final step for **Platinum Partner** — and the story resolves into one of multiple endings shaped by your choices.

## Confirmed Choice Moments (Launch Build)

| Choice Moment | The Decision | Known Outcomes |
|---------------|--------------|----------------|
| **The landlord's post-it number** | Hand over the phone number found inside his phone, or keep it | One of the game's most-cited branching moments |
| **The former gangster's phone** | Report disturbing content found inside, or stay quiet | Headline branching moment from press materials |
| **The lovestruck student** | Help him confess to his crush, or let it be | Affects the student's story; no "perfect" answer per community reports |
| **The kid's request** | Complete the request and refuse payment | Refusing earns the **Origami** achievement — the courier returns with origami |
| **Privacy vs. truth** | Protect a character's privacy or expose what you found | Reported to ripple into the late game and endings |

**Key insight from reviewers:** some choices feel low-stakes in the moment (Gamekult calls the writing "anecdotal"), but Sirus Gaming notes the choices genuinely affect "what happens to characters by the conclusion." Keep choices consistent with the ending you want.

## First-Week Walkthrough (Concrete Plan)

| Day | Goals | Story Notes |
|:---:|-------|-------------|
| **1** | Complete Hashimoto's Pokia (first fix), clear the desk, meet the landlord | First Fix! + Clean Job achievements; rent clock starts |
| **2** | Set up internet, accept first online orders, install the tip jar | Master of the Internet; courier joins the cast |
| **3** | Batch simple repairs; save for the first license | Buy any license → **Official Partner** + new device types |
| **4** | Buy first license; meet the upstairs neighbor's Atari joystick request | Pac-Man tournament job; cast expands |
| **5** | Grind clean-only jobs at night; buy brush upgrades | Cash flow stabilizes |

| **7** | Keep orders to what you can finish; pay bills on time | Avoid the **Bankrupt** achievement's overdue warning (unless you want it) |

## Common Walkthrough Questions

**Can I miss story content?**
Yes and no — the campaign continues regardless of individual choices, but some character outcomes and endings are locked by your decisions. Replays are the intended way to see everything.

**Do I have to win the assembly competition to finish the story?**
The final license is competition-only, and buying every license is required for **Platinum Partner** and the completionist achievements. The story's climax also centers on the competition.

**What happens after the credits?**
The shop continues — online orders, achievements and the 365-day milestone keep the loop going. See the [How Long to Beat & 100% Guide](/guides/game-length-guide).

**Are choices telegraphed?**
Not always — reviewers note some choices are "unclear and can unintentionally derail storylines." Save before a moment you suspect is a fork, and treat the walkthrough tables above as your map.
