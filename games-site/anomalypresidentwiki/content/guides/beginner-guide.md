# Anomaly President Beginner Guide — Your First Election & First Run

**Anomaly President** is a single-player presidential-campaign roguelike by **Phew Phew Games** (the studio behind
Anomaly Agent). It launched into **Early Access on August 3, 2026**. You play an underdog candidate trying to unseat a
ruthless president who "pushes the world toward disaster" — and you cannot do it with speeches alone.

The game runs on two loops that feed each other:

- **By day** you campaign: canvass for votes, give speeches, manage your image, recruit **Fluffies**, and build your
  **campaign bus**.
- **By night** you fight: side-scrolling roguelike runs against the criminal gangs controlled by the current president,
  ending in bosses whose **Anomaly Powers** you can steal.

This guide explains the loop and what to prioritize in your first few hours. For the daytime half in depth, see the
[campaign management guide](/guides/campaign-management); for the night half, see the [night combat guide](/guides/night-combat).

## The Core Loop, Step by Step

A full cycle looks roughly like this:

1. **Morning — manage the campaign.** Check your Fluffies, run daytime activities (canvassing, speeches, image
   management, timing minigames), spend campaign resources, and improve your bus.
2. **Night — run a mission.** Deploy from the overworld into a combat run. Each run hands you a "clean slate of
   abilities and power-ups" [Community], so every night is a fresh experiment.
3. **Return — bank your gains.** Materials, money, and meta-progression persist. Boss kills grant permanent Anomaly
   Powers [Community].
4. **Repeat.** Your campaign grows stronger, which opens better options for the next run — and the next election cycle
   approaches.

The two halves are deliberately tied: reviewers describe combat victories feeding "directly into your campaign's
meta-progression" and daytime minigames acting as a "palate cleanser between your high-octane roguelike runs"
[CultureCombine, Community].

## What to Prioritize First

Early Access reviewers and the developer's own pillars (Action, Base Building, Exploration) point to the same
priorities. In rough order:

1. **Keep your Fluffies alive.** Fluffies are your workforce — assigned to campaign, equipment-upgrade, or combat
   departments. If you neglect them, they "might abandon you or worse, lose their lives" [Official]. A dead or
   deserted Fluffy is a permanent loss, so make welfare a daily habit. See the [Fluffies guide](/guides/fluffy-guide).
2. **Unlock bus rooms that pay for themselves.** Reviewer-confirmed Early Access rooms include a bedroom, a TV studio
   for media appearances, an upgrade ("blacksmith-style") room, and a vote-canvassing room [GameBrief, Community].
   Rooms change your abilities and playstyle each run, so invest early in ones that produce votes or revenue. See the
   [campaign bus guide](/guides/campaign-bus-guide).
3. **Learn one weapon, then branch.** Pick a weapon and learn its timing before you experiment with two-weapon
   loadouts. The developer's stated launch arsenal runs from Sword and Nunchucks to Claws, Kusarigama, Staff, Hammer
   and Octo-Arms [Road to EA #2, Community]. See the [weapons guide](/guides/weapons-guide).
4. **Do the day activities even when they feel slow.** Votes and campaign revenue are the "day" currency that makes
   night missions winnable. Skipping the campaign half starves your meta-progression.
5. **Expect to lose runs.** Dying resets the run, but you keep materials, money, and campaign progress. That is the
   roguelike contract — losing a night is not losing the campaign.

## Day Activities You Will Meet

Reviews describe the daytime side as speeches, image management, recruiting, and a **card-based dialogue** system plus
**timing minigames** [GameBrief, Community]. The most-reported minigame is a **dance/rhythm timing minigame** for public
appearances, where you hit "perfect" inputs to build a combo streak [Community]. The demo already showed the shape of
these activities, and developer posts describe **Dance, Gift, and Marketing** bus rooms that unlock more of them
[Road to EA #2, Community]. Exact minigame lists and reward tables are [Unconfirmed] and may change during Early Access.

## Night Runs You Will Meet

At night you fight **criminal gangs controlled by the current president** [Official]. Reviewer descriptions of Early
Access enemies include "red-eyed enemies with clubs" and waves of visually similar mobs, plus a rhino-like boss seen in
screenshots [GameBrief, Community]. Combat is movement-focused: weaving between attacks, repositioning, and chaining
abilities. Elemental damage types — **Ice, Fire, Electric** — are confirmed in the Early Access build, though one
reviewer found Ice "vastly overpowered" at launch [CultureCombine, Community].

## Common First-Hour Mistakes

1. **Ignoring Fluffy welfare.** Treating Fluffies as a set-and-forget resource is the fastest way to lose your team.
   One reviewer described the management layer as a "Tamagotchi on the side" that you must actually feed [GameBrief,
   Community].
2. **Rushing into hard night missions.** The store page's "three pillars" include Exploration — not every route is meant
   to be cleared on the first try. Farm the routes you can win.
3. **Hoarding instead of upgrading.** Because the campaign bus and gear upgrades persist, spend resources on rooms and
   upgrades rather than banking them for a run that death will reset.
4. **Assuming the story is finished.** The ending is not yet written and part of the map is closed in Early Access
   [Official]. If you hit a "not yet" wall, that is the EA boundary, not a bug.

## What Carries Over Between Runs

Confirmed persistent progression [Virtual Grip + GameBrief, Community]:

- **Money and materials** earned in runs and day activities.
- **Anomaly Powers** stolen from bosses — usable permanently.
- **Campaign strength** — new Fluffies, better bus rooms, expanded tools for future expeditions.
- **Unlocked abilities** from the meta-progression tree (the demo offered a "slice" of this tree).
