# Anomaly President Builds Guide — Swordmaster, Cyber Mage, or Something Between

The store page gives Anomaly President two named build fantasies and invites you to invent your own: master your skills
to become **"a swordmaster, a cyber mage, or anything in between"** [Official]. In a game where each night run hands you
a "clean slate of abilities and power-ups," a *build* is what you do with that slate before the run ends.

This guide covers the two confirmed archetypes, the element system that powers them, and how to build runs that
synergise — without pretending the Early Access meta is settled.

## The Two Confirmed Archetypes

### Swordmaster (parry-focused)

The swordmaster is the **melee-identity** build: a sword/katana user who parries enemies "at the right time" and turns
their defences against them [Official]. What makes it a *build* rather than just "using a sword":

- **Parry opens finishers.** Break an enemy's stamina (a successful parry is the fastest way) and you trigger an instant
  finisher [Respawning, Community]. A swordmaster's damage ceiling is tied to how often it can parry-finish.
- **Parries can trigger companions.** Reviewers describe successful parries activating small companions that deal
  elemental damage or complete certain combos [Respawning, Community] — so the parry is not just defence, it is your
  synergy engine.
- **The cost is timing.** The parry window is the most-criticised input in Early Access — tight and "needs tuning"
  [GameBrief, CultureCombine, Community]. Swordmaster is high-skill, high-reward right now.

For the timing work this archetype demands, see the [combat tips guide](/guides/combat-tips-guide).

### Cyber Mage (anomaly-power / element focused)

The cyber mage is the **area-damage** fantasy: deal mass damage through **anomaly powers** and elements rather than raw
melee [Official]. In practice this means:

- **Your Anomaly Power pick matters more than your weapon.** A stolen boss power can define the whole run (see the
  [Anomaly Powers guide](/guides/anomaly-powers-guide)).
- **Elements do your heavy lifting.** Ice, Fire and Electric are the confirmed damage types [CultureCombine, Community],
  and a "cyber mage" leans on them. Fire got a direct buff in patch v0.5.0.18, which increased the starting damage of the
  **Ground Flame** Anomaly Power [Official, Community] — a concrete sign that elemental powers are the mage's toolkit.
- **Weapon choice is secondary.** Pair any decent melee weapon with your element and power picks; the guide to pairing
  is the [weapons guide](/guides/weapons-guide).

## The Elements: Ice, Fire, Electric

Three elemental damage types are confirmed in the Early Access build by reviewer testing [CultureCombine, Community]:

| Element | What reviewers report |
|---------|------------------------|
| **Ice** | **Widely reported as vastly overpowered** at launch compared to Fire and Electric [CultureCombine, Community]. Freeze-style control is strong in a game where you are often outnumbered. |
| **Fire** | Underperformed at launch, then directly buffed: v0.5.0.18 increased the starting damage of the Ground Flame Anomaly Power and improved the Fire element's visuals [Official, Community]. |
| **Electric** | Confirmed in the build; less discussed than Ice in launch coverage. |

**Honest balance note:** "Ice is overpowered" is a **launch-balance observation**, not a permanent law. The developer has
already shown willingness to tune elements (the Fire buff in v0.5.0.18), so a guide written now should not promise that
Ice stays king. The [patch notes & roadmap guide](/guides/patch-notes-roadmap-guide) tracks element changes as they ship.

## What a "Run Build" Actually Looks Like

Because runs start you with a clean slate [CultureCombine, Community], a build is assembled *during* the run from three
layers:

1. **Weapon pair** — the two weapons you carry. A reach weapon (Kusarigama) plus a fast weapon, or a weapon plus your
   element, are the two sensible axes (see the [weapons guide](/guides/weapons-guide)).
2. **Anomaly Powers** — the run-defining abilities you pick up and steal. For a cyber mage these *are* the build; for a
   swordmaster they add finisher/parry payoffs.
3. **Random run abilities** — reviewers list wild examples: summoning giant blades, transforming into a sumo wrestler,
   firing ice or fire projectiles, and attacking with ballot papers [Respawning, Community]. These are the raw material
   your build is made of.

Plus a **Fluffy layer**: recruited Fluffies trigger on specific actions and can genuinely shape a build (see the
[Fluffies guide](/guides/fluffy-guide) for their battle-support role) [Respawning, Community].

## Build Advice That Survives Balance Patches

1. **Pick an identity early and commit.** A messy grab-bag of every power-up loses to a focused direction. Decide "melee
   + parry" or "element + power" within the first few rooms of a run.
2. **If you want the easiest current wins, test Ice.** It is the most-reported strong element at launch. But have a
   Fire/Electric plan ready, because balance is moving.
3. **Do not chase a "best weapon."** With per-weapon numbers unconfirmed and balance shifting, any precise ranking is
   guesswork. Choose a weapon whose *feel* you like and build around it.
4. **Parry builds need practice, not just gear.** The swordmaster ceiling is locked behind the parry timing. Spend
   deliberate practice sessions (see the [combat tips guide](/guides/combat-tips-guide)) before judging the archetype.
5. **Watch the patch notes before trusting any guide.** If a patch buffs Fire or nerfs Ice (as the roadmap suggests is
   coming), the "best element" answer changes overnight. The [patch notes & roadmap guide](/guides/patch-notes-roadmap-guide) is the place to check.
