# Anomaly President Bosses Guide — Face the President's Inner Circle

Bosses are the spine of Anomaly President's night loop. The Steam store page frames them in one sentence: the president's
**"vice president bosses"** each hold a unique Anomaly Power, and **defeating a boss lets you acquire its power**
[Official]. That turns every boss from a difficulty spike into a **permanent upgrade you can farm**.

Because the game is in Early Access and its boss roster is still growing, this guide sticks to what is actually confirmed
and labels the rest honestly.

## The Boss Layer: Vice-President Bosses

Thematically, the people you fight at the end of runs are the **current president's inner circle** — "vice president
bosses" rather than random monsters [Official]. Each one carries a named Anomaly Power, and that power is the reason to
fight them: GameBrief describes the loop as chasing "anomaly-power drops from bosses," with boss kills granting
**permanent** powers you can use immediately [Community]. See the [Anomaly Powers guide](/guides/anomaly-powers-guide)
for how acquisition works.

Two consequences follow from this structure:

1. **Bosses are a shopping list, not a wall.** Every boss you can beat is a permanent power you will own forever. If a
   district's boss is too hard, farm earlier bosses and gear up first.
2. **Some boss powers are not combat abilities.** The confirmed example — the PR chief's power of **HEART**, which makes
   people fall in love with her — is as much a political/campaign tool as a fighting one [Community]. Expect powers to
   feed the day half too.

## Confirmed Boss: The Minister of Public Relations (PR Chief)

The best-documented boss is the president's **PR chief**, also described as the **Minister of Public Relations**. She is
the **first boss of the free demo** [Official demo page, Community], carries the power of **HEART** [Store, Community],
and is effectively the game's tutorial boss for the "every vice-president has a power" idea.

What reviewers describe about the fight is light — it is the *opening* boss — so treat any specific attack-pattern advice
as [Unconfirmed]. The consistent takeaway is that she demonstrates the core boss cadence: learn the telegraphs, use the
parry on blockable attacks, dodge the rest, and finish the fight to claim her power.

## The "Dean" Boss (Named in Patch Notes)

If you are reading Steam patch notes you will see a boss called **the Dean**. The v0.5.0.11 update (August 7) changed
"attack patterns/timings for the first phase of the Dean boss fight," and v0.5.0.16 (August 14) improved the Dean fight's
finisher and phase-transition [Official patch notes, Community].

What we can confirm from those notes:

- The Dean is a **multi-phase boss** (patch notes reference a first phase and phase transitions).
- His fight was still being **balanced and polished through August**.
- **His relationship to the named vice-president bosses is [Unconfirmed]** — we have not found a source that explicitly
  ties the Dean to the Minister of Public Relations or another named inner-circle member. Treat "Dean" as a real boss in
  the current build whose identity and district are not yet documented.

## Announced Boss: Bigman

In the developer's "Road to Early Access" material, a boss called **the Bigman** was announced as **one of the strongest
henchmen of the president**, with the developers describing his fight as focused on his massive size and overwhelming
presence [CultureCombine, Community]. His animations were still work-in-progress before launch, so **whether he has
shipped into the Early Access build by September 2026 is [Unconfirmed]** — the [patch notes & roadmap guide](/guides/patch-notes-roadmap-guide) tracks his status.

## Boss Fight Mechanics That Generalise

Even with the roster in flux, several boss-fight rules are confirmed by patch notes and reviews:

- **Phases and finishers are real.** Multiple patches tuned a boss's "phase 1" timing and its finisher/phase-transition
  (v0.5.0.11, v0.5.0.16). Expect bosses to change behaviour between phases and to have a finisher moment when their
  stamina is broken.
- **Finishers interrupt bosses.** Patch v0.5.0.17 notes that **boss ranged attacks are now cancelled during finisher
  animations** [Official, Community] — a buff to the "break stamina, then finish" play.
- **Read yellow vs red.** Blockable boss attacks (yellow) are parry material; unblockable ones (red) need a dodge. The
  [combat tips guide](/guides/combat-tips-guide) covers reading these indicators.
- **Visuals get cluttered.** Reviewers note that boss fights can get visually busy, hurting readability [Respawning,
  Community] — one more reason to learn attack *sounds* and telegraphs rather than watching the whole screen.

## How to Approach Bosses in Early Access

1. **Treat every boss as a permanent power-up.** Because powers persist after the run, farm the bosses you can beat
   before pushing into harder districts.
2. **Bring the right tool.** If you are struggling with a boss's adds (minions), a crowd-control weapon like the
   Kusarigama helps (see the [weapons guide](/guides/weapons-guide)); if it is a damage check, make sure your gear is
   upgraded on the campaign bus first.
3. **Do not trust a "complete boss list."** Any article (including this one) that lists every boss in the September 2026
   build is guessing. The confirmed pieces are the vice-president structure, the Minister of Public Relations/PR chief,
   the Dean (from patch notes), and the announced-but-unconfirmed Bigman. Everything else is [Unconfirmed].
4. **Expect the roster to grow.** The September roadmap promises **new enemies** that show "the true scale of the
   president's tyranny" [Official roadmap, Community] — and new vice-presidents (and therefore new powers) are a stated
   part of the Early Access plan.
