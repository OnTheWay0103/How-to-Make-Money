# Games Like Anomaly President — What to Play Next

Anomaly President is an unusual mash-up: **presidential-campaign management by day, side-scrolling beat-'em-up
roguelike by night**. Depending on which half of the loop you love, different games will scratch the itch. This guide
starts with the obvious comparison — the same studio's previous game — then branches into genre neighbours, with every
recommendation clearly labelled as editorial.

## The Direct Comparison: Anomaly Agent

If you have not played **Anomaly Agent**, it is the first question to answer, because it is the same developer's previous
game in the **same "Anomaly Universe"** [Official].

- **Who made it:** Phew Phew Games (co-founded by Enis Kirazoğlu), the same Turkish studio behind Anomaly President.
- **What it is:** a **2D side-scrolling action platformer** with a time-twisting story — you play a special agent with
  temporal powers fighting robotic henchmen, rather than a presidential candidate fighting gangs.
- **How it did:** a surprise hit — rated **Overwhelmingly Positive** on Steam (about 95% positive across roughly 9,500
  reviews), with Turkish business press reporting more than 120,000 copies sold
  ([N24](https://n24.com.tr/sirket/enis-kirazoglunun-oyun-studyosu-phew-phew-games-buyuyor)), plus awards including Best
  Console Game and Best Audio Art at Turkey's Kristal Piksel Game Awards [Community].
- **The honest difference:** Anomaly Agent is a **linear, tighter** action game; Anomaly President is a **bigger,
  stranger roguelite** that layers campaign management, the campaign bus, Fluffies and a day/night split on top of the
  same fluid combat DNA [Community].

**Bottom line:** if the *combat feel* of Anomaly President is what you love, Anomaly Agent is the closest single
comparison and is a finished, released game. If the *campaign/bus/Fluffy management* is what you love, Anomaly Agent will
not satisfy that — it has none of those systems. You do not need to have played it to enjoy Anomaly President; they are
standalone stories in a shared universe.

## The Structural Neighbours (Editorial Picks)

These are the games most often recommended to people who like Anomaly President's structure. The pairings are editorial
comparisons, not official statements.

### If you love the campaign bus + management loop → **Cult of the Lamb**
Cult of the Lamb is the most-cited structural cousin: a roguelike action game wrapped around a **persistent base that you
build and maintain between runs**, with followers you must care for — very close to Anomaly President's bus-and-Fluffy
loop. It is a polished, finished game and the most direct "management + roguelike" comparison in the genre.

### If you love the side-scrolling action → **Skul: The Hero Slayer**
Skul is a **side-scrolling action roguelite** where you swap character "heads" to change your moveset mid-run. It shares
Anomaly President's 2D melee DNA and its run-to-run build experimentation, minus the campaign layer. It is a strong pick
if the night half is your favourite.

### If you love the "one more run" power fantasy → **Hades** or **Dead Cells**
Both are the genre's reference points for fluid action-roguelite combat with permanent meta-progression. Hades leans on a
story told through repeated runs; Dead Cells is pure 2D combat-and-build variety. Neither has the campaign-management
layer, but both deliver the "each run builds something permanent" loop that reviewers praise in Anomaly President.

### If you specifically want hack-and-slash + big synergies → **Death Must Die** / **UnderMine**
Aggregators that cluster games by play style recommend these two for Anomaly President fans: Death Must Die for its
screen-clearing build synergies and UnderMine for its relic-driven dungeon runs [Community]. They trade Anomaly
President's political satire for fantasy, but the build-arithmetic itch is similar.

## What NOT to Compare It To

Two frequent "is it like X?" questions get honest "no" answers:

- **Not a beat-'em-up like Streets of Rage.** Anomaly President's night loop is a *roguelike* — runs reset, builds are
  random, death sends you back to the bus. Pure arcade beat-'em-ups have none of that structure.
- **Not a strategy/political sim.** The campaign layer is real but light — card dialogue, timing minigames and resource
  management — not a Crusader Kings-style simulation. If you want a deep politics sim, Anomaly President will feel thin
  on the day side.
