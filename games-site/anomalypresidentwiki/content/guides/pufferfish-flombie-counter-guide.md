# How to Beat Pufferfish and Flombie in Anomaly President

Anomaly President's **v0.5.1.21** update added two enemies that ask different questions. The **Pufferfish** is a ranged problem with an unusual answer: its Posture meter, not its health bar, is the thing you are trying to empty. The **Flombie** is a pressure problem — an enemy that the patch itself treats as more annoying than lethal, but one that can turn a clean fight into a messy one.

The site's [enemies guide](/guides/enemies-guide) was written before these two shipped, so this page covers only the new pair. The tactics below are labelled: confirmed behaviour comes from the official patch announcement, while spacing and priority advice is editorial.

## What the Update Actually Confirmed

The official announcement is short, and it is worth separating what it states from what players have inferred.

| Enemy | Confirmed behaviour | What it means in practice |
|-------|---------------------|---------------------------|
| **Pufferfish** | Slow but powerful ranged attacks; has a **Posture** meter; reducing Posture to zero makes it **self-destruct** | Your win condition is Posture pressure, not raw damage |
| **Flombie** | New enemy type; not very threatening at first, but can become annoying | Low burst threat, high disruption potential; do not ignore it |
| **All enemies** | React more reliably after spotting the player; continue patrolling when the player is out of sight | Old stealth timing is less reliable; assume patrols keep moving |

That last row matters more than it looks. If you previously waited for an enemy to forget you, the updated detection behaviour makes that less dependable. The patch did not remove stealth or patrols; it made them behave more consistently. Plan around the patrol rather than around an exploit.

## Pufferfish: Attack the Posture Bar, Not the Health Bar

The Pufferfish's defining trait is its ranged attack. It is **slow but powerful**, which gives you a clear rhythm: the shot is dangerous, but the wind-up is long enough to read. The mistake is to treat that rhythm as a damage race and try to out-shoot it from range. You are usually better served closing the gap.

Its real weakness is **Posture**. Reduce that meter to zero and the Pufferfish self-destructs. The official notes do not spell out which attacks deal the most Posture damage, so the practical rule is to use your most reliable stagger tools rather than your highest raw-damage combo. In Anomaly President that generally means:

- **Parry the attacks you can read.** A successful parry is already the game's fastest way to open an enemy up, and the Pufferfish's slow timing makes it a better parry target than a fast mob.
- **Use heavy or multi-hit options when you are safe.** Posture pressure rewards consistent hits, not one big number. If a weapon's finisher or heavy attack leaves you exposed for too long, it is the wrong tool for this enemy.
- **Do not chase it across the arena.** Closing distance is useful, but chasing a retreating ranged enemy can pull you into the rest of the wave. Move through the gap between shots, not after the enemy.

### The Self-Destruct Is the Kill — Treat the Last Hit Carefully

The self-destruct is how the Pufferfish dies, but that does not automatically mean it is safe to stand next to it. **Whether the explosion damages the player is [Unconfirmed]** in the sources checked for this guide. The safe habit is to break Posture from a short distance, then move out rather than staying in contact for a final animation. If the explosion does hurt, you lose nothing by spacing it; if it does not, you lose only a fraction of a second.

This is also why you should not treat the Pufferfish as a pure damage check. If you ignore Posture and try to burn it down, you are spending time inside its preferred range while its slow, powerful shots keep coming. Break the bar and let the enemy remove itself.

## Flombie: The Annoyance Tax

The official patch notes are deliberately modest about the Flombie: it does not look very threatening at first, but it can become annoying. Community summaries go further, describing a persistent enemy with burst attacks that can slow you down [Community-reported; exact slow values [Unconfirmed]]. Either way, the design intent is clear — this is not the enemy that kills you in one hit. It is the enemy that makes the dangerous one land.

That changes how you should prioritize it:

1. **Do not let it stack with a Pufferfish.** A Flombie that slows or staggers you while a Pufferfish is charging a shot is far more dangerous than either enemy alone. Clear the annoyance first when the two appear together, then deal with the ranged threat.
2. **Kill it during downtime, not during a boss wind-up.** The Flombie's threat comes from interference. Spending a few safe seconds to remove it is usually better than leaving it alive through a long animation.
3. **Do not overcommit to chasing it.** If the Flombie is not between you and the next objective, it can wait. The same patch that added it also made patrols more persistent, so "I will lose it around the corner" is a weaker plan than it used to be.

The Flombie is also a good argument for keeping one dodge in reserve. Its burst attacks are designed to catch players who have already spent their stamina on offence. If your bar is empty when the burst comes, the enemy has done its job even if it never lands a clean hit.

## Target Priority in a Mixed Wave

When both enemies are on screen, the correct order is usually **Flombie first, Pufferfish second** — but with one exception. If the Pufferfish is already charging a shot and the Flombie is not currently interfering, break the Pufferfish's Posture first; a self-destruct removes a ranged threat immediately, while a Flombie can be cleaned up afterward.

A simple decision rule:

- **Pufferfish charging, Flombie nearby:** dodge the shot, then break Posture. Do not try to kill the Flombie through the projectile.
- **Pufferfish at range, Flombie on you:** clear the Flombie first. The Pufferfish's slow cadence gives you a window.
- **Both at a distance:** close ground using the Pufferfish's recovery, not a straight line through the Flombie.
- **Low stamina:** leave. The store page frames fight-or-avoid as a real choice, and a mixed wave is exactly the situation where avoiding is cheaper than a reset.

## Loadout Notes

The **Chakram**, added in **v0.5.2.02**, is unlocked from the **Permanent Upgrades** menu [Official]. Its spinning discs are described as speeding up combat, which makes it a reasonable option for applying steady pressure while you stay mobile. **Whether the Chakram is specifically strong against Posture is [Unconfirmed]** — the patch notes do not give it a Posture modifier. Treat it as a mobility-friendly pressure tool, not as a hard counter.

The safer general loadout advice is the same as elsewhere in the game: bring something with a reliable parry follow-up, and do not build entirely around ranged damage for a fight that punishes standing still. The [combat tips guide](/guides/combat-tips-guide) covers the yellow/red telegraph system and the stamina-break finisher loop that these enemies plug into.

## What Is Still Unconfirmed

- Exact **Posture values**, Posture damage per weapon, and whether Posture regenerates.
- Whether the Pufferfish's **self-destruct explosion** damages the player.
- The Flombie's exact **slow magnitude**, burst timing and spawn frequency.
- Whether either enemy appears in every district or only in specific mission pools.

Those gaps are normal for an Early Access build that patches quickly. The confirmed core is enough to fight them: **break the Pufferfish's Posture and respect the self-destruct; remove the Flombie before it can interfere; and assume patrols will keep moving.**

## FAQ

**Does the Pufferfish die when Posture reaches zero?** Yes — the official update says it self-destructs when its Posture meter is reduced to zero.

**Should I use a gun or a ranged Anomaly Power on the Pufferfish?** The patch does not say ranged damage is ineffective. The point is that Posture is the win condition, so use whatever applies Posture safely. Standing at range and trading shots is the risky part, not ranged damage itself.

**Is the Flombie a boss or a normal enemy?** It is a normal enemy type added in the same update as the Pufferfish. The official notes do not describe it as a boss.

**Do the new enemies appear in the demo?** The demo predates v0.5.1.21, so it should not contain them [Unconfirmed for any later demo build]. Treat them as Early Access content.

**Why does the area feel harder after the patch?** The same update improved enemy detection and patrol behaviour. Enemies now react more reliably after spotting you and continue patrolling when you are out of sight, so old timing habits may stop working.
