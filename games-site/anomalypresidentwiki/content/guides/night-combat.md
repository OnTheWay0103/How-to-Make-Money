# Anomaly President Night Combat Guide — Surviving the Dark Hours

At night, the candidate puts down the microphone and picks up a weapon. The store page describes it plainly: *"transform
into a warrior to take down your enemies by night"* [Official]. You fight the **criminal gangs controlled by the current
president**, and — as the store page frames it — you do it by mastering skills to become **a swordmaster, a cyber mage,
or anything in between** [Official].

This is the half of the game that plays like a **side-scrolling beat-'em-up roguelike**. This guide explains how the
night runs work and what makes them winnable.

## The Run Structure

CultureCombine describes the structure precisely: deploying from the overworld "kicks off a fresh run, handing you a
clean slate of abilities and power-ups" [Community]. In practice:

1. **Deploy.** Pick a route / district from the overworld and start a run.
2. **Fight through waves.** Melee combat against the president's gangs. Runs are single-player; your help comes from
   battle-support Fluffies and your own weapon.
3. **Grab power-ups.** Abilities and upgrades appear during the run and shape that run's build.
4. **Reach the boss.** The run's capstone is a boss — often a vice-president carrying an **Anomaly Power** you can steal
   (see the [bosses guide](/guides/bosses-guide) and [Anomaly Powers guide](/guides/anomaly-powers-guide)).
5. **Die or win.** Death resets the run, but you keep materials, money, and campaign meta-progression [Community].

Reviewers describe the combat feel as movement-focused: "weaving between attacks, repositioning, chaining abilities
together" [Virtual Grip, Community]. One compared the character movement to a retro arcade game — "slightly weighty, but
tight and responsive" [CultureCombine, Community].

### Choosing Where to Deploy

Not every overworld district is meant to be cleared on your first attempt. The store page lists **exploration** as one
of the game's pillars [Official], and the overworld is where that pillar lives: districts differ in difficulty and
reward, and some are deliberately out of reach until your campaign — gear, Anomaly Powers, and Fluffy support — catches
up. If you hit a route you cannot clear, treat it as a target to grow into rather than a wall: bank the materials from
the easier districts you can win, reinvest them on the campaign bus, and come back stronger. Pushing the same
unwinnable district over and over is how you burn time without building anything.

## Fighting the President's Gangs

Enemy types in the current Early Access build are described in broad strokes by reviewers:

- **Red-eyed enemies with clubs** and waves of **visually identical mobs** [GameBrief, Community].
- The developer's pre-launch posts introduced planned enemies with working titles: **"The Viking"** (deflect-capable and
  highly mobile) and **"The Gentleman"** [Road to EA #2, Community] — whether these have shipped is [Unconfirmed].
- Reviewers note **thin enemy variety** and reused models as an Early Access weakness [GameBrief, Community].

Because the roster is explicitly growing during Early Access, treat any enemy count as a snapshot, not a promise.

## Elements: Ice, Fire, Electric

Critic testing confirms **three elemental damage types** in the Early Access build: **Ice, Fire, and Electric**
[CultureCombine, Community]. The element system is a key part of build identity — a "cyber mage dealing mass damage with
anomaly powers" [Official] leans on these elements rather than on raw melee.

One important balance note from launch: CultureCombine reported **Ice feeling vastly overpowered** compared to Fire and
Electric [Community]. That is a balance observation, not a rule — expect the developer to tune elements during Early
Access. See the [builds & strategy guide](/guides/builds-strategy-guide) for how elements shape archetypes.

## What Death Costs You (and What It Doesn't)

The roguelike contract is forgiving in the long term:

- **Lost on death:** the current run's build — abilities and power-ups picked up that run.
- **Kept on death:** materials, money, and campaign progression [Community]. Boss kills grant **permanent Anomaly
  Powers** [GameBrief, Community].
- **The bus is safe.** Campaign improvements never reset, which is why the [campaign bus guide](/guides/campaign-bus-guide)
  matters — your permanent power lives there, not in the run.

## If You Are Dying in Every Run

When deaths pile up, the cause is usually one of three things rather than bad luck (editorial advice based on the
confirmed systems above):

1. **Outdated gear.** If you are not spending materials in the bus's upgrade room, your damage and survivability are
   stuck while district difficulty rises. Treat gear upgrades as the default answer before blaming your skill.
2. **Mashing instead of breaking.** Enemies have stamina, and a parry or well-placed hits open them to finishers.
   Draining health bars directly is slower than setting up the finisher window — the [combat tips & parry
   guide](/guides/combat-tips-guide) has the drill.
3. **Fighting in the middle of a crowd.** When mobs stack up, readability collapses. Weave, pull enemies into a line,
   and reposition instead of standing your ground — movement is the skill reviewers emphasise first [Virtual Grip,
   Community].

## Night Combat Tips for Beginners

1. **Learn the movement before the builds.** Positional play — weaving, spacing, repositioning — is the foundation
   Virtual Grip emphasizes [Community]. A mid-tier weapon played well beats a top-tier weapon played badly.
2. **Parry is powerful but strict.** The parry is central to the swordmaster fantasy, but reviewers report the window
   is tight and needs tuning [GameBrief, CultureCombine, Community]. Practice it deliberately — see the
   [combat tips & parry guide](/guides/combat-tips-guide).
3. **One clean build beats a messy grab-bag.** Because runs hand you a clean slate of abilities, commit to a direction
   (melee or elemental) rather than taking every power-up. Synergies are covered in [builds & strategy](/guides/builds-strategy-guide).
4. **Bring gear from the bus.** The upgrade room's improvements are permanent and carry into runs — never fight with
   outdated gear if you can help it.
