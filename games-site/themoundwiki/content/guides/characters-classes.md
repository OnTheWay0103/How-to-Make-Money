## Characters Overview

The Mound: Omen of Cthulhu launched on July 15, 2026 with **four playable conquistador characters**. More become playable through progression and by rescuing stranded survivors on expeditions, and the Deluxe Edition adds the **Fortune Hunters Characters Pack** (see [Price & Platforms](/guides/price-platforms)).

The one thing to know before you plan a build: **there are no classes and no character stats.** Characters are cosmetic — an appearance and a short backstory. No character has a unique trait, ability, or bonus to health, stamina, damage or sanity, and every character moves, fights and goes mad at the same rates. **Your role is defined entirely by the weapons, armour and consumables you carry into the expedition** — the same design as Left 4 Dead.

Players describe the same thing in Steam reviews — *"the characters don't have unique special abilities—they all feel the same"* [Community, [Steam review, August 6, 2026](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198009669889/recommended/2569760/)]. The recurring complaint from players who *want* character variety is worded as a request, not a description of what exists: *"Why have the ability to rescue different characters if they are just skins?"* [Community, [Steam review, August 17, 2026](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198003079070/recommended/2569760/)]. Our [FAQ](/guides/faq-content) covers the same question.

**Bottom line:** pick the character whose look you like. Then build the role you actually want to play with your loadout.

## The Four Squad Roles — Built From Your Loadout

Because no character is locked to a playstyle, "character build" in The Mound really means **loadout archetype**. These are the four roles a full squad usually covers, with the gear that does the work. Weapon notes live in the [Weapons Guide](/guides/weapons-guide), what a contract actually issues is in the [Loadout Guide](/guides/loadout-guide), and there is no perk list anywhere because **the game has no perks** — see [Perks & Progression](/guides/perks-guide).

| Squad Role | Primary Weapon | Secondary / Tool | Armour | The rest of the kit | Job on the Team |
|------------|----------------|-------------------|--------|--------------------|-----------------|
| **Point Man** | Machete (melee front line, backstabs) | Matchlock Arquebus (burst on elites) | The heaviest set you can carry | A block-capable weapon and the party's spare heals | Leads the advance, takes the first hit, holds the line |
| **Support** | Oil Lamp (light + fire utility) | Machete (self-defence fallback, and it can block) | Light — you are carrying everyone else's supplies | The party's healing stock and a light source | Keeps the squad stocked and lit in the centre of the formation |
| **Scout** | Crossbow (silent kills, rain-proof) | Machete (silent melee finish) | The lightest set — you are the loot mule | The lantern/torch allocation and free slots | Finds threats and treasure first; carries the squad's loot |
| **Burst** | Matchlock Arquebus (highest single-shot damage) | Francesca Axe | Whatever the contract issued | Ammunition and a position to shoot from | Answers elite spawns — *not* a boss killer, because the game has no bosses |

## Why There Are No Character Builds

Guides that hand each character a trait — "takes less sanity damage", "heals more", "spots traps further away" — are not describing *The Mound*. There is no trait table in this game, so there is no "trait-to-build logic" to follow: nothing about your chosen conqueror changes a single number on your character sheet.

What actually varies between two players on the same expedition is gear. That is why the same four roles keep appearing in every squad guide on this site — they describe **what you brought**, not **who you picked**:

- **Survivability** comes from armour choice and from actually blocking — blocking is a real, documented ability (patch 1.02).
- **Sustain** comes from the healing items you carried in, and nothing else. There is no healing passive to unlock.
- **Information** comes from the Medallion and from scouting with a light source.
- **Burst damage** comes from the Arquebus or Francesca Axe, and from the ammunition you spent slots on.

## Build Adjustments by Squad Size

| Squad Size | Recommended Change |
|------------|--------------------|
| **Solo (1)** | Take the Point Man or Scout loadout — the tank build for survival, the scout build for loot; the Burst DPS build's low stamina makes solo play punishing |
| **Duo (2)** | Point Man + Scout (tank + information), or Scout + Burst DPS (burst when the scout controls the pull) |
| **Trio (3)** | Point Man + Scout + Support — the classic safety triangle |
| **Full (4)** | One of each of the four roles — the intended experience |

## Quick Pick by Playstyle

| I Want To... | Pick |
|--------------|------|
| Survive my mistakes and learn the game | **Point Man loadout** — heavy armour plus a block-capable weapon is the most forgiving setup |
| Keep my team alive through madness | **Support loadout** — healing items, sanity items and the light source |
| Find every secret and maximize loot | **Scout loadout** — silent weapons and every free slot kept empty |
| Kill elites fast, accept the risk | **Burst loadout** — Arquebus burst windows, and no boss at the end of it |

## Choosing a Character

Since there are no mechanical differences, the only real criteria are cosmetic and narrative:

- **Pick the look you enjoy** — you will be looking at the same character for dozens of expeditions. Characters display the weapons and armor you equip, so the silhouette changes with your loadout anyway.
- **More characters are content, not power** — the ones you rescue, unlock through progression, or get from the Deluxe Edition's Fortune Hunters pack play the same as the starting four.

If you want a difficulty lever, use the squad-size and contract-difficulty systems instead — contract difficulty scales with the **host's** level, which matters far more than who anyone picked.

## How Many Characters Are There?

Four are playable from launch. Additional characters are unlocked by rescuing stranded survivors during expeditions and through progression, and the Deluxe Edition's **Fortune Hunters Characters Pack** adds more. There is an achievement tied to unlocking seven of them in total [Unconfirmed — reported by a single community character guide, not stated in any official Steam announcement we checked].

The official Steam store page does not publish a roster, and the Steam announcements do not list character names beyond the rescue mechanic. A [community character guide](https://tposegaming.com/the-mound-omen-of-cthulhu-characters/) lists **eight** playable characters — four available immediately, two from the Deluxe Edition / "Characters & Items Pack", two from progression [Unconfirmed — single editorial source, unverified against an official roster]. We do not name them here because no official source confirms the list.

## Character Progression

Characters don't have traditional level-ups. Progression is **account-level and item-level, not
per-character**. What the official record actually documents:

- **Equipment** — Better weapons and armour, obtained from the contract gear the Captain issues and from looting bodies. Patch 1.0's **Weapon Upgrade Anvil** *"lets you spend tokens to improve or upgrade weapons given by contracts."*
- **Logbook** — *"Discover abandoned forts during your expedition and recover their logbooks to unlock them as new starting point for future expeditions."* That is the documented reward: **new spawn points on the map**, not stat bonuses. Players confirm the same thing — *"Remember to collect Logbooks whenever you enter a new area — they unlock additional locations."*
- **Tokens and the occult shop** — extraction pays out tokens that buy support items and relics (the Medallion, the Mask of Nyarlathotep, the Crucifix, and from patch 1.04 the Rosary).

### What the game does *not* have

> **No perk system.** `perk` appears **0 times** in all 39 official Steam announcements and **0
> times** on the store page. In the review corpus the word appears only in **requests** — *"I
> believe small perks to the characters would be a good addition"*, *"A more meaningful
> progression system, such as permanent perks … could add a lot of replayability"*. A player
> asking for a feature is evidence the feature is **missing**, not evidence it exists.
>
> This page previously listed *"Perks — Unlocked through achievements and milestones"* as a
> progression path. That line has been removed, and the separate *Perks Guide* has been rewritten
> for the same reason — see [Perks & Progression](/guides/perks-guide).

Players describe the progression system as the game's thinnest area — *"there's no sense of
progression from either direction: not from what you carry, and not from what you are"* — which
is consistent with a game whose only documented growth systems are gear, tokens and map unlocks.
