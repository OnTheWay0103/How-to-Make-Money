# The Mound Sanity Management — Survive What Isn't There

**The Mound: Omen of Cthulhu / Updated: August 4, 2026**

Sanity is the second health bar in The Mound, and it lies more than it hurts. Every player has an independent Sanity meter, and the lower it drops, the more the world fabricates things for you — chests that aren't there, teammates that aren't your teammates, extraction points that lead nowhere. This guide covers the advanced play: how to budget Sanity across an expedition, how to confirm every hallucination, and how a squad talks to each other without losing anyone to a fake.

---

## 1. The Sanity Drain Table (What Actually Costs You)

| Trigger | Sanity Loss | Notes |
|---------|-------------|-------|
| Witnessing enemy kills | Small | Repeated exposure builds up |
| Being hit by supernatural enemies | Medium | Y'm-bhi attacks drain more |
| Wearing Mask of Nyarlathotep | Continuous | Drain rate increases over time — unequip it in dark zones |
| Entering dark zones or caves | Slow | Oil Lamp slows this significantly |
| Teammate death nearby | Large | Proximity matters — stay away from dying allies |
| Failing a Sanity check on a hallucination | Large | Engaging with a fake object costs you |

**Regeneration is nearly useless** — Sanity recovers very slowly outside combat. The real tools are items and Galleon rest between expeditions. Plan around that: Sanity is a *budget per expedition*, not a stat you can recover mid-run.

---

## 2. The Budget: Expedition-Phase Planning

| Phase | Sanity Budget | What You Do |
|-------|--------------|-------------|
| Approach (0-10 min) | Spend freely | Avoid combat, take the lamp-lit route, stay at 90%+ |
| Objective (10-25 min) | Spend carefully | This is where hallucinations start — trigger discipline matters |
| Extraction (25+ min) | Save 30%+ | The Forest Awakens phase spawns 3x enemies; you need the wits for the extraction sprint |

**The hard rule:** never enter the extraction phase below 40% Sanity without a Tonic. Hallucinated extraction points and false teammates are exactly the failures that happen at low Sanity — and they cost you the run (see the [Extraction Guide](/guides/extraction-guide) for what the extraction phase actually does).

## 3. The Confirmation Protocol (Every Hallucination, Checked Twice)

Hallucinations are unique to each player — what you see may not exist for your teammates, and vice versa. The confirmation rules:

| Hallucination | What You See | Reality | How to Confirm |
|--------------|-------------|---------|----------------|
| Fake Treasure Chest | A glinting chest | Nothing — or a trap | Ask a teammate to look at the same spot |
| False Enemy | A patrol that doesn't exist | Empty corridor | Watch teammates' reactions; no reaction = fake |
| Teammate as Monster | An ally appears as a creature | That is your friend | Check the nameplate — real nameplates are stable |
| Duplicate Teammate | A copy of your ally elsewhere | Only one real version | Spatial voice — the fake does not speak |
| Moving Walls | Shifting geometry | Static walls | Mark a reference point and watch it |
| False Extraction Point | A fake extraction marker | Wrong location | Cross-reference with the team map |
| Loot Mirage | Valuable item on the ground | Nothing | Ask a teammate to pick it up |

**The two-rule protocol:** (1) never interact with an object or enemy you haven't confirmed with a teammate, and (2) if you're solo, treat every "free" chest, "lucky" drop, and "obvious" shortcut as a hallucination until you see it from two angles.

---

## 4. Squad Communication Rules

1. **Call every confirmation out loud.** "I see a chest at my two o'clock — confirm." One word from a teammate is worth any amount of instinct.
2. **Nameplates are ground truth.** If the nameplate is stable, it's real — even if it looks wrong. If it flickers or is missing, it's fake.
3. **Spatial voice: the fake never speaks.** The duplicate teammate hallucination is confirmed by silence — the real one will answer you.
4. **Designate a Sanity officer.** In four-player squads, one player tracks everyone's Sanity and calls for Tonics at 40% — prevention beats reaction.
5. **Don't cluster near a dying teammate.** Proximity to a death costs large Sanity; if someone is going down, the squad spreads rather than watches.

## 5. Advanced Tools and Perks

| Tool / Perk | Effect | Best Use |
|-------------|--------|----------|
| Sanity Tonic | Large recovery | The 40% threshold — see Section 2 |
| Oil Lamp | Slows dark-zone drain | Every expedition; upgrade it first (see the [Beginner Loadouts Guide](/guides/beginner-loadouts)) |
| Mask of Nyarlathotep | Massive stat boost, continuous drain | Boss and legendary-hunt fights only — unequip immediately after |
| Galleon rest | Full recovery between expeditions | Mandatory between runs; never start a run below 75% |
| Perks (see [Perks Guide](/guides/perks-guide)) | Various sanity-budget effects | Pick sanity perks for Nightmare Mode |

**Nightmare Mode adjustments:** Nightmare (see the [Nightmare Mode Guide](/guides/nightmare-mode)) increases all drain rates and lowers hallucination confirmation windows — the two-rule protocol becomes a one-step team callout, and the Sanity officer role becomes mandatory, not optional.

---

## 6. Common Mistakes

1. **Chasing hallucinations.** Interacting with fakes costs the *large* Sanity penalty — every fake you ignore is free Sanity.
2. **Saving Tonics "for later."** There is no later — the extraction phase is the hardest part. Use the Tonic at 40% and finish the run.
3. **No Oil Lamp in dark zones.** It's the cheapest drain reduction in the game and beginners skip it for weapon upgrades.
4. **Silent squads.** The only defense against personal hallucinations is team confirmation — a quiet team dies to ghosts of its own making.
5. **Failing to rest on the Galleon.** Starting at 60% Sanity is starting already in danger.
