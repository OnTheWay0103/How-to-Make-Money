## Combat Fundamentals: Deliberate, Not Reflexive

Combat in The Mound is slow and punishing by design. Aiming is deliberate, sprinting is heavy, and every swing, block, and dodge costs **Stamina**. You cannot out-rotate the enemy — you survive by controlling engagement: *who* you fight, *where* you fight, and *whether you fight at all*.

The single most important survival skill is knowing when to not fight.

## Noise Is the Enemy

The Mound's monsters are attracted to **noise**, not sight alone. Three actions loudly pull enemies toward you:

| Action | Noise Level | Risky? |
|--------|-------------|--------|
| Sprinting | High | Yes — always avoid near enemies |
| Firearms (Matchlock, Flintlock) | Very high | Yes — every shot alerts the area |
| Ringing the Medallion | High | Only ring it when you want attention |
| Walking / crouching | Silent | Safe |
| Melee takedowns | Silent | Safe — the default for stealth |

The reward for silence is control: you can clear a camp of Y'm-bhi one backstab at a time and never trigger an alarm. If a fight does break out, expect reinforcements — finish quickly or leave.

## Backstabs and Silent Kills

The backstab is the strongest attack in the game and the foundation of survival:

- **When it works:** sneaking up behind an enemy, or when an ally distracts them (grabs their attention from the front).
- **When it does NOT work:** dodging/rolling behind an enemy mid-fight. The game does not reward that — the enemy turns with you.
- A successful backstab **one-shots most standard enemies** and removes them silently.

Melee is also the only weapon class that never attracts noise, which is why every loadout carries a blade.

## Blocking and Parrying

Blocking reduces incoming damage but does not stop everything:

- **Block (hold block button):** reduces melee damage, costs Stamina per hit absorbed. When Stamina empties, your **guard breaks** and you stagger.
- **Perfect Parry:** time the block just before the attack lands to stagger the enemy and open a critical window. The timing window is tight (~200ms) — practice on Y'm-bhi Scouts first.
- **Cannot block:** projectiles, AoE attacks, and Sanity-based damage. These must be dodged or avoided entirely.

One warning: every enemy hit on *you* triggers a brief stumble cutscene where you cannot act — which can chain into a death spiral if you are surrounded. Do not let yourself get cornered.

## Dodging and Movement

The dodge roll has invincibility frames in the opening of the roll — the only reliable answer to unblockable attacks and grapples. Large twisted beasts can **grapple**, draining all your health and breaking armor; the dodge is your only counter, and early on the safest answer to a grapple beast is to avoid it entirely.

Movement costs Stamina: sprinting drains it continuously, and fighting at low Stamina means no emergency block or dodge. Keep a reserve.

## Surviving the Sanity System

The Mound does not show a Sanity meter — instead, **the longer you spend in a mission, the more bizarre things become**, and it escalates until you leave:

- Allies turning into trees, teammates appearing as monsters, flies crawling on screen, blackouts that reawaken you elsewhere
- One player may see a monster where another sees a teammate — or a clear path where another sees spikes
- Witnessing these effects can cost **health**

Rules to survive it:

1. **Never trust a lone visual alone** — verify with teammates before acting (or before shooting).
2. **Stay near the Ox Wagon** — being close to it periodically reduces your Insanity.
3. **Extract when effects start stacking** — a full contract completion is worth nothing if you die on the way back.

## The Ox Wagon as a Survival Tool

The ox wagon is your best non-combat survival tool:

- **Cuts Insanity** — camp near it periodically to keep your mind stable
- **Drops a paint streak** on the ground behind it, creating a navigation anchor back to the boat
- **Extra carry capacity** — deposit heavy Idols and treasure so your inventory slots stay free
- Caveat: its pathing is erratic — it can turn around or block a path, so never rely on it as a guaranteed escort

## Weather and Environment

Always check the contract modifiers before choosing a loadout:

| Condition | Effect | Counter |
|-----------|--------|---------|
| Rain / storm | Firearms disabled (fuse dies) | Crossbow, melee, Oil Lamp |
| Night / fog | Visibility cut, enemies more aggressive | Oil Lamp, stay in formation |
| Deep water | Reduced dodge i-frames, Leech Swarms | Avoid fighting in water; fire kills swarms |

## When to Fight, When to Flee

The extraction rule that saves more runs than any combat tip: **dying means losing all gathered loot.** The Boatman at the starting location lets you end an expedition early with partial loot — forfeiting completion Tokens but keeping everything you found. If your Sanity is depleted, your squad is down a player, or the haul is already good, extract.

## Survival Priority Checklist

- [ ] Quiet movement — no sprinting near enemies
- [ ] Melee takedowns first, firearms only when necessary
- [ ] Stamina reserve above 30% at all times
- [ ] Ox Wagon visits to manage Insanity
- [ ] Weather check before choosing the loadout
- [ ] Escape route identified before any fight
