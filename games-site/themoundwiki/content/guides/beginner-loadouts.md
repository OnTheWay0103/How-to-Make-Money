# The Mound Beginner Loadouts — Survive Your First Expeditions

**The Mound: Omen of Cthulhu / Updated: August 4, 2026**

Your loadout is the difference between extracting with loot and feeding the forest. The Mound gives you eight equipment slots, six consumable slots, and a weight system that punishes packrats — and on a beginner budget, every item choice matters. This guide covers three proven starter loadouts, the weight rules you must respect, and what to upgrade first with your first few hundred coins.

---

## 1. The Slot and Weight Rules You Must Know

| Slot | Max Items | Degrades On Use? |
|------|-----------|-------------------|
| Primary Weapon | 1 | Yes — each attack |
| Secondary Weapon | 1 | Yes — each use |
| Melee Tool | 1 | Yes — each swing |
| Light Source | 1 | Yes — per expedition |
| Armor / Clothing | 1 set | Yes — per hit taken |
| Consumables | 6 slots | Yes — consumed on use |
| Special Item | 2 slots | Varies |
| Loot Slots | 8 slots | N/A |

**The weight law:** exceeding **70% weight capacity** cuts movement speed by 15%. Exceeding **90%** cuts speed by 30% **and disables sprinting** — in a game where extraction is a 60-90 second sprint to the flare, being overweight is a death sentence. Beginner rule: leave the expedition at or under 70% so you have headroom for loot.

## 2. Three Starter Loadouts (All Under 100 Coins Total)

### Loadout A: Budget Stealth (Recommended for Solo Beginners)

| Slot | Item | Why |
|------|------|-----|
| Primary | Crossbow | Silent kills at medium range; the forgiving beginner weapon |
| Secondary | Hunting Knife | Silent melee finish, light weight |
| Melee Tool | Hatchet | Breaks barricades, emergency melee |
| Light Source | Oil Lamp | Silent light; slows Sanity drain in dark zones |
| Armor | Light Clothing | Cheapest set; keeps weight low |

**Weight:** ~45% — leaves 25%+ of headroom for loot. **Best for:** solo play, Reconnaissance contracts, learning map layouts. **Weakness:** poor against Y'm-bhi berserkers and swarms — don't pick fights.

### Loadout B: Budget Combat (For Squad Play)

| Slot | Item | Why |
|------|------|-----|
| Primary | Pump Shotgun | Cheap, devastating at close range |
| Secondary | Revolver | Reliable backup when the shotgun empties |
| Melee Tool | Crowbar | Quiet door opening, decent melee |
| Light Source | Lantern | Brighter than the Oil Lamp — better in squad firefights |
| Armor | Padded Clothing | Small damage reduction for the combat role |

**Weight:** ~60% — close to the limit; drop one consumable if you want more loot room. **Best for:** combat contracts, squads that intend to fight. **Weakness:** loud — expect every patrol in the zone to come to you.

### Loadout C: Squad Support (For Teams With a Stealth Player)

| Slot | Item | Why |
|------|------|-----|
| Primary | Double-Barrel Shotgun | Burst damage for when stealth breaks down |
| Secondary | First Aid Kit | No — that's a consumable; use a light backup weapon |
| Melee Tool | Hatchet | Barricade breaking while the stealth player scouts |
| Light Source | Oil Lamp | Keeps Sanity drain low for the whole approach |
| Armor | Light Clothing | Weight discipline; support roles carry more consumables |

**The support role's real gear is the consumable slots** (see below) — this loadout's edge is carrying the squad's heals and tools, not its weapon.

## 3. The Six Consumable Slots — What a Beginner Carries

| Slot | Item | When to Use |
|------|------|-------------|
| 1 | Salve (healing) | After any hit from a supernatural enemy — don't hoard it |
| 2 | Salve (healing) | Reserve; only if the first is consumed |
| 3 | Sanity Tonic | Sanity below 40% — before it becomes hallucinations (see the [Sanity Management Guide](/guides/sanity-management)) |
| 4 | Bandage | Cheap secondary heal for late expedition |
| 5 | Flare | Extraction signal backup — if your extraction point shifts (see the [Extraction Guide](/guides/extraction-guide)) |
| 6 | Lockpick | Opens locked chests for bonus loot |

**Consumable discipline:** two healing items minimum on every loadout. The cheapest mistake in The Mound is a full loot bag and an empty health bar.

## 4. Upgrade Order on a Beginner Budget

1. **Light Source first.** Upgrading to a better lamp slows Sanity drain in dark zones and expands your safe map area — the single best value upgrade.
2. **Primary weapon second.** A one-rarity-better crossbow or shotgun is the largest damage jump available at low cost.
3. **Armor third.** Damage reduction only matters if you're already surviving long enough to take hits.
4. **Melee tool last.** The hatchet's utility is identical at every rarity.

| Priority | Upgrade | Cost | Payoff |
|----------|---------|------|--------|
| 1 | Light source | Low | More safe map, slower Sanity drain |
| 2 | Primary weapon | Medium | Faster, cleaner kills |
| 3 | Armor | Medium | Survive one extra hit per expedition |
| 4 | Melee tool | Low | No real payoff — cosmetic |

## 5. Common Beginner Loadout Mistakes

1. **Overweight at departure.** 60%+ weight before loot means you'll hit the 90% no-sprint threshold the moment you grab two good items. Leave at 50-60%.
2. **No Sanity Tonic.** Dark zones and Mask of Nyarlathotep drains are the first silent killers; the tonic is cheaper than a wipe.
3. **Skipping the secondary weapon.** A revolver backup costs nothing and saves you when the shotgun runs dry mid-swarm.
4. **Buying the best armor first.** New players spend their first 200 coins on armor and then can't afford the lamp that prevents the Sanity deaths that kill them more often.
5. **Copying a veteran loadout.** Veteran builds assume map knowledge and rare perks — budget builds (this guide's Section 2) are the correct starting point.

**Final note:** every loadout above works with the cheapest gear in the shop — the skill is in the consumables and the weight math, not the weapon tier.
