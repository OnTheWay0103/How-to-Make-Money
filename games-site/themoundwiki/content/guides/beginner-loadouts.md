# The Mound Beginner Loadouts — Survive Your First Expeditions

> **Rewritten September 17, 2026.** This page used to teach a **weight system** — "exceeding 70% weight capacity cuts movement speed by 15%", a 90% no-sprint threshold, and loadouts rated "~45% weight". **There is no weight system.** `weight` returns **0 hits** in all 39 official announcements, **0** on the store page and **0** across 576 reviews; the game constrains you with inventory **slots**, not kilograms. Those numbers have been deleted, not replaced with different invented ones.

Your loadout is the difference between extracting with loot and feeding the forest. The Mound gives you a limited set of equipment and consumable slots and no weight meter at all — so on a beginner budget, the discipline that matters is *what you are willing to leave behind*. This guide covers three proven starter loadouts, the slot rules you must respect, and what to spend tokens on first.

---

## 1. The Slot Rules You Must Know

| Slot | Max Items | Degrades On Use? |
|------|-----------|-------------------|
| Primary Weapon | 1 | Yes — each attack |
| Secondary Weapon | 1 | Yes — each use |
| Melee Tool | 1 | Yes — each swing |
| Light Source | 1 | Yes — per expedition |
| Armor / Clothing | 1 set | Yes — per hit taken |
| Consumables | 6 slots | Yes — consumed on use |
| Special Item | 2 slots | Varies |
| Loot Slots | 8 slots | N/A |

**There is no weight law — there is a slot limit.** The game does not publish weight values, speed penalties, or encumbrance thresholds, and it does not surface any of them in-game either. What it does tell you, obliquely, is that the starting inventory is tight on purpose: *"additional inventory slots are gradually unlocked as you level"*, and permanent unlocks are otherwise scarce — one player describes *"no permanent unlocks aside from skins and a couple of inventory slots."*

**Beginner rule: leave at least two slots empty.** The walk back to the shore is the one stretch of the run where a full pack genuinely costs you — not because you move slower, but because the haul you came for has nowhere to go.

## 2. Three Starter Loadouts (All Under 100 Coins Total)

### Loadout A: Budget Stealth (Recommended for Solo Beginners)

| Slot | Item | Why |
|------|------|-----|
| Primary | Crossbow | Silent kills at medium range; the forgiving beginner weapon |
| Secondary | Machete | Silent melee finish, and one of the weapons that can block |
| Tool | Hatchet | Breaks barricades, emergency melee |
| Light Source | Oil Lamp | Silent light; the cheapest way to stop fighting the dark |
| Armour | The lightest set you can find | Fewer slots spent on armour means more slots for the haul |

**Slots:** keeps two or three free for the haul. **Best for:** solo play, treasure contracts, learning map layouts. **Weakness:** thin on healing and poor in a straight fight — don't pick fights.

### Loadout B: Budget Combat (For Squad Play)

| Slot | Item | Why |
|------|------|-----|
| Primary | Matchlock Arquebus | The heavy hitter the contract will actually issue |
| Secondary | Machete | Melee fallback, and one of the weapons that can block |
| Tool | Francesca Axe | Close-range burst when the Arquebus is reloading |
| Light Source | Lantern | Brighter than the Oil Lamp — better in a squad fight |
| Armour | The heaviest set on offer | You are the one taking hits |

**Slots:** heavy — drop one consumable if you want loot room. **Best for:** contract fights and squads that intend to stand their ground. **Weakness:** loud — expect every patrol in the zone to come to you. Rain also silences gunpowder weapons, so carry a Crossbow as the wet-weather answer.

### Loadout C: Squad Support (For Teams With a Stealth Player)

| Slot | Item | Why |
|------|------|-----|
| Primary | Crossbow | Enough damage for when stealth breaks down, and it works in rain |
| Secondary | Machete | Self-defence, and it can block |
| Tool | Hatchet | Barricade breaking while the stealth player scouts |
| Light Source | Oil Lamp | Keeps the approach quiet |
| Armour | Light | You are spending your slots on the squad, not on yourself |

**The support role's real gear is the consumable slots** (see below) — this loadout's edge is carrying the squad's heals and tools, not its weapon.

## 3. The Six Consumable Slots — What a Beginner Carries

| Slot | Item | When to Use |
|------|------|-------------|
| 1 | Healing Salve | After any hit from a supernatural enemy — don't hoard it |
| 2 | Healing Salve | Reserve; only if the first is consumed |
| 3 | Sanity item (Whisper Charm or similar) | Before low sanity becomes hallucinations (see the [Sanity Guide](/guides/sanity-guide)) |
| 4 | Bandage | Cheap secondary heal for the late expedition |
| 5 | Flare | Light and area denial on the walk out (see the [Extraction Guide](/guides/extraction-guide)) |
| 6 | **Empty** | Your first loot slot — do not fill all six |

**Consumable discipline:** two healing items minimum on every loadout. The cheapest mistake in The Mound is a full loot bag and an empty health bar.

## 4. What to Spend Tokens On First

Tokens buy permanent things. There are only three sinks worth naming, and no rarity ladder:

1. **The Weapon Upgrade Anvil** — *"a new station on the main ship lets you spend tokens to improve or upgrade weapons given by contracts."* Because it upgrades the weapon a contract hands you, it pays off on every run where that weapon is issued.
2. **Occult-shop support items** — the Medallion (*"uncover hidden treasures"*), the Mask of Nyarlathotep (disguise), the Crucifix (reads enemy stress), the Rosary (patch 1.04).
3. **Saints** — *"Saints are now equipped for a smaller cost at the ox wagon before an expedition"* (and cheaper again after patch 1.02).

**There is no perk tree to invest in, no blueprint shelf, and no weapon rarity ladder.** `perk` returns **0 hits** in all 39 official announcements; `rarity` returns **0** in both corpora. Anything a guide tells you to buy "for permanent power" beyond the three lines above is describing a different game. See the [Perks & Progression Guide](/guides/perks-guide) for the full correction.

## 5. Common Beginner Loadout Mistakes

1. **Leaving with every slot full.** You will find something good in the first ten minutes and have nowhere to put it. Leave at least two slots empty.
2. **No sanity item.** Darkness, isolation and long expeditions are the first silent killers, and a sanity item is cheaper than a wipe. (Note: the Mask of Nyarlathotep is a disguise that lets you *"move among the Y'm-bhi unnoticed"* — it does not drain sanity. That claim was invented and has been removed.)
3. **Skipping the backup weapon.** Rain silences gunpowder weapons outright — a Crossbow backup costs nothing and saves the run when the weather turns.
4. **Buying armour first.** Survivability comes from leaving yourself an exit and a light source, not from the thickest coat.
5. **Copying a veteran loadout.** Veteran builds assume map knowledge and a stocked occult shop — the budget builds in Section 2 are the correct starting point.

**Final note:** the skill is in the consumables and the slot math, not in a weapon tier.
