# Legendary-Tier Contracts — What "Endgame" Actually Means

> **Rewritten September 17, 2026.** This page used to describe a "Legendary Hunts" system with four
> named hunt targets, a 20-minute despawn timer, a skull-icon contract marker and a reward table.
> None of it exists. See [Why this page was rewritten](#why-this-page-was-rewritten).

**There is no Legendary Hunt system in The Mound: Omen of Cthulhu.** The phrase `legendary hunt`
returns **0 hits** across all 40 official Steam announcements and **0 hits** across the 576-review
corpus. There are no named hunt targets, no lairs, no despawn timer, no hunt lockout and no exclusive
drop table.

There *is* a word "Legendary" in this game — but it belongs to **contract tiers**, and it comes from
players, not from the developer. That is a real, useful thing to understand, so this page now covers
what the endgame genuinely is.

---

## "Legendary" as a contract tier

The single best piece of evidence is a long, detailed review by a player who finished the game:

> *"You can be clear-cutting your way through the jungle on Legendary treasure-missions, then you
> take-on a Basic portal-mission and the jungle decides to get a lot more difficult."*
> — review `234554812`, 5 Sep 2026, 50 upvotes

Read that carefully, because it tells you two things:

1. **Contracts have tiers, and "Legendary" is one of them.** "Basic" is another. The reviewer is
   describing a *treasure* mission versus a *portal* mission, so tiers apply across mission types.
2. **Tier does not equal difficulty.** The reviewer's actual complaint is that the label is
   unreliable — a Basic mission can be harder than a Legendary one. If you are planning a run around
   the tier name, plan around what it costs instead.

That player vocabulary — Legendary / Basic — is **player-reported**. What is officially documented is
that the tier system exists and that it pays differently:

> *"Slight increase for tokens given for contract tiers."* — Patch 1.02 notes

## The real endgame, in four parts

### 1. The clock is not a difficulty setting — it is your choice

The single biggest change to how a run plays came in patch 1.01:

> *"The mandatory hard cap at the end of missions has been removed: players can now remain on the
> mainland as long as they can survive."* — Patch 1.01 notes

Before that patch, a mission ended on a timer whether you were finished or not. After it, **the only
thing that ends your run is the forest waking up or your squad dying.** That is the endgame dial in
this game: not a boss gate, but how greedy you are willing to be with a haul already in the cart.

The forest side of the equation is official too: *"Enemies take longer to spawn when the forest wakes
up"* (patch 1.01). The clock is tuned, and it is tuned against you holding still.

### 2. Token scaling and the upgrade loop

Contracts pay in **tokens** and **experience** — the developer's own framing is that a contract is
signed for *"Experience to sharpen your skills, and Tokens to spend at the occult shop"*. The
endgame loop is converting those tokens into better tools:

- **Weapon Upgrade Anvil** — *"a new station on the main ship lets you spend tokens to improve or upgrade weapons given by contracts"* (launch update notes)
- **The occult shop** — where tokens buy support items and relics, including *"powerful support items like The Medallion to uncover hidden treasures"*
- **Saints** — patch 1.01 moved them off the woodshop: *"Saints are now equipped for a smaller cost at the ox wagon before an expedition"*, and patch 1.02 *"Reduced Saints blessing costs"*
- **The Rosary** — patch 1.04 added hidden jewel boxes across the mainland: *"Find them all using the map to unlock a new Occult Item from the shop!"*

What does **not** scale is your character. There are no permanent unlocks beyond cosmetics and a
couple of inventory slots, and players say so bluntly: *"there is no progression whatsoever"*. If
"endgame" to you means a character that gets stronger, this is the wrong game, and no amount of
Legendary-tier treasure changes that.

### 3. The Ox Cart is the real risk/reward decision

High-tier contracts ask you to carry more value, and all of it rides in the cart. The cart is a mobile
base that follows the trail and can be ordered around, but sending it back early is a trap that
players warn about repeatedly:

> *"There is really no point in sending the cart back because it causes enemies to fight you and
> constantly makes the cart run away so if you need something out of it you are screwed."*
> — review `234246354`, 1 Sep 2026

> *"It has an interaction that will have the cart return to the extraction point, however there is
> legitimately no reason to ever use it. It causes the driver to blow a horn which makes a lot of
> noise…"* — review `234554812`, 5 Sep 2026, 50 upvotes

Patch 1.04 did improve cart control — *"Command Wheel 2.0: By popular demand we have added a new
function to allow you to stop the cart and give it instructions. Proximity determines whether or not
you use the horn."* Even so, the horn is loud, and loud is how the forest notices you. See the
[Ox Cart Guide](/guides/ox-cart-guide) for the full control breakdown.

### 4. The last missions are the only real escalation

The game has a final act, and players describe it: a stretch of artifact missions ending in a mission
inside the Mound itself — *"Then came the final mission, entering the mound"* (review `234975735`,
11 Sep 2026) — followed by the free **Temple of Yig** DLC mission and a level the player base calls
Kn'yan. Patch 1.05 even names the location in a bug fix: *"Fixed the orb from the Mound level not
handling damage properly."*

The reviews are mixed on whether this lands. The same reviewer calls the last levels *"a lot of
build-up then it just dropped off"*; another calls the Temple of Yig and last mission *"too easy
despite being a feast for the eyes"*. That is the honest state of the endgame.

## The boss question, one more time

The old page's "endgame content" was four hunt targets, and it framed them as the reason to keep
playing. In reality the most common late-game request in the review corpus is for content that does
not exist:

- *"no bosses, every mission is pretty much the same, no real progression"* — review `234197520`, 31 Aug 2026
- *"was dissapointed in ending, was hoping a boss fight or something more"* — review `234630362`, 6 Sep 2026
- *"We need some mini bosses or more of a threat from certain baddies"* — review `234478102`, 4 Sep 2026

A recurring player wish is a gap signal, not documentation. We cover this in full — including what the
game escalates with instead — on [Is There Boss Content?](/guides/boss-weakness-drops-chart).

## A real endgame prep checklist

Every line below maps to a documented mechanic, not to an invented hunt.

- [ ] **Pick by payout and objective, not by tier label.** Tiers pay differently (patch 1.02) and player reports say the label lies about difficulty
- [ ] **Decide your greed point before you land.** With the mission cap removed (patch 1.01) nothing but the forest ends your run — set the value at which you turn around
- [ ] **Watch the forest warnings.** Patch 1.02 fixed the *"time warnings from the forest state not appearing"* — that readout is the timer you actually need
- [ ] **Do not send the cart back.** Player-reported: the horn draws enemies and the cart does not come back
- [ ] **Spend tokens before you leave port.** Anvil upgrades (launch update), Saints at the ox wagon (patch 1.01, reduced cost in 1.02)
- [ ] **Take a block and use it.** `Q` on keyboard, `RB` / `R1` on controller (patch 1.02), and blocking stuns enemies as of patch 1.03
- [ ] **Backstab enemies that already have aggro.** Patch 1.03 changed this: *"Players can now backstab enemies that have aggro to deal significant damage"*
- [ ] **Sleep on no "hunt reward" table.** Late-game payouts come from delivering the contract, not from a named kill

## Why this page was rewritten

| Removed claim | Evidence |
|---------------|----------|
| A "Legendary Hunts" system | `legendary hunt` = **0 hits** in the 40 announcements and **0 hits** in the 576 reviews |
| "Legendary" as a named hunt tier | The only announcement use of `legendary` is figurative — *"we saw some absolutely legendary moments of survival, panic, and accidental squad betrayals"*. The real usage is the player-reported contract tier |
| The Drowned Baron, The Pale King, The Crawling King, The Gilded Widow as hunt targets | **0/0 each**. Their lairs — Shallow Graves, The Hollow Woods, The Deep Warrens, Mansion of Fools — are also **0/0** |
| Loot: Baron's Seal, Drowned Idol, Pale Crown, Veil Shard, King's Mandible, Chitin Plate, Widow's Thread, Gold Silk | **0/0 each**. "Gold Silk sells for 2,000+ Gyldenblood" — `Gyldenblood` is **0/0** and is not a currency in this game |
| "Hunt target despawns after 20 minutes" | No despawn timer is documented anywhere. `hunt target` = **0/0**; the only `20 min`-family hits in reviews are unrelated |
| "Announced on the contract board with a target name and a skull icon" | `skull` = 1 announcement hit and 1 review hit, neither about contracts. The contract board shows objectives and payouts, per *"The Captain's bargain: Choosing your contract"* |
| "One hunt reward per cycle per account" / "Lockout" / "Galleon Rank" | `Galleon Rank` = **0/0**. There is no documented rank, lockout or weekly hunt reset |
| "Legendary hunt drops are the only source of most best-in-slot items" | Invented. Gear comes from the contract's shared arsenal, the Anvil and the occult shop |
| Hunt prep numbers ("3 Healing Salve, 3 Bandages, 1 stamina tonic", "web break costs 40 stamina", "200% burn damage", "50% ballistic reduction") | Invented percentages and item counts. Nothing in either corpus states them |

Two habits made the old page survive review, and both are worth naming:

- **It borrowed real vocabulary.** "Legendary", "contract", "tokens", "extract" are all real words in
  this game. Wrapping invented targets in real terms makes an invented system feel documented.
- **It stated mechanics as numbers.** "20 minutes", "40 stamina", "200% burn" read like datamined
  values. Precision is not evidence.

### Method

- **Official corpus:** Steam `ISteamNews` for app ID `2569760`, all **40** items, full text.
  Reproduced with case-insensitive regex over the 40 news blocks: `legendary hunt` = **0**;
  `legendary` = **1** (figurative, quoted above); `hunt` = **6**, all of which are "treasure hunters",
  "Fortune Hunters Characters Pack", "wakes up to hunt you", "hunt down the rest of the squad",
  "does not hunt - it punishes proximity" and "The monsters hunt differently" — none is a system;
  `boatman` = **0**; `Endless Expedition` = **0**; `Corrupted Key` = **0**; `20 min` / `despawn` = **0**.
- **App-ID check:** `2569760` plus four add-on apps (`4630450`, `4630440`, `4630460`, `4925780`), each
  with **0 news items** — no second announcement stream exists.
- **Community corpus:** Steam reviews, **576** unique items, IDs and dates retained for every quote.
  `legendary hunt` = **0**; `Galleon Rank` = **0**; `Baron's Seal` = **0**; `Drowned Idol` = **0**;
  `Pale Crown` = **0**; `Veil Shard` = **0**; `King's Mandible` = **0**; `Chitin Plate` = **0**;
  `Widow's Thread` = **0**; `Gold Silk` = **0**; `Gyldenblood` = **0**. `legendary` = **1**, and it is
  the contract-tier quote reproduced above.
- **Context, not counts:** every hit listed was read. The 6 announcement `hunt` hits and the 11 review
  `hunt` hits were all inspected — the review hits are overwhelmingly comparisons to *Hunt: Showdown*,
  which is a different game and not evidence of a hunt mode here.
- **Web cross-check:** performed by the audit lead; cited, not re-run for this page.
