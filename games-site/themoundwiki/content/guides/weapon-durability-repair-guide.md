# The Mound Weapon Durability & Repair Guide — Why Weapons Break & How to Fix Them

> Launch Version / Updated: August 27, 2026

Your Machete was fine a minute ago. Now it is gone — snapped mid-swing while a Y'm-bhi patrol closes in. If this has happened to you, you are not alone. "Weapon durability is poorly explained" and "weapons/armor break after only a few hits, sometimes mid-engagement" are among the most common complaints in Steam reviews for The Mound: Omen of Cthulhu. The game never teaches you the durability system, so most players learn about it the hard way — by losing a weapon at the worst possible moment.

This guide fixes that. Here is why your gear breaks, how to repair it at the Galleon Anvil, which weapons survive the longest, and the maintenance habits that stop mid-fight breakage.

---

## Why Does My Weapon Break After a Few Hits?

There is no tutorial for durability in The Mound. The system is never explained in-game, which is why it feels random. In practice it is simple: **every attack, swing, and use wears the weapon down, and when the invisible bar empties, the weapon breaks.**

- **Melee weapons wear on every swing.** The Machete, sword, and spear degrade each time you attack. Under sustained combat, players report melee weapons breaking after only "two or three uses" (Community).
- **Firearms wear on use and misfire when degraded.** "Decayed" guns are described as "rusted and prone to misfires," and low-condition weapons deal reduced damage (Editorial — GameWatcher).
- **Armor wears per hit taken**, not per swing — covered in this wiki's [Loadout Guide](/guides/loadout-guide).
- **There is no durability bar visible by default**, which makes breakage feel sudden (Community consensus).

The [Loadout Guide](/guides/loadout-guide) already flags the real danger: durability is the difference between "weapon not breaking mid-fight" and "ignoring degradation until it fails." A broken weapon in an expedition is not just an inconvenience — it can end the run.

## What Weapon Grades Mean

Weapons come in conditions that affect how long they last:

| Grade | Effect | Applies To |
|-------|--------|-----------|
| Standard | Full stats | All weapons |
| Aged | Reduced damage, and reduced range for guns | Melee + guns |
| Decayed | Rusted, prone to misfires | Guns only |

Exact per-weapon durability values and the precise number of hits before breakage have not been published anywhere we could verify — treat any specific "N hits" number as `[Unconfirmed]`.

## How to Repair Your Weapons — The Galleon Anvil

Repairs and upgrades happen **between expeditions** at the **Weapon Upgrade Anvil** on the Galleon (Editorial — NoobFeed).

### Step-by-Step

1. **Start an Expedition Contract** so the Galleon issues your weapons for that run. The Anvil only shows options once a contract is active — it is easy to miss before that.
2. **Take the weapon out of the cart** and put it directly in your character inventory. The Anvil cannot detect weapons stored in the cart.
3. **Interact with the Anvil** and spend Tokens on the improvement you want.
4. **Repeat every contract.** Anvil upgrades apply only to the current expedition — they do not permanently improve the weapon, so you must re-apply them each run.

### What to Buy

- **Melee durability upgrades are the priority.** Additional durability "helps the weapon survive an entire expedition instead of breaking early" (Editorial — NoobFeed).
- **Crossbow reload speed** makes your most reliable weapon even more efficient.
- **Matchlock → Flintlock conversion** on suitable pistols and muskets makes them fire reliably in damp weather — a durability-adjacent fix that stops rain misfires.

> **Note on costs:** exact Token prices are not published in any guide we found — treat specific repair costs as `[Unconfirmed]`. Some guides also mention anvils found out in the field for mid-mission repairs, but we could not independently confirm that mechanic, so treat it as `[Unconfirmed]` too.

## Which Weapons Last the Longest?

Community consensus across Steam discussions, player reviews, and guides is clear: **the Crossbow and the Francesca Axe are the most reliable**, because they let you avoid melee wear entirely.

| Weapon | Durability Verdict | Why |
|--------|--------------------|-----|
| **Crossbow** | Best | Ranged, silent, rain-proof, and bolts are recoverable — you barely touch melee durability |
| **Francesca Axe** | Excellent | Thrown and retrieved for silent kills; avoids sustained melee combat |
| **Flintlock Pistol** | Good | Used sparingly as a finisher; low wear in practice |
| **Matchlock Arquebus** | Situational | Big burst, but reliability is a weather question more than a durability one; Decayed muskets misfire |
| **Machete** | Weak | Breaks fastest under sustained melee — always carry a fallback |
| **Sword / Spear** | Weak | The weapons players most often lose mid-fight |

> **Golden rule:** the least durable loadout is a melee-only one. If you fight with the Machete or sword as your primary, expect it to break before extraction and plan around that.

## Practical Maintenance Habits

1. **Repair at the Anvil between every expedition.** A sword or spear that breaks "at the worst possible moment" ruins an otherwise successful run (Editorial — vgtimes beginner guide).
2. **Backstab and headshot instead of trading blows.** Backstabs instantly kill most standard enemies regardless of weapon, preserving durability. Stealth kills are free kills.
3. **Carry a ranged or thrown option.** Even if your melee breaks, a Crossbow or Axe keeps you in the fight.
4. **Pick up spare weapons in the field.** Enemies and crates drop weapons — grabbing a spare means a breakage is not a death sentence.
5. **Never store your working weapon in the cart.** The Anvil cannot see it there, so a "broken" loadout is often just a misplaced one.
6. **Watch the weather.** A Decayed or low-condition gun misfiring in a sudden downpour is the durability problem nobody warns you about.

---

## The Bottom Line

The Mound's durability system is simple to understand once you know it exists: gear wears with use, breaks without warning, and is repaired at the Galleon Anvil between runs. Play around it — prefer the Crossbow and Axe, repair after every contract, and keep a spare. Do that, and the worst moment of your expedition will never be the moment your sword snaps.
