# Maps & Navigation — What's Actually There

> **Rewritten September 17, 2026.** This page used to list eight named locations — Isle of Voices,
> Catacombs, Sunken Grotto, Shallow Graves, The Hollow Woods, The Deep Warrens, Mansion of Fools and
> The Mound Interior — alongside a Reputation unlock ladder, per-map enemy profiles and POI tables.
> Every one of those names was invented. See [Why this page was rewritten](#why-this-page-was-rewritten).

**The Mound: Omen of Cthulhu does not have an island list.** There is no map-select screen with eight
named regions, no "2,000 Reputation unlocks the Sunken Grotto" ladder, and no boss chamber at the
bottom of a named grotto. All eight of those names return **0 hits** in the 39 official Steam
announcements and **0 hits** in the 576-review corpus.

What the game has instead is a **procedural mainland** that you land on, cross and leave. That makes
navigation the real map skill here — and the tools for it *are* documented. This page now covers those.

---

## What the game actually calls its places

These are the only location terms in the official record. Note how general they are — that is the
point.

| Term | Status | Evidence |
|------|--------|----------|
| **The mainland** | Official | The developer's word for the expedition area. Patch 1.01: *"players can now remain on the mainland as long as they can survive"*. Patch 1.02: *"Rescuing a character in the mainland means a contract is successful…"*. Patch 1.04: *"hidden jewel boxes scattered across the mainland"* |
| **The Galleon** | Official | Your hub ship: *"Meet up at your galleon hub to coordinate your squad of up to 4 players, purchase vital gear, and select your destination before rowboating into the unknown"* |
| **The Valdivian jungle** | Official | The setting, stated in the launch post and in *"Crafting the Jungle"*: the team *"drew from real-world inspirations, especially the Valdivian temperate rainforest of South America"* |
| **The Mound** | Official | The objective the whole game is named for. Patch 1.05 names the level directly: *"Fixed the orb from the Mound level not handling damage properly"* |
| **The Logbook Camp** | Official | A fixed location, named in the patch 1.01 known-issues list: *"Crash when attacking the centipedes around the Logbook Camp"* |
| **The Temple of Yig** | Official | A free DLC mission area: *"The Temple of Yig Exclusive Mission is available now as a free DLC. Unearth ancient mysteries in a unique area of the cursed jungle."* |
| **Forts** | Official | The demo briefing announced *"5 forts are available for exploration!"* The store page describes recovering their logbooks (see below). Players use "reclaim a fort" as a mission-type name |
| **Kn'yan** | Player-reported | A late-game section players single out; 2 review mentions, 0 announcements. Not a map-select name |

## The third-party region names — clearly labelled

Independent guides (gamerguides, nerdschalk, allthings.how, noobfeed, gamewatcher, intoindiegames)
report a set of region names: **The Jungle, White Beach, Rio Verde, Cliffs of Ibara, Shambler's
Hunting Grounds, Abandoned Mapuche Settlement, The Watching Trees, South Landing, Scarlet Flower
Woods, Flooded Forest, Wreck of La Favorita, Near the Mound, Saint Jerome's Fall, The Hallowed Hills**,
plus a roughly 25-entry creature roster.

> **Read this before using them.** These names are **reported by third-party guides, not by the
> developer.** We re-ran all fourteen against both corpora and every one returns **0 hits in the 39
> official announcements and 0 hits in the 576 reviews**. We are listing them so you can recognise
> them when another site uses them — *not* as an official map list, and **not** as the basis for
> point-of-interest tables. If you see a guide giving you "the northeast corner of Shallow Graves",
> it is building detail on names the developer never published.

One exception worth noting: **Mapuche** appears once in the announcements, in the *Tue Tue* creature
profile, which traces the creature to *"Chilean and Mapuche folklore"*. That is folklore, not a
region label.

## The navigation tools that are real

This is what actually gets you home. Every item below is documented; source given per row.

### 1. The Ox Cart / Ox Wagon — your mobile base

The cart is not a map feature, it is your inventory, your light and your anchor. Official support:

- *"As you venture into it, you'll need to escort your ox cart, scavenge valuable loot such as forbidden relics, and survive against the growing threats"* (expedition briefing)
- *"Ability to organise items in your own inventory with a mouse during interactions with the wagon added"* — Patch 1.01
- *"Multiple players can now interact with the ox-wagon inventory simultaneously"* — launch update
- *"Command Wheel 2.0: By popular demand we have added a new function to allow you to stop the cart and give it instructions. Proximity determines whether or not you use the horn."* — Patch 1.04
- *"You can now pet 'Carlito' the Ox on the ship."* — Patch 1.03

**Carlito** is the only officially named character in this whole system, and he is an ox. Players add
one navigation detail worth knowing: the cart is reported to leave a trail you can follow back. Full
control breakdown in the [Ox Cart Guide](/guides/ox-cart-guide).

### 2. The Medallion — the treasure locator

> *"With them, you can obtain powerful support items like The Medallion to uncover hidden treasures,
> or even forbidden relics like the Mask of Nyarlathotep, which offers a terrifying form of
> protection."* — *The Captain's bargain: Choosing your contract*

Bought with tokens for the run. Two caveats from the reviews: it helps you *find* artifacts, and
players report it lasts one expedition only — *"if you buy the medallion to help you find artifacts
then it's for the one expedition you bought it for and disappears when you…"*. Patch 1.01 also had to
fix a conflict with it: *"The Map item no longer behaves like the Medallion item."*

### 3. Fort logbooks — the unlock system

This is the closest thing the game has to a map unlock, and it is on the game's own store page:

> *"Discover abandoned forts during your expedition and recover their logbooks to unlock them as new
> starting point for future expeditions."*

Players treat this as the single most important route habit in the game:

> *"Remember to collect Logbooks whenever you enter a new area — they unlock additional locations and
> are essential for progressing further"* — review `230534657`, 15 Jul 2026, **425 upvotes**

So: **your map grows by recovering logbooks, not by earning Reputation.** There is no Reputation
system at all — the word returns 0 hits in both corpora.

### 4. The forest timer and its warnings

The island escalates on a clock, and the game tells you:

- *"Enemies take longer to spawn when the forest wakes up."* — Patch 1.01
- *"Fixed time warnings from the forest state not appearing."* — Patch 1.02

That second line confirms a **warning readout exists and was briefly broken**. If you have ever felt
blindsided by the forest waking, you may have been playing the build where the warnings did not show.
Watch for it in the UI and treat it as your extraction cue.

### 5. The rowboat map with hand-drawn art

> *"Hand-drawn art pieces now accompany logbook narrations on the rowboat map."* — launch update notes

The rowboat map is where logbook narrations play back. That is also where the game's story is
delivered, which is why the [Walkthrough Guide](/guides/walkthrough-guide) keeps pointing at logbooks.

### 6. The destination is chosen at the Galleon, not on the map

You do not navigate to a region — you are sent to one. The developer's own loop summary is *"Meet up
at your galleon hub to coordinate your squad of up to 4 players, purchase vital gear, and select your
destination before rowboating into the unknown."* That is why there is no map-select screen with named
islands: the destination is a contract parameter, and what you do with the ground you land on is the
skill being tested.

### 7. The landing point — where the run ends

Every expedition ends where it started: the shore, in the boat. Players describe the whole loop around
it — *"Sprint back to the rowboat and get out with a sliver of health"* — and one notes that extraction
in this game is inherently backtracking: *"das ist die Extraktion… sondern Backtracking ist schon
absolut vorprogrammiert."*

> **Note on the "Boatman".** The old version of this page said an NPC called the Boatman waits at the
> shore. `boatman` returns **0 hits** in the announcements and **0 hits** in the reviews. There is no
> documented boatman NPC. What is real is the **landing point**: the shore where you arrive and the
> point you must walk back to. Players call it "the boat". The [Extraction Guide](/guides/extraction-guide)
> covers the walk out in detail.

## Route planning rules that survive the rewrite

1. **Fix the landing point in your head before you move inward.** Every run is an out-and-back; the
   extraction is the walk you have already made once.
2. **Treat the cart as your base, not your shuttle.** Sending it back is player-reported as a trap —
   the horn draws enemies and you lose access to your own supplies.
3. **Recover every fort logbook you find.** It is the only documented way to open new starting points.
4. **Read the forest warnings and set a greed point.** With the mission time cap removed (patch 1.01),
   nothing but the forest ends your run.
5. **Learn one route per landing, then optimise that route.** Since the mainland layout varies, the
   skill that transfers is knowing the *loop* — land, push, load, turn around.
6. **Do not plan around named landmarks from other sites.** If a guide sends you to "the northeast
   corner of Shallow Graves", that region does not exist in the developer's material.

|---------------|----------|
| Isle of Voices, Catacombs, Sunken Grotto, Shallow Graves, The Hollow Woods, The Deep Warrens, Mansion of Fools, "The Mound Interior" as a zone label | Each name = **0 hits** in the 39 announcements and **0 hits** in the 576 reviews. | Per-map enemy profiles (Y'm-bhi Sentries, Ghost Bats, Giant Centipedes, Wraiths, Leech Swarms, Y'm-bhi Priests, Wraith Archers, Elite Stalkers) | Invented. "Ghost Bat", "Wraith Archer", "Leech Swarm", "Elite Stalker", "Y'm-bhi Priest" and "Y'm-bhi Sentry" are **0/0**. `elite` = **0 announcement hits** |
| POI tables ("Eastern beach — 3 Y'm-bhi Sentries", "Shrine room — 1 Y'm-bhi Priest", "three spore chambers") | Invented. Exact counts and layouts do not exist in the record |
| "The Lurker Below awaits in the lowest chamber" / "Drowned Captain mini-boss" | Both **0/0** |
| The three-floor Mound Interior with The Antechamber, Hall of Whispers and Inner Sanctum | Invented. There is one documented Mound level (patch 1.05) and no floor structure anywhere |
| "The Boatman" as a named NPC | `boatman` = **0/0** |
| "Forest timer (Tab)" / "press Tab" | A timer and its warnings are real (patch 1.01, 1.02), but no announcement or review states the key binding or the Tab UI |
| "Wading creates noise at 25m", "patrol loops of 50m" | Invented distances. Nothing in either corpus gives a noise radius in metres |

The old page's *"Launch Version / Updated: August 4, 2026"* banner made an invented atlas look like a
maintained reference. A date is not a source, and a table is not evidence.

### Method

- **Official corpus:** Steam `ISteamNews` for app ID `2569760`, all **39** items, full text.
  Reproduced with case-insensitive regex over the 39 news blocks: `Isle of Voices` = **0**;
  `Catacombs` = **0**; `Sunken Grotto` = **0**; `Shallow Graves` = **0**; `Hollow Woods` = **0**;
  `Deep Warrens` = **0**; `Mansion of Fools` = **0**; `Mound Interior` = **0**; `reputation` = **0**;
  `boatman` = **0**; `abandoned fort` = **0**; `elite` = **0**; `wraith` = **0**.
- **Positive controls reproduced in the same pass:** `mainland` = **4**; `logbook` = **2**;
  `medallion` = **3**; `ox cart` / `ox wagon` / `ox-cart` = **3**; `rowboat` = **2**; `Valdivian` = **2**;
  `Temple of Yig` = **2**. The same script that returns 0 for the invented names returns real hits for
  the real ones, which is how we know the query works.
- **Third-party names re-tested:** `White Beach`, `Rio Verde`, `Cliffs of Ibara`, `Shambler`,
  `Watching Trees`, `South Landing`, `Scarlet Flower`, `Flooded Forest`, `La Favorita`,
  `Near the Mound`, `Saint Jerome`, `Hallowed Hills` — **0 hits each** in both corpora.
  `Mapuche` = 1, and it is folklore attribution in the Tue Tue profile, not a region.
- **App-ID check:** `2569760` plus four add-on apps (`4630450`, `4630440`, `4630460`, `4925780`),
  each returning **0 news items**.
- **Community corpus:** Steam reviews, **576** unique items, IDs and dates retained.
  `reputation` = **0**; `boatman` = **0**; `Catacombs` = **0**; `logbook` = **7**;
  `medallion` = **2**; `ox cart` / `oxcart` / `ox-cart` = **3**. The 4 review hits for `priest` are the
  playable *Priest* character and the cart driver, never an "Y'm-bhi Priest" — that exact string is
  **0/0**.
- **Store page:** the fort-logbook line is quoted from the game's Steam store page, not the news feed —
  which is why it does not appear in the announcement counts above.
