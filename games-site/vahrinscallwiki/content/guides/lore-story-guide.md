# Lore & Story Summary — Vahrin, the Khraiax, and the World of Leyda

Vahrin's Call drops you into a war-torn city with almost no hand-holding — and, as launch reviews point out, **no in-game encyclopedia**. The lore is dense: factions, kings, wars, and histories are introduced through conversations, letters, notes, and environmental clues rather than cutscene exposition. It is one of the game's richest qualities, and also one of its least accessible.

This guide collects everything about the story and world that is **confirmed by official materials** — the Steam store page, the official FAQ, and the developer interview with Titan Roc director Craig Smith — and presents it in order. Where something is unknown or unconfirmed, we say so plainly instead of filling the gaps with invented names.

## The World of Leyda and the Forsaken Realms

Vahrin's Call is set in the high fantasy world of **Leyda**, a continent split between **endless darkness and endless sun**. Those hostile extremes are the "**Forsaken Realms**" of the game's full title — the wastelands beyond the settled lands, too dangerous to live in.

The story itself takes place in the **Vale of Vahrin**, the war-torn region around the city of the same name. Per the developers, this story is one entry in an epic, continent-spanning narrative — but it stands as its own complete tale, so you can follow it without having read anything else.

## The City of Vahrin

Vahrin was a great city of the Free Cities before its fall. The player character is a **mercenary** traveling toward the city, unaware of the role they will play in its fate. One thing the developers were explicit about: **you are not a chosen hero**. You are a sellsword with a stake in the city's recovery — and, eventually, in the danger beneath it.

## The Khraiax Invasion

The central event of the game's backstory is the invasion of Vahrin by the **khraiax** — grotesque, insect-like creatures that swarmed the city and overwhelmed its defenders. The invasion did not come from outside. Per official lore, the khraiax were driven **out of the darkness beneath the city itself**.

The response was a rallying of the realm:

- The **Combined Order of Humankind** gathered the armies of the **Free Cities** and marched to Vahrin's aid.
- **King Aldous Symme** led the combined armies into the depths beneath the city, intending to drive the khraiax back "into the darkness from whence they came."
- The armies succeeded — the khraiax "stood little chance against the might of the combined forces."

### The Victory Was Short-Lived

Here is where the official story gets interesting. While the khraiax were being defeated, **"a far greater danger" rose from beneath the city's streets**. The identity of that danger is the game's central mystery — the only confirmed fact is that the true threat is underground. See our [Boss Guide](/guides/boss-guide) for why this matters for endgame encounters.

## The Aftermath

The lore of the game's present day is defined by the consequences of that war:

- The armies of the Free Cities were **decimated**.
- The surviving forces **abandoned Vahrin to its fate** and fled home to their own lands.
- **Vahrin's king lies dead**, along with thousands of the soldiers who marched to the city's aid.
- Only the **most stalwart defenders** remain, fighting desperately to reclaim their beloved city.

The Vale of Vahrin is not only overrun by khraiax remnants — it is beset by **rival mercenaries, bandits, and monstrous beasts** as well. Law and order have collapsed along with the city.

### The Aethspawn Explained

The Aethspawn deserve special attention because their design is deliberately tied to gameplay. The developers took a "yin and yang" approach: the pure spirit appears as **light blue**, while the body is made of dark, oily "**spirit mire**." The spirit shines through the body — showing, from a lore perspective, that the person is still in there — and creating **obvious weak points** on the creature for combat. A spirit's brightness is effectively an invitation to aim there. See the [Enemies Bestiary Guide](/guides/enemies-bestiary-guide) for how they behave in combat.

### No Single "Big Bad"

Director Craig Smith has been explicit: the khraiax are **not** the game's one overarching threat. Titan Roc deliberately avoided building the world around a single enemy faction like a demon army or a zombie apocalypse. The khraiax war is just one small story in a living world with many — including the chaos faction, the Aethspawn, rival mercenaries, and whatever rose from beneath the streets.

## Your Place in the Story

As a mercenary, you work the city's remaining settlements and survivor camps, taking assignments that explore personal tales "of grief, love and even humour." The main quest follows a chapter-based structure of assignments, while side quests are widely reported to be where the world opens up — one early side quest chain reportedly hides a "whole huge area" behind it (see our [Quest Walkthrough](/guides/quest-walkthrough)).

## How the Story Is Told — and Why It Feels Dense

The game deliberately delivers lore through **conversations, letters, notes, and environmental clues** rather than cutscenes. Names of cities, factions, and historical events are introduced gradually. Combined with the absence of an in-game encyclopedia — a criticism raised in launch reviews — this means players are expected to hold a lot of context in their heads.

The practical takeaways:

- **Read everything you find.** Notes and letters carry more worldbuilding than any dialogue option.
- **Talk to every named NPC more than once.** Survivors unlock new context as the story progresses.
- **Return to early zones after main quest beats.** New notes and changed dialogue appear in places you cleared hours earlier.

## The Mystery Beneath the City — What We Know and Don't

To be direct about what remains unknown:

- **Confirmed:** A "far greater danger" rose from beneath the city's streets after the khraiax were defeated, and the endgame threat is underground.
- **Unconfirmed:** What that danger is. The identity of the chaos faction. The fate of the rest of King Aldous Symme's armies. Whether the khraiax have returned in force.

If you come across in-game evidence for any of these, the wiki will update — until then, treat any online answer naming "the true enemy of Vahrin" as speculation.
