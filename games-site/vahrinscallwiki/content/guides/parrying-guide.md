# Parrying Guide — Should You Try to Parry?

"Should I parry in Vahrin's Call?" is one of the most-asked questions about the game — community articles have been written just to answer it. The honest answer, as of launch, is: **parrying is not officially confirmed, but blocking — and the timing skill parrying needs — absolutely is.** This guide separates what Titan Roc and launch reviews confirm from what community guides report, so you can build a defensive kit that works either way.

## What Is Confirmed About Defense

These are the defensive facts you can build on without any speculation:

### Blocking Works

Launch reviews are explicit: **blocking works, and hitboxes are acceptable** — one of the most positive notes in an otherwise mixed review pool. The [Boss Guide](/guides/boss-guide) adds the practical detail: **timing your guard is a viable defensive tool** against the game's heavier enemies.

### Precise Dodge Timing Is the Core Skill

The khraiax fight in swarms, and the consistent advice from confirmed behavior is to **dodge with precise timing** rather than attack through them. Positioning and timing — not raw damage — carry the early game.

### Stamina Paces Everything

A **stamina system limits prolonged magic use** (confirmed), and combat fundamentals follow from it: every defensive action you take competes with every offensive one. If you spend stamina on offense, you have less for the block or dodge that keeps you alive.

### Pattern Recognition Is the Design Language

Boss fights reward **pattern recognition** (confirmed by reviews): attacks have wind-up telegraphs, and the game's deliberate, committed feel rewards watching before acting. This is true of regular combat too — human enemies telegraph their moves, beasts are harder to read.

## What Community Guides Report About Parrying

Community game guides and discussion threads describe a parry mechanic with consistent details. **None of this is confirmed by official sources** — treat it as "community reports suggest," not fact:

| Community report | Detail |
|------------------|--------|
| Parry timing | Block **just before** an enemy's attack lands to parry, causing the enemy to stumble and leave themselves open |
| Counter-attack | Pressing attack immediately after a successful parry performs a counter-attack |
| Not everything can be parried | Giant or heavy enemies' attacks reportedly smash through a parry — some attacks are block-breakers |
| Shields | Shields are reported to offer a more defensive playstyle, automatically deflecting enemy attacks |
| Guard break | Blocking while low on stamina reportedly lets enemies break through your defense |
| Dodge rolls | Reported to grant temporary invincibility frames (i-frames), Souls-style |

The [Combat Guide](/guides/combat-guide) currently classifies **parry timing windows and i-frames as unconfirmed** — that remains the honest position. If parry mechanics exist, they have not been officially documented, and exact windows have not been measured.

## How to Practice the Timing Anyway

Here is the useful part: **the timing skill parrying would require is the same skill confirmed blocking already rewards.** Practicing it costs nothing, because the classless system means nothing you practice is wasted.

### 1. Watch Wind-Ups, Not the Enemy's Body

The game telegraphs attacks — that is its design language. Pick one enemy type (bandits are the most readable confirmed human enemies) and practice reacting to the start of the swing, not the moment of impact. Learn each animation's rhythm before you fight for real.

### 2. Treat Your Guard as a Timing Tool

Since blocking is confirmed functional and timing your guard is confirmed viable, practice **blocking at the last moment** rather than holding guard. If a parry mechanic does exist, this is exactly the habit that activates it; if it does not, you have lost nothing — a timed block is still a block.

### 3. Budget Stamina for Defense First

Whatever defensive style you choose, the stamina bar is the constraint. A defensive rhythm that leaves you no stamina to dodge away from a swarm is a death rhythm. Spend defensively first, offensively second.

### 4. Punish the Recovery Window

After a telegraphed attack misses or is blocked, the enemy is briefly committed. That recovery window is your confirmed opening — counter with a practiced attack, then back off before the next wind-up.

### 5. Experiment Early

The first hours are explicitly the time to experiment — playstyles are switchable at any point, and the classless system grows whatever you practice. Try a last-moment guard rhythm for a few fights. If the game rewards it, you have a style; if not, your block timing still improved.

## The Verdict: Should You Try Parrying?

The honest answer, given what is confirmed:

- **If you already block-and-dodge well**: parry-style timing is a free experiment with no downside. Practice last-moment guards; you may find the game rewards it, and your defense improves regardless.
- **If you are new or struggling**: do not build your defense around an unconfirmed mechanic. Use the confirmed kit — timed block, precise dodge, stamina discipline — and treat parry as an experiment once the fundamentals feel natural.
- **Either way**: do not trust anyone selling you exact parry frames or i-frame counts. No official data exists, and precise numbers online are unverified.

## What Is NOT Confirmed

- Whether a parry mechanic exists at all
- Parry timing windows or i-frame values
- Counter-attack damage multipliers
- Whether shields auto-deflect or provide passive defense
- Guard-break mechanics and stamina damage values
- Which attacks are unblockable or unparryable
- Any parry-related perk, skill, or upgrade

When Titan Roc publishes combat documentation — or when verified in-game footage appears — this guide will be updated with the real numbers.
