There is a specific reader for a boss guide: someone whose character just died, who is now reading on a second screen or a phone, and who wants one thing — what do I do differently.

They are not reading your lore section. They are not reading your 400-word intro about when the boss appears in the story. They are scanning for the mechanic that killed them.

## Structure for the interrupted reader

The ordering that works:

1. **One paragraph: what kills you.** The single most common death, stated in a sentence or two.
2. **The phases, in order, as headings.** Each phase gets its tell, its punish window, and the mistake people make.
3. **What to bring.** Equipment and consumables, in a short list.
4. **Optional: the long version.** Lore, variants, difficulty differences — after everything above.

The last item is not a demotion of the writing. It is an acknowledgment that the reader who wants lore will scroll, and the reader who is about to die will not.

## Write the tell, not the attack

"Watch out for the sweep" is useless. The reader cannot identify a sweep. What helps:

> The boss raises the weapon overhead and holds for about a second before the sweep. That hold is your window to get behind it.

A tell has three parts: what you see, how long you have, what to do. If a sentence is missing one of the three, it is not yet a guide.

## Underline the punish window

Every boss fight is a sequence of "you cannot act" and "you can act". The guide's job is to mark the second. Readers will find the attacks themselves; what they cannot figure out is which openings are real, which is why pages that list "safe windows" are the ones people bookmark.

## Keep the numbers honest

Health thresholds, phase transitions, damage values — these are the most useful and the most fragile parts of the page. If you quote them, quote them with a version. If you cannot verify them, describe the behaviour instead ("switches to the second phase after the arena darkens"), which stays true across patches.

## Why this shape survives

The [guide to the Delverium bosses](https://delveriumwiki.vercel.app/guides/boss-guide) on [Delverium Wiki](https://delveriumwiki.vercel.app) is organised that way — deaths first, phases second, preparation third, everything else last — not because it reads better as an essay, but because it is *findable* by someone scrolling under pressure.

That is the whole test for this page type. If a reader who is currently dying can find the relevant sentence in five seconds, the structure is right. If they have to read the intro to get there, it is not.
