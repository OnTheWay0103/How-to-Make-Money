Every content site starts with the same spreadsheet: a long list of suggested queries, most of them generated by autocomplete, none of them ranked by whether the site should answer them.

Sorting that list by volume is the fastest way to write pages you cannot make good.

## Three filters, applied in order

**1. Can you answer it better than the current result?** Not "can you write something" — can you write something a searcher would prefer. If the answer is a single number you can look up in a table, you probably cannot, and the page will be noise.

**2. Is the answer verifiable?** Either it is in the game, or you can test it, or you can cite an official source. A page built on inference alone is fine as a mechanics page, not as a "what is the best X" page.

**3. Does it belong to a page you already have?** Most suggested queries should not become new pages. They should become sections of an existing page, or a sentence in the opening paragraph. Every new page is a permanent maintenance cost.

Queries that pass all three are worth writing. Most do not.

## What the suggestions are actually good for

Not for discovering topics — for discovering *wording*. A suggestion tells you what people type when they do not know the name of the thing. That phrasing is the most valuable part of the exercise: it is the difference between a page titled with your vocabulary and a page titled with theirs.

So I collect the list, then mostly use it to check that my existing pages use the words people actually search. The gap between the two is where titles get rewritten.

## The part nobody likes

Volume is a filter, not a target. A page that answers a genuine question badly is worse than no page: it competes with the page you should have written, and it makes the site look thin.

The pages that earn their keep are the ones where you can say something specific and checkable — which is why, on a small site, a handful of good pages beats forty autocomplete-shaped ones.

The [beginner guide to Taival](https://taivalwiki.vercel.app/guides/beginner-guide) on [Taival Wiki](https://taivalwiki.vercel.app) exists because it passed all three filters: an answer that is better than the alternatives, verifiable, and a question that a new player genuinely asks. Plenty of higher-volume suggestions did not, and they are sections inside pages rather than pages of their own.
