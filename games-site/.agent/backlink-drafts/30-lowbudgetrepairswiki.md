Search "hidden secrets in [any game]" and you will find pages listing things that do not exist. Not deliberately — they were assembled from other pages that were assembled from other pages, and the original claim was wrong, or in a different game, or cut in a beta.

Secrets pages are the highest-risk content type on a game site, because the whole promise is "this is real and you can find it".

## Rule one: only publish what you have seen

Not "what a video showed" — what you did, in the current build, at the location you describe. Everything else gets dropped or explicitly labelled as unverified.

That sounds obvious. It removes about half of what a secrets page could contain, which is precisely why most secrets pages contain that half.

## Rule two: describe the trigger, not just the reward

"Touching the third pipe reveals a hidden room" is useless without: which pipe, how to tell them apart, whether it needs an item, and whether it is one-time.

A findable entry has four parts:

- **location**, specific enough to walk to;
- **trigger**, what exactly to do;
- **result**, what happens — and whether it is permanent;
- **prerequisites**, items, progression, or patches.

If you cannot fill all four, you have a rumour, not an entry.

## Rule three: separate secrets from easter eggs

A secret changes the game — an item, an area, an ability. An easter egg is a joke. Mixing them means a reader who is looking for a mechanic wades through references, and a reader who wants the references gets a list of mechanics.

Two sections, clearly labelled, and the mechanics first.

## Rule four: say when something does not work

The most trustworthy line on a secrets page is a negative one: "the rumoured cave behind the workshop does not exist in the current build — it was present in the demo and was cut."

That sentence costs you nothing, saves a reader an hour, and is the reason they believe the rest of the list.
