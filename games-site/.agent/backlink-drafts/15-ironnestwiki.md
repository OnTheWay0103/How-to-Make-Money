On a site with no history, every page starts at zero. There is one page type that can compound anyway: the beginner page.

Not because beginners are numerous (they are), but because of what they do afterwards.

## Why it compounds

A beginner guide sits at the start of the funnel. Someone arrives with the vaguest possible intent — "how does this game work" — and leaves with a mental map of your site. If that map is good, the same reader returns for the specific pages later, this time searching for them directly.

That is the only mechanism a small site has for turning one visit into many. A page about a mid-game boss cannot do it: by the time a reader needs it, they already know where they look for answers.

There is a second effect. Beginner pages are linkable. Other people writing about the game, forum answers, aggregators — they all point beginners somewhere. A page that clearly says "start here" is the one that gets picked.

## What makes one work

- **Scope discipline.** It is not a table of contents with commentary. It answers the first hour: what matters, what does not, what to ignore.
- **Concrete first steps.** "Talk to the smith, buy the cheap pickaxe before the expensive one" beats "explore the crafting system".
- **A short answer up top.** Forty to sixty words that stand alone, for the reader who will not scroll.
- **Honest about uncertainty.** If something changed in a recent patch, say so; the reader will trust the rest more.
- **One clear next step.** Link to the two or three pages that follow naturally. Not eight.

That last point matters for internal linking too: the beginner page is the most natural place to route traffic to pages that would otherwise be orphans.

## The mistake I kept making

Writing it last. The beginner page felt like the easy one — no research, no depth, everyone knows what to say. So it got an hour at the end of the build, and it read like it.

The version that works takes as long as any other page, because the difficulty is not knowledge, it is selection: deciding what a new player does *not* need to know yet, and resisting the urge to explain everything you just learned.

The [beginner guide to Iron Nest](https://ironnestwiki.vercel.app/guides/beginner-guide) on [Iron Nest Wiki](https://ironnestwiki.vercel.app) is written to that rule: the first hour only, one next step, everything else deliberately omitted.

On a site with 15 pages, that page is the one that decides whether anyone reads the other 14.
