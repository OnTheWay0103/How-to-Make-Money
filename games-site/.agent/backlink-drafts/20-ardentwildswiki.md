Open any ten game guides and count how many answer the question in the first paragraph. Usually two or three. The rest begin with context: what the game is, when the mechanic unlocks, why it matters.

That intro is written for a reader who arrived with no question. Most readers arrive with one.

## What answer-first looks like

Not a summary. A specific, standalone answer:

> The fastest way to unlock the second island is to finish the three starter deliveries before buying any equipment. Do that first; equipment can wait.

That paragraph works if the reader reads nothing else. It also sets up everything that follows: the rest of the page explains the why, the edge cases, and the alternatives — for the reader who wants them.

The version without it looks like:

> Ardent Wilds is a survival game released in early access. In this guide, we will cover everything you need to know about unlocking new areas, including…

That promises a guide. The first version gives one.

## Why 40–60 words

Long enough to contain a real answer, short enough to be read on a phone before the reader decides to scroll. Under 40 words and it is usually a fragment that needs the next paragraph to make sense; over 60 and you are writing an introduction again, just faster.

It also happens to be the length that search engines reliably use for snippets, which is a nice side effect of the same constraint.

## What goes after

The rest of the page, in order of how likely the reader needs it:

- **The steps**, if the answer involves a process.
- **The exceptions**, if the answer is not universal.
- **The alternatives**, if there is more than one reasonable path.
- **The context**, last. Lore, patch history, and background are for the reader who stayed — and they will still be there.

## The harder half

Answer-first writing is easy to adopt and hard to keep, because it forces you to commit. You cannot write "it depends" in the first paragraph, even when it does. You have to name the common case, answer for it, and then say who the answer does not apply to.

That is uncomfortable, and it is exactly the work a reader is paying attention for. A page that hedges in the first paragraph announces that it does not know the answer either.

The [beginner guide to Ardent Wilds](https://ardentwildswiki.vercel.app/guides/beginner-guide) on [Ardent Wilds Wiki](https://ardentwildswiki.vercel.app) is written to that rule: the first paragraph is the answer, and the rest of the page earns its length.
