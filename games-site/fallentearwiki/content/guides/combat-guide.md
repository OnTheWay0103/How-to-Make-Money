# Fallen Tear: The Ascension Combat Guide

Fallen Tear: The Ascension's combat is **fast, skill-based, and built around three verbs: slash, parry, and dodge** [Official]. The Steam description emphasizes skill-based combat, and Early Access reviews describe it as "rapid, based on millimetric dodges and parries" [Community]. Because the full 1.0 game launches September 16, 2026, exact damage values, skill names, and enemy stats are [Unconfirmed] — this guide covers the confirmed structure and how to approach it.

## The Three Pillars

### Slash — The Korr Blade
Hira fights with the **Korr Blade**, an enchanted melee weapon [Community]. Your basic attacks chain into combos, and the game rewards aggressive, well-timed strings over button mashing.

- **Basic string** — a series of slashes that keep enemies locked in hitstun.
- **Launching and juggling** — some attacks launch enemies into the air, letting you follow up with aerial combos. This juggling is a signature of the combat feel [Community].
- **Blink-Strike** — a teleport-style backstab that closes distance and deals bonus damage from behind [Community].

### Parry
Parrying deflects incoming attacks and opens enemies to counterattacks. The window is **strict** — "millimetric" is the word reviewers use [Community].

- **When to parry:** single, telegraphed enemy attacks and boss combos.
- **When not to:** attacks with lingering hitboxes, or when surrounded.
- **Payoff:** a successful parry usually staggers the attacker, creating a free damage window [Editorial].

### Dodge
Dodging moves Hira through or around attacks. It is your most reliable defensive tool and the foundation of survival in multi-enemy rooms.

- **Use dodges** to reposition, escape corners, and weave through boss patterns.
- **Dodge-cancel** attack recoveries to stay mobile — this is where high-level players spend their time [Editorial].

## The Overgrowth Ultimate

**Overgrowth** is Hira's ultimate ability — a transformation into a **faster, stronger bestial form** that can be unleashed in combat [Community]. Early reporting describes it as a dramatic burst option for tough fights.

- **When to use it:** boss phases, overwhelming mobs, and recovery situations where raw damage is the answer [Editorial].
- **When to save it:** exploration, where a well-placed Blink-Strike or parry does the job without the cooldown cost [Editorial].

Exact unlock conditions, duration, and cooldown are not published [Unconfirmed].

## Fated Bond Combat Support

Fated Bonds aren't just map tools — they **fight with you** [Official]. Allies provide:

- **Burst damage** — summoned for combined attacks that add a strategic layer missing from many Metroidvanias [Community].
- **Buffs** — temporary power-ups that shift a fight in your favor.
- **Enemy disruption** — crowd control that splits up dangerous groups.

The Joypad Journal review notes that "summoning companions for combined attacks adds a strategic layer that is missing in many other genre representatives" [Community]. This means **rotating your active bond** per fight is part of the combat loop — bring a healer to endurance fights, a damage ally to DPS checks, and a trickster to chaotic mobs [Editorial].

## Combat by Situation

### Exploration Fights
- Keep parries for ambush enemies and dodge through crowds.
- Save Overgrowth for emergencies; basic combos clear most trash.
- Let your Fated Bond handle disruption so you can focus on positioning [Editorial].

### Boss Fights
- **Learn the parry rhythm first.** Most bosses have a tell that leads into a punishable string [Community].
- **Dodge the unparryable.** AoE and grab attacks usually can't be parried — dodge them.
- **Unleash Overgrowth in the danger phase.** Bosses often speed up when low on health; a well-timed ultimate ends the fight before the pattern kills you [Editorial].
- **Rotate bonds.** If a boss resists your current ally's damage type, swap.

See the [Boss Guide](/guides/boss-guide) for the full pre-release boss strategy framework.

## Building a Combat Toolkit (Pre-Release)

Combat power comes from four confirmed sources [Official]:

1. **Korr Blade upgrades** — your weapon's base stats and moveset.
2. **Ascension and skill trees** — passive and active upgrades. The 1.0 adds 3 Ascensions, 6 Hunter Skills, 4 Elemental Imbuements, and 16 Mastery Skills [Official].
3. **Fated Bonds** — allies that fight with you and reshape your options.
4. **Overgrowth** — the ultimate that sits on top of everything else.

A simple pre-release rule: **have one parry tool, one mobility tool, and one burst tool.** The game gives you parry (the blade), dodge (movement), and Overgrowth (burst) — build the rest of your kit around covering their gaps [Editorial].
