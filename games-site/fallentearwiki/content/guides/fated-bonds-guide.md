# Fallen Tear: The Ascension Fated Bonds Guide

**Fated Bonds** are the heart of Fallen Tear: The Ascension. The Steam description puts it plainly: *"A story-driven Metroidvania where the allies you choose to bond with change how you fight and explore"* [Official]. These recruitable allies are mages, swordsmen, tricksters, and healers whose unique combat and traversal abilities reshape the game [Official].

Early Access shipped with **10 Fated Bonds**. The 1.0 update on September 16, 2026 adds **11 more** — roughly **21 total** [Official]. (Note: the Steam store description says "up to 22 allies," a figure not yet reconciled with the press-release total of 21 [Unconfirmed].)

## How Recruitment Works

Fated Bonds are recruited by befriending characters and completing their story content [Official]:

- **Find the character** — named allies appear throughout Raoah, often behind side paths.
- **Complete their sidequest** — each bond has sidequest and story content tied to them.
- **Unlock their ability** — once bonded, the ally's combat and traversal ability becomes available to Hira.

The official material frames bonds as the game's party system: you collect allies the way a JRPG collects party members, but each one mechanically reshapes a Metroidvania moveset [Editorial].

## The Confirmed Roster

These allies are named in the official **Fated Bonds trailer** and press material [Community]:

**Trystan · Lenela · Nesta · Cadel · Magnus · Dagoth · Primorin · Sendrin · Korin · Durin · Zozrin · Kinoro · Demir · Enid · Gwinri · Gunnar · Runa · Idris · Annie**

Two allies have confirmed roles from reviews:

- **Annie — the puppeteer.** A combat-support ally whose puppeteering disrupts enemies [Community].
- **Zozrin — the cartographer.** An ally tied to map abilities — a traversal-style bond that helps you navigate Raoah [Community].
- **Miah — the healer.** Miah is the healer who adopted Hira [Community]. Whether Miah becomes a recruitable Fated Bond is [Unconfirmed].

The remaining allies' individual roles have not been published — their abilities are [Unconfirmed] until 1.0.

## Role Archetypes (Confirmed)

The official description confirms the game's ally archetypes [Official]:

| Archetype | What They Do | Best For |
|-----------|-------------|----------|
| **Mage** | Ranged burst damage and elemental coverage | Bosses with weaknesses; large enemies |
| **Swordsman** | Direct melee burst and combo support | Fast duels, sustained pressure |
| **Trickster** | Utility, disruption, crowd control | Mobs, traversal puzzles |
| **Healer** | Recovery and sustain | Long explorations, endurance bosses |

The 1.0 **Elemental Imbuements** likely pair with mage-type bonds for weakness-based fights [Editorial].

## Which Fated Bonds to Recruit First

This is editorial pre-release guidance — not verified stats [Editorial]:

1. **A traversal bond first.** Map-opening abilities (like a cartographer's) pay off for the entire run by unlocking regions, items, and other bonds.
2. **A combat bond second.** Have one ally who joins fights before the first major bosses.
3. **A healer before the long expeditions.** Sustain matters in the 35–40 hour campaign.
4. **Everyone, eventually.** Every bond carries sidequest and story content, and bonds shape the multiple endings [Official]. Completionists and ending-chasers will want the full roster.

## How Bonds Interact With Other Systems

- **Temple of Oras:** The hub is where you strengthen bonds with allies, unlock minigames, and improve Hira's stats [Official]. Your bond roster lives here.
- **Endings:** The official press release confirms multiple endings shaped by player choices, including bonds formed with Fated Bonds [Official]. The exact ending requirements are [Unconfirmed].
- **Combat:** Allies join fights with burst damage, buffs, and disruption — rotating your active bond per fight is part of the loop [Community].

## Bond Traversal vs Combat, Explained

The single most important distinction in the Fated Bonds system is **traversal bonds vs combat bonds** [Editorial]:

| Type | What They Do | Why They Matter |
|------|--------------|-----------------|
| **Traversal bonds** | Grant new ways to move around Raoah | Open the map — the core Metroidvania progression |
| **Combat bonds** | Join fights with burst, buffs, or disruption | Make boss fights manageable |

The official description says allies have "unique combat and traversal abilities" [Official] — so most bonds likely do both, but each is weighted toward one. A cartographer like **Zozrin** is the clearest traversal example (map abilities) [Community]; a puppeteer like **Annie** is the clearest combat example [Community].

## How to Rotate Bonds for Bosses

Bond abilities are likely **equippable or swappable**, and rotating your active bond is part of the strategy [Editorial]:

- **Endurance bosses** → bring a healer-type bond for sustain.
- **DPS checks** → bring a burst-damage bond (a swordsman or mage).
- **Mob-heavy arenas** → bring a trickster for disruption.
- **New regions** → bring a traversal bond to unlock paths.

Pre-release, the safest approach is to **recruit a balanced early trio** — one traversal, one combat, one healer — and swap based on the next objective [Editorial].
