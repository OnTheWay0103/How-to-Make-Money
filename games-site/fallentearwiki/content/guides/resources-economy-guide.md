# Fallen Tear: The Ascension Resources & Economy Guide

Every Metroidvania runs on a resource loop, and **Fallen Tear: The Ascension** is no exception. You gather materials while exploring Raoah, then spend them on upgrades at the **Temple of Oras** hub [Official]. Because the full game launches September 16, 2026, exact currency names and costs are [Unconfirmed] — this guide covers the confirmed loop and how to spend wisely.

## The Resource Loop

The confirmed economy loop:

1. **Explore and fight.** Regions of Raoah are full of materials, items, and upgrade resources dropped from enemies and found in the environment [Editorial].
2. **Return to the Temple of Oras.** The hub is where you "improve Hira's stats via Hira Skills and Mastery Skills" [Community].
3. **Spend on upgrades.** Skills, stat boosts, and Temple customization all draw from your gathered resources.
4. **Build the Temple.** The 1.0 announcement frames the hub as a castle you build and customize into "a living reflection of Hira's journey" [Official].

## What Resources Fund

Based on the confirmed systems, resources feed four buckets [Editorial]:

| Bucket | Confirmed? | What It Funds |
|--------|-----------|---------------|
| **Hira's stats** | Community | Health, damage, and mobility via skills |
| **Skill trees** | Official | Ascension, Hunter, Mastery, and Imbuement purchases |
| **Temple building** | Official | Hub customization and structure |
| **Bond sidequests** | Official | Fated Bond story content and unlocks |

## Where Resources Come From

- **Enemy drops** — defeated enemies drop upgrade materials.
- **Environment pickups** — items scattered through regions, often behind traversal-gated paths.
- **Boss rewards** — major bosses are the reliable source of large resource payouts [Editorial].
- **Bond rewards** — completing Fated Bond sidequests returns resources alongside story [Official].

## How to Spend Wisely (Pre-Release)

Because exact costs are [Unconfirmed], this is a framework [Editorial]:

1. **Invest in movement first.** Mobility upgrades (dash, double jump) open the map and make every future resource run faster. This is the highest-ROI spending in any Metroidvania.
2. **One damage skill before bosses.** A single well-upgraded Hunter Skill beats three half-upgraded ones.
3. **Sustain before survivability.** Healer bonds and recovery items keep expeditions going longer than raw HP.
4. **Don't hoard for the hub's sake.** The Temple customization is cosmetic-to-progression; spend on stats before decoration if you're struggling.

## Avoiding a Resource Wall

The classic Metroidvania failure is hitting a boss under-leveled with no resources left. To avoid it:

- **Clear side paths before boss gates.** Boss areas usually sit behind traversal-gated side content; clearing it funds your attempt.
- **Return to the Temple before every boss.** Even a small upgrade swing changes a fight.
- **Farm efficient loops.** Learn which regions have dense enemy clusters and fast respawns — that's your upgrade fuel [Editorial].

## Temple of Oras as an Economy Hub

The Temple is the economic center of the game [Official]:

- **Stat upgrades** — improve Hira's stats via skills [Community].
- **Bond management** — strengthen bonds and unlock their minigames [Official].
- **Customization** — build the Temple into a reflection of Hira's journey [Official].

Spending at the Temple is how gathered resources become power. See the [Temple of Oras Guide](/guides/temple-of-oras-guide) for the full breakdown.

## Likely Resource Types

While exact names are [Unconfirmed], a Metroidvania-JRPG hybrid economy usually includes several tiers of currency and material [Editorial]:

- **Common drops** — from standard enemies; the everyday upgrade fuel.
- **Rare materials** — from optional bosses, hidden chests, and minigame rewards; fund the big Temple buildings.
- **Bond-specific items** — gifts or materials needed for Fated Bond sidequests and bond strengthening [Official].
- **Boss loot** — large resource payouts that make main-boss clears feel rewarding.

Expect the common materials to be abundant and the rare ones to gate the most powerful upgrades and Temple expansions [Editorial].

## The Risk of Over-Farming

A common mistake in games with a strong hub-economy is **over-farming early** — spending hours grinding resources that become trivial later. To avoid wasted time [Editorial]:

- **Farm only what unlocks the next gate.** If you can attempt the next boss or region, do that instead of grinding.
- **Let bond sidequests be your resource source.** Sidequests return resources alongside story, so you are never farming just for materials [Official].
- **Spend before you farm more.** Resources in your inventory are power not yet realized — convert them at the Temple [Community].

## Minigames as an Economy Source

The Temple of Oras unlocks **minigames** [Official]. Their rewards are [Unconfirmed], but minigames in hub-based JRPGs typically feed the resource economy — expect them to be a reliable, low-risk source of materials or bond-gift items [Editorial]. If you are short on a rare material, check whether a minigame offers it before grinding enemies.

## A Sample Early-Game Spending Plan

A pre-release template for your first few hours [Editorial]:

1. **First upgrade: mobility.** Dash or double-jump upgrades pay off all game [Community].
2. **Second: one damage skill.** Pick a Hunter Skill and take it to its tier unlock [Official].
3. **Then: a heal/recovery option.** Sustain extends expeditions and reduces retries.
4. **Temple building last.** Cosmetic/customization spending is satisfying but should wait until your combat kit is solid [Editorial].
