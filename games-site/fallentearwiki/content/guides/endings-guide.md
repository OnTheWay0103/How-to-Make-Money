# Fallen Tear: The Ascension Multiple Endings Guide

**Fallen Tear: The Ascension** is a story-driven Metroidvania with **multiple possible endings** [Official]. The 1.0 press release confirms that the story's outcome is **shaped by player choices** — including the **bonds you form** with Fated Bonds allies [Official].

Because the game's ending content ships with 1.0 on September 16, 2026, the *specifics* of how each ending unlocks are [Unconfirmed]. This guide covers what is confirmed about the ending system and how to approach it.

## What Is Confirmed

1. **Multiple endings exist.** The press release explicitly confirms "multiple possible endings" [Official].
2. **Choices shape the outcome.** Player decisions during the campaign influence which ending you get [Official].
3. **Fated Bonds matter.** The bonds you form are called out as a factor in the ending [Official].
4. **The EA build had no ending.** Early Access covered about 35% of the game and stopped mid-story — reviews noted the story's ending was missing [Community].

## Why Bonds Likely Matter

Fallen Tear blends "the emotional weight and party-building of classic JRPGs" with Metroidvania exploration [Official]. In JRPG tradition, ending branches usually hinge on **relationship/party choices** — which allies you recruit, complete sidequests for, and keep close [Editorial].

The confirmed ingredients of the ending system [Editorial]:

| Factor | Confirmed? | Likely Effect |
|--------|-----------|---------------|
| **Bond recruitment** | Official | Missing allies may lock out certain endings |
| **Bond sidequests** | Official | Completing them may be required for the "best" endings |
| **Story choices** | Official | Dialogue and moral decisions branch the narrative |
| **Gods defeated** | Community | Which corrupted gods you face (Haslan/Ymara/Siv/Evan) may gate story paths |

## The Corrupted Gods and the Narrative Spine

Raoah is a world "balanced by its gods" who have **fallen from grace** [Official]. The four confirmed gods — **Haslan** (Earth), **Ymara** (Fire), **Siv** (Wind), **Evan** (Water) [Community] — are the story's antagonists and the source of the corruption.

In a choice-shaped JRPG-style story, your path through the gods likely determines which ending branch you're on [Editorial]. Whether the ending is "restore the gods," "destroy them," or "ascend Hira instead" is [Unconfirmed].

## How to Chase Multiple Endings (Pre-Release Strategy)

Until the requirements are documented, use this framework to maximize your chances of seeing everything [Editorial]:

1. **Recruit every Fated Bond.** Bonds are confirmed to shape endings. Skip none if you want the full story.
2. **Complete all bond sidequests.** Bond story content is confirmed to exist and is the most likely ending gate.
3. **Keep a save before major choices.** The story branches; keeping a save at the point before the final act lets you replay for different outcomes.
4. **Save before the last boss.** Different final choices usually fork near the end.
5. **Expect a "true" ending requirement.** Games with bond systems usually gate the best ending behind near-complete bond collection [Editorial].

## The "Golden Ending" Question

It is common for games like this to have a **"true" or "golden" ending** that requires near-completionist play — all bonds, all sidequests, all optional bosses [Editorial]. Since the 1.0 adds 19 optional bosses and 11 new bonds, a golden ending would tie into those [Official]. This is inference — [Unconfirmed].

## How JRPG-Style Endings Usually Work

Fallen Tear draws on "the emotional weight and party-building of classic JRPGs" [Official]. In that tradition, multiple endings typically follow one of these structures — use these as a mental model, not confirmed fact [Editorial]:

1. **Bond-requirement endings.** The "best" ending requires recruiting and completing all or most Fated Bonds, while a leaner run yields a bittersweet or dark ending.
2. **Choice-fork endings.** A late-game decision (often near the final boss) splits the story into two or more distinct conclusions.
3. **Accumulated-flag endings.** Small choices throughout the campaign set invisible flags that determine which ending you unlock — the hardest structure to game without a guide.

Because the press release explicitly names both **choices** and **bonds** [Official], Fallen Tear most likely combines structures 1 and 2 [Editorial].

## Replaying for Multiple Endings

The 1.0 content spans 35–40 hours for one story path [Official]. Replaying the full campaign for each ending is expensive — most players will instead:

- **Keep a save at the final-act branch.** If the endings fork late (as most JRPG-style endings do), one save covers multiple outcomes [Editorial].
- **Save before the last boss.** Even if the fork is a single dialogue choice, this save is your cheapest replay point.
- **Do the bond-completion sweep on one run.** A single near-completionist run unlocks the highest requirement, so you only need alt-endings from the final fork afterward [Editorial].

## How to Read the Ending Without Spoilers

Until launch, avoid ending guides if you value surprises. Instead, track your own run:

- **Note which bonds you missed.** If you want to see more endings on a future run, recruit the ones you skipped.
- **Track your key choices.** A screenshot or note of major decisions makes a second run's differences obvious.
- **Use the completion checklist.** All 21 bonds, all 11 main bosses, and all 22 optional bosses [Official] is the likeliest path to the fullest ending [Editorial].
