# Factions & Affinity Guide — Merchants, Mystics, Scholars, Nobles, the People

Every decision you make in Sovereign Tower moves the realm's opinion of you. The official description frames it as balancing your affinity with **the people, the nobles, and even other rulers**; community guides list the core groups as **Merchants, Mystics, Scholars, Nobles, and the People**, with the **Treasury** as a sixth pressure point. This guide explains how the system works and how to keep every faction — barely — happy.

## The Five Factions

| Faction | What They Represent | Pleasing Them Costs |
|---------|--------------------|--------------------|
| **Merchants** | Trade, gold, shop stock | Favourable trade deals, market decisions |
| **Mystics** | Magic, the supernatural | Supporting arcane interests over practical ones |
| **Scholars** | Knowledge, research | Funding study over spending |
| **Nobles** | Aristocracy, tradition | Privilege, ceremony, restraint on the People |
| **The People** | Common folk | Cheap food, fairness, relief |

Every audience choice and quest resolution shifts these meters — and **almost every choice raises one faction while lowering another**. Guides are unanimous: there is usually **no answer that pleases everyone** (per finalboss.io).

## The Treasury Is a Faction Too

Gold is a strained resource competed for by armour repairs, equipment, consumables, and audience requests (per finalboss.io). Financial decisions shape which audience choices are even available. Letting the Treasury empty is a fail state as surely as any faction revolt — see the [Economy guide](/guides/economy-guide).

## Balance First, Specialise Second

The community consensus (finalboss.io, Tpose Gaming):

- **Steady, moderate approval across all factions beats one sky-high faction.** Consistently favouring one side makes later decisions harder and triggers crises — an **aristocratic uprising** if you repeatedly side with the People against the Nobles, or a **civil-war event** if the People fall to your lowest faction.
- **High standing unlocks content**: new dialogue options, better shop items, and different quests.
- **Penalties snowball.** Ignoring a faction long enough causes penalties to accumulate, so treat audience choices as long-term relationship management.
- That said, the game's own description allows the chaotic alternative: spend it all and backstab everyone. Both approaches are viable — they just lead to very different reigns.

## Your Four Sovereign Stats

Your dialogue choices also build **Audacity, Wisdom, Tyranny, and Kindness** — four personality stats that are separate from faction meters. Each has three thresholds unlocking new dialogue options, and some options only appear when one stat clearly leads another (per finalboss.io). A Tyranny-heavy Sovereign plays differently from a Kindness-heavy one, and the faction meters remember it.

## Diplomacy Beyond the Court

Faction diplomacy reaches beyond audiences:

- **Quests** involve trade disputes, mediating between druids and angry peasants, neighbouring kingdoms, and alliances — and **alliances can be lost through failed missions** (per reviews).
- **Knight preferences interact with diplomacy**: Angelica loves mediation, so diplomatic missions keep her satisfied.
- **Marriage is a diplomatic tool.** One reviewer describes avoiding a noble uprising by marrying a recruited knight — the wedding assuaged the nobles and prevented war. See the [Romance guide](/guides/romance-guide).

## Rival Rulers: The Sixth Actor

The official description adds one more pressure group: **"even other rulers."** Neighbouring kingdoms appear through quests — trade disputes, alliances, mediation between druids and angry peasants — and **alliances can be lost through failed missions** (per reviews). The rival-ruler layer means faction management is not just internal: your reputation with your own factions shapes how outside powers treat you, and diplomatic marriages can settle foreign conflicts that faction meters alone cannot (see the [Romance guide](/guides/romance-guide)). [Unconfirmed — the exact scope of the rival-ruler system beyond quests and alliances is not documented by the community at the time of writing.]

## Crisis Playbook

When a faction is about to break:

1. **Identify which choice pattern caused it.** The meters don't drift randomly — your last several audience picks did this.
2. **Rewind with a correction plan**, not a do-over. The Demon's rewind is the escape hatch for reputation spirals (see the [Time Rewind guide](/guides/rewind-guide)).
3. **Feed the wounded faction with quests**, not just audience picks — some quests explicitly court a faction's favour.
4. **If the uprising fires anyway, let it.** Civil-war and uprising events are content: Daguez's recruitment *requires* the People hitting rock bottom, and surviving an uprising opens routes a clean run never sees.

## Quick Reference

| Rule | Why |
|------|-----|
| Never max one faction at another's expense | Crises and uprising events |
| Keep the Treasury solvent | Empty Treasury = fail state |
| Dialogue builds Audacity/Wisdom/Tyranny/Kindness | Unlocks options at thresholds |
| Marriage can stop a war | Romance is a faction tool |
| Rewind spirals, not small losses | The Demon is a slippery slope |
