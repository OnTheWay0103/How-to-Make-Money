# Sovereign Tower FAQ — 12 Questions Players Actually Ask

Short answers to the questions players keep asking about **Sovereign Tower**. For full detail, follow the links to the dedicated guides.

## What Is Sovereign Tower?

Sovereign Tower is a story-rich, **round-table management RPG** developed by **WILD WITS** (a Rennes-based French indie studio) and published by **Curve Games**. You are the new Sovereign of a magical tower: recruit eccentric knights, send them on quests, balance the realm's factions, and rewind time with the help of a Demon in the crypts. It blends visual-novel storytelling with court management. See the [Beginner Guide](/guides/beginner-guide) for how the loop actually plays.

## When Did Sovereign Tower Release?

**August 6, 2026** on PC (Steam), available from 5pm BST (per the official Curve Games press release). A free extended demo (Act 0 + Act 1) was available during Steam Next Fest from June 15, 2026, and an earlier demo appeared on itch.io on January 16, 2026. See the [Price, Platforms & System Requirements guide](/guides/price-platforms) for the full timeline.

## How Much Does Sovereign Tower Cost?

**$19.99** on Steam, with a **15% launch discount ($16.99)** during the first week. A supporter bundle with the soundtrack (including two songs by Hildegard Von Blingin') was also available at launch. Prices change — check the Steam store page before buying.

## What Platforms Is It On?

Windows and Linux via **Steam**, and it is **Steam Deck Verified**. It does **not** support macOS, and no console versions have been announced. See the [Price & Platforms guide](/guides/price-platforms).

## Is Sovereign Tower Multiplayer?

No. It is a **single-player** game — no co-op or multiplayer mode.

## How Many Knights Can I Recruit?

Community guides list **23–24 recruitable knights**, but the Round Table only holds **6 seats in Act 1, 8 in Act 2, and 10 in Act 3**. Some recruits are mutually exclusive (e.g., Brunhilda vs Tarcus), and you cannot dismiss knights — so a single run cannot collect everyone. New Game Plus is the intended way to fill the roster. See the [Knight Recruitment guide](/guides/knight-recruitment-guide).

## Does Sovereign Tower Have Romance?

Yes. The official feature list promises "forbidden romances" between quests, and satisfied knights can develop into "deep friendships or even romances." Romance is also a diplomatic tool — one reviewer describes avoiding a noble uprising by marrying a recruited knight. See the [Romance Options guide](/guides/romance-guide).

## How Does Saving Work?

The game saves your progress automatically, with Steam Cloud sync available. Community players recommend saving often, especially before risky quest assignments — the game has a low error tolerance (per a community post on GCORES). The exact save-file location on disk is **[Unconfirmed]** at the time of writing.

## What Are the System Requirements?

Per the Steam store page — **Minimum:** Windows 10, Intel Core i5-4670K, 4 GB RAM, GeForce GT 1030 (2 GB), DirectX 12, 3 GB storage. Linux/SteamOS is supported. Full detail in the [Price & Platforms guide](/guides/price-platforms).

## How Long Is Sovereign Tower?

Per IGN: roughly **8 hours** for the main story and around **18 hours** for story plus side content. Third-party tracker Gamalytic reported an average playtime of about **7.3 hours** as of late August 2026. The time-rewind mechanic makes playthrough length vary a lot, and New Game Plus adds more. See the [How Long to Beat guide](/guides/game-length).

## What Languages Are Supported?

The Steam Next Fest demo was localized in **English, French, Simplified Chinese, German, Korean, and Japanese** (6 languages), and the full game launched with the same set per store listings.

## Is the Game Any Good? What Do Reviews Say?

At the time of writing: **~91% "Very Positive"** on Steam (about 1,640 reviews per third-party snapshots) and **Metacritic 86**. PC Gamer called it "one of the best management games around, featuring gaming's best implementation of time travel" (91/100). Common criticisms: UX friction (you can't dismiss knights, and the skill-upgrade menu is easy to miss), late-game grammar errors (the studio is French-speaking — the English text has quirks late in the campaign), and gameplay that "runs out of steam" toward the end.

## What Games Is It Most Like?

Reviewers consistently compare the knight-dispatch core to **Dispatch** and **Reigns** — you assign the right person to the right task under constant pressure — but note Sovereign Tower builds a far richer structure around it: full character arcs, romance, faction politics, and the knowledge-retaining time rewind. It is also the "thoughtful evolution of the Kingdom Management genre" per early reviews, blending interactive fiction with management-puzzle depth. It is not an action game and not a strategy 4X — it is a management RPG you mostly play by reading and choosing.
