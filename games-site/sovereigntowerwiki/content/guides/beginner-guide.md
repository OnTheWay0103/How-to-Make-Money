# Beginner Guide — Getting Started in Sovereign Tower

Welcome to **Sovereign Tower**, the story-rich round-table management RPG from **WILD WITS**, published by **Curve Games**. The game launched on **August 6, 2026** on PC (Steam) with a **91% "Very Positive"** rating (about 1,640 reviews at the time of writing, per third-party snapshots) and a **Metacritic score of 86**. This guide covers what the game is, how its daily loop works, and a plan for your first cycles.

## What Kind of Game Is This?

Sovereign Tower is a **management RPG fused with a visual novel**. You play the new Sovereign of a magical tower — an Arthurian-inspired ruler of Britannia — who must keep the kingdom alive while managing a court of eccentric knights. Reviewers have compared the knight-dispatch core to *Dispatch* and *Reigns*, but Sovereign Tower builds a richer structure around it: recruitable knights with backstories and grudges, five factions to balance, and a time-rewind mechanic that lets you fix your worst decisions.

The game is **text-heavy and dialogue-driven** (per multiple reviews), so expect to read a lot. There is no combat you control directly — you win or lose through the knights you assign and the choices you make.

## The Daily Loop: Three Phases

Each cycle follows the same rhythm (per IGN's hands-on preview and multiple reviews):

1. **Morning audience.** Sit on the throne and hear your subjects' requests, complaints, and grievances. Your dialogue choices matter — they shift faction approval and your own personality stats.
2. **Round Table meeting.** Assign knights to quests. Each quest asks for different stats (Strength, Agility, Charisma, Magic, Wit — and Luck in some community stat sheets).
3. **Rest and reports.** The cycle ends, results come in, and knights level up, get hurt, or — if you misjudged them — quit or die.

## Your First Cycle: What to Do

Per community guides (allthings.how's first-cycle walkthrough and tposegaming's beginner guide), the opening is forgiving if you follow the story prompts. The essentials:

- **Angelica is your starter knight** and your best early asset. She joins during the opening story. She is an animal lover with high Agility and Luck — send her on quests that match her profile.
- **Read the quest text carefully.** Bold or highlighted words in quest descriptions are hints about which traits and preferences matter. Matching traits beats raw stats (per the IGN preview).
- **Do not send the wrong knight to the wrong quest.** Failure is not the only risk: knights can be offended so badly they leave the Round Table for good, or die on the quest. Their levels leave with them.
- **Keep the Treasury healthy.** Tax revenue funds equipment, armour repairs, and tower upgrades. You cannot repair every suit of armour every cycle — the blacksmith fixes one knight's armour per cycle (per the COGconnected preview).

## Biggest Early Mistakes

1. **Ignoring knight affinity.** Affinity runs roughly −10 to +10. At the low end knights resign permanently — community data points to −7 as the resign threshold. Feed them favourite meals (+1.5 affinity per community guides) and send them on quests they enjoy.
2. **Siding with the same faction every time.** Faction approval is a zero-sum balancing act. Consistently favouring the People against the Nobles can trigger an aristocratic uprising (per finalboss.io's faction guide).
3. **Avoiding the time rewind entirely — or spamming it.** The Demon in the crypts rewinds time and you keep your knowledge, but the cost is deliberately mysterious, and rewinding erases good branches too (per tposegaming and the PC Gamer review). Use it with a specific correction in mind, not for minor annoyances.
4. **Ignoring quest stat requirements.** Quest requirements are known to the community (e.g., a Magic 14+ knight for "Repel the dragon," Wits 12+ for "Suspects interrogations"). Sending an under-qualified knight risks death.
5. **Letting everyone die for lack of armour.** A knight's armour score hitting zero means permanent death. Prioritise repairs for your best knights.

## What to Do When Things Go Wrong

The game has explicit fail states — death, uprising, ruin (per the official feature list and reviews). When a run goes sideways:

- **Rewind time** via the Demon in the crypts. You keep your knowledge from the failed timeline, which unlocks new dialogue options and paths (per the official press release).
- **Start a new run.** New Game Plus is available, and multiple playthroughs surface new content (per PC Gamer's review). Several knights have mutually exclusive recruitment routes, so the game is designed to be played more than once.

|--------|--------------|

| Affinity | −10 to +10; low affinity = knights resign for good |
| Armour | Hitting zero = permanent death; blacksmith repairs one suit per cycle |
| Time rewind | Demon in the crypts; keeps your knowledge; cost is mysterious |
| Length | ~8h main story, ~18h with side content (per IGN) |
