# Treasury & Economy Guide — How to Keep Your Reign Solvent

Sovereign Tower runs on gold, and gold is always scarce. **Tax revenue** funds equipment (horses, swords, magic potions), castle facilities (the Blacksmith's Forge, the Alchemy Room), and new annexes (per the official press release). The Treasury is also the fuel for audience choices, quests, and repairs. This guide lays out where the money goes and what to spend it on first.

## Where Money Comes From

- **Taxes.** Your kingdom generates revenue each cycle. Revenue is not a knob you directly control — it follows your reign's stability, faction approval, and completed quests (per the official feature list and finalboss.io's cycle analysis).
- **Quest rewards.** Knight quests pay out, but the rewards are not the point — completing the *right* quests keeps factions happy, which keeps tax revenue stable.
- **Faction-linked shop access.** Higher standing with a faction unlocks better shop items (per finalboss.io). Money buys access only when the factions are healthy.

## Where Money Goes

The constant drains, per previews and community guides:

| Expense | Cost Driver | Notes |
|---------|-------------|-------|
| **Armour repairs** | The blacksmith (Carina's Forge) | Repairs only **one knight's armour per cycle** — prioritise |
| **Equipment** | Horses, swords, potions | Knights perform better (and survive more) with gear |
| **Tower annexes** | One-time builds | The Forge and Alchemy Room pay for themselves over a run |
| **Map Room summons** | 25 gold per summon | Emergency recruitment when a knight is unavailable |
| **Audience requests** | Variable | Saying yes costs gold, saying no costs faction approval |

## The Repair Bottleneck

The single most important economic constraint early on: **the blacksmith can repair only one knight's armour per cycle** (per the COGconnected preview). A knight whose armour hits zero is permanently dead, so:

1. **Repair the knight you are about to send into danger** — not the one sitting in the tower.
2. **Rotate wounded knights.** Send the fresh one out while the wounded one recovers in the court.
3. **Do not let repairs compete with recruitments.** If a Map Room summon (25 gold) would leave you unable to repair a questing knight, the summon can wait.

## Alchemy: Potions Are Worth It

Belladonna's **Alchemy Room** brews **stat potions** that assist knights on quests (per the official press release and IGN preview). Potions are the only way to boost a knight past a quest threshold you cannot meet otherwise — a Magic potion on a 13-Magic knight makes "Repel the dragon" (Magic 14+) survivable. If a critical quest is one stat point out of reach, potions beat gambling on a bad send.

## Spending Priorities by Phase

**Early game (Act 1):** repairs > equipment for your best knight > annexes. Your starter knights (Angelica, Goberto, Gwendan) are level 1 — gear and repairs keep them alive while they level.

**Mid game (Act 2):** annexes (Forge + Alchemy Room) > Map Room access > potions. By now the Round Table has 8 seats and knight deaths hurt more than gold.

**Late game (Act 3):** repairs for your tier-1 knights > potions for boss quests > everything else. Victoria-tier knights are worth any price to keep.

## When the Treasury Is Empty

- **Cancel the luxury choices in audiences** — the faction hit is smaller than the spiral from an empty Treasury.
- **Rewind is not a money printer.** Rewinding resets the timeline, not the economy's rules — but it can undo the *decisions* that emptied the Treasury (see the [Time Rewind guide](/guides/rewind-guide)).
- **Let a faction quest go undone rather than the Treasury.** Quest failure costs standing; bankruptcy can cost the reign.

## Quick Reference

| Rule | Why |
|------|-----|
| One repair per cycle — prioritise the questing knight | Armour 0 = permanent death |
| Build the Forge and Alchemy Room early | They convert gold into survival |
| Keep 25 gold spare when possible | Map Room emergency summon |
| Potions bridge one-stat gaps | Cheaper than a dead knight |
| Solvency beats faction favour | Empty Treasury is a fail state |

## The Money Psychology

The economy's real difficulty is *opportunity cost under uncertainty*. You never know exactly what next cycle will demand — a 25-gold summon you skip today may be the knight that saves the run tomorrow, and the repair you pay for today is a potion you cannot brew tonight. The community's workable answer is a simple reserve rule: **treat the Treasury as two accounts.** Keep a floor of ~25 gold for emergencies (Map Room summons and the last repair), and spend everything above the floor freely. The floor never gets touched except for a knight's life — and the game is very good at presenting a knight's life as the alternative to spending it.
