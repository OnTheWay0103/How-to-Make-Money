# Tower Upgrades & Progression — Annexes, Forge, Alchemy and the Map Room

Your tower is a character in Sovereign Tower. Expanding it is one of the four official headline features ("Expand Your Tower"), and the annexes you build quietly decide whether your knights survive the mid-game. Here is what exists, what it does, and the order to build it.

## The Four Expansion Pillars

Per the official press release and community guides:

| Facility | Purpose | When It Matters |
|----------|---------|-----------------|
| **Carina's Forge** | Repairs knight armour; equips knights | Every cycle — one repair per cycle |
| **Belladonna's Alchemy Room** | Brews stat potions | Bridging quest stat gaps |
| **Map Room** | Summons knights for ~25 gold | Emergency recruitment |
| **General annexes** | Various facilities and upgrades | Expands available actions |

## Carina's Forge: The Survival Anchor

The forge repairs **one knight's armour per cycle** (per the COGconnected preview). Because armour zero equals permanent death, the forge is not a luxury — it is the difference between a stable roster and a graveyard. Build order logic:

1. **Build the forge before you recruit beyond your first three knights.** Every extra knight adds a repair claimant.
2. **Repair the outgoing knight** — the one being sent into quest danger — before the idle one.
3. Later upgrades expand what the forge can do (horses, swords), which directly raises quest success odds (per the official description of equipment purchases).

## Belladonna's Alchemy Room: The Gap-Bridger

The alchemy room brews **stat potions** that assist knights on quests (per the IGN preview). Potions matter most when a quest threshold is just out of reach — a potion turns a "probably fail" into a "survivable" send. Community guidance recommends:

- Brew **Magic potions** if you lack a Magic 14+ knight for "Repel the dragon."
- Keep a **potion stockpile** for Act 1's boss quest and Act 2's 3-cycle deadline quests (Ligia's lamp quest, the jousting tournament).
- Potions do not fix a *wrong knight* — trait matching still rules (see the [Knight Stats guide](/guides/knight-stats)).

## The Map Room: Emergency Recruitment

The Map Room can summon a knight when your roster is short — community guides cite a **25 gold** cost (e.g., Gideon's 25-gold fallback invite; Ari and Edith's 25-gold Map Room routes). Rules of thumb:

- **Only summon when a seat is open and you need a specialist now.** 25 gold is a big slice of the early Treasury.
- **Some recruits only exist via the Map Room** (Edith as "the mystical knight," Ari as "the younger brother of the Basalt brothers") — it is a recruitment route, not just a backup.
- Map Room summons are often the *fallback*: the normal route (audience or quest chain) is usually free.

## Progression Shape: Acts and Seats

The game is structured in three acts, and progression is measured in two ways:

1. **Round Table seats:** 6 in Act 1, 8 in Act 2, 10 in Act 3 (per community guides). More seats = more quest coverage = more revenue and faction favours.
2. **Knight levels:** Knights level through quests; levels leave with any knight who resigns. Protecting high-level knights is a strategic goal in its own right.

Act 1 closes with the **Dragon Knight boss fight** — see the [Dragon Knight guide](/guides/dragon-knight-guide) for how to prepare.

## Suggested Build Order

| Priority | Build | Reason |
|----------|-------|--------|
| 1 | Forge | One repair per cycle — needs to exist from day one |
| 2 | Alchemy Room | Potions unlock risky-but-winnable quests |
| 3 | Equipment (horses, swords) | Raises quest success and survival |
| 4 | Map Room | Summons only when a seat is open and gold is spare |
| 5 | Cosmetic/other annexes | Whatever the story offers — no mechanical urgency [Unconfirmed] |

## Outwitting Rival Rulers

The official feature list pairs tower expansion with a second goal: **"outwit rival rulers."** The annexes you build are not just utility — they are part of your standing as a ruler compared to neighbouring kingdoms. A tower with a forge, an alchemy room, and a map room can handle quests that impress or pressure your rivals, and failed missions can cost alliances (per reviews). There is no community-verified "tower prestige" meter yet [Unconfirmed], but the framing is consistent: every annex is both a tool and a statement.

## Money Comes First

Everything above costs Treasury, and the Treasury is the true progression system. Read the [Economy guide](/guides/economy-guide) for the income side, and the [Core Mechanics guide](/guides/core-mechanics) for how upgrades fit the daily loop.
