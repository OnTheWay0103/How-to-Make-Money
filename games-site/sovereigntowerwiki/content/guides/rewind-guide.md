# Time Rewind Guide — How the Demon Rewrites Fate (and When to Use It)

The time rewind is the signature mechanic of Sovereign Tower — PC Gamer called it "gaming's best implementation of time travel." A **Demon lurking in the tower's crypts** can turn back time to key moments, and here is the twist: **you keep your knowledge from the failed timeline.** That knowledge unlocks new paths, storylines, and dialogue options. Here is how it works, and how to use it without ruining a good run.

## What the Demon Does

Per the official press release: *"A Demon in the crypts lets players rewind time to specific moments (e.g., a Knight's death), enabling new paths, storylines, and dialogue options."*

In practice (per reviews and player reports):

- You can rewind after **failures** — a knight's death, a ruined Treasury, a collapsing realm.
- **Knowledge persists across the reset.** Dialogue options and paths that were invisible before become available, because your Sovereign "remembers" what the first timeline taught them.
- Rewinding is the intended answer to the game's brutal moments: losing a beloved knight, or a possible romantic interest, is cited by reviewers as a primary motivation to rewind.

## The Cost: It Erases Good Branches Too

The rewind is not a free save-scum button. Reviews warn:

- **Rewinding erases the good branches you built in the failed timeline too** — quests completed, relationships deepened, secrets learned (per Tpose Gaming's guide).
- **The cost of using the Demon is deliberately mysterious.** Reviewers describe it as a "slippery slope" temptation; Rock Paper Shotgun even reported feeling "ennui" at the first *mandatory* time reset.
- Some outcomes require you to *not* rewind. Alternative recruitment paths unlock through consequences (e.g., unearthing Gideon's past, or finishing the game and starting New Game Plus).

## When to Rewind

From the community guidance (finalboss.io's cycles 1–3 walkthrough and Tpose Gaming):

**Worth a rewind:**
- A **knight's permanent death** — especially a knight with unique recruitment value (Dullahan's route *requires* letting Goberto die, so rewind is not always wanted there — think first).
- A **faction reputation spiral** that cannot be corrected (e.g., the People tanked so hard the civil-war event triggers).
- **Locking yourself out of a key path** you discovered you needed.

**Not worth a rewind:**
- Minor unfavourable quest results. Affinity and faction standing recover.
- Every choice you dislike — the game has New Game Plus and multiple endings; a slightly messy first run is fine.
- **Rewind with a specific correction in mind**, not as a general "do-over." Reviewers' biggest warning is treating the Demon as an undo button and spiralling.

## Rewinding the Right Way

1. **Identify the exact decision that caused the failure.** The rewind targets key moments — have the target moment in mind.
2. **Write down (or remember) what you learned.** The knowledge carry-over is the whole point. A rewind without a plan is just a do-over.
3. **Check your recruitment goals.** Some knights are mutually exclusive or consequence-gated. If your rewind would erase progress toward Brunhilda or The Wolf, weigh it carefully.
4. **After the rewind, take the new path.** Hidden dialogue options unlocked by your knowledge often lead to better outcomes — including saving knights who died in the first timeline.

## The First Mandatory Reset

Reviewers note the game forces a rewind early — the opening story is designed to teach the mechanic. Don't fight it: use the moment to study what the knowledge carry-over actually unlocks (per Rock Paper Shotgun and the IGN preview). Everything after that reset is where the real game begins.

## Rewind and Knight Deaths: The Temptation Loop

The most common rewind trigger is a knight's permanent death — armour zero, gone for good, levels and romance options included. Reviews describe the loop precisely: you send a knight on a mismatched quest, they die, and the Demon's offer feels like the only honest response. Before you take it:

- **Was the death your mistake, or the game's design?** Some deaths are intended content. Dullahan's entire recruitment exists because Goberto can die; a run where you deliberately lose a knight is a different (and valid) strategy (see the [Dullahan guide](/guides/recruit-dullahan)).
- **Did the death unlock something?** Watch what happens in the two cycles after a death before rewinding — the game sometimes answers first.
- **Is your run otherwise good?** If the only problem is the death, rewind. If the run is a cascade of small failures, a fresh start (or New Game Plus) is cheaper than the Demon's slippery slope.

## What the Community Still Does Not Know

The game is 19 days old at the time of writing, and the rewind's long-form consequences are the community's biggest open question: whether heavy rewind use locks out endings, whether the Demon's cost escalates, and whether certain branches *require* rewinding to reach. Treat every claim about rewind-specific endings as [Unconfirmed] unless a source is cited. What is confirmed: knowledge carries over, good branches are erased, and reviewers love and fear the mechanic in equal measure.

## Time Rewind and Playthrough Length

Because you keep knowledge, every rewind makes your run *longer* and denser — reviewers report first runs of roughly 16 hours with heavy rewind use, versus ~7–8 hours if you accept losses and push on. See the [How Long to Beat guide](/guides/game-length) for the full breakdown.
