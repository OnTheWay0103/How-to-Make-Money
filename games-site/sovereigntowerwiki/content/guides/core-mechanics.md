# Core Mechanics — The Daily Cycle, Audiences & Round Table Explained

Sovereign Tower looks like a visual novel, but underneath it is a tight management puzzle. Understanding the **cycle loop**, **quest stat checks**, and **affinity math** is the difference between a golden reign and an uprising. Here is how the machine works.

## The Cycle Loop

Every cycle runs through the same three phases (per IGN's hands-on preview and multiple reviews):

1. **Morning audience.** Sit on the throne. Subjects bring requests, complaints, and grievances. Every dialogue option you pick shifts faction approval — usually raising one faction while lowering another. There is almost never an answer that pleases everyone (per finalboss.io's faction analysis).
2. **Round Table meeting.** Assign your available knights to the day's quests. Each quest lists what it needs — and the knights you send are the whole story.
3. **Rest and reports.** Results resolve at cycle end: knights level up, gain or lose affinity, get wounded, and occasionally die or storm off.

## How Quests Actually Work

Each quest checks specific stats. Known community-verified requirements include (per Tpose Gaming's knight data):

| Quest | Requirement |
|-------|-------------|
| Repel the dragon | Magic 14+ |
| Suspects interrogations | Wits 12+ |
| In search of the gem | Luck 11+ |
| The lich will not return | Magic 10+ |
| A Thesis Thief | Magic 10+ |
| The Vampire of Clovermont | Charisma 10+ |
| Animal bodyguards | Strength 10+ |
| Diplomatic Meeting | Wits 8+ |
| Slugs invasion | Wits 8+ |
| Hydra Hunt | Magic 7+ |
| The Possessed Reverend | Magic 7+ |
| The False Bank | Wits 3 **or lower** |

The False Bank is the classic trap: sending your smartest knight is the mistake. Knights with 0 Wits — Goberto, Dullahan, Daguez — are the right call.

**Matching traits beats raw stats.** Beyond visible attributes, knights have hidden traits and preferences. Angelica, for example, loves animals: she is a poor pick for creature-killing quests if you want a clean kill, but the *perfect* pick if you want a tamed outcome. **Bold or highlighted words in quest text are hints** about which traits will matter (per the IGN preview).

## Affinity: The Loyalty Meter

- Knight affinity runs roughly **−10 to +10** (per community guides).
- **Favourite meals** are the cheapest affinity boost — about **+1.5 affinity** each (per Tpose Gaming).
- Affinity governs performance, dialogue unlocks, and retention. **If it drops too low — around −7 per community data — the knight resigns for good, taking their levels with them**, with no warning.
- Two knights are immune: **Alwena and Ari never resign**.

## Armour and Death

Every knight has an **armour score**. If it hits zero, the knight is **permanently dead** (per the COGconnected preview). The **blacksmith repairs only one knight's armour per cycle**, so you must prioritise. Knights also die if sent to quests they are catastrophically mismatched for.

## The Four Sovereign Stats

Your own dialogue choices build four personality stats — **Audacity, Wisdom, Tyranny, and Kindness** (per finalboss.io). Each has three thresholds that unlock new dialogue options, and some options only appear when one stat clearly leads another. These are the "you" stats, separate from knight attributes.

## Factions and the Treasury

Your audience decisions move approval with **Merchants, Mystics, Scholars, Nobles, and the People** (the official description names all five), while the **Treasury** is the fuel for choices, quests, and repairs. Low faction approval can end your reign; starving the Treasury does the same. See the [Factions & Affinity guide](/guides/factions-guide) for the full picture.

## Fail States

The game is honest about failure: **death, uprising, and ruin** are all on the table (per the official feature list). When it happens, the [Time Rewind guide](/guides/rewind-guide) explains how the Demon lets you claw a run back — and when starting fresh is smarter.

## Quick Mental Model

> Each cycle you convert three resources — **gold** (Treasury), **knight availability** (seats + assignments), and **faction goodwill** — into quest completions, knight levels, and story progress. The game ends when any one of them hits zero.

## The Official Frame

The official feature list frames the loop in four pillars: **"Your Reign, Your Rules"** (the choices and consequences), **"Recruit eccentric Knights"** (the roster), **"Manage Your Round Table"** (the assignment puzzle), and **"Bend Time and Tempt Fate"** (the rewind). Notice what is *not* in the pillars: combat. There is no combat you control — the game's tension comes entirely from matching knights to quests under scarcity, and from the story consequences of your audience choices. If you arrive expecting a tactical RPG, reset your expectations; if you enjoy *Reigns*-style judgment calls wrapped in a rich narrative, this is exactly that, deeper.
