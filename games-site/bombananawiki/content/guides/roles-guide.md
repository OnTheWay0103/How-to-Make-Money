# Blind / Deaf / Mute Roles Guide

BOMBANANA! is built on three asymmetric roles, each missing one sense. Understanding exactly what each monkey can and cannot do — and how information flows between them — is the fastest way to go from panicking to defusing.

## The Three Monkeys

| | Blind Monkey (🙈) | Deaf Monkey (🙉) | Mute Monkey (🙊) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sees the bomb** | No — blurry silhouette | Yes | No |
| **Touches the bomb** | **Yes — the only one** | No | No |
| **Reads the manual** | No | No | **Yes — the only one** |
| **Speaks** | Yes | Yes | **No** |
| **Hears** | Yes | **No** | Yes |
| **Reads Braille panels** | **Yes, by feel** | No | No |

- **Blind Monkey** — the executor. You can touch and operate the bomb and read Braille dot panels by feel, but you cannot see colors or read screens, so you depend entirely on your teammates for visual details [Official].
- **Deaf Monkey** — the relay. You can see the bomb clearly and speak to the Blind Monkey, but you cannot hear teammates or in-game voice. You read the Mute Monkey's gestures with your eyes [Official].
- **Mute Monkey** — the manual holder. You are the only one who can read the defusal manual and know the answers, but you cannot speak a single word. You communicate through gestures, pointing, and the emote wheel [Official].

## The Information Triangle

Information flows one way, like a game of telephone with a manual:

1. **Mute Monkey** reads the manual and gestures the answer to the Deaf Monkey.
2. **Deaf Monkey** watches both the bomb and the Mute player's gestures, relaying the instruction out loud to the Blind Monkey.
3. **Blind Monkey** repeats the instruction back, gets a confirm, and acts on the bomb.

The **Deaf Monkey is the bottleneck** — they must watch the bomb screen and the Mute player's gestures at the same time while speaking clearly. If they glance away during a gesture, the whole chain breaks [Community].

## Which Role Should Beginners Pick?

Most beginner guides recommend starting as the **Blind Monkey** [Community]:

- **Blind Monkey — easiest entry.** The lightest mental load. You do not read the manual, solve puzzles, or track multiple answers — you wait for a confirmed instruction and carry it out. Great for learning the rhythm of the game.
- **Deaf Monkey — most demanding on attention.** You split focus between the bomb screen and the Mute player's gestures while still talking. New players often find this overwhelming at first.
- **Mute Monkey — hardest for beginners.** You must mime every answer one signal at a time without speaking. It requires a strong shared gesture vocabulary and a patient Deaf relay.

Many groups **rotate roles every few runs** so one person does not get stuck on the toughest job, and so everyone learns the full information chain [Community].

## Communication Phrases & Callout Templates

Good teams use the same callout structure every time. It sounds robotic, and that is the point — robotic calls are unambiguous.

### Universal Callout Order
> **Module name → Position → Details → Manual answer → Repeat-back → Confirm → Act**

### Per-Module Example Callouts [Community]

| Module | Example Callout |
|--------|-----------------|
| **Wires** | "Wires, top right. Four wires. Left to right: green, blue, red, yellow. Red LED. Waiting for the cut." |
| **Numpad / Math** | "Result is 67, odd, greater than five, yellow LED. Waiting for the final number." |
| **Switch Panels** | "Switches, top row. Light order is red, green, blue, yellow. Digit string is 8972. Waiting for switch states before enter." |

Start every callout with the **module name and position**, because several manual pages involve colors, wires, buttons, or lights, and the Mute Monkey needs to know which page to open [Community].

## The Gesture Dictionary

Because the Mute Monkey cannot speak, a small agreed gesture set prevents mid-round arguments. Here are the gestures every guide recommends agreeing on before the timer starts [Community]:

| Gesture | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **Yes / correct** | Confirm an instruction before the Blind Monkey acts. |
| **No / wrong** | Reject a step before anyone acts. |
| **Wait** | Pause the module while the manual reader catches up. |
| **Repeat** | Ask for a detail to be said again. |
| **Go back / rewind** | Correct a switch panel before pressing enter. |
| **Numbers 0–9** | A fixed number sign so the Mute Monkey can signal digits. |

## How to Play as the Mute Monkey

If you draw the Mute Monkey, your whole job is **manual + gestures**:

1. **Know your manual.** When the Deaf Monkey calls a module name and position, you find the matching page. See the [manual guide](/guides/manual-guide) for page-locating tips.
2. **Signal one answer at a time.** Do not try to gesture the whole solution at once. The Deaf Monkey needs one clear signal, then relays it, then comes back for the next.
3. **Use the emote wheel and pointing.** The game gives you non-verbal tools beyond free-form gesturing — pointing at the bomb and the emote wheel are part of your vocabulary [Official].
4. **Watch for the repeat-back.** Once the Blind Monkey repeats your answer and the Deaf confirms it, that is your cue that the chain worked.

## How to Play as the Deaf Monkey

As the relay, your attention is the team's scarce resource:

1. **Split your eyes.** Watch the bomb and the Mute player. If you look away during a gesture, the chain breaks.
2. **Speak in the same callout order.** Module name → position → details → manual answer — every time.
3. **Confirm everything.** Relay the Blind Monkey's repeat-back to the Mute Monkey, and relay the Mute's confirm back.

## How to Play as the Blind Monkey

Your job is to not act until told to:

1. **Touch and read what only you can.** Braille panels are yours to read by feel [Community].
2. **Repeat the instruction back.** "Four wires, cut the third one, green?" gets a confirm before you cut.
3. **Use the Blind Monkey's left/right for directions.** The team should lock left and right to *your* perspective to avoid mirror-image mistakes [Community].
