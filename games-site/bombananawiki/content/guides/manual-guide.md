# BOMBANANA! Manual Quick Reference

In BOMBANANA!, the defusal manual is the Mute Monkey's superpower — and their prison. **"You are the only one who can read the bomb disposal manual. If only you could tell the others what you know."** [Official]

This guide is the quick reference for running the manual: how pages map to modules, how you locate the right page fast, and the gesture shorthand that translates manual answers into actions.

## What the Manual Is

The manual contains the rules for every bomb module — wires, numpad/math, switch panels, D-pad, Braille panels, and any modules the full release adds. It is the source of truth for the team. The catch: only the Mute Monkey can read it, and the Mute Monkey cannot speak [Official].

The manual turns "what the Deaf Monkey sees" into "what the Blind Monkey does."

## Page-Mapping: How to Find the Right Page

Because the Mute Monkey cannot ask questions verbally, the team must feed them the right information to find the page:

| The Deaf Monkey calls... | The Mute Monkey needs it to... |
|--------------------------|--------------------------------|
| **Module name** | Pick the right manual section (wires ≠ numpad ≠ switches). |
| **Position** | Confirm it is the module being worked, not a twin module elsewhere on the bomb. |
| **Details** (colors, count, lights, expression, centre value) | Pick the right *row/table* within the module's page. |

The reason every callout starts with **module name and position** is manual navigation: several manual pages involve colors, wires, buttons, or lights, and the Mute needs to know which page to open before anything else [Community].

## Reading a Page, Step by Step

1. **Hear the module name.** The Deaf Monkey says "Wires, top right." You know the section.
2. **Hear the details.** "Four wires, left to right: green, blue, red, yellow, red LED." You find the matching row.
3. **Find the answer.** The manual says which wire to cut, which switch to set, which number to press.
4. **Signal the answer.** One gesture at a time. Numbers get the agreed number signs; yes/no/wait get the agreed gestures.
5. **Watch for the repeat-back.** The Blind Monkey repeats your answer; the Deaf confirms it; then the Blind acts. Your answer is now a fact, not a guess [Community].

## The Repeat-Back Loop

The manual is only useful if its answer actually reaches the bomb. The loop that guarantees it:

> **Mute signals answer → Deaf relays out loud → Blind repeats back → Deaf confirms → Blind acts**

If any link in that chain is missing, freeze. **Nobody acts until the instruction has been repeated back once** — this is the single most-repeated rule in every BOMBANANA! guide [Community].

## Gesture Shorthand for Manual Answers

Since the Mute Monkey cannot speak, speed comes from a pre-agreed gesture set. The manual-specific ones:

| Situation | Gesture to agree on |
|-----------|---------------------|
| **Yes / correct** | Confirm the manual answer matches the Blind's repeat-back. |
| **No / wrong** | Reject — the repeat-back misheard the manual. |
| **Wait** | Pause while you re-read the manual page. |
| **Repeat** | Ask the Deaf to re-call the details — you need another read. |
| **Go back / rewind** | A switch was set wrong; revisit a previous position before enter. |
| **Numbers 0–9** | A fixed number sign for digit strings and numpad answers. |

Agree on these **before the timer starts**, not during the first numpad panic [Community].

## Fast-Manual Tips for the Mute Monkey

1. **Memorize module sections.** If you know roughly where wires lives versus switches, you shave seconds off every page turn.
2. **Signal one answer at a time.** Do not gesture the whole solution. The chain moves one confirmed step at a time [Community].
3. **Use the emote wheel and pointing** for emphasis. The game gives you non-verbal tools beyond free-form gestures [Official].
4. **Pre-solve the easy stuff.** For wires, the answer is usually fast once you have the count and colors. Get comfortable with wires first.
5. **Ask for a re-read when unsure.** Better to burn five seconds re-reading the page than to gesture a wrong answer and lose the run.
