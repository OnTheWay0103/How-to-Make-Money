# How to Play BOMBANANA! — Beginner Guide

**BOMBANANA!** is a chaotic, asymmetrical **3-player co-op bomb-defusal party game** developed by Lefto Studio and published by TARK. Three monkeys — one **blind**, one **deaf**, one **mute** — each hold only part of the information needed to defuse a bomb. Your job as a team is to combine those fragments fast enough to stop the timer.

The full game releases **September 2, 2026** on Steam. A free demo has already been played by more than 6 million players, so the core loop below is well documented.

## The Core Idea: Split Information

No single player can solve a bomb alone. That is the whole game:

| Role | Sees / Reads | Can Touch | Can Speak | Can Hear |
|------|--------------|-----------|-----------|----------|
| **Blind Monkey** (🙈) | Almost nothing — no colors, no screens | Yes — the only one who touches the bomb | Yes | Yes |
| **Deaf Monkey** (🙉) | The bomb and the other monkeys | No | Yes | No |
| **Mute Monkey** (🙊) | The defusal manual | No | No | Yes |

The information flows one way: **Mute Monkey → Deaf Monkey → Blind Monkey**. The Mute reads the manual and gestures the answer; the Deaf watches the bomb and the Mute, then relays out loud; the Blind repeats the instruction back and performs it on the bomb.

## The Core Loop

A round of BOMBANANA! follows a simple cycle:

1. **Start the bomb.** The host starts the timer. Depending on the level, there may be environmental hazards — power outages, obstructions, or loud interruptions [Official].
2. **Name the module.** The first player to see the bomb calls out which module it is and where it is on the bomb. Every module has different rules, so the Mute Monkey needs to know which manual page to open [Community].
3. **Read the details.** The Deaf Monkey describes what they see: wire colors, a math expression, lights, a digit string, or a Braille panel [Community].
4. **Consult the manual.** The Mute Monkey finds the matching page and signals the answer with gestures [Official].
5. **Confirm and act.** The Deaf relays the answer, the Blind repeats it back, and only after a confirm does the Blind cut, press, flip, or enter [Community].
6. **Repeat until defused.** Work through the modules one at a time before the timer hits zero.

## Your First Bomb — Step by Step

### 1. Get a team of exactly three
BOMBANANA! requires exactly three players — "no more, no less" [Official]. You can matchmake for a third, but the easiest start is to invite two Steam friends. See the [co-op guide](/guides/co-op-guide) for the invite flow.

### 2. Assign roles before the timer starts
Each player takes one role. Teams often rotate roles every few runs so nobody is stuck on the toughest job, but for your first bomb, put your most patient player on the **Deaf Monkey** (the relay bottleneck), your calmest on the **Blind Monkey** (the executor), and your most expressive on the **Mute Monkey** (the manual holder).

### 3. Agree on three gestures
Before starting, agree on at least three gestures: **yes**, **no / wrong**, and **wait**. Teams that skip this end up arguing mid-round. A full gesture dictionary is in the [roles guide](/guides/roles-guide).

### 4. Use one callout structure
Good teams sound boring. Every module call should follow the same order:

> **Module name → Position → Details → Manual answer → Repeat-back → Confirm → Act**

For example: *"Wires, top right. Four wires. Left to right: green, blue, red, yellow. Red LED. Waiting for the cut."* [Community]

### 5. Repeat before you act
Nobody cuts, presses, flips, or enters until the instruction has been repeated back once. The Blind Monkey repeats the target, the Deaf confirms, and then the Blind acts. This single rule prevents most dumb deaths [Community].

## Module Basics for Beginners

The official store page confirms that "each bomb has different modules and different rules," but does not list them. Demo players and multiple independent guides have documented these types [Community]:

- **Wires** — The starter module. The Blind counts and names wire colors left to right; the manual decides which wire to cut. Wire count always leads (three-wire and four-wire use different rules).
- **Numpad / Math** — The hardest common module. A math expression must be solved, but the answer is not always the number you press — wait for the manual.
- **Switch Panels** — Several switches must be set in the right state before a final "enter." More dangerous than wires.
- **D-pad** — A directional pad with a centre value.
- **Braille** — Dot panels only the Blind Monkey can read by feel.

The full release may include more module types [Unconfirmed]. See the [modules compendium](/guides/modules-guide) for detailed callouts for each.

## Common First-Run Mistakes

1. **Not naming the module first.** The Mute Monkey cannot find the manual page if you say "there are wires and a screen" without a module name. Always start the callout with the module name and position [Community].
2. **Skipping the wire count.** For wires, the count changes the manual logic. Say "four wires" before listing colors [Community].
3. **Calling two modules at once.** Finish the current module before describing the next. You lose the manual reader otherwise [Community].
4. **Trusting a normal keypad layout.** Numpad numbers are jumbled — position does not equal value. Press the requested number wherever it appears [Community].
5. **Acting before the confirm.** If the instruction has not been repeated back and confirmed, do not touch the bomb.

## What to Do Before Launch

- **Play the demo.** It is free on Steam and teaches the roles plus the core modules. See the [demo guide](/guides/demo-guide).
- **Invite your two teammates now.** Exactly three players are needed, and the game is best when your friends already know the gesture set.
- **Check your hardware.** Requirements are modest — around 300 MB of storage and 8 GB RAM minimum on Windows [Official]. Full specs are in the [price & platforms guide](/guides/price-platforms-faq).

## Parting Advice

BOMBANANA! is a communication game disguised as a bomb-defusal game. The team that talks clearly, repeats instructions, and agrees on gestures before the timer starts will defuse far more bombs than the team that yells. Start simple, solve wires first, and work through one module at a time.
