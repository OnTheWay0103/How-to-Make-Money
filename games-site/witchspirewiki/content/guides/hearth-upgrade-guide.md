# Hearth & Base Building Guide — Upgrades, Construction, Defense & Locations

> Last verified: August 10, 2026

Your Hearth is the most important structure in Witchspire. Every upgrade tier unlocks new crafting stations, gameplay systems, and progression gates. If you feel stuck, the answer is almost always "upgrade your Hearth." This guide covers the full upgrade path, construction basics, where to build, and how to keep your base safe.

> **Note on values:** material costs and timers below come from community reports and are not officially published. The most consistently reported values are marked as such; check the in-game Hearth menu for the exact requirements.

---

## Hearth Overview

The Hearth is your home base. Interact with the central crystal to:
- View upgrade requirements
- Allocate Luminary skill points
- Access Astral Projection mode (Tier 4+)
- Set your respawn point

Your Hearth creates an invisible influence radius. Within it, crafting stations reportedly stay active, placed items persist, and enemies are less likely to spawn. You can see the radius boundary as a shimmering ring when holding your Hearth item.

## Astral Projection Mode

Unlocked at **Hearth Tier 4**. Enter it by interacting with your Hearth crystal and selecting "Astral Project."

**What Astral Projection does (community reports):**
- Separates your spirit from your body
- Free-flight building mode — place and move structures from the air
- No fall damage
- Preview structures before committing materials

**Use it to:**
- Build multi-level bases
- Place garden plots on elevated terrain
- Arrange furniture precisely
- Scout the surrounding area safely

## Construction Basics

### Build Mode

Enter building mode by pressing **B** (default key; rebindable in settings). A radial menu appears with all available piece categories.

### Basic Placement Steps

1. Press **B** to enter build mode
2. Select a category from the radial menu
3. Hover over a valid snap point — it glows green
4. Left-click to place, scroll wheel to rotate
5. Right-click to cancel or exit build mode

**Snap point tips**: Foundation pieces snap to terrain and other foundations. Walls snap to foundation edges. Roof pieces snap to wall tops. Pillars can be placed independently to support larger structures.

### Rotation and Precision

| Action | Default Key |
|--------|-------------|
| Rotate piece 45 degrees | Scroll wheel |
| Rotate piece 90 degrees | R key |
| Toggle snap mode | Z key |
| Raise/lower piece | Hold Shift + scroll |
| Move piece (already placed) | Hold E + drag |

### Building Categories

| Category | Pieces | Function |
|----------|--------|----------|
| Foundation | Floor tiles, pillars, stairs | Structural base for all builds |
| Walls | Full walls, half walls, window walls | Enclosure and defense |
| Roof | Sloped roof, flat roof, skylight | Weather protection |
| Decor | Fences, lanterns, signs, rugs | Aesthetics and lighting |
| Stations | Workbench, Furnace, Garden Plot, etc. | Crafting and progression |

### Building Material Tiers

| Tier | Materials | Community Notes |
|------|-----------|-----------------|
| Wood | Wood, Fiber | Available from the start |
| Reinforced | Refined Wood, Stone Block, Iron | Unlocked around Hearth Tier 3 |
| Stone | Stone Block, Sturdy Fiber | Unlocked around Hearth Tier 4; the community's recommended long-term tier |

> **Note:** exact per-piece material costs and durability values have not been independently verified. The three tiers above (Wood → Reinforced → Stone) are the consistently reported progression.

## Base Layout & Design

Good base design saves time and keeps you safe. Here are the most effective layouts from community builders.

### The Compact Tower (Solo Players)

Build vertically to minimize material cost while accessing all stations.

```
Floor 1: Hearth + Workbench + Furnace
Floor 2: Garden Plots (sunlight access)
Floor 3: Broom Parking + Chest Storage
Floor 4: Witchcraft Circle + Enchantment Table
Roof:  Lookout platform + Landing pad
```

**Why it works**: Vertical builds use fewer foundation pieces and are easier to defend. A single staircase keeps you oriented.

### The Sprawling Compound (Co-op / Groups)

Best for 3+ players. Build wide with dedicated buildings for each function.

```
Center:  Open courtyard with Hearth
North:   Furnace + Workshop building
East:    Housing wing (1 room per player)
South:   Garden + Farm plots
West:    Storage + Witchcraft Circle
Perimeter: Stone walls with gate
```

**Why it works**: Multiple players can work simultaneously without blocking each other.

### Creative Building Ideas

- **Treehouse base**: build around the massive trees in Timberline Basin using the trunk as a central pillar — natural defense against ground enemies.
- **Cliffside base**: use cliff faces as natural walls; excavate into the terrain to reduce wall material needs.
- **Island base**: on Mira Isles, bridge-connected builds combine views with safety.

### Common Building Mistakes

1. **Building too large too early** — every piece costs materials. Start compact and expand only when you have a surplus.
2. **Ignoring structural support** — roofs and upper floors require pillars or walls below them. If a piece turns red during placement, you need support underneath.
3. **Placing the Hearth in an open field** — prefer spots near resources with natural barriers or high ground.
4. **Forgetting a landing pad** — you will land your broom constantly; build a flat platform so you do not clip through walls.

## Base Location Suggestions

| Region | Overall Rating | Best For | Worst For |
|--------|---------------|----------|-----------|
| Vyrs Meadows | S | Beginners, balanced gameplay | Endgame resource diversity |
| Timberline Basin | A | Resource gathering, solo players | Group content access |
| Mira Isles | A | Mid-game progression, explorers | Early game (too dangerous) |
| Shuddersands | B | Endgame farming, challenging content | Beginners (too dangerous) |

| Location | Pros | Cons |
|----------|------|------|
| Vyrs Meadows (Starting area) | Central, flat, close to resources | Gets boring visually |
| Vyrs East Ridge | High ground, good view | Longer trek to early resources |
| Timberline Basin (Central Grove) | Beautiful, abundant materials | Tougher enemies nearby |
| Timberline Basin (Cliffside) | Defensible, looks epic | Windy terrain, harder building |
| Mira Isles (near the dock) | Dock fast travel, unique vibe | Requires flight or boat to reach |

**Our recommendation for first playthrough:** Build in **Vyrs Meadows** for the early tiers, then relocate to **Timberline Basin** (Central Grove or a cliffside spot) for the mid-to-late game. The early-access convenience of Vyrs is worth more than aesthetics.

### Base Location Decision Matrix

| Your Playstyle | Recommended Region | Secondary Location |
|---------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| **Beginner / Casual** | Vyrs Meadows | None needed |
| **Crafter / Gatherer** | Timberline Basin | Vyrs Meadows (storage) |
| **Explorer / Quester** | Mira Isles | Vyrs Meadows (main Hearth) |
| **Endgame Farmer** | Shuddersands | Mira Isles (outpost) |

**Base location tips:**
- Check the nearest Flight Pillar before placing foundations — a long walk to the nearest pillar adds up over hundreds of trips
- Build on elevated terrain where possible; low-lying areas reportedly flood during storms
- Do not start your first base in Shuddersands — heat and aggressive enemies make it unsuitable for early-game bases

### Moving Your Hearth

Community reports differ on the relocation method: some describe crafting a **new Hearth Crystal** at the Workbench, others describe a **Hearth Relocation Kit** that stores your structures. Both approaches reportedly let you deconstruct and rebuild at a new location without losing everything.

> **Note:** one older guide claims you "cannot move a Hearth upgraded past Tier 2" — other community sources contradict this. This has not been resolved; test carefully before relying on it.

## Defending Your Base

Witchspire is a PvE game — there is **no PvP**, so you never need to defend against other players (see the [PvP Status page](/guides/pvp-guide)). Against the environment and hostile creatures, community builders recommend:

- **Layered walls** — a single wall means one breach exposes everything. Build in rings: outer perimeter, middle ring around main buildings, inner ring directly around the Hearth.
- **Funnel entrances** — leave a single gated entrance and place traps (spikes, fire, slows) along the approach.
- **Defense towers** — community reports describe tower structures (arrow towers early, arcane towers later) that attack nearby enemies automatically.
- **Repair often** — damaged walls are cheap to fix early and expensive to replace after a long raid cycle.

> **Note:** older guides describe detailed raid mechanics — 2-minute warnings, wave compositions, battering rams. These specifics have **not been independently verified**. Treat defensive preparation as good practice rather than a confirmed raid system.
