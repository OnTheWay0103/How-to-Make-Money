# How to Get Materials Fast in Phantom Tower — The Resource Economy Explained

Every Phantom Tower guide says the same thing: bring materials back out of the tower. But what materials, from where, and what are they actually for? The official record answers some of this precisely and stays silent on the rest. This guide maps the confirmed resource economy — where materials come from, how they flow through the Blacksmith and the Soulbinder, and what to prioritize at each stage of Early Access.

## The Confirmed Resource Loop

The whole economy reduces to one confirmed loop:

```
Kill monsters → harvest materials → extract from the tower
  → forge gear at the Blacksmith → Soulbind stats (level-99 ceiling)
  → climb higher → repeat
```

Every part of that chain is in the official materials. Climb Mode's official description says you explore the tower "collecting materials, resources, and equipment to bring back outside." The Blacksmith forges gear from monster materials — "Forge your equipment, develop your combat style" is the official line. The Soulbinder permanently locks stats onto gear, developable up to level 99. Resources in, power out.

## Where Materials Come From (Confirmed)

**Monsters are the material source.** The game has **60+ enemy types**, and the official description says materials are "harvested from fallen monsters." That is the only confirmed material source — with that many enemy types, expect a wide material pool, but the exact drop lists have not been published.

Practical reading of the confirmed system:

- **Every run is a harvest run.** Even a failed deep climb produced materials on the way up. Nothing you kill is wasted.
- **Enemy variety = material variety.** 60+ enemy types across 100 floors means early floors and deep floors feed different forges. If a material is missing for a craft, it likely comes from a floor band you haven't reached.
- **Elite and boss enemies are the richest question mark.** Bosses drop **Boss Unique Weapons** (confirmed, Steam version). Whether elite monsters drop rarer materials is officially unstated — treat it as `[待确认]` and farm what the rooms actually give you.

## The Extraction Rule: Bring It Back Out

The single most important mechanic in the economy is extraction. Climb Mode lets you collect materials, resources, and equipment "to bring back outside" — and what you bring out is yours to keep. What you die with is lost.

- **Extract when your inventory is full or your odds are falling.** The run that ends with a full extraction beats the run that dies one floor deeper.
- **Bank before boss attempts.** Boss floors are where hauls get lost. Cash out first, then fight.
- **Extraction is what compounds.** Materials you bank become forged gear; gear becomes Soulbound stats; stats raise your next run's ceiling. A run that extracts nothing is a failed run even if you survived.

## Where Materials Go: The Blacksmith

The Blacksmith is the confirmed crafting hub. Materials harvested from monsters are used to forge new equipment — weapons, armor, and artifacts — and to upgrade the pieces you keep.

Confirmed facts:

| Fact | Source |
|------|--------|
| Materials come from fallen monsters | Official game description |
| The Blacksmith forges weapons, armor, and artifacts | Official materials |
| Forging and upgrading are how you "develop your combat style" | Official tagline |
| There are 128 weapons (six types) and 200+ equipment pieces | Official count |
| Exact forging costs and upgrade caps | **Not published — `[待确认]`** |

Spending advice that follows from the confirmed system:

1. **Forge what your class uses.** The Warrior is melee, Mage and Archer are ranged casters — materials spent on gear you will equip beat materials spent on novelty.
2. **Save rare materials for keepers.** Since material pools vary by enemy type, hold scarce drops for pieces you will Soulbind rather than prototypes.
3. **Do not hoard everything.** Banked materials only pay off when they become gear. If you never forge, extraction is just inventory management.

## The Soulbinder: The Sink That Makes It Permanent

The Soulbinder is where resources stop being per-run and become permanent. You lock a stat onto a piece of equipment, and that stat is now permanent on that piece — a specific stat can be developed up to level 99.

Why this matters for the economy:

- **It is the end of the pipeline.** Materials → forged gear → Soulbound gear. The level-99 ceiling is the officially described maximum investment for one stat.
- **It converts materials into meta-progression.** A Soulbound weapon is worth more than any single run's loot, because it survives every future run.
- **Commit to one piece at a time.** Spreading binds across five pieces produces five mediocre stats instead of one strong one.

## The Currency Question — What the Official Record Says

Players ask this constantly: **is there gold, souls, or coins in Phantom Tower?** The honest answer: no in-game currency has been officially named for the Steam version.

What we do have:

- **Materials** are the confirmed currency of gear — harvested, extracted, forged.
- The Steam achievement **"Billionaire"** is officially listed (name revealed; requirement not published), which hints at a wealth or economy system — but that is inference, not confirmation `[待确认]`.
- The mobile version has its own separate systems (a Necromancer class that collects souls from enemies, rune sockets on gear), but the mobile and Steam versions are separate products — do not assume mobile systems exist on Steam.

Any guide naming a specific currency ("Phantom Coins," "Tower Souls") and quoting prices for it is inventing the numbers.

## Early Game Priorities

If you are starting out, the confirmed economy says to do this in order:

1. **Learn what drops.** Kill everything on the early floors and note what materials come out. Your first runs are scouting runs.
2. **Extract religiously.** Even small hauls compound. Bring materials out every time you can.
3. **Forge your first upgrade.** Do not hoard your first materials — the Blacksmith exists to turn them into power.
4. **Soulbind one good stat.** The moment you own a weapon you will use for many floors, lock its best stat. That is the start of your permanent economy.
5. **Plan a boss farm.** Boss Unique Weapons are the confirmed chase items; boss floors are where the economy's most valuable drops live.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How do I get materials fast?**
A: The confirmed source is harvesting fallen monsters across the 60+ enemy types. Fast farming means clearing floors efficiently and extracting — depth is a bonus, extraction is the requirement.

**Q: What should I bring back out of the tower?**
A: Materials, resources, and equipment — all three are explicitly listed in Climb Mode's official description. Bring the rarest materials and any gear worth forging or Soulbinding.

**Q: Is there money in the game?**
A: No named currency has been officially published for the Steam version `[待确认]`. Materials are the economy.

**Q: Do materials persist if I die?**
A: Only what you brought out before dying. What you were carrying is lost — which is why extraction discipline matters more than push depth.

**Q: What is the best material investment?**
A: Materials you forge into gear you will Soulbind. The level-99 stat ceiling is the confirmed long-term sink.
