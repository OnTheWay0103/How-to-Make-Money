# Phantom Tower Beginner Guide — Essential Tips for Getting Started

Phantom Tower drops you at the base of a mysterious tower and asks one question: how far can you climb? It is a fast-paced hack-and-slash roguelike action RPG from Horien Studio, a two-person Italian team, and it launched into Steam Early Access on July 13, 2026. The premise is simple — climb the tower's 100 floors, fight what is inside, and bring your loot back out — but the combat is built around a deeper system: four elements you can swap mid-fight to trigger elemental reactions.

This guide covers what every new player needs for their first runs, based on what the developers have officially confirmed. Where the details have not been published yet, we say so instead of guessing.

## Best Starting Class for Beginners

Phantom Tower launches with **8 playable classes**, each with its own unique skills, elemental affinity, and combat identity. The official press materials only name four of them so far:

- **Warrior** — the confirmed starting class, and the safest recommendation for your first run
- **Mage** — a spell-focused class (per official store materials)
- **Archer** — a ranged class (per official store materials)
- **Assassin** — described by the developer as a high-mobility, high-lethality "Shadow Specialist" class that uses Shadow Clones for synchronized strikes

The other four launch classes have not been officially named yet. Note that the mobile version of Phantom Tower has also added a **Necromancer** class, which collects souls from defeated enemies to summon allies once enough are gathered — but the mobile and Steam versions have separate content lines, so check patch notes for what arrives on Steam.

**Start with the Warrior.** It is the game's official starting class, and a straightforward melee fighter is the right tool for learning how element swapping and boss patterns work before you try a glassier class.

## Understanding the Core Loop

Every Phantom Tower run follows the same rhythm:

1. **Prepare at camp** — Forge and upgrade gear at the Blacksmith
2. **Enter the tower** — Start your climb
3. **Clear floors** — Defeat enemies, collect materials, find Tower Blessings
4. **Defeat bosses** — Powerful bosses guard your progress upward
5. **Return or push on** — Bring materials, resources, and equipment back outside, or risk going deeper

The key tension: the deeper you go, the better the rewards — but the more you stand to lose if you fall. In Climb Mode, though, nothing is truly lost forever: gear you forged and brought back out stays with you, and Soulbinder stats are permanent.

## Elemental Combat Basics

Phantom Tower's combat revolves around **four elements** you can swap between mid-fight:

| Element | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **IGNIS** | Fire |
| **AQUA** | Water |
| **TERRA** | Earth |
| **VENTI** | Wind |

The signature mechanic is **free mid-combat element swapping**. You are not locked into one element per build — you switch in real time to trigger the reaction the current situation demands.

The game ships with **16 elemental reactions**. Only three have been officially named so far:

- **Freeze** — immobilizes enemies (AQUA-based)
- **Erode** — a corruption effect that spreads from fallen enemies and chains across groups (TERRA-based)
- **Shockwaves** — damage that traverses entire rooms (VENTI-based)

The other 13 reaction names and their exact combinations have **not been officially published yet**. Until the official wiki fills them in, treat any list you see online claiming all 16 combos as unverified.

**Beginner tip:** Get into the habit of swapping elements in every fight. The reaction system is the game's main damage and control tool — using only one element ignores half the combat design. When enemies overwhelm you, a freeze is worth more than another fireball.

## Survival Tips for Your First 10 Floors

These are universal roguelike survival principles that apply to any class in the early tower:

1. **Do not be greedy.** The number one cause of early deaths is squeezing in one extra attack. Learn enemy patterns and only commit when you have a clear opening.
2. **Check every room.** Tower Blessings — 47 of them in total — are described as "powerful abilities hidden across its floors." Slow down and search before you move on.
3. **Bring loot back out.** The official Steam description says Climb Mode lets you collect materials, resources, and equipment "to bring back outside." Bank your finds when you can instead of always pushing one more floor.
4. **Watch enemies before attacking.** Phantom Tower has 60+ enemy types. Each new type usually means a new attack pattern — spend the first seconds of each encounter observing.
5. **Use the Blacksmith.** Forge your equipment and develop your combat style between runs. Materials harvested from monsters feed the forging system, so every run contributes to your long-term gear.
6. **Do not over-specialize early.** Until you know which elements and reactions you like, keep your blessings and gear choices flexible.

## Death and Meta-Progression

Death in Phantom Tower works differently depending on your mode:

**Climb Mode (Main Mode):**
- Progress carries over between runs — this is the mode's official design
- Materials, resources, and equipment you collected can be brought back outside the tower
- Soulbinder stats locked onto gear are permanent
- Whether runs start from checkpoints after death has **not been officially detailed** (TBD)

**Survival Mode (Endless):**
- Infinite hordes of monsters, and "every defeat, however, is final" — true permadeath
- You choose your equipment before entering
- Designed for players testing optimized builds

**What persists permanently:**
- Soulbinder stats on gear (per the official Soulbinder description)
- Classes you have unlocked (8 at launch, all available per press materials)
- Steam achievements

## Your First Boss — What to Expect

Phantom Tower definitely has bosses: press materials describe "unique monsters and challenging bosses" guarding the climb, the Steam version adds **Boss Unique Weapons** (weapons only bosses can drop), and the Early Access roadmap promises new bosses over time.

What has **not** been published: the number of bosses, their names, which floors they guard, and their attack patterns. Any guide claiming specific boss names and move sets right now is unverified. Here is what we do know about preparing:

- **Bring your best gear.** Forge and upgrade at the Blacksmith before a boss attempt
- **Learn the floor first.** The rooms leading to a boss teach you the enemy types you will fight alongside it
- **Keep a reaction ready.** A mid-fight element swap into a freeze or shockwave is worth more than any single attack

## Tower Blessings: What to Look For

Tower Blessings are the game's roguelike modifiers — 47 abilities that reshape each run, hidden across the tower's floors. Only two have been officially named:

- **Artemis Dive** — evasion summons tracking arrows (a dodge-based offensive blessing)
- **Chain Quake** — shockwaves chain between enemies (a damage-spreading blessing)

The other 45 blessing names and effects are **not yet published**. Early on, keep two ideas in mind: blessings that trigger from actions you already do (like dodging) are reliable, and a blessing that fits your current run is better than one that sounds powerful on paper.

## Quick-Start Checklist

- [ ] Pick Warrior as your first class
- [ ] Learn to swap elements mid-fight (IGNIS → AQUA is a safe early rotation)
- [ ] Search every floor for Tower Blessings
- [ ] Forge and upgrade your main weapon at the Blacksmith
- [ ] Bring materials and gear back out of the tower instead of always pushing deeper
- [ ] Start Soulbinding stats on gear you plan to keep long-term
- [ ] Treat boss names and pattern guides as unverified until the official wiki catches up
