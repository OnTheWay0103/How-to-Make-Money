# Phantom Tower Runes Guide — Identify, Socket, and Choose the Right Runes

Runes are the deepest gear system in Phantom Tower. They arrived in Patch 0.5.00: you find unidentified stones, pay to reveal what they do, then socket them into gear sockets that start closed. A single rune can complete a set bonus, upgrade a blessing, or add a stat your build cannot otherwise reach. Get it wrong and removal destroys the rune.

This guide covers the confirmed system: where runes come from, what each grade costs, the six paths, and how to decide which runes deserve your Alteria.

**Early Access note:** Phantom Tower launched in Early Access on July 13, 2026. The official wiki is updated with each patch, but drop rates, exact item pools, and UI labels can change. Anything not stated by the wiki or patch notes is marked **[Unconfirmed]**.

## What Runes Actually Do

A rune is a socketable modifier for **high-rarity gear**. The official wiki is precise about the restrictions:

- Only **Mythic, Legendary, and Supreme** gear can hold runes.
- **Mythic = 1 socket, Legendary = 2 sockets, Supreme = 3 sockets.**
- **Sockets start closed.** You must pay the Blacksmith to open them.
- Some dropped gear arrives with an **open socket** or a **pre-filled rune**, so always inspect high-rarity drops before selling them.

Runes come in two states:

| State | What it is | Can you socket it? |
|-------|------------|--------------------|
| **Dormant** | Unidentified, stacks by grade | No |
| **Awakened** | Identified, unique, fixed path and effect | Yes — the only socketable kind |

Enemies drop both dormant and awakened runes. Identification is the step that turns a stack of unknowns into a specific build tool.

## The Rune Workflow: Identify, Socket, Remove

Three stations matter:

1. **The Blacksmith** opens closed sockets on high-rarity gear. This costs **Alteria**.
2. **The Rune Station in the Glyph Master's house** handles identification, socketing, and removal.
3. **Your inventory** holds dormant runes until you identify them.

The costs are grade-based and are paid entirely in **Alteria**:

| Grade | Identify | Socket | Remove |
|-------|---------:|-------:|-------:|
| **Mythic** | 10,000 | 8,000 | 4,000 |
| **Legendary** | 18,000 | 12,000 | 5,000 |
| **Supreme** | 30,000 | 20,000 | 8,000 |

Two rules make this expensive:

- **Removal destroys the rune.** It frees the socket, but you do not get the rune back. The official wiki is explicit: removing is how you clear a socket, not how you recover a rune.
- **There is no reroll and no rune-upgrade path.** The wiki describes none. A bad identification is a sunk cost, so identify deliberately.

**Pro tip:** do not identify every rune the moment it drops. Bank dormant runes until you have a piece of gear you actually intend to keep, then identify the grade that matches that gear's sockets. Identifying a Mythic rune for a build you are about to replace is the most common way to waste Alteria.

## The Six Rune Paths

Every awakened rune has one of six paths; the path sets its effect pool, set-piece coverage, and blessing upgrades.

### Valor — raw offense

Rolls attack power, crit, attack speed, and physical damage. Set-piece coverage: **Barbarian, Scarlet, Secret Agent, Vampiric**. Blessing upgrades include **Chains of Agony**, **Ethereal Swords**, **Elemental Blade IGNIS**, **Overdrive**, and **Thundershock**.

Best for physical crit and fast-attack builds.

### Vigor — endurance and sustain

Rolls HP, defense, damage reduction, lifesteal, and mobility. Set-piece coverage: **Draconic, Miasma, Phantom, Verdant**. Blessing upgrades include **Chain Quake**, **Essence Drainer**, **Namtar Affliction**, **Pact with the Forest**, and **Twilight Rift**.

Vigor also has a special **poison-cap** effect: the wiki lists a cap increase of **×1 at Legendary** and **×2 at Supreme**. If you run a poison build, this is one of the few ways to raise the per-tick ceiling.

Best for melee bruisers and sustain builds.

### Wisdom — arcane power

Rolls magic attack, crit, and magical/reaction damage. Set-piece coverage: **Azure, Tactician, Tempest, Tidal**. Blessing upgrades include **Elemental Blade AQUA**, **Cryo Shards**, **Mana Eater**, **Oath of the Corrupted**, and **Reaction Discharge**.

Best for: casters and reaction-focused builds.

### Resolve — bruiser balance

Rolls attack and HP with defensive and physical-damage bonuses. Set-piece coverage: **Blazing, Golden, Guardian**. Blessing upgrades include **Entropic Arrows**, **Lifeline**, **Soulfire**, and **Elemental Blade TERRA**.

Best for hybrid melee.

### Resilience — magical warding

Rolls magic, resistance, reaction/condition power, and damage reduction. Set-piece coverage: **Earthen, Harbinger, Radiant**. Blessing upgrades include **Distortion Field**, **Guardian Angel**, **Prismatic Mirror**, and **Elemental Blade VENTI**. Best for tanks and resistance stackers.

### Balance — the generalist

Rolls a little of everything, and it is the **only path that also rolls condition-damage and poison-cap runes**. Set-piece coverage: **Glacial, Harmony, Mist, Voltic**. Blessing upgrades include **Elemental Spirit**, **Pact with the Forest**, **Phantom Blades**, and **Radiant Onslaught**.

Balance is the home of the condition-damage runes: **Bleed, Burn, Decay, Frostbite, Poison, and Shock DMG +24-30% at Legendary / +30-36% at Supreme**. If your build applies a condition, this path is often the difference between a good run and a great one.

## Grade Ranges: Why Supreme Matters

Each effect has separate ranges for each grade. The official wiki's example is Valor's **ATK**:

- **Mythic:** +48-60
- **Legendary:** +60-75
- **Supreme:** +72-90

Two consequences follow:

1. **A Supreme rune rolls a higher ceiling.** If you are choosing between identifying a Mythic and a Supreme, the Supreme has more upside.
2. **A low roll is still a low roll.** A minimum-roll Supreme can be beaten by a high-roll Legendary, so grade alone does not decide the outcome.

## Set-Piece Substitution Is the Hidden Power

The most important line in the official rune documentation is easy to miss: **a rune can stand in for a missing piece of its set, at the rune's grade.**

That means a rune is not only a stat stick. If you have three pieces of a four-piece set and cannot find the fourth, a rune from the matching path can complete the bonus. This is often worth more than the rune's raw stats, because set bonuses tend to be multiplicative or behavior-changing.

Use the path-to-set table above in reverse:

- Need a **Barbarian, Scarlet, Secret Agent, or Vampiric** piece? Look for **Valor**.
- Need **Draconic, Miasma, Phantom, or Verdant**? Look for **Vigor**.
- Need **Azure, Tactician, Tempest, or Tidal**? Look for **Wisdom**.
- Need **Blazing, Golden, or Guardian**? Look for **Resolve**.
- Need **Earthen, Harbinger, or Radiant**? Look for **Resilience**.
- Need **Glacial, Harmony, Mist, or Voltic**? Look for **Balance**.

**Pro tip:** before you socket a rune for stats, check whether it completes a set. A mediocre rune that finishes a four-piece bonus usually beats a great rune that does nothing else.

## Blessing Upgrades: Only Socket If You Have the Blessing

Runes can also empower specific Tower Blessings. The wiki's wording is conditional: **while socketed, the rune empowers that blessing once you pick it.** If you never pick the blessing, the blessing effect is dead weight.

That makes blessing runes a two-step commitment: you must find and take the blessing during the run, and you must keep the rune socketed when you do. Because blessings are run-random, blessing runes are high-variance. The stat portion still works either way, so a blessing rune with good stats is never wasted — but do not pay Supreme-level Alteria for an effect you may never trigger.

## A Practical Socketing Strategy

Here is the order we recommend for Early Access:

1. **Pick one weapon and one armor piece you will keep for several floors.** Do not open sockets on gear you are about to replace.
2. **Open sockets only on Legendary or Supreme gear.** Mythic gear has one socket; the Alteria is usually better spent elsewhere.
3. **Match the rune grade to the gear grade** so you do not pay Supreme identification costs for a Mythic socket.
4. **Prioritize set completion over raw stats.** A rune that finishes a set beats a slightly bigger ATK roll.
5. **Socket condition runes on condition builds.** Balance-path Frostbite, Shock, Bleed, Burn, Decay, and Poison runes are build-defining for the 0.5.05 Cryofield and Shock Conductor sets.
6. **Never remove a rune casually.** Removal destroys it. Experiment on a cheap Mythic first.

## Common Mistakes

- **Socketing a dormant rune.** It cannot be socketed until identified.
- **Removing a rune to "try something else."** The rune is destroyed.
- **Ignoring pre-filled gear.** Some drops already have a rune. Check before selling.
- **Chasing Supreme grade for a stat you can get elsewhere.** Grade raises the ceiling, not the floor.
- **Taking a blessing rune without the blessing.** The conditional effect does nothing.
- **Forgetting the Blacksmith.** Sockets start closed; the Rune Station cannot help until the socket is open.

## FAQ

**How do I get runes?** Enemies drop dormant and awakened runes. Dormant runes must be identified at the Rune Station in the Glyph Master's house.

**How many sockets does each grade have?** Mythic 1, Legendary 2, Supreme 3. Sockets start closed.

**Can I move a rune?** Not without destroying it. Removal frees the socket but consumes the rune.

**Can I reroll a rune?** No reroll is documented. Identification rolls the path and effect once.

**Do runes count as set pieces?** Yes. A rune can stand in for a missing piece of its set at the rune's grade.

## Final Verdict

Runes turn Phantom Tower's high-rarity gear from a stat pile into a build. Identify before you socket, open sockets only on gear you will keep, prefer set completion over raw numbers, and never remove a rune unless you are willing to lose it. Spend Alteria on the rune that answers your build's biggest problem — not the one with the highest grade.
