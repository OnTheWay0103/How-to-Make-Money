# Heavy Class Guide — Zombie Assault Resurgence Build

The Heavy is the durability-first class in Zombie Assault: Resurgence, built around standing your ground while the horde breaks against you. It shares the same skeleton as the [Assault](/guides/assault-class-guide) and [Medic](/guides/medic-class-guide) — six Core skills, six Class skills, three Activated Abilities and two Speciality branches — so the Heavy's identity comes from how you weight survival against output.

## What the Heavy is for

Resurgence's mission list rewards a class that can hold a position. Cache Recovery and Uplink both ask a squad to defend an objective while a timer runs, and in those modes the player who does not get pushed off the point is the most valuable one on the team. The Heavy is the natural fit for that job.

The store page frames the three classes as distinct picks without printing individual role blurbs, and the developer diaries describe a shared skill structure rather than per-class powers. So the honest statement is this: the Heavy *reads* as the tank and sustained-fire class, the systems support that reading, but the launch build has not published a public table of Heavy skill names. Anyone who gives you exact percentages today is quoting the older SAS4 game or making them up.

That does not leave you without a plan. The structure is enough to build around.

## The point economy, briefly

Skill points come in three types, and the Heavy build is shaped by the fact that they are rationed separately.

| Type | When it arrives | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| Basic | Levels 1, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16, 18, 20 | Shared Core skills |
| Class | Levels 2, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15, 17, 19 | Heavy-specific Class skills |
| Subclass | First at level 20, then every other level | The locked Speciality branch |

By level 20 you have 10 Basic, 10 Class and 1 Subclass point ([Community] Levels (ZAR)). The trap for a new Heavy is spending all ten Class points on offence and leaving the Core tree thin, then discovering that "tanky" was the whole reason to pick the class.

## A tank-first build order, level 1–20

This is an editorial plan. It assumes your job is to survive the objective and keep firing, not to top the damage chart.

| Phase | Levels | Priority | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Survive first | 1–6 | Core survivability and a first weapon-handling node | Early gear is weak; raw toughness keeps you in the fight long enough to level |
| Sustain | 7–12 | Class nodes that support sustained fire and crowd control | The Heavy's value is uptime, not burst |
| Third ability | 10 | Unlock and slot the third Activated Ability | More cooldowns means more ways to survive a bad wave |
| Branch preview | 13–18 | Bank a few Class points and read both Speciality branches | The branch locks in; do not commit without reading it |
| Subclass | 20+ | Put the first Subclass point into the chosen branch | Subclass points only fit the locked branch |

The one deviation worth considering is a damage-heavy Heavy for solo play. Solo, you cannot rely on a teammate to finish what you started, so trading some toughness for output is reasonable — but do it after level 10, when you have the points to spare.

## Tough Mode changes the maths

The playtest build included a Tough Mode modifier that raises difficulty and rewards at the same time. For a Heavy this is the mode that justifies the class most clearly: the survivability you bought with Core points is exactly what lets a squad attempt the higher difficulty without wiping to the first elite pack.

If you plan to farm Tough Mode, build defensively first and treat damage as the secondary axis. A dead Heavy contributes nothing, and the reward multiplier only pays out on a completed run.

## How the Heavy reads across the mission types

The five mission types are not equally kind to a tank, and knowing which is which stops you from concluding the class is weak when it is simply in the wrong mode.

| Mission type | Heavy value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Cache Recovery | High | Cache drops arrive on a cadence you have to survive between |
| Uplink | High | Holding a point under pressure is the class's home ground |
| Close the Breach | Medium-high | Contained fights reward durability |
| Eradication Surge | Medium | Waves come to you, but they also come from every direction |
| Eradication | Low | Clear speed matters more than staying power |

In a pure Eradication run the Heavy is the slowest way to finish, and that is a fair criticism rather than a build problem. If your group farms Eradication for XP, consider bringing the class only when the squad needs someone to hold a doorway while the rest rotate.

## Which weapons fit a Heavy

The Heavy is not locked to one gun family, but the role pushes toward weapons that reward standing still and pouring fire into a lane. The [full weapon catalogue](/guides/all-weapons-guide) lists what the launch build actually contains, grouped by category and manufacturer.

| Need | What to look for | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Lane control | High-capacity automatic or LMG-class weapon | Sustained fire beats reload-heavy burst when the horde is a stream |
| Objective defence | Area denial from grenades or a turret | The HVM Grenade and Turret were both in the demo |
| Elite answer | A heavier secondary for armoured targets | Do not rely on one gun for both horde and elite |
| Mobility check | Watch the movement penalty | Heavy weapons slow you; Uplink still needs repositioning |

Because Resurgence lets you modify and upgrade gear, the strongest play is usually to pick one workhorse weapon and invest in it through the [mod and crafting system](/guides/weapon-mods-and-upgrading) rather than chasing every drop. The [best weapons tier list](/guides/best-weapons-tier-list) ranks the launch arsenal by role if you want a starting shortlist.

## Armour and survivability

Armour is part of the loadout system, and the demo included Common and Uncommon armour pieces alongside mods. For a Heavy, armour is not cosmetic: it is the second half of the survivability plan, and it should be upgraded in step with your Core points. A Heavy with strong skills and neglected armour is only half-built.

The practical rule is to keep armour current as you level and to prioritise pieces that support the way you actually play — holding a point, absorbing chip damage, and staying upright long enough for the Medic to reach you.

## Solo and co-op

Solo, the Heavy is a forgiving class: the extra durability gives you room to learn enemy patterns and mission layouts without losing the run to a single mistake. It is a good first class for that reason.

In co-op, the Heavy's job is to anchor. Stand where the objective is, let the Assault roam for damage, and give the Medic a stable patient to keep alive. The failure mode is a Heavy that chases kills into the open and leaves the point undefended; the class rewards discipline more than aggression.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Buying damage before durability.** The Heavy's edge is surviving; spend accordingly.
- **Ignoring armour.** Skills and gear are two halves of the same defence.
- **Committing to a Speciality branch early.** Bank points, read both branches, then lock.
- **Bringing a slow weapon to a movement-heavy mode.** Check the penalty before you commit.
- **Assuming SAS4 numbers carry over.** Resurgence has its own mods, rarity and Subclasses.
