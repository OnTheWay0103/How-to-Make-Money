# Assault Class Guide — Zombie Assault Resurgence Build

The Assault is one of three launch classes in Zombie Assault: Resurgence, alongside [Heavy](/guides/heavy-class-guide) and [Medic](/guides/medic-class-guide). Every class draws from the same skeleton — six Core skills, six Class skills, three Activated Abilities and two Speciality branches — so the real difference is where your points go and when. Here is how the system works and a build order you can follow from level 1.

## What is officially true about every class

Before any opinion, here is what the store page and the developer diaries actually confirm. This is the framework every Assault build sits inside.

| System | Confirmed detail | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Classes | Assault, Heavy, Medic | Steam store page |
| Core skills | 6, shared across classes | Steam store page |
| Class skills | 6, unique per class | Steam store page |
| Activated Abilities | 3 per class; the third unlocks at rank 10 in the playtest build | Store page + Dev Diary #8 |
| Speciality | 2 branches; you pick one and "lock in" its traits | Steam store page |
| Respec | Allowed, but costs in-game currency | Dev Diary #8 |
| Mode balance | Built to be equally playable solo and in 4-player co-op | Dev Diary #8 |

The Assault's identity as a direct-damage class is the natural reading of the name, and it is the class the store page lists first. What the launch build does *not* publish is a public, ZA:R-specific list of skill names and damage numbers. That matters: any guide that hands you exact percentages right now is either quoting the older SAS4 game or guessing. This page stays on the safe side of that line and explains the decisions instead.

## How skill points actually arrive

Skill points are not a single currency. The community XP table for Resurgence splits them into three types, and each type feeds a different part of the tree.

| Level | Total XP | Point granted |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 850 | 1 Basic |
| 2 | 1,870 | 1 Class |
| 3 | 3,094 | 1 Class |
| 4 | 4,563 | 1 Basic |
| 5 | 6,326 | 1 Class |
| 6 | 8,441 | 1 Basic |
| 8 | 14,025 | 1 Basic |
| 10 | 22,066 | 1 Basic |
| 20 | 145,850 | 1 Basic + 1 Subclass |

By level 20 that works out to 10 Basic points, 10 Class points and your first Subclass point ([Community] Levels (ZAR)). The practical consequence is simple: you cannot dump everything into one tree. Basic points are your shared foundation, Class points are where the Assault differentiates itself, and Subclass points only start at 20 and then arrive every other level.

Two rules follow from that. First, do not spend Basic points as if they were Class points — they are rationed separately. Second, plan your first twenty levels before you spend anything, because a respec costs currency you would rather put into a weapon.

## A practical Assault build order, level 1–20

This is an editorial plan, not a datamined one. It assumes the Assault's job is to convert points into sustained damage while staying mobile enough to survive the five mission types.

| Phase | Levels | What to prioritise | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Foundation | 1–6 | Spread early Basic points across survivability and a first damage node | You have very few points and no gear yet; a glass cannon dies before it earns XP |
| Core damage | 7–14 | Push the Class tree toward its damage and fire-rate nodes | This is where Assault pulls ahead of the other two classes |
| Ability timing | 10 | Take the third Activated Ability as soon as it unlocks | A third cooldown is a large jump in output and escape options |
| Branch decision | 15–20 | Hold Class points until you have seen both Speciality branches | The branch is a lock-in; do not commit blind |
| Subclass | 20+ | Spend the first Subclass point into the branch you locked | Subclass points are scarce and only fit the chosen branch |

The key judgement is the branch. Because the choice is permanent for that character, the safe play is to bank a few Class points around level 15–18, read both branches, then commit. A respec can undo a mistake, but it costs currency, and early currency is better spent on [weapon upgrades](/guides/weapon-mods-and-upgrading).

## Abilities: three slots, one clear rule

Every class gets three Activated Abilities, and the third arrives at rank 10. Treat them as your emergency and burst buttons rather than something you fire on cooldown. In practice that means one ability for "I am about to be overwhelmed", one for "this boss is staggered and I want damage now", and one flex slot that matches the mission type.

Mission type changes the flex pick more than the class does. Cache Recovery and Uplink ask you to hold a position while an objective completes, so area denial and defensive tools earn their slot. Eradication and Eradication Surge are pure pressure, so a mobility or damage cooldown is better. Close the Breach sits between the two. If you only run one loadout, build around the objective modes, because those are where a wipe actually costs you the run.

## Which guns to pair with an Assault

The Assault does not have a weapon restriction, so the pairing is about range coverage. A mid-range primary plus a close-range secondary covers most rooms, and the [full weapon list](/guides/all-weapons-guide) shows what is actually in the launch build. Three pairing rules hold up across the mission types:

- **One automatic, one burst.** Automatics handle the horde; a harder-hitting secondary handles elites and boss windows.
- **Watch the movement penalty.** Heavy weapons slow you down, and Resurgence leans on repositioning during Uplink and Cache Recovery.
- **Upgrade one gun properly.** A modified mid-tier weapon usually beats two unmodified drops, so pick a favourite and invest through the [crafting and mod system](/guides/weapon-mods-and-upgrading).

For a class-by-class shortlist, the [best weapons tier list](/guides/best-weapons-tier-list) ranks the launch arsenal by role rather than by invented DPS numbers.

## Solo versus co-op

The developers have said the game is built to be equally playable solo and in a four-player squad, and that is the right lens for an Assault build. Solo, you are the only damage source, so survivability nodes are not optional — a down ends the run. In co-op, you can lean harder into damage because a Medic can cover mistakes and a Heavy can hold the line.

That does not mean the Assault should ignore defence in a group. The most common co-op failure is an Assault that over-commits, gets surrounded, and forces the team to fight through a revive. Position so the horde meets you at a chokepoint, not in the open.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Spending every point in the Class tree on day one.** Basic points are a separate pool; ignoring them leaves you fragile.
- **Locking a Speciality branch at level 15 because a video said so.** The branch is permanent until you pay to respec.
- **Treating the third ability as a level-1 tool.** It unlocks at rank 10; plan the early game without it.
- **Hoarding Strongboxes.** Gear you never open cannot help you; open and equip as you go.
- **Copying SAS4 builds wholesale.** Resurgence is a sequel with its own mods, expanded rarity and Subclasses; older numbers do not transfer.

Until then, use the structure above, spend conservatively, and keep enough Class points in reserve to commit to a branch once you have read it in-game.
