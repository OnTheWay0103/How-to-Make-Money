# Quest Guide — Main Story, Side Quests & Missions Explained

Questing in DragonSword: Awakening is the backbone of progression. Because the game was designed as a buy-to-play package with no gacha, quests are how you earn everything — heroes, gear, and story progression. This guide covers what is confirmed about the quest structure, what remains unconfirmed, and how to prioritize your time.

## Quest Types

Based on the game's official description and player reports, quests fall into these categories:

### Main Story Quests

The main story follows **Lute**, a young boy who falls in with a mercenary band alongside **Johnny** and **Castella**, as the party traces the footsteps of the **six Heroes of Old** across the continent of **Orbis**. The story is structured in chapters, and completing main quests:

- Advances the narrative toward the final boss (see the [Walkthrough](/guides/walkthrough) for the confirmed story beats)
- Unlocks new regions, heroes, and systems
- Is the fastest reliable progression in the early game

### Side Quests

The game's official description mentions "solving local residents' problems" as a core side activity, alongside cooking, treasure hunting, and dungeon exploration. Side quests in DragonSword: Awakening are how the world feels alive — the residents of Orbis have personal stories, and helping them pays in gold, materials, and occasionally hero recruitment. Specific side quest names, locations, and rewards have not been comprehensively documented yet (TBD), so this wiki lists them as we verify them.

### Promotion Quests

DragonSword: Awakening tracks your overall progress through a **Mercenary Rank** system, and each rank up requires completing a **promotion quest**. These are the game's version of a mastery test — solo challenges that gate your rank, gear access, and content unlocks. Exact rank names, the full rank ladder, and what each promotion requires are not yet publicly documented; we mark these details as **unconfirmed** until the community documents them.

### Hero Recruitment Quests

There is no gacha in DragonSword: Awakening — all 19 launch heroes are earned through gameplay. A meaningful share of them come from story progression, but others are tied to quests, exploration, and secret content. See the [Hero Unlock Guide](/guides/hero-unlock-guide) for everything that is confirmed about recruitment.

## The Confirmed Quest Framework

While individual quest names are not yet well documented, the following is confirmed from official sources:

- **The prologue** introduces Lute and the tutorial combat, ending with a first boss encounter against an **Orc Warrior** attacking a town gate.
- **The story spans chapters** across the continent of Orbis, from meadows and ruins to underwater areas and cities. Players commonly describe three main story chapters at launch.
- **The final story boss is Ursula**, a three-phase fight that is the endpoint of the main quest line.
- **Boss encounters along the way** include Furcas, the Grassland Lesser Dragon, Barduk, and a Lich — confirmed via a full-bosses gameplay compilation (details in the [Boss Guide](/guides/boss-guide)).
- **The six Heroes of Old** are a lore framework: legendary figures who drove back the dragon 60 years before the game's events. They are not playable; the main quest traces their footsteps.

## How to Prioritize Quests

Until comprehensive walkthroughs are published, follow this priority order:

1. **Main story first.** It unlocks new regions, heroes, and systems at the fastest rate. Side content is always waiting for you; story gates are real.
2. **Do side quests as you pass them.** Since side quests feed your economy (cooking, materials) and some lead to hero recruitment, a quick detour is almost always worth it.
3. **Push promotion quests when available.** Each Mercenary Rank promotion is a one-time gate that unlocks new content tiers — doing them early keeps your progression smooth.
4. **Hunt for treasure and hidden content between chapters.** The open world hides dungeons and secret areas that reward exploration.

## Common Questions

**Are quests missable?**
Based on community reports, the main story cannot be permanently missed, and most side content remains available after the story. The game's single-player, no-live-service design makes permanent missables unlikely, but this is not officially confirmed.

**Do quests unlock heroes?**
Yes — hero recruitment is tied to story, quests, and exploration. Exact methods for each hero are documented where confirmed in the [Hero Unlock Guide](/guides/hero-unlock-guide).

**Is there a daily or weekly quest system?**
No daily quest rotation has been confirmed. The game has no live-service loop of daily chores; endgame activities like [World Bosses, Hunts, and the Tower of Trials](/guides/endgame-guide) provide repeatable content instead.

**Do choices in quests matter?**
No branching-choice system has been confirmed. The story is linear, with the focus on combat and exploration rather than dialogue choices.

## Bottom Line

DragonSword: Awakening's quest design is straightforward: a linear main story with side quests, promotion quests, and hidden content layered on top — all feeding into a single progression track with zero gacha.
