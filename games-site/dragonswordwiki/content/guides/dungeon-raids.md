## Raids in DragonSword: Awakening

**Raids are 3-player cooperative boss encounters** — the game's most substantial multiplayer content. HOUND13's official description confirms co-op for **up to 3 players in Raid**, and the raid content lives in the endgame alongside the Tower of Trials, Hunts, and World Bosses.

One important correction for players coming from MMO guides: **there are no 8-player raids in DragonSword: Awakening.** The game is a single-player action RPG with optional co-op; raids scale for three players, not raid-sized parties. Any guide claiming larger groups, weekly lockouts, or raid classes is describing a different game.

## What Is Confirmed

- **Party size:** up to 3 players in Raid co-op
- **Session model:** host-based lobbies; no regional restrictions on who can join
- **Content tier:** raids are endgame encounters, harder than story bosses
- **Combat model:** raids use the same tag-team combat — every player's party of heroes chains status ailments and Signal Skills (see the [Combat Guide](/guides/combat-guide))

## How Co-op Raids Work with the Combat System

Because DragonSword: Awakening's combat is built around ailment stacking and switching, raid success depends on team synergy:

- **Stack matching ailments.** With up to three players, you have up to nine heroes in play. Keep ailments focused — two or three shared effects build Signal Skill triggers far faster than nine different ones (see the [Team Compositions Guide](/guides/team-compositions)).
- **Cover the breaker role.** Super Armor breaking opens finisher prompts. Make sure someone brings **Castella** (Break + Stun) or another confirmed breaker.
- **Diversify your Signal chains.** Avoid three heroes with identical ailments — you want a chain where one hero's ailment sets up the next hero's Signal Skill.

## Raid Etiquette and Preparation

While the community catalogues raid mechanics, these habits will serve you well:

1. **Communicate your party composition before entering.** Agree on which ailments you are stacking.
2. **Learn the bosses solo first.** Story bosses and Hunts (re-fighting story bosses at higher difficulty) teach the attack patterns that raid bosses likely build on. See the [Boss Guide](/guides/boss-guide).
3. **Bring upgraded gear.** Raids are endgame content — make sure your equipment has been upgraded through the [gear progression](/guides/gear-progression) before you queue up.
4. **Have the free Abyssal Direwolf familiar installed.** The free DLC familiar gives a movement edge that helps in any co-op session (see the [Familiars Guide](/guides/familiars-guide)).
