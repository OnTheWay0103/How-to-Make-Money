**Dressmaker is a cozy dressmaking simulation developed by Cozy Lives and published by Free Lives, releasing on Steam on September 21, 2026 for Windows and macOS.** Fabric is the first real decision you make in it, and unlike most crafting games, it is not a single decision — you choose cloth for each panel of the garment separately.

This guide covers every fabric the official store description names, what each one is actually like to handle, how patterned cloth changes your layout, and — importantly — where the confirmed information stops.

## What the Official Description Actually Confirms

Three sentences carry the whole fabric system, and they are worth reading closely:

> "Buy from a wide range of fabrics, including cotton, linen, wool, silk and velvet."

> "Take full control over what fabric to use for each panel of your garment."

> "Choose fabric, arrange and cut out patterns…"

So: there is a shop, the five named fibres are in it, and the choice is **per panel**, not per garment. That last point is the one that changes how you play.

## Per-Panel Fabric Is the Core Idea

Most dress-up and crafting games treat an outfit as one material. Dressmaker does not. A dress is several panels, and each panel gets its own cloth.

That mirrors real dressmaking, where mixing fibres within one garment is normal and deliberate:

- A **structured bodice** wants something with body that holds a shape — wool or a firmly woven cotton.
- A **flowing skirt** wants weight and drape — silk, or a softer cotton.
- **Velvet** on a full panel reads as luxury; velvet on a small one reads as trim.
- **Linen** creases, which is charming on a casual summer dress and wrong on a formal commission.

Because the game lets you decide panel by panel, "what fabric should I use?" is the wrong question. The right one is **"what should this specific panel be made of?"** — and the answer changes depending on which part of the dress you are looking at.

If you are still learning the parts of a garment, see [Sketchbook and Dress Parts](/guides/dressmaker-sketchbook-dress-parts) for what bodice, collar, sleeves and skirt each do.

## Choosing Fabric for a Commission

Customers in Dressmaker are described officially as wanting *"custom-made dresses tailored specially to important events and their individual tastes."* That sentence is your fabric brief, and it gives you two filters.

**The event sets the register.** A dress for an important occasion is not a dress for everyday wear. Heavier, richer cloth signals formality; light cloth signals the opposite. Since the game explicitly frames garments around events, matching the cloth to the occasion is the safe default before you get creative.

**The taste sets the colour and finish.** Individual taste is called out as a real variable. A customer who likes a particular look is a customer you should not dress in your own favourite fabric.

There is also a confirmed third option: the official description says you can *"satisfy (or sabotage!)"* the townsfolk. Dressing someone deliberately wrong is a supported choice, not a failure state.

**Fabric preference tables per customer have not been published [Unconfirmed].** Preview coverage has suggested that garment parts carry style characteristics that can be stacked to match a customer (see [Customer Requests](/guides/dressmaker-customer-requests-style-tags)), but no named customer list or preference chart exists in any official material.

## Patterned Fabric — Seen in Previews

Plain cloth is only part of the shop. Pre-release preview coverage has shown **patterned fabrics including houndstooth, floral, plaid, damask and gingham** [**preview-sourced — [Unconfirmed]** for the release].

Pattern matters for two reasons beyond looks:

1. **Patterns have a direction.** Stripes, plaids and one-way prints are not rotationally symmetric. A pattern piece rotated to save cloth can end up with its pattern running sideways against the panel next to it. This is the single most common way a layout that looked efficient turns into a garment that looks wrong.
2. **Patterns interact with the grain.** A piece can be correctly aligned to the grain and still have its pattern running the wrong way, because the two are separate properties.

Practical rule: **decide whether the pattern has a direction before you start laying pieces out.** If it does, treat rotation as a last resort rather than a free optimisation.

## Fabric and Layout Are the Same Decision

Fabric choice is not finished when you pick the cloth — it continues into how you arrange the pattern pieces on it. The official description ties the two together directly:

> "Whether your pattern is aligned to the grain or cut on bias, how you choose to arrange and cut out your fabric is what appears on the final dress."

Velvet is the clearest example. Its nap is directional, so two panels cut with the nap running opposite ways will look like two different colours under the same light. No amount of careful sewing fixes that. It has to be right on the cutting table.

That mechanic is covered in full in [Grain Direction and Bias Cut](/guides/dressmaker-grain-direction-bias-cut), and the layout economy side in [Pattern Placement](/guides/dressmaker-pattern-placement-cutting).

## Buying Fabric Sensibly

The description says you **buy** fabric, so cloth is a resource with a cost. Two habits follow:

- **Buy for the garment you are about to make**, not for a hypothetical one. Cloth sitting in your stash is coin you cannot spend on the commission in front of you.
- **Sketch before you shop.** Because the sketchbook accepts fabric samples and colour, you can settle the look before committing money — see the [Beginner Guide](/guides/dressmaker-beginner-guide).

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many fabrics are in Dressmaker?**
Five are named officially: cotton, linen, wool, silk and velvet. The description says "a wide range of fabrics, including" those five, which reads as a non-exhaustive list — so more may exist at launch [Unconfirmed].

Not published [Unconfirmed]. The game has not shown a numeric fabric stat system publicly.

**Can I mix fabrics in one dress?**
Yes — the official description confirms per-panel fabric choice as a core control.

**Is there a best fabric?**
Not without published stats. Choose by what the garment needs: structure, drape, warmth or surface.

Unknown [Unconfirmed]. No unlock system has been described.
