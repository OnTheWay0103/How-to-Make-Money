**Dressmaker is a cozy dressmaking simulation developed by Cozy Lives and published by Free Lives, releasing on Steam on September 21, 2026 for Windows and macOS.** Decoration is the last thing you do to a dress and the first thing anyone notices about it. It is also where the game's most frequently reported friction point lives — so this page covers both the creative side and the known rough edge.

---

## The Confirmed Decoration Kit

The official description lists the options directly:

> "Decorate your dress with buttons, bows and appliqué. Add lace trims and other accessories wherever you like with complete creative freedom."

Four confirmed categories, plus an open-ended fifth:

| Category | What it does on a garment |
|---|---|
| **Buttons** | Functional or purely decorative fastening; the strongest vertical or repeated accent |
| **Bows** | A deliberate focal point — reads as emphasis wherever it sits |
| **Appliqué** | A fabric shape applied onto the surface; the most graphic option |
| **Lace trims** | Edge finishing; softens a hem, neckline or sleeve edge |
| **Other accessories** | Unspecified in official material — the description leaves the category open |

The phrase doing the real work in that sentence is **"wherever you like with complete creative freedom."** Placement is a player decision, not a fixed slot. That is what makes decoration a design problem rather than a checklist.

## Appliqué Appears Twice

Worth flagging, because it is easy to miss: **appliqué is listed both as a sketchbook material and as a decoration.**

> "Design and colour your dress in your sketchbook, adding fabric samples and appliqué."

> "Decorate your dress with buttons, bows and appliqué."

The consistent reading is that appliqué is a **design decision you plan in the sketchbook and execute on the finished garment**. If you treat it as a last-minute addition, you may find it does not fit the design you already committed to. Plan it early — see [Sketchbook and Dress Parts](/guides/dressmaker-sketchbook-dress-parts).

**Whether the sketchbook appliqué and the decoration-step appliqué use the same assets or are two separate systems is not documented [Unconfirmed].**

## Where to Put Things

Real dressmaking has conventions about decoration placement, and they are worth using because they are the reason certain placements read as intentional:

**Buttons**
- Down the centre front of a bodice — the classic vertical line.
- A single statement button at the neckline, waist or back closure.
- A short row on a sleeve cuff, which adds weight to the arm without cluttering the body.

**Bows**
- At the waist, where the bow can act as the seam between bodice and skirt.
- At the back, for a detail the customer discovers rather than presents.
- On a shoulder or sleeve head, for asymmetry.
- Rule of thumb: **one bow is a focal point; three are a pattern.** Both are valid, but decide which you are doing.

**Appliqué**
- Where the garment has empty space to fill — a plain skirt panel, a bodice front.
- Where it reinforces the silhouette rather than fighting it.
- Not across a seam line you want the eye to follow.

**Lace trims**
- Along a hem, a neckline or a sleeve edge — anywhere there is an actual edge to finish.
- Layered over another fabric to add texture without changing the shape.

None of these are game rules; they are the real-world conventions the game says it is inspired by. Use them as defaults, then break them deliberately.

## The Known Placement Problem

This is the part of decoration that most guides skip.

**Prototype players have repeatedly described certain angles or positions where a decoration refuses to attach** [**community-reported from the prototype — [Unconfirmed]** for the September 21 release].

The important thing to know is that this is a **reported rough edge, not something you are doing wrong.** If you have placed decorations successfully on the front of a garment and then cannot get one to sit on a shoulder or an inside curve, the likely explanation is the placement surface, not your input.

Practical responses that do not depend on unpublished mechanics:

1. **Try a slightly different angle or position on the same panel.** If the surface is the problem, a small move may succeed where the exact spot fails.
2. **Move the decoration to an adjacent area that does accept it.** A bow that will not sit exactly on the shoulder may sit at the sleeve head.
3. **Decorate before the garment is fully assembled if the release allows it.** Whether decoration can be applied to flat panels is not documented [Unconfirmed], but it is worth testing.
4. **Plan fewer, better-placed decorations.** If some positions are unreliable, a design that needs three precise placements is more fragile than one that needs one.
5. **Do not restart the garment.** A dress with one decoration in a slightly different place is not a ruined dress.

If the release changes this behaviour, this section will be the first thing updated.

## Decoration and the Customer

Decoration is where a correct dress becomes a personal one, and customers are officially described as having **individual tastes** and dressing for **important events**.

That gives decoration two jobs:

- **Match the taste.** A customer with a restrained style is not served by heavy lace. Taste is the filter.
- **Match the occasion.** A dress for an important event can carry more ornament than everyday wear.

And it gives decoration one trap: **over-decorating a dress that was already right.** The decoration step is the last one, so it is the easiest place to add something the brief did not ask for. If the dress matched the request before you started decorating, the burden is on the decoration to justify itself.

See [Customer Requests](/guides/dressmaker-customer-requests-style-tags) for how tastes and events are framed.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What decorations are in Dressmaker?**
Buttons, bows, appliqué and lace trims are named in the official description, plus "other accessories" left unspecified.

**Can I place decorations anywhere?**
The official description says "wherever you like with complete creative freedom". Prototype players report some positions refusing to accept a decoration [Unconfirmed].

**Why will my decoration not attach?**
Most likely a reported placement rough edge rather than an error on your part [Unconfirmed]. Try a nearby position.

**Can I remove a decoration?**
Not documented [Unconfirmed].

**Does decoration affect my quality score?**
Not documented [Unconfirmed]. No published scoring rules mention decoration.

**Should I decorate a commission or keep it plain?**
Match the customer's taste and the event. The official description frames customers around individual tastes, so there is no universally correct amount of ornament.
