# Why Your Dressmaker Order Fails — Quality, Stat Minimums and Mandatory Rules

A Dressmaker commission can look correct and still come back as a failure. The silhouette matches, the colours are close, the customer's taste seems right — and the card still does not pass. The reason is usually that the order was judged as a **stack of independent rules**, not as a single style impression.

This is the post-launch version of the question. The earlier [customer requests guide](/guides/dressmaker-customer-requests-style-tags) explains the pre-release picture: customers have individual tastes and events, and garment parts carry style characteristics. That is still true, but it is only the top layer. Community card databases now document the layer underneath — Quality thresholds, numeric style minimums and caps, mandatory accessory counts, fabric shares and colour restrictions.

## The Pass/Fail Stack

Every commission asks you to clear several separate checks:

1. **Quality** must reach a minimum — or, on a few cards, stay below a maximum.
2. **Style stats** must reach their green minimums.
3. **Style caps** must not be exceeded — more is not always better.
4. **Mandatory rules** must be satisfied: specific accessories, measured trim, fabric percentages, colour restrictions or allowed fabric lists.
5. **The budget** determines your pay, not what you are allowed to spend.

If any one of those fails, the dress fails, even if the other four are excellent. That is why a beautiful, expensive dress can lose to a plain one that followed every red rule.

## Quality Is Construction, Not Cost

The single most useful correction is this: **Quality is not bought with expensive fabric.** Community guides describe it as coming from fit, cutting on the grain and sewing accuracy. An expensive silk cut off-grain can score worse than a cheap cotton cut properly.

This connects directly to the site's existing construction pages:

- [Grain direction and bias cut](/guides/dressmaker-grain-direction-bias-cut) explains why a misaligned pattern piece can hard-cap the finished Quality.
- [Sewing clean seams](/guides/dressmaker-sewing-guide-clean-seams) covers the stitching accuracy that feeds the same score.
- [Pattern placement](/guides/dressmaker-pattern-placement-cutting) covers the cutting stage that decides how much of the fabric's quality you actually get to keep.

Community card lists put minimum Quality anywhere from **60 to 95** across the full set, with story commissions in one catalogue sitting at **60-80**. The exact number depends on the card, so read the requirement before you choose materials. A useful benchmark from the same data: many gift commissions ask for **Quality 80+**, while the hardest cards go higher.

Two exceptions break the "higher is better" assumption:

- **Not Haughty** requires **Quality 70 max**. A dress that is too well made fails the brief.
- **Chilly Overtime** asks for Quality 70+ but caps **Cute at 20** and **Romantic at 20**. You need clean construction without pushing the dress into a cute or romantic register.

If a card gives you a ceiling, treat it as a hard rule. Do not assume the game will ignore the excess because the rest of the dress is good.

## Style Minimums and Caps

The style bars are usually shown as green minimums and red limits. Green means "reach this." Red means "do not cross this." They are not decorative guidance.

Community databases list requirements ranging from roughly **20+ to 85+** depending on the card, with examples such as Risqué 20+, Cute 25+, Professional 75+ and Gothic 80+. Caps are just as varied: Chilly Overtime's Cute 20 max, Tomb Raider's Elaborate 5 max, Goat Mediation's Formal 40 max and Never the Bride's Flowers 0 max.

The practical consequence is that you should build to the **brief**, not to the highest total. A dress that stacks every elegant, glamorous and elaborate tag may clear the minimum and then fail a cap on the same card. The ixbt review describes roughly **23 order indicators** in the release build; community databases currently expose a core set of named style tags rather than a single official vocabulary. Treat the exact taxonomy as build-dependent **[Unconfirmed]**.

The reliable workflow is:

1. Read the card's green minimums first.
2. Read every red cap and restriction second.
3. Choose the pattern and fabric combination that lands inside both.
4. Use accessories only to close the remaining gap.

Doing it in the opposite order — decorate first, then discover the cap — is the most common way to waste both materials and a commission.

## Mandatory Rules That Silently Fail You

These are the requirements most likely to be missed because they are not a "score." They are pass/fail conditions.

**Accessory counts.** A card may require at least one brooch, one jewel, three bows, five bells, five tassels, four feathers, ten feathers, three flower accessories or ten jewels. If the count is short, the dress fails even if every style bar is full.

**Measured trim.** Some cards ask for a length rather than a count: 10 cm of lace trim, 20 cm of trim, or 100 cm of lace trim. The category matters. A VGTimes walkthrough notes that one lace requirement accepts only the "Lace Trim" accessory category — lace fabric or ordinary trim does not satisfy it.

**Fabric percentages.** Cards can require at least 50% velvet, at least 60% cotton, 50% wool, 40% lace, 25% silk with 25% lace, or even 5% each of cotton, lace, linen, sequin, silk, velvet and wool. "Velvet only" and "cotton only" also appear. These are shares of the garment, not optional flourishes.

**Colour rules.** A card may ban black, require black only, require at least 50% black, allow only grey/black/brown, allow only orange/yellow/green, require green 40%, blue 75% or purple 50%. Violating a colour rule fails the commission.

**Exact-only rules.** Some cards say "silk only" or "white only." A near-match is still a fail. Read the word "only" literally.

## Budget Is Pay, Not a Spending Limit

The budget shown on a card is what the customer pays you. It is not a cap on what you may spend on materials. Community lists show budgets from **200 to 5,000** across the full card set; the 17 story commissions catalogued by VGTimes sit at **200-1,500**.

Two consequences follow:

- **Delivering a failed dress still pays the budget**, but it earns far less friendship and the card cannot be redone.
- **Spending more does not improve the score by itself.** Quality comes from construction; style comes from the right tags; mandatory rules come from compliance. A larger material bill can reduce your profit without changing the result.

That makes the budget a profitability signal. A 200-coin card with a 60 Quality requirement is not worth the same material investment as a 1,500-coin card with an 80 Quality requirement.

## A 60-Second Pre-Delivery Checklist

Before you hand the dress over, run the card from top to bottom:

1. **Quality:** Is it at or above the minimum — and below the maximum if one exists?
2. **Every green stat:** Does each one clear its number?
3. **Every red cap:** Is each one still under its limit?
4. **Accessory count:** Does the dress contain the exact required number and category?
5. **Trim length:** Is the measured amount actually present?
6. **Fabric share:** Does each required percentage add up on the garment?
7. **Colour rule:** Does the dress obey the prohibition, the "only" rule or the minimum share?
8. **Checker:** Use the in-game "Will my dress pass?" checker where available. It is faster than guessing and will tell you which requirement is short.

If you are unsure about a rule, do not deliver. A failed card cannot be redone, and the friendship loss is the part that compounds.

## Why Community Numbers Sometimes Disagree

You will see different totals in different guides: 77 cards in one database, 78 orders in another, Quality 60-95 in one list and 60-80 in a story-only list. These are not necessarily contradictions. They can reflect:

- all cards versus story commissions only;
- the build the guide was written on;
- post-launch adjustments;
- translation differences in stat names.

Treat the **structure** as stable — Quality, style minimums, caps, mandatory rules, budget — and treat the specific numbers as version-dependent. If a number decides whether you deliver, check it on the card in front of you.

## The Short Version

A Dressmaker order fails when one independent rule fails. Quality is construction, not cost. Green bars are minimums; red bars are caps. Accessory counts, trim lengths, fabric shares and colour rules are pass/fail, not scoring suggestions. The budget is your pay, not your spending limit. Read the whole card first, build to its limits, and use the checker before delivery.
