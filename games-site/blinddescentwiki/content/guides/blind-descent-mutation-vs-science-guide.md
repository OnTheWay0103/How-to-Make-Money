# Blind Descent Co-op: Mutation vs Science Path Guide

> Last verified: October 28, 2026 · Pre-release build (Early Access opens December 2, 2026)

Blind Descent asks you to choose between staying human and joining the ecosystem. The science route gives you tools, equipment and access to new technology. The symbiosis route gives you mutations, with telekinesis and wall running named as examples. The choice is per player, it is described as an either-or rather than a both, and it changes what your co-op crew can reach.

## The fork, in the game's own words

The store page frames the decision as a philosophy, not a class pick:

> "Take a scientific approach by crafting tools and equipment that help you and your team endure while preserving your humanity" or "become part of the ecosystem by undergoing mutations that enhance your abilities." It closes with: "Science or symbiosis—the choice is yours."

The premise that makes the choice matter is infection. You are infected by an alien growth, and the publisher's October 2026 release note frames the same fork mechanically: symbiosis "unlocks powerful mutations like telekinesis or wall running," while staying pure "means access to new technology."

So the trade is clean on paper: body versus gear. What is not published is the full list of mutations, the full list of locked technology, or whether the choice can be reversed. Those gaps matter and are marked below.

## What the symbiosis route gives you

Confirmed, from official material:

| Ability | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Telekinesis | Named as an example of a symbiosis mutation | Official press release, 2026-10-12 |
| Wall running | Named as an example of a symbiosis mutation | Official press release, 2026-10-12 |
| Ability gain through alien spores and fruits | Described as the mechanism | Official press release, 2025-07-30 |
| Improved combat and exploration | Described as the purpose of adaptations | Third-party coverage, 2026-10-14 |
| Full mutation list | [Unconfirmed] | Not published |

The mechanism the studio described early on is exposure: abilities come from alien spores, fruits and strange infections. That lines up with the setting, where you are already infected, and it implies the mutation route is fed by the ecosystem you are exploring rather than by a crafting bench.

For a player, that means the symbiosis route probably rewards exploration depth. The deeper and stranger the biome, the more likely you are to find the input for the next adaptation. We are labelling "probably" deliberately: no official source describes a progression curve for mutations.

## What the science route gives you

The science route is the crafting route. You research the local lifeforms and build equipment that counters the environment, and the official description ties it to preserving your humanity and to access to new technology.

What is confirmed:

- Tools and equipment built specifically to help you and your team endure the environment.
- Access to new technology that the mutation route gives up.
- The same scanning system that drives crafting feeds the research side.

What is not confirmed: which specific technologies are gated behind staying pure, whether "pure" is a hard lock or a sliding scale, and whether the endgame differs between routes.

## Head to head

| Dimension | Symbiosis (mutation) | Science (pure) |

| Core input | Alien spores, fruits, infections | Scanning, research, crafted equipment |
| Named abilities | Telekinesis, wall running | New technology, environmental countermeasures |
| Combat impact | Adaptations described as improving combat | Weapons and tools, with every weapon type playing differently |
| Exploration impact | Movement abilities change how you traverse | Climbing gear and grappling hook remain the baseline tools |
| Team role | Reach and mobility | Sustain, countermeasures and gear for the crew |

One caution on that table: the grappling hook, climbing gear and environmental puzzles appear in the confirmed feature set without being assigned to either route. Do not assume the science route is the only way to get traversal tools, or that the mutation route loses them.

## Can you take both routes?

No official source describes a hybrid, and the language consistently presents the two as alternatives: "the choice is yours," "science or symbiosis." Third-party coverage is explicit that the two are alternate character-development paths.

The honest answer to "can I do both" is therefore: not as described. We are not going to invent a respec system or a dual-path endgame, because none has been announced. If a dual path exists, it will be visible in the build on December 2, and this page will be updated then.

## What the fork means for a four-player crew

Because mutations are separate per player, this is the one decision where a co-op group should deliberately diversify.

**Do not all pick the same side.** If all four players go symbiosis, nobody has the technology side of the toolkit, and the reverse is also true. The documented design is a trade, and a trade only pays off if someone takes each end of it.

**Assign the fork the way you assign roles.** The player who likes movement and risk takes symbiosis and becomes the crew's vertical scout. The player who likes building and preparation stays pure and becomes the crew's quartermaster and countermeasure specialist. This is our recommendation, not an official class system.

**Expect asymmetry in what you can reach.** A wall-running player can get somewhere a pure player cannot, and a pure player has technology a mutated player does not. Plan routes around who is present rather than assuming everyone can follow the same path.

**Do not plan the fork around a respec you have not seen.** If there is no reset, the decision is a campaign-long commitment, and four players locking into the same route is a four-player mistake.

## How to decide

Ask three questions in this order.

1. **Do you want movement or preparation?** Telekinesis and wall running are movement answers. New technology is a preparation answer. Pick the verb you enjoy.
2. **What does the crew lack?** In co-op, fill the gap rather than doubling up. In solo, pick the side that covers your weakest habit: if you never prepare, take science; if you hate being blocked by terrain, take symbiosis.
3. **How much do you trust the unknown?** The science route's payoff is at least partly legible, because technology is a known quantity. The mutation route's payoff is deliberately alien, and the full list is unpublished.

If you are still undecided when the game launches, spend the first session on the [scanning and crafting guide](/guides/blind-descent-scanning-crafting-guide) instead. Science is the route that compounds with scanning, so an early crafting session doubles as a trial of that side.

## Common assumptions worth dropping

**"Mutation is the damage path, science is the support path."** Not supported. The official text ties symbiosis to abilities and science to equipment, and neither is labelled as damage or support. The third-party description of adaptations improving combat is one line, not a role definition.

**"Science means no abilities."** Also not supported. Science means tools and technology; whether those tools function like abilities is unpublished.

**"You can flip later."** Unconfirmed. Do not build a plan on it.

**"The choice is cosmetic."** Almost certainly not. The two routes are described as giving access to different things, which is the opposite of cosmetic.

Treat the fork as a role decision, not a power decision. Decide with your crew, diversify on purpose, and do not bank on a respec.
