# Blind Descent Survival Game: Base Building and Decay

> Last verified: October 28, 2026 · Pre-release build (Early Access opens December 2, 2026)

Blind Descent gives you a base and then tries to take it back. You build log by log, furnishing a structure that has to survive weather, creatures and the ecosystem itself, and anything left unattended gets overgrown, devoured or corrupted. This guide covers how construction works, what the decay system actually does, and the maintenance routine that keeps a base standing.

## Two base phases: inherit, then build

The store page describes a specific sequence, and it is worth following because it saves you materials: "discover the lost team's abandoned base. Clear it, make it yours, and improve it."

So the first base is not yours. It is the missing pioneer team's, and claiming it gives you a structure you did not have to build. The official site adds the construction layer on top: "Construct and furnish buildings; make them durable against weather and creatures."

The practical reading is a two-phase game:

| Phase | What you are doing | Main risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Inherit** | Clear and claim the abandoned base, repair what is broken | Something is already living in it |
| **Improve** | Add furniture, workbenches, containers, defences | Overgrowth starts the moment the base is neglected |
| **Expand** | Build new structures log by log | Every new structure is another thing that can decay |

Do not skip the improve step to start expanding. A large base with no storage and no repair materials is a liability, because the decay system does not care how much effort the structure cost.

## Log-by-log construction

The publisher's 2025 release note is specific about the building method: "Build log-by-log in a world where nature reclaims what's left unattended." That phrase tells you two things at once.

**Construction is granular.** You are placing pieces, not dropping a finished prefab. That means a wall is a series of decisions, and a badly placed piece is a repair job later.

**The world is an active participant.** The same sentence pairs building with reclamation, which is the design thesis: anything you assemble, nature will try to disassemble.

Third-party coverage describes furniture and interior finishing as part of the base layer, which is why the official site's phrase is "construct and furnish" rather than just "build". Furnishing is not cosmetic if it also establishes what counts as a maintained structure.

## The Organic Restoration System

This is the name third-party coverage gives the decay mechanic: an organic restoration system in which nature reacts to cut trees and constructed buildings. The official wording is less mechanical but says the same thing.

Confirmed behaviour:

- **Vines, fungal growths and creeping alien organisms react to neglect.** This is stated on the store page.
- **Bases can be overgrown, devoured or corrupted.** This is stated in the publisher release note.
- **Dropped items are affected too.** Third-party coverage describes items weakening, sprouting vines and eventually turning into fungus.
- **Infection clusters can engulf player-built structures.** Observed in the playtest build.

Not confirmed:

- **The decay timer.** Nobody has published how long "neglect" means. Hours? Real days? We do not know, so we will not invent a number.
- **Whether offline time counts.** Not stated. Assume it does.
- **Whether repair restores a fully collapsed structure.** Not stated.
- **Whether there is a maintenance item that pauses decay.** Not stated.

That gap is the most important thing on this page. Every guide that gives you an exact "your base decays in X hours" figure is guessing, because the studio has not published one.

## What the decay actually costs you

Treat decay as a recurring materials tax rather than a one-time failure state. The store page frames it as a reaction to neglect, not a punishment, which suggests the intended loop is inspection and upkeep.

| What decays | Likely cost | How to reduce it |
|---|---|---|
| Structural pieces | Repair materials and time | Inspect after every expedition, not before |
| Furniture and storage | Lost organisation, exposed materials | Keep storage away from exterior walls |
| Dropped items on the ground | Total loss once fully overgrown | Never leave loose loot; container everything |
| Exterior surfaces | Faster re-infection | Clear growth early, before it spreads inward |

The last row is the one players underestimate. If growth spreads from the outside in, the cheap move is clearing a small patch every session rather than rebuilding a wing every month.

## A maintenance routine that works

This routine is our recommendation, built on the documented behaviour. It assumes decay is present but unknown in rate.

| When | Do this | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Every time you return to base | Walk the exterior once, clear any visible growth | Early growth is cheap; late growth is structural |
| Before every long expedition | Repair anything damaged, empty loose loot into containers | You cannot repair from three biomes away |
| Before logging off | Check storage, close off exposed sides, note anything sprouting | If offline time counts, this is the only session where you can act |
| Weekly, in co-op | Assign one player the exterior sweep | Shared worlds need named owners for shared chores |
| After any storm | Inspect the roof and outer walls first | Weather is a documented threat to structures |

The single highest-value habit is inspecting on return rather than on departure. If you check the base the moment you get back, you always have materials in your pack. If you check it as you leave, you are already committed to the trip.

## Defending the base

The official site says to make buildings "durable against weather and creatures", which confirms two threat classes: environment and fauna. Neither has published numbers.

What you can do without guessing:

- **Build inward, not outward.** A compact structure has less exterior surface to infect and defend.
- **Keep one clear exit.** Aggressive creatures are a confirmed threat class; a base you cannot leave quickly is a trap.
- **Store materials centrally.** In co-op, separate inventories mean the crew's repair stock has to be in a container everyone can reach.
- **Do not build in a hazard corridor.** Underground storms and toxic gases are the other two documented hazard classes; a base under either is a base you cannot maintain.

The [hazards and creatures guide](/guides/blind-descent-hazards-creatures-guide) covers how to read those threats before you commit to a location.

## Relocating a base

Sooner or later the ecosystem wins, or you outgrow the inherited structure. The game does not document a moving mechanic, so plan a manual relocation:

1. **Build the new site's shell first**, while the old base still functions as storage.
2. **Move materials in batches**, respecting weight limits.
3. **Strip the old base last.** Dismantling early leaves you with nowhere to store the output.
4. **Abandon the old structure deliberately.** If decay is real, an unattended shell will be reclaimed, which is the system working as designed rather than a loss.

Do not assume you can pick up a placed structure and walk away with it. Nothing published says you can.

## Base building mistakes

| Mistake | Cost | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Expanding before storing | Weight lock, exposed materials | Containers and a workbench before new wings |
| Leaving loot on the floor | Items overgrow and are lost | Container everything, every session |
| Never inspecting the exterior | Small growth becomes structural damage | One exterior sweep per return |
| Building on an exposed hazard route | Base unusable during storms or gas | Scout the location before committing |
| Assuming a fixed decay timer | Bad planning in both directions | Inspect, do not schedule |
| Treating furnishing as cosmetic | Less maintained-looking structure | Furnish as you build; the game sells it as part of the base |

## The one-line version

Claim the abandoned base, furnish it before you expand it, and sweep the exterior every time you come home. Blind Descent does not punish building, it punishes forgetting, and the fix is a five-minute routine rather than a bigger stockpile.
