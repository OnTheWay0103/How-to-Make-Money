# Blind Descent Survival Game: Hazards and Creatures

> Last verified: October 28, 2026 · Pre-release build (Early Access opens December 2, 2026)

Blind Descent throws four kinds of threat at you: underground storms, toxic gases, aggressive creatures and the creeping growth that eats your base. Each one asks for a different answer, and the game's own design note says as much: every enemy type requires different strategies. This page sorts the threats, separates confirmed facts from playtest observations, and gives a defence order.

## The four threat classes

| Class | What it is | How it hurts you | Documented? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Underground storms** | Weather events in the cavern system | Forces you to shelter; damages exposed structures | Official |
| **Toxic gases** | Gas pockets or clouds in the environment | Environmental damage, route denial | Official |
| **Aggressive creatures** | Hostile fauna | Direct combat, ambush in dark caves | Official |
| **Creeping growth** | Vines, fungi, infection clusters | Destroys bases and dropped items | Official |

Two of these are weather and two are living. That split matters, because weather is answered with preparation and living things are answered with tools or avoidance. Conflating them is how players end up carrying a rifle into a gas cloud.

Note what is **not** in this list. Temperature and oxygen systems appear nowhere in the official store page, the official site, the official FAQ or the press releases, and they do not appear in the playtest reports either. If you have seen them claimed elsewhere, that claim has no source. Blind Descent is not a suit-management game on the evidence available.

## Underground storms

The store page lists "underground storms" first among the ever-changing threats, and the official site pairs it with "bizarre weather" in the underground world. The publisher's early release note also describes bases being built to withstand weather.

What that means practically:

- **Storms are a shelter problem, not a combat problem.** The answer is a structure, not a weapon.
- **They damage exposed structures.** The official site's line about making buildings "durable against weather" only makes sense if weather is a real structural threat.
- **They arrive in the cavern system, not on the surface.** You cannot outrun a storm by climbing out; you shelter.

No storm timing, duration, frequency or damage numbers are published, and we are not inventing them. The safe routine is to know where your nearest shelter is before you push a new route.

## Toxic gases

Toxic gases are confirmed as an environmental hazard on the store page, and third-party coverage repeats them alongside storms and aggressive creatures. What is not published is whether gas is a static zone, a moving cloud, or a byproduct of certain creatures or growth.

How to treat gas without guessing:

| Situation | Sensible response |
|---|---|
| A new route smells or looks hazy | Turn back and scan from a distance; do not test your health bar |
| You must cross a gas area | Cross fast, do not stop to gather, and do not fight in it |
| Gas near your base | Relocate the base rather than ventilate it |
| Gas and creatures together | Leave; no confirmed tool trivialises both at once |

Because medicine is a confirmed crafting category and the game tracks body-part injuries, a treatment response probably exists. We do not know the specific item, so treat "bring medicine" as preparation rather than a named counter.

## Aggressive creatures

The official site gives the most useful combat guidance in the whole source set: "Dodge, block, and use surroundings," with bows, spears, axes and rifles as the weapon families, and a warning that "dark caves are especially dangerous."

The October release note adds the design philosophy: "Every weapon type plays different and every enemy type requires different strategies."

That gives you four weapon families and no stat table. Treat the families as answers to situations rather than as a tier list:

| Weapon family | What it is for | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Axe | Gathering tool that doubles as melee | Official (family named) |
| Spear | Reach, and a confirmed crafting-style tool | Official (family named) |
| Bow | Ranged, quiet engagement | Official (family named) |
| Rifle | Ranged, later-game firepower | Official (family named) |

We are not ranking them. There are no published damage numbers, and a ranking without numbers is fan fiction. What the sources do support is a situational reading: reach for the spear when you want distance without ammo, the bow when you want to start a fight on your terms, and the axe when you are already gathering.

Two behavioural rules follow from the sources:

- **Darkness is a force multiplier for the enemy.** The official site calls dark caves especially dangerous. Light, or daylight, is a tactical resource.
- **Dodge and block are the defensive baseline.** The game's own combat description leads with avoidance, not damage. Positioning is the primary skill.

## Reactive flora and infection clusters

The fourth threat class is the one that does not chase you. Vines, fungal growths and creeping alien organisms react to neglect, and the playtest reported infection clusters that can engulf player-built structures.

This is a base problem more than an expedition problem, and the [base building guide](/guides/blind-descent-base-building-guide) covers the maintenance routine. On expedition, the relevant points are:

- **Dropped items degrade.** Third-party coverage says loose items weaken, sprout vines and turn into fungus. Container everything.
- **Growth is a signal.** A heavily overgrown area is telling you it has been undisturbed, which is useful when you are looking for the missing team's traces.
- **Not every plant is scenery.** Some flora is reactive or destructive; the playtest named a small set of plants with defensive or toxic properties.

## What the playtest actually named

These names come from one playtest review, not from official material. We are listing them as community observations because they help you recognise the pattern, not as a bestiary.

| Category | Names observed | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Plants | resistant vines, red belly succulents, a fruit described as toxic | [Community] |
| Creatures | rainbow crab, a creature the reviewer called "quote-unquote rabbits", froglike organisms | [Community] |
| Structures | infection clusters that engulf built structures | [Community] |
| Environment | day-night cycle, reactive fauna behaviour | [Community] |

That is the entire published name list. There is no boss roster, no enemy stat sheet and no official creature catalogue. Any site presenting a full Blind Descent bestiary before launch is either extrapolating or inventing.

## The defence order

This order is our recommendation. It follows the threat classes and the documented systems rather than any published difficulty curve.

| Phase | Priority | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Before leaving shelter | Water, food, and a known return route | Exposure and dehydration kill more new players than creatures do |
| 2. Early expeditions | Axe and club, plus a lean-to as a fallback | Cheap melee and somewhere to wait out weather |
| 3. First hostile biome | Spear or bow, and a rule to disengage | The sources say dodge and block first, so plan an exit rather than a kill |
| 4. Before a dark cave | Light and a ranged option | The official site specifically flags dark caves as dangerous |
| 5. Around the base | Exterior sweep and repair stock | Creeping growth is the threat you can prevent |

## Reading a new biome safely

A short routine that costs nothing and prevents most deaths:

1. **Stop at the threshold.** Look before you walk in.
2. **Scan the lifeforms you can see.** Scanning is the documented route to knowing what is edible, useful or dangerous.
3. **Identify which threat class you are facing.** Weather, gas, fauna or growth. The answer differs for each.
4. **Set a retreat point.** A lean-to, a cave mouth, or the base.
5. **Push one screen, then reassess.** Do not commit to a long route on first contact.

The playtest build had no tutorial prompts, so the game will not do this for you. The routine is the tutorial.
