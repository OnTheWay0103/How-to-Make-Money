# Blind Descent Survival Game: Platforms and Specs

> Last verified: October 28, 2026 · Early Access opens December 2, 2026

Blind Descent launches in Early Access on December 2, 2026 for PC on Steam, GOG and the Epic Games Store, with crossplay across all three. PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S are planned for the full 1.0 release with no date announced. The listed minimum is Windows 11, an Intel Core i3, 16 GB of RAM and 40 GB of storage.

The PC stores are the only confirmed launch platforms. An older official FAQ on the developer's own site said the game was "only targeting Windows 10 64-bit on the Steam platform" and that "there is no release date at the moment". That page is out of date: the current Steam listing and the October 2026 publisher release note both supersede it. We are recording the conflict rather than quietly picking a side.

## Early Access versus the full release

Early Access is not the finished game, and the sources are unusually honest about it.

- **Content:** Early Access contains "only part of the planned story" according to third-party coverage of the announcement.
- **Length:** no playtime figure has been published by the developer or the publisher, and we are not aware of a credible measurement yet. Plan on an open-ended survival loop rather than a fixed campaign.
- **Duration:** Early Access is expected to run about a year, described as tentative and subject to feedback.
- **Updates:** later updates are expected to add narrative content plus additional biomes, creatures and resources.
- **1.0:** the full version is intended to include the complete storyline and further survival improvements, plus the console releases.

So the December 2 build is a large slice, not a finished campaign. If you are deciding whether to buy at launch, that is the real question: do you want to play an unfinished survival game for a year, or wait for 1.0? Both are reasonable, and the price is unknown, which makes the decision harder than it should be.

## System requirements

The Steam listing gives two tiers, and the jump between them is larger than usual.

| Component | Minimum | Recommended |
|---|---|---|
| OS | Windows 11 64-bit | Windows 11 64-bit |
| Processor | Intel Core i3 | i5 or higher |
| Memory | 16 GB RAM | 32 GB RAM |
| Graphics | DirectX 12 compatible GPU with 2 GB VRAM | DirectX 12 compatible GPU with 6 GB VRAM |
| DirectX | Version 12 | Version 12 |
| Network | Broadband internet connection | Broadband internet connection |
| Storage | 40 GB available space | 40 GB available space |
| Notes | 64-bit processor and OS required | 64-bit processor and OS required |

Three things stand out:

**Windows 11 only.** The listed minimum is Windows 11 64-bit, not Windows 10. The old official FAQ said Windows 10, but the store page is the current source of truth. If you are still on Windows 10, this is worth checking before launch.

**16 GB minimum, 32 GB recommended.** That is a high floor for a minimum spec. If you have 16 GB and a modest GPU, expect the minimum tier to be genuinely minimal.

**40 GB either way.** Storage does not change between tiers, which suggests a fixed asset footprint rather than a texture-quality split.

There are no published benchmarks, so we cannot tell you what frame rate an i3 and a 2 GB GPU will produce. The store page is the only authority here.

## Steam Deck status

This is the most-searched platform question around the game, and the honest answer is: **status unknown**.

What we know:

- The game has **full controller support** according to the Steam listing, which is the prerequisite for Deck playability.
- The recommended memory is 32 GB and the minimum is 16 GB, both above the Steam Deck's shared memory pool in practice.
- No Steam Deck compatibility rating has been published, and no source describes Deck testing.

The controller support is a good sign and nothing more. A game can have full controller support and still be unplayable on Deck for performance or text-legibility reasons. Check the Steam Deck compatibility badge on the store page after launch; until then, treat Deck support as `[Unconfirmed]`.

## Price

Not announced. The Steam listing shows no price because the game is not yet purchasable, and third-party coverage of the announcement explicitly states that the Early Access price has not been revealed.

Anyone quoting a price right now is quoting a guess, a regional store placeholder, or a different game. The 300,000-plus wishlist figure that appears in coverage is a demand signal, not a pricing signal.

Note also that Early Access pricing is not necessarily final pricing. A studio can raise the price at 1.0, discount it during the Early Access period, or hold it flat, and none of those options has been announced. If budget matters, wait for the store page to show a number rather than planning around a rumour.

## Crossplay and storefront choice

Because crossplay spans Steam, GOG and the Epic Games Store, the storefront you buy on does not determine who you can play with. That is a genuine advantage over games that lock multiplayer to one launcher.

What storefront choice does affect, based on confirmed features:

- **Cloud saves:** Steam Cloud is listed for the Steam version. GOG and Epic equivalents are [Unconfirmed].
- **Achievements:** Steam Achievements are listed. Whether the other stores have their own is [Unconfirmed].
- **Controller and HDR:** listed on the Steam page. Whether they are Steam-specific is [Unconfirmed].

If you have no strong preference, buying on the storefront where your friends already own games is the low-risk choice, since crossplay makes the lobby portable but social features are usually not.
