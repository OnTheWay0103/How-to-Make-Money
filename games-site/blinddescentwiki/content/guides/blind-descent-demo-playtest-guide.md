# Blind Descent Demo and Playtest: What You Can Play

> Last verified: October 28, 2026 · Early Access opens December 2, 2026

Blind Descent has had two different test rounds and a free demo on Steam, and they are not the same thing. The 2025 alpha was solo-only. The October 2026 round tested four-player co-op. Early Access on December 2 is the paid build. This page separates the three, says what each one contains, and explains how access works.

## Demo, playtest and Early Access are three different things

Players use these words interchangeably, which is why so much of the search traffic around this game is confused. Here is the split.

| Round | What it is | Cost | Co-op | Status as of 2026-10-28 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Demo** | A free slice listed on the Steam store page | Free | [Unconfirmed] for the current demo | Entry point lives on the Steam page |
| **Alpha playtest (2025)** | Open Steam playtest of core mechanics | Free, opt-in | No — the July 2025 release note says co-op was not in that alpha | Finished |
| **Co-op playtest (Oct 2026)** | Four-player test round | Free, access required | Yes, with crossplay across Steam, GOG and EGS | Started October 14, 2026 |
| **Early Access** | Paid build, full opening of the game | Paid, price [Unconfirmed] | Yes, up to four players | Launches December 2, 2026 |

The important distinction is the middle two rows. The 2025 round was explicitly built to "test core mechanics and observe player behavior" and was "focused on solo playthroughs." The October 2026 round exists because the studio needed multiplayer data before launch. If you are reading a 2025 impression and wondering why nobody mentions co-op, that is why.

## What the current demo contains

The demo is a free slice tied to the Steam page, not a separate store listing you have to hunt for. What we can say with confidence is narrow:

- It shows the opening: the elevator crash, the underground ecosystem and the no-tutorial survival loop.
- It includes scanning, basic crafting and the beginning of base building, because those are the systems the playtest coverage describes playing.
- It does **not** establish how long the slice runs, whether it is time-limited, or whether it matches the October multiplayer build. Those are all [Unconfirmed].

Third-party coverage from the playtest describes roughly the first stretch of the game: waking as the Pathfinder, meeting Hollowatch, finding a wounded teammate, and establishing water, food and a first shelter. Treat that as a description of the content area, not a promise that every demo build contains all of it.

## Can you play the demo in co-op?

This is the single most-asked question in the keyword data, and the honest answer is split by round.

- **The 2025 alpha: no.** The publisher's own release note said co-op was "not currently available in Alpha playtest version."
- **The October 2026 round: yes.** That round was announced as a co-op playtest with up to four players and crossplay between Steam, GOG and the Epic Games Store.
- **The current demo: [Unconfirmed].** Nothing official states whether the standalone demo inherits the multiplayer build or stays on a solo slice.

If co-op is the reason you want to try the game, do not assume the demo gives it to you. Check the Steam page entry point on the day you install, and if the demo is solo, use the [co-op guide](/guides/blind-descent-coop-multiplayer-guide) to plan for December 2 instead.

## How playtest access works

Access has been handled through the Steam store page rather than a separate signup site.

1. Open the Blind Descent store page on Steam.
2. Use the playtest or demo entry point shown on the page. In the October round this appeared as a request-access style button; the exact label changes between rounds.
3. If the round is open, the build appears in your library. If it is selection-based, you wait for an invite.
4. Check the Steam page again after a round ends, because the entry point is removed or repurposed rather than left up as a dead button.

The October round was described by third-party coverage as starting October 14, 2026, with participants selected through the Steam page. Because that round was specifically a multiplayer test, expect it to have closed or changed by the time Early Access opens; the paid build supersedes it.

## What carries over to Early Access

Nothing has been confirmed. There is no official statement that demo or playtest progress, unlocks, scans or base layouts transfer into the Early Access build, and there is no statement that they do not. Plan for a fresh start.

That is the normal and safer assumption for a pre-launch build: test rounds exist to generate data, not to seed saves. It also means you should use the demo to learn the systems rather than to farm progress.

## What the demo is actually good for

Given that progress probably does not carry over, the demo's value is diagnostic. Use it to answer four questions before you spend money on December 2.

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Does the no-tutorial opening click for me? | The game deliberately withholds guidance, and that is the most divisive design choice in the coverage |
| Can my machine hold a stable frame rate? | The listed minimum is an Intel Core i3 with 16 GB of RAM, which is a wide band; see the [platform and specs page](/guides/blind-descent-platforms-system-requirements) |
| Does scanning feel like progression or like chores? | Everything downstream depends on your answer |
| Do I want to play this alone or with three friends? | The two experiences are structurally different, and the co-op round was a different build |

The playtest reports flagged interface friction: inventory drag limits, awkward stacked-resource interactions, hotbar conflicts, and environmental audio drowning out the AI assistant. Those are exactly the things a demo lets you judge for yourself. They were also listed as actively being worked on, so a rough demo does not necessarily mean a rough launch.
