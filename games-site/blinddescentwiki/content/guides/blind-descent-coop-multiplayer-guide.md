# Blind Descent Co-op Guide: How Multiplayer Works

> Last verified: October 28, 2026 · Pre-release build (Early Access opens December 2, 2026)

Blind Descent supports online co-op for up to four players, with crossplay between Steam, GOG and the Epic Games Store. Everyone shares the same world and the same quests, but each player keeps a separate inventory and separate mutations. This guide covers what the mode officially includes, how a crew should split the work, and the problems a four-player team actually runs into.

## What co-op officially supports

The store page and the publisher's October 2026 release note settle most of the basics. Anything not listed here has not been confirmed, and we say so rather than guess.

| Feature | Status | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Player count | Up to 4 ("up to three pioneers" plus you) | Steam store page |
| Online co-op | Yes | Steam store page categories |
| Crossplay | Steam ↔ GOG ↔ Epic Games Store | Publisher release note, 2026-10-12 |
| Shared world | Yes | Publisher release note |
| Shared quests | Yes | Publisher release note |
| Separate inventory | Yes | Publisher release note |
| Separate mutations | Yes | Publisher release note |
| Story playable in co-op | Yes, every single-player story element | Official site FAQ |
| Couch co-op / split screen | [Unconfirmed] | No official mention |
| Dedicated servers | [Unconfirmed] | No official mention |
| Cross-save between platforms | [Unconfirmed] | No official mention |

Two things are worth pulling out of that table. First, "separate inventory" is a real design decision, not a menu setting: loot is per player, so nobody can carry the crew's water supply on your behalf without a handoff. Second, "separate mutations" means the symbiosis-or-science choice is made per person. One player can go full mutation while another stays pure and leans on technology, and the game does not force the group onto a single path.

## How to start a co-op session

The publisher has confirmed that co-op is in the Early Access build, but the exact menu path has not been published, so treat the steps below as the shape of the flow rather than a screenshot-accurate walkthrough.

1. **Install the same build on every machine.** Early Access launches on Steam, GOG and the Epic Games Store on December 2, 2026. Crossplay is only useful if all four installs are on the same patch.
2. **One player hosts the world.** Co-op is described as a shared world with shared quests, which implies a host-owned save rather than four parallel saves. Who hosts matters: the host's progress is the world's progress.
3. **Invite through the platform layer.** Because crossplay spans three storefronts, invitations go through the game's own friends or lobby flow rather than a single platform overlay. Expect to exchange an in-game code or an account name rather than a Steam invite, though this is [Unconfirmed] until the build ships.
4. **Check that everyone's objectives match.** With shared quests but separate inventories and mutations, the group can drift apart in capability even while the quest log stays in sync.

> **Practical warning:** the co-op playtest that began on October 14, 2026 was explicitly a test of multiplayer, and the developers were still working on combat, crafting, interface and performance. Expect launch-week friction in lobbies and reconnects. That is a normal Early Access cost, not a sign you set it up wrong.

## Shared world, separate bodies: what that means in practice

"Shared world, separate inventory and mutations" sounds like a small footnote, but it changes how a crew should play.

**Resources are personal until you make them communal.** If each player has their own inventory and a weight limit, the crew cannot treat one person as the walking warehouse. The standard fix is a shared base with labelled containers, which the game supports through workbenches and containers in the base-building layer. One player becomes the quartermaster, and everyone drops raw materials there before heading out.

**Mutations diverge, so capability diverges.** A player who has taken telekinesis or wall running can reach places the pure-technology player cannot, and the reverse is also true, since staying pure is described as unlocking access to new technology. That asymmetry is an advantage if you plan it and a liability if you do not.

**Quest progress is shared, so nobody is "behind".** The crew will not have to replay a story beat for a late joiner as long as they are in the same world. That also means a single player rushing the main objective drags everyone forward, whether or not they were ready.

**Base upkeep is a group problem.** Vines, fungal growths and creeping alien organisms react to neglect. If the base decays while three of four players are offline, the one who logs in first pays for it. Agree on a maintenance rotation before the first long session.

## Roles that work for a four-player crew

This split is our recommendation, built from the systems the game actually documents. It is not an official class system, because Blind Descent does not have classes.

| Role | What they own | Why it fits the mechanics |
|---|---|---|
| **Scanner** | Scanning new lifeforms and structures, feeding the recipe pool | Scanning is the documented route to new tools and insights, so one dedicated scanner accelerates the whole crew |
| **Builder** | Base structure, repairs, decay checks | The base is the only thing that degrades while you are away, and repairs are cheaper than rebuilds |
| **Quartermaster** | Containers, food, water, weight discipline | Weight and durability are confirmed as managed resources; a single organiser prevents four half-full packs |
| **Scout** | Route-finding, climbing gear, first contact with threats | Climbing gear and the grappling hook are the documented traversal tools, and the scout absorbs the risk of meeting new creatures first |

Rotate the scanner role if one player wants to see the content. Scanning is the most interesting system in the game, and locking one person into it for a whole campaign is a good way to lose a teammate.

## The co-op problems nobody warns you about

**1. The base decays on real time, not play time.** The official description says alien growth reacts to neglect, and the press material says nature reclaims what is left unattended. We do not know the decay timer, and nobody has published one, so the honest advice is: log in together, and check the base before you leave rather than after you return.

**2. Separate mutations break the "one build" habit.** In most co-op survival games the group converges on the same meta build. Here the two documented routes, symbiosis and science, are described as alternatives. If all four players pick the same one, you lose the access the other route provides.

**3. A shared world means shared consequences.** One player dismantling a wall for materials is a decision that lands on everyone. Give the builder veto power over structural changes, and let anyone place decorative furniture.

**4. Crossplay invites are the most likely launch-week failure.** Three storefronts, one lobby system, first public build. Have a fallback plan: agree on a time, agree on who hosts, and be willing to restart the lobby rather than debug it for twenty minutes.

**5. Voice chat is not confirmed.** Hollowatch, the onboard AI, is a single-player narrative device, and in co-op the environmental audio is already loud enough to bury dialogue in the playtest. Do not assume in-game voice; plan on an external call. This is [Unconfirmed] for the shipped build.

## Should you play co-op first, or solo first?

Play the first two hours solo. The game deliberately gives you no tutorial, and learning the survival loop with three other people talking over the audio is how crews bounce off. Once you understand water, food, scanning and the base, bring the group in.

The exception is a crew that already knows survival games well. In that case, start together on day one: the shared world means nobody has to replay anything, and the role split above is easier to establish at the start than to retrofit later.

## What to settle before December 2

Write down four things before your crew's first session: who hosts, who builds, who scans, and what time you all log in for maintenance. Those four answers prevent most of the friction that shared worlds create. Everything else, including the exact lobby flow and whether voice chat exists, will be obvious within ten minutes of the build being live.
