# Blind Descent Survival Game: Beginner Guide

> Last verified: October 28, 2026 · Pre-release build (Early Access opens December 2, 2026)

Blind Descent gives you no tutorial. You wake under the Martian surface after an elevator crash, and the game expects you to read the world instead of a pop-up. The survival order that works is water first, scanner second, shelter third, base last. This guide walks that order through the first two hours and names the mistakes that cost the most time.

## Why there is no tutorial, and what replaces it

The developers built the opening around observation. In the playtest, there were no prompts telling you to drink, eat or scan; you learn by watching what the ecosystem does. The official store page frames this as the point of the game: "Observing how alien creatures interact with their environment can lead to powerful insights."

What replaces a tutorial is a small set of readable signals:

- **Your own status.** Hydration and hunger exist, and the playtest build punished ignoring them quickly.
- **The scanner.** Almost everything is scannable, and scans are how the crafting pool grows.
- **The AI assistant.** Hollowatch narrates discoveries and pushes the story forward, which is the closest thing to a hint system.
- **The adaptations interface.** Objectives, biome opportunities and new blueprints surface here rather than in a quest log.

If you are coming from survival games with heavy onboarding, the first twenty minutes will feel empty. That is the design. Start poking at things.

## The first two hours, in order

This is an editorial order, not an official checklist. It is built from what the playtest actually required and what the store page says the game rewards.

| Step | What to do | Why it comes here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Find and secure drinking water; boil it | Hydration is the fastest way to die in the opening, and boiling is a documented early task |
| 2 | Scan everything within sight of your crash site | Scanning expands the recipe pool, so early scans are what unlock the tools you need next |
| 3 | Craft the basic tool set: axe, club, fire pit, water container, lean-to | These are the documented early crafts and they cover gathering, defence, heat and sleep |
| 4 | Cook food rather than eating it raw | Cooking is a confirmed early system and a cheap way to stretch what you gather |
| 5 | Push one route outward and scan new lifeforms | The recipe pool only grows from new scans, so staying put stalls progression |
| 6 | Find the abandoned pioneer base and claim it | The store page explicitly tells you to clear the lost team's base, make it yours and improve it |
| 7 | Set up a workbench and containers | Base expansion in the playtest used workbenches and containers, and storage fixes the weight problem |
| 8 | Decide your first adaptation or first science tool | The symbiosis-versus-science fork opens early; see the dedicated guide before committing |

> Do not skip step 2 to rush step 6. Arriving at the base with an empty recipe pool means you cannot repair or improve it, and the base is the one thing in the game that degrades while you are away.

## Water, food and the mistakes that actually kill you

The playtest reports are consistent on one point: the lethal mistakes are boring ones. You run out of water while exploring, you eat something you did not scan, or you cook nothing and rely on raw finds.

**Boil before you drink.** Boiling is a confirmed early action. Treat any water source as suspect until you have a fire pit.

**Scan before you eat.** The scanner tells you what a plant or creature is. The playtest named a small set of flora and fauna in the starting area, including what the reviewer called resistant vines, red belly succulents and a fruit described as toxic. Those names are community observations from a playtest build, not an official catalogue, so treat them as examples of the pattern: some plants feed you, some poison you, and the scanner is the difference.

**Cook in batches.** Cooking is a system, not a single recipe. Batch cooking before a long push saves a return trip.

**Carry water, not just food.** Weight is a managed resource. Water is heavy, which is exactly why the water container is an early craft: it converts a stack of sips into one item.

## Scanning is the progression system

If you take one thing from this guide, take this: in Blind Descent, scanning is not a lore feature, it is the tech tree.

The playtest reviewer described it as a recipe pool that expands with each scan, producing "tech-tree-like progression without explicit unlock trees." The official store page says the same thing in its own words: "Scan and study the lifeforms around you to gather resources and craft essential tools."

That has three consequences for a new player.

1. **Scan aggressively, including things that look boring.** Structures and non-hostile creatures are scannable too.
2. **Change biome when progress stalls.** If you have scanned everything in reach and your recipe pool has stopped growing, you are not stuck, you are in the wrong place.
3. **Do not hoard scans.** There is no confirmed downside to scanning early and often.

The [scanning and crafting guide](/guides/blind-descent-scanning-crafting-guide) covers how modular crafting converts those scans into gear, and why there is no fixed recipe list to memorise.

## Claiming the abandoned base

The store page is unusually direct about this: "discover the lost team's abandoned base. Clear it, make it yours, and improve it." That is the intended mid-opening beat, and it is also where the game's second system, base decay, starts working against you.

Three rules for the first base:

- **Clear it before you furnish it.** Something has been living there.
- **Repair before you expand.** Vines, fungal growths and creeping alien organisms react to neglect, so a half-fixed structure is a structure you will rebuild.
- **Build storage first.** With separate inventories in co-op and a weight limit in solo, containers are the difference between a base and a backpack you cannot carry.

The [base building guide](/guides/blind-descent-base-building-guide) explains the decay system and the maintenance routine that keeps a base standing.

## What to do in hour two

By the end of the first hour you should have water under control, a basic toolkit, and a handful of scans. Hour two is where the game opens up, and the honest recommendation is to spend it on breadth rather than depth.

**Follow one story thread.** The official site describes tracking the pioneer team's footsteps and using a printer and a signal booster to reach the surface again. Those are the long-term goals, and touching them early gives the world a direction.

**Meet one new threat on purpose.** Underground storms, toxic gases and aggressive creatures are the three documented hazard classes. Meeting one with a lean-to and a club teaches you more than reading about it. The [hazards guide](/guides/blind-descent-hazards-creatures-guide) has the defensive order.

**Test the fork.** You will hit the symbiosis-or-science decision early. Do not agonise over it before you understand what each side gives you; the [mutation versus science guide](/guides/blind-descent-mutation-vs-science-guide) breaks down the documented trade-off.

**Play co-op only after hour two.** The game has no tutorial, and three friends talking over Hollowatch is the fastest way to miss what the world is telling you. Once you can survive alone, the [co-op guide](/guides/blind-descent-coop-multiplayer-guide) covers how to split four players across the systems.

## The beginner mistake table

| Mistake | Cost | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Exploring before securing water | Death in the first hour | Boil and carry water before any long push |
| Eating unscanned plants | Poisoning, wasted health | Scan first, cook second |
| Ignoring the scanner because it feels optional | Recipe pool stalls, gear stops improving | Scan structures and passive creatures too |
| Building a large base before storage | Weight lock, constant trips | One workbench, several containers, then expand |
| Leaving the base unattended without checking it | Organic overgrowth damages or destroys it | Check growth before you leave, not after you return |
| Treating the mutation fork as cosmetic | Locked out of one route's access | Read both sides before the first commit |
| Starting co-op on day one | Missed tutorial-by-observation, crew chaos | Two solo hours first, then group up |

## Beginner questions, answered

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Is there a tutorial? | No, the game teaches through observation, the scanner and Hollowatch |
| What should I build first? | Water container and fire pit for survival, axe and club for gathering and defence |
| How do I unlock new recipes? | By scanning lifeforms and structures; the pool expands with new scans |
| When should I claim the base? | As soon as you can repair it, which means after your first solid scan run |
| Should I pick mutation or science immediately? | No, but know that the two routes are alternatives, not a combined tree |
| How long is the opening? | Nobody has published a measured playtime yet, and Early Access is described only as "part of the planned story" — plan for an open-ended loop, not a fixed campaign |
| Can I respec? | [Unconfirmed] — no official statement on resetting mutations |
| Does the base decay offline? | The growth reacts to neglect, and "offline" is not excluded; treat it as yes until proven otherwise |

## The short version

Water, then scanner, then tools, then base, then the mutation fork. Scan everything, cook everything, and do not build wide before you can store what you gather. Everything else in Blind Descent is learnable in play, which is exactly what the developers intended when they removed the tutorial.
