# Expeditions: Samurai — Best Party Composition Guide (Act One)

> Early Access Build (Act One) / Updated: August 7, 2026

Eight companions, **four active slots**. That single gap — the ratio of 8 to 4 — is the heart of party building in Expeditions: Samurai. Your active four determine which of the three pillars (stealth, diplomacy, steel) are available in any given situation, which dialogue checks you can pass, and which ambush setups you can run. This guide covers the role framework, the strongest Act One lineups, and how to rotate the roster without stalling loyalty quests.

## The Four-Slot Framework

Every strong party covers four jobs. The named companions fill them cleanly:

| Slot | Job | Best fits |
|------|-----|-----------|
| **Frontline** | Survives the first exchange, holds enemy attention, deals melee damage | Johan, Piet Ruisch, or a combat-built Captain |
| **Infiltrator** | Scouting, locks, ambush setups, silent takedowns | Antje (uniquely), or a stealth-built Captain |
| **Talker** | Dialogue checks, negotiations, de-escalation | Étienne Durand (uniquely), or a socially built Captain |
| **Flex** | Ranged damage, gadgets, or backup for any pillar | Your Captain, Piet, or a rotating companion |

Two of these slots deserve special notes:

- **Antje and Étienne are irreplaceable for their pillars.** No other confirmed companion covers locks or negotiations as their specialty. If either is benched, that pillar is effectively closed for the mission.
- **Your Captain is the free slot.** Companions cover the fixed roles well, which means your class choice (one of four at creation) should fill what the party lacks — if you run Antje + Étienne + a fighter, a combat-oriented Captain completes the set; if you skip a companion, your Captain can stand in.

## The Strongest Act One Lineups

### 1. The Balanced Default (recommended first run)

| Slot | Character |
|------|-----------|
| Frontline | Johan |
| Infiltrator | Antje |
| Talker | Étienne Durand |
| Flex | Captain (any class) |

This is the pillar-complete party: every approach is on the table in every situation, which matters enormously for a first run still learning the systems. It is also the party the [Companion Recruitment Guide](/guides/companion-recruitment-guide) is built around — all three key companions are early recruits.

### 2. The Shadow Party (stealth-first)

| Slot | Character |
|------|-----------|
| Infiltrator | Antje |
| Scout/Duelist | Captain (stealth class) |
| Frontline | Johan |
| Flex | Piet Ruisch |

Built for the ambush playstyle: split the party, thin sentries with takedowns, open every fight with a surprise attack from two flanks. Trading Étienne for Piet costs you negotiation options — bring this party when the map shows stealth grounds and battlefield levels, not courts.

### 3. The War Party (combat-first)

| Slot | Character |
|------|-----------|
| Frontline | Johan |
| Hybrid | Piet Ruisch |
| Ranged/Support | Captain (ranged class) |
| Infiltrator-lite | Antje (or a stealth-built Captain) |

Maximum damage output with sword, pistol, and powder. Antje stays for the ambush opener — the surprise attack bonus is too valuable to drop even in a brawling party — but the talker is gone, so plan to fight (or intimidate with Piet) rather than negotiate.

### 4. The Court Party (diplomacy-first)

| Slot | Character |
|------|-----------|
| Talker | Étienne Durand |
| Muscle for intimidation | Piet Ruisch or Johan |
| Captain (social class) | You |
| Flex | Rotate for quests |

For faction-seat missions and negotiation-heavy story beats. Intimidation checks need a threatening presence at the table, so pair the priest with a brawler.

## How to Choose Your Party for Each Mission

The world map tells you what's coming — use it:

- **Stealth grounds** → Shadow Party. Locked doors and patrols pay for Antje's slot twice over.
- **Diplomatic hotspots / faction seats** → Court Party. You cannot fail to bring your best talker to a negotiation.
- **Battlefields** → War Party, or Balanced with the fight-focused flex.
- **Scavenge zones** → any party — these are supply runs, bring whoever needs quest progress.

## Rotation: Keep All 8 Alive

Your active four is a decision *per mission*, not per campaign:

- **Loyalty quests are time-sensitive to story chapters** — check in with everyone regularly and rotate bench riders through easy events so no chain stalls.
- **Bench riders still contribute** — companions you leave behind advance through their personal quests, but only the active four earn combat progression and dialogue moments.
- **Don't rotate mid-arc.** If you're inside a companion's personal quest chain, keep them active until the chain resolves — chain steps often require the companion on the field.

## Party Composition in Co-op

Co-op is a two-captain game: each player controls half of the party in simultaneous combat. The strongest division of labor:

| Role | Twin A | Twin B |
|------|--------|--------|
| Pillar focus | Stealth or Combat | The other (or Diplomacy) |
| Companion focus | Antje + melee | Étienne + ranged |
| Combat role | Frontline / initiator | Support / control |

Doubling up on the same pillar wastes half the toolbox — split stealth and combat, and use the shared dialogue checks (both captains' skills count) to cover diplomacy between you.

## EA-Specific Composition Notes

- **Build for the content that exists.** Early Access is Act One only (~8 hours). A party that comes online at level 40 is wasted — pick lineups that work from hour one.
- **Saves carry to 1.0.** Your Act One party, their loyalty, and their progression all carry into the full release — so the composition you build now is your 60-hour campaign's foundation.
- **Expect balance patches.** Early Access builds get tuned quickly; treat any "must-pick" claim from day one as provisional.

## Party Composition Mistakes

1. **Doubling up on one pillar.** Two infiltrators and no talker loses negotiations; three brawlers can't open locks.
2. **Benching Antje on stealth maps.** She is the only confirmed locks specialist — locked routes close when she's out.
3. **Negotiating without Étienne.** Your best dialogue options literally don't exist without a talker in the active four.
4. **Forgetting the Captain is a flex.** Your class should fill the gap — building a third melee fighter when your party already has Johan and Piet wastes the slot.
