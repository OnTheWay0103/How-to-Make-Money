# Expeditions: Samurai — Companion Recruitment Guide (Act One)

> Early Access Build (Act One) / Updated: August 7, 2026

Eight companions. Eight personal stories. Each one joins through a **dedicated quest chain** — and because your active party is only four characters, *who you recruit first* shapes every hour of Act One. This guide covers the confirmed roster, the best recruitment order for the Early Access build, where the companions are expected to be found, and how recruitment works. Exact quest names and map pins are being mapped by the community on launch day — anything not yet confirmed is marked **TBD**.

All **8 companions** exist in the Early Access build with personal stories and dedicated quest chains. **Five are officially named**; the remaining three were shown in the Companions trailer and will be revealed in the launch-week media cycle.

## How Recruitment Works

The official companion framing tells you what to expect before you start hunting:

- **Recruitment is story-gated, not a hire.** Each companion joins through their own quest chain — expect to complete a personal objective, win a dialogue check, or prove yourself in combat before they pledge to the crew. You don't buy loyalty; you earn it.
- **Recruitment quests live on the world map.** The map is Act One's playground (45+ levels and 100+ events/encounters across the full game), and companion quests are spread across it — one reason the game rewards wandering.
- **Dialogue matters at the moment of recruitment.** Companions "may not share the same values," and first impressions feed the friendship/rivalry/romance track from the very first conversation. Bring your talker — see the [Diplomacy Guide](/guides/diplomacy-guide) for how checks work.
- **Your reputation precedes you.** Choices you make as captain before meeting a companion can shape how (and whether) they join. The game's choices "can alter the political landscape of Japan" — and your standing with it.

## The Best Recruitment Order for Act One

You never need all eight at once — your active party is four. The strongest launch-week order, built around covering the three pillars as early as possible:

### 1. Antje (recruit first, if you can)

The burglar unlocks the stealth pillar for your crew. Locked doors "blocking the way forward" simply stop being obstacles, scouting gets faster, and every ambush setup from the [Stealth Guide](/guides/stealth-guide) becomes available in full. **Recruiting her first is the single biggest quality-of-life jump in Act One.** Expect her recruitment early — likely in or near the first settlements after the *De Albatros* arrival.

### 2. Étienne Durand (second)

The crew's "diplomatic superhero" unlocks the diplomacy pillar. Dialogue checks draw on your characters' abilities — without a talker in the active four, your best negotiation options literally don't appear. Grab him second so the mid-Act faction talks are fought with the right tool.

### 3. Johan or Piet Ruisch (third)

Either swordsman gives you a reliable frontline. Johan is the purer damage dealer (arrogant, deadly); Piet is the flexible hybrid (sword *and* pistol). Pick based on whether you want a dedicated duelist or a flex slot — the [Party Composition Guide](/guides/party-composition-guide) breaks down the trade-off.

### 4. Tora-Hime (fourth)

The onna-musha is expected mid-Act, tied to the faction politics of the Sekigahara road. She bridges combat and story — her personal quest ("seeking her place in history") is likely to open unique story routes in Act One's second half, so don't stall her recruitment past the mid-act point.

### 5–8. The rest, as quests appear

Rotate the remaining companions (including the three unnamed) through missions to keep their personal quest chains alive — loyalty quests are often time-sensitive to story chapters, and every chain rewards gear, loyalty, and story.

## Recruitment Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Leaving recruitment for later "when I'm stronger."** Companion quests compound — the earlier a companion joins, the more loyalty content you can complete inside Act One.
2. **Recruiting without a talker in the party.** Many recruitment conversations likely hinge on dialogue checks; without Étienne (or a socially built Captain), options vanish.
3. **Benching new recruits immediately.** A companion who joins and never fights misses personal-quest triggers and loyalty growth.
4. **Assuming all eight join in Act One.** The full roster exists in the build, but the exact availability of each recruitment within the 8-hour act is being confirmed — don't panic if one chain points past the launch content.
