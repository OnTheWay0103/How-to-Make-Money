# Expeditions: Samurai — Classes Guide

> Early Access Build (Act One) / Updated: August 1, 2026

At character creation you build **Captain Flynn** — the surname is fixed, but your **first name, appearance, and class** are yours. Official materials confirm **four classes** are available, and that your choice "will decide your approach to combat." The full class names, ability trees, and stat lines will be revealed with the Early Access launch on **August 7, 2026** — this guide lays out everything officially confirmed plus how class selection works across the Expeditions series, so you can plan your first character now.

## What We Know About the Four Classes

From the official announcements and trailers:

- **Four classes** are selectable during character creation.
- The choice **decides your approach to combat** — the class system is combat-facing, not story-facing.
- Classes pair with **weapon skills**: the game ships an East-meets-West arsenal (flintlocks, cutlasses, grenades, katanas, naginatas, longbows), and your class determines which weapons you are naturally strong with.
- **Crafting and loadouts** are deep enough that the same class can be played several different ways — "experiment with tactical abilities, and leverage weapon skills to tackle diverse enemy types."

## How Classes Work in the Expeditions Series

Expeditions: Rome and Viking gave classes **three specialization branches** each, chosen as you level. Expect Samurai to follow the same DNA, adapted to the three new pillars:

- **A stealth-leaning branch** — movement, silent takedowns, ambush bonuses, firecracker/smoke gadget proficiency.
- **A weapon-mastery branch** — raw damage, armor-breaking, dual-wielding, and weapon-specific skill unlocks.
- **A leader branch** — aura effects, dialogue bonuses, initiative control, and support abilities that make the party better.

None of this is confirmed for Samurai, but it is how the studio has designed classes in every Expeditions game since Conquistador, and the three-pillar system makes the pattern almost inevitable.

## Choosing a Class: What to Consider

Since class names are unconfirmed, choose with these questions in mind:

1. **Do you want to lead from the front?** If you plan to fight every battle personally, pick the class that best matches your favorite weapon family — katana duelists will want melee mastery, while flintlock fans will want a ranged-skewed class.
2. **Do you want to be the party's talker?** Dialogue checks in Expeditions draw on the speaking character's skills. If you want Captain Flynn himself to win negotiations, look for the class with the strongest social skills.
3. **Do you want to solo stealth?** Stealth lets you split the party, and some of the best ambushes are set up by a lone infiltrator. A stealth-capable class gives you that option.
4. **Who are your companions?** Your companions are fixed: Antje covers burglary and sneaking, Étienne covers diplomacy, Johan and Piet cover melee. Your class should cover what **they** don't — if you plan to run all five, a flexible class that can do a bit of everything is the safest first choice.

## Class Roles Your Party Needs

Regardless of which class you pick, a balanced four-person party needs:

| Role | Why | Who can fill it |
|------|-----|-----------------|
| **Frontline** | Holds enemy attention, survives the first exchange | Johan, Piet Ruisch, or your Captain |
| **Stealth/infiltration** | Opens fights with ambushes, bypasses locks and guards | Antje, or a stealth-built Captain |
| **Diplomacy** | Wins dialogue checks, opens peaceful routes | Étienne Durand |
| **Flex** | Ranged damage, gadgets, or backup for any pillar | Your Captain, or a customized companion |

The good news: because companions cover the fixed roles well, your Captain class is the one slot you are free to build however you like.

## Progression: What to Expect After Creation

Series veterans should plan for:

- **Leveling into your class tree** — expect three branches with meaningful choices, not a linear skill ladder.
- **Weapon skills that grow with use** — the more you fight with a katana, the better you get with one.
- **Crafting your loadout** — custom gear is core to the endgame of Act One, and your class determines which crafted items shine.
- **Respec availability** — Expeditions games have historically been generous with respec options; check the launch build, but don't fear locking yourself in permanently.
