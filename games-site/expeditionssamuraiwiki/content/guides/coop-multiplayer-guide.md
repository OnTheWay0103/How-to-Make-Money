# Expeditions: Samurai — Co-op Guide

> Early Access Build (Act One) / Updated: August 1, 2026

Expeditions: Samurai is the **first co-op game in the Expeditions series** — and the team built it into the campaign itself, not as a side mode. The full campaign is playable in **optional 2-player online co-op with drop-in/drop-out support**, with the developers' promise that "the adventure is better shared." This guide covers everything officially confirmed about the mode and how to play it well.

## How Co-op Works

The confirmed mechanics:

- **Twin captains.** In co-op, you play as a pair of twin captains — both of you are Flynns, sharing the campaign.
- **Full campaign.** Co-op covers the entire story, not separate missions.
- **Drop-in/drop-out.** A second player can join or leave mid-campaign without restarting.
- **Shared dialogue scenes.** Both players participate in conversations at the same time — and both characters' skills count in dialogue checks.
- **Competition is built in.** Players can "wrestle for control of important decisions or compete for a companion's affections."
- **Simultaneous combat.** Both captains control their halves of the party in the simultaneous turn-based system.
- **Coordinated stealth.** The stealth system's tools — firecrackers, takedowns — are explicitly designed for two-player coordination.

## The Twin Captain Fantasy

Playing as twin Flynns changes the story's texture: one campaign, two captains, and a shared crew. Key implications:

- **Combined dialogue stats.** When both characters' skills count in checks, you can split skill investment — one twin the talker, the other the warrior — and still pass checks a solo player would have to build around.
- **Competing for decisions.** Narrative decisions aren't automatically shared: you can wrestle for control of them, which makes every major choice a discussion — or an argument.
- **Two classes in one run.** The four classes combine: a stealth-classed twin and a combat-classed twin cover more ground than any single build.

## The Best Co-op Division of Labor

The most effective setup (and the one the official materials point toward):

| Role | Twin A | Twin B |
|------|--------|--------|
| **Pillar coverage** | Stealth or Combat | The other (or Diplomacy) |
| **Companion focus** | Antje + melee companions | Étienne + ranged companions |
| **Romance targets** | Pick one companion | Pick a different one |
| **Combat role** | Frontline/initiator | Support/ranged control |

Divvying up pillars and companions prevents both players from stepping on each other's story beats — and avoids the romance competition entirely.

## Coordinated Stealth: The Signature Co-op Moment

The stealth system is where co-op shines brightest, per the official materials:

- **The firecracker pass:** "Throw a distracting firecracker at the right moment to let a partner sneak past a sentry."
- **The double takedown:** "A flawlessly timed double stealth takedown" — both players strike simultaneously.
- **Split-party ambushes:** Each player positions their half of the party in real time, then opens combat from two directions at once.

The timing skill expression here is real — mistimed takedowns trigger the alarm, and the ambush evaporates. Use voice chat; the game's design expects it.

## Co-op Combat Tactics

- **Pincer openers.** Two captains control two flanks — set up the surprise attack from both sides and the enemy can't respond to both.
- **Combo abilities.** Official materials describe coordinating combat maneuvers in real time, "combining abilities for devastating combos" — coordinate your ability timing, don't just play two solo games side by side.
- **Trade aggro.** One twin tanks the pressure while the other flanks — then swap.

## Romance Competition: The Co-op Twist

Because all 8 companions are romanceable and co-op dialogue is shared, competing for the same companion is an official, supported scenario: "compete for a companion's affections." Practical advice:

- **Coordinate targets.** Agree on who romances whom before the campaign starts.
- **If you compete anyway:** shared dialogue checks count both players' choices — whichever twin's choices the companion responds to wins the day. Invest in the right skills and dialogue options.
- **Expect consequences.** Rivalry between the twins over a companion is a story the game will happily tell.

## Co-op Questions Answered

**Can I join mid-campaign?** Yes — drop-in/drop-out is confirmed.

**Does my co-op partner need their own save?** Twin captains share the campaign state; the host's progression is the shared world (details at launch).

**Does co-op lock the other pillars?** No — both players can use stealth, diplomacy, or combat as the situation demands.

**Is there more than 2-player co-op?** No — the series-first co-op is exactly 2 players.

**Is co-op available on Early Access launch day?** TBD. The Steam page's Early Access notes list the co-op mode among features being added during Early Access, and the official launch-features list (combat, exploration, stealth, world map) doesn't mention it — it may arrive with one of the first content updates. ## Co-op Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Doubling up on the same pillar.** Two stealth-only captains struggle in fights neither built for.
2. **Romancing the same companion** without agreeing on the outcome first.
3. **Splitting the party too far apart.** The stealth system wants splits — but keep takedown support in reach.
4. **Ignoring shared dialogue checks.** Both skills count — use both characters' strengths in conversations.
