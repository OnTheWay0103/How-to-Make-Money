# Expeditions: Samurai — Diplomacy Guide

> Early Access Build (Act One) / Updated: August 1, 2026

Every swordfight in Expeditions: Samurai is a choice — and so is every conversation. Diplomacy is one of the game's **three pillars** (alongside stealth and steel), and the series has always rewarded a silver tongue: skill checks in dialogue draw on your characters' abilities, and a diplomatic solution costs **no lives and no supplies**. This guide covers how the system works, what official materials confirm, and how to talk your way through Act One.

## The Diplomacy Pillar: What's Confirmed

Official materials are clear about the pillar's role:

- **Dialogue can resolve situations entirely.** A successful negotiation ends an encounter without a single turn of combat.
- **Skill checks draw on character abilities.** The characters you bring to a conversation determine what you can say and pull off — which is why a talker belongs in your active party.
- **Étienne Durand is your diplomatic specialist.** The crew's priest is officially described as the party's "diplomatic superhero," leading negotiations and talking the crew through difficult situations.
- **Diplomacy is a full path, not a side option.** Like stealth and combat, it can carry you through most of the story — and mixing pillars is almost always stronger than relying on one.

## How Dialogue and Skill Checks Work

The exact check system is **TBD until the launch build** — Expeditions games have historically used ability-driven dialogue checks, and the official framing ("skill checks in dialogue draw on your characters' abilities") points the same direction. Expect the classic CRPG pattern:

| Check type | What it does | Who's good at it |
|------------|--------------|------------------|
| **Persuasion** | Convince, soothe, and bargain | Your captain, Étienne, characters with high charisma |
| **Intimidation** | Threaten, dominate, and cow | Brawlers like Piet, high-command characters |
| **Knowledge** | Recall history, customs, and politics | Scholars, priests, and well-traveled crew |

Notes on these check types:

- **Exact names and stats are TBD.** The three categories above are the expected framework based on the series' design DNA — the launch build will confirm the real labels and the attributes behind them.
- **Party composition matters.** The active party of four determines which checks are available. Walk into a negotiation without a talker and your best options simply won't appear.
- **Failures aren't always fatal.** The Expeditions series rewards persistence — a failed check often leads to a worse path (combat, higher costs), not a dead end. Expect Act One to punish and recover gracefully.

## Faction Reputation and Its Weight

The game is set in 1600, weeks before the Battle of Sekigahara decided Japan's future. Your words have political weight:

- **Choices "can alter the political landscape of Japan"** — that's the official framing, and dialogue is the main lever.
- **Two great sides.** The era's conflict (Tokugawa Ieyasu's Eastern Army vs. Ishida Mitsunari's Western Army) frames your reputation choices. Every faction you impress or insult is a faction you'll meet again.
- **Reputation is carried, not reset.** What the warlords think of you in Act One is your political starting position in the later acts — treat reputation as a resource.

## De-escalation Tactics That Work

### Talk Before You Draw

- Every encounter begins with the option to negotiate — exhaust the dialogue before triggering combat.
- A failed persuasion check often escalates to a fight, so **have your ambush position set before you start talking**. Negotiate from a position of strength.
- De-escalation isn't just avoidance: a negotiated withdrawal saves injuries, ammunition, and supplies for the fights that can't be talked out of.

### Bring Leverage

- **Stealth + diplomacy:** sneak into position first, acquire leverage (a hostage, a document, a safe), then negotiate from strength. This is the classic pillar combination.
- **Knowledge + diplomacy:** know the local politics before you speak. High-knowledge characters unlock dialogue options that lower the difficulty of the checks that follow.

### Know When Words Fail

- Some enemies cannot be negotiated with — bandits with nothing to lose, sworn oaths, orders from above.
- When dialogue fails, a clean surprise attack opener (see the [Stealth Guide](/guides/stealth-guide)) is the best fallback: the fight starts on your terms, which the [Combat Guide](/guides/combat-guide) explains is half the victory.

## Key Diplomatic Encounters in Act One

A note on honesty: the specific quest list of Act One is **not confirmed** — no walkthrough exists yet (the game launches August 7, 2026). What follows is the expected shape based on official framing:

1. **The first contact (expected)** — Your crew arrives injured and alone in a country at war. How you approach the first local powers sets your reputation tone for the entire act.
2. **The alliance talks (expected)** — Deciding which faction to serve is the act's defining diplomatic decision.
3. **The companion persuasions (expected)** — Several recruits likely require conversation — or argument — before they join.
4. **The Sekigahara road (expected)** — Act One builds toward the era's great confrontation; expect negotiation attempts before battles, and treachery in both directions.

## Building a Diplomatic Party

| Slot | Who | Why |
|------|-----|-----|
| Lead negotiator | **Étienne Durand** | Officially the party's diplomatic superhero |
| Captain | **You** | Your own ability scores shape the check options |
| Muscle for intimidation | **Piet or Johan** | Intimidation checks need a threatening presence |
| Wildcard | **Rotate for quests** | Companion loyalty quests often hinge on dialogue choices |

## Diplomacy Checklist

- [ ] Keep a talker (Étienne) in the active party for negotiation-heavy missions
- [ ] Set up the ambush position *before* starting the conversation
- [ ] Scout for leverage before you negotiate
- [ ] Check which companion's skills best match the check type on offer
- [ ] Track faction reputation — choices carry into later acts

## Common Diplomacy Mistakes

1. **Skipping dialogue entirely.** Free solutions are the best solutions — talk first, fight second.
2. **Negotiating without a talker.** Your best options simply don't exist if the right character isn't in the party.
3. **Threatening everyone.** Intimidation is a tool, not a personality — overuse turns allies into rivals.
4. **Talking when you should ambush.** Some fights are easier won from stealth; not every encounter is a negotiation.
