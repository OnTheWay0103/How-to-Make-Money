# Expeditions: Samurai — Weapons & Armor Guide

> Early Access Build (Act One) / Updated: August 1, 2026

The armory of Expeditions: Samurai is the series' most distinctive: an **East-meets-West arsenal** that lets a 1600 AD privateer crew trade cutlasses for katanas mid-campaign. Officially confirmed families include **flintlocks, cutlasses, and grenades** on the Western side and **katanas, naginatas, and longbows** on the Japanese side — plus **custom gear crafting** and **weapon skills** (in the series' tradition, weapon skills grow with use — expected, not yet confirmed for Samurai). This guide covers everything confirmed about gear and how to think about loadouts.

## The Confirmed Weapon Families

### Western Arsenal (Privateer's Heritage)

- **Flintlocks** — The series' reliable sidearm: solid burst damage at medium range, useful for opening volleys in the simultaneous combat system.
- **Cutlasses** — Quick, dependable melee — the workhorse blade of the crew.
- **Grenades** — Area damage and crowd control; the loud answer to clustered enemies.

### Japanese Arsenal (New Arrival)

- **Katanas** — The icon. Expect duelist-style weapon skills, counters, and the highest single-target damage ceiling in melee.
- **Naginatas** — Reach weapons that control space — ideal for holding chokepoints and punishing approaching enemies before they can strike.
- **Longbows** — Ranged pressure with better consistency than powder weapons (expected: no reload step) — the sniper option for position-based play.

## Armor and Defense

Armor details beyond the official note that Early Access "adds quests, weapons, and armor over time" are yet to be fully detailed, but the series' formula is established:

- **Armor reduces incoming damage** — heavier armor protects against melee, but watch mobility costs in a system where positioning is king.
- **Injury prevention** — Armor that stops injuries is worth more than armor that stops damage; injuries compound across a campaign.
- **Loadout weight** — The "resources, injuries, weapons, armor" economy means every slot is a tradeoff between protection and flexibility.

## Crafting: Confirmed for the Full Release, Timing TBD

Crafting is confirmed as a core feature of the game ("craft custom gear, experiment with tactical abilities, and leverage weapon skills") — **but official materials indicate it arrives with the full release**: the Steam page lists crafting among full-version additions, while Early Access "adds quests, weapons, and armor over time." Whether the EA launch build includes any crafting is **TBD** as of this writing — don't plan an Act One endgame around it until launch confirms it. When it ships, the system is expected to follow Expeditions: Rome's proven formula:

- **Recipes and materials** are expected to come from quests, encounters, and the world map's 100+ events (full release).
- **Crafted gear exceeds looted gear** — custom stats and modifiers make crafting the primary progression path for loadouts (series precedent).
- **Weapon skills reward focus** — the more you use a family, the better you are with it, so your crafted weapons should match your skill investment.

### Crafting Priority Order (When Crafting Arrives)

1. **Weapons before armor.** A good weapon amplifies every skill point; armor is a safety net.
2. **Match the build.** Don't craft a katana for a build invested in cutlass skills — and vice versa.
3. **Craft for matchups.** "Leverage weapon skills to tackle diverse enemy types" means keeping a second weapon family ready for resistance checks.
4. **Upgrade the damage dealers first.** Your frontline and duelists benefit more from weapon upgrades than your diplomat.

## Loadout Philosophy: Two Families, One Role

The confirmed design ("experiment with tactical abilities, and leverage weapon skills to tackle diverse enemy types") points to loadouts built around **one primary weapon family plus one secondary**:

| Role | Primary | Secondary | Why |
|------|---------|-----------|-----|
| Duelist | Katana | Flintlock | Close with the blade, open with the powder |
| Zone control | Naginata | Longbow | Reach both close and far |
| Gunslinger | Flintlock | Cutlass | Open with powder, finish with steel |
| Skirmisher | Longbow | Katana | Range until forced, then punish in melee |

## Where to Find the Best Gear (Expected Sources)

The exact loot tables aren't known yet — treat these as expected sources based on the series' design:

- **Companion quest chains** — each companion's personal story is expected to reward signature gear tied to their arc.
- **World map events** — 100+ events/encounters (full release) are expected to include optional encounters with unique equipment.
- **Crafting** — the only source of fully custom loadouts, once it ships.
- **Major encounters** — Act One's big fights are expected to drop strong base gear (TBD until launch).

## Gear Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Selling crafting materials.** You will want them at the Act One midpoint.
2. **Ignoring weapon skill matchups.** A great weapon you have no skill in is a wasted slot.
3. **Heavy armor on everyone.** Mobility is king — speed matters more than the armor number in the simultaneous combat system.
4. **Forgetting grenades exist.** Area damage is the fastest answer to clustered enemies — stock up when you find them.
