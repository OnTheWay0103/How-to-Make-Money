# Expeditions: Samurai — Progression & Skill System Guide

> Early Access Build (Act One) / Updated: August 7, 2026

The class trees ship with the Early Access build — and because **your Early Access saves carry to the full 1.0 release**, every skill point you spend in Act One is a down payment on your 60-hour campaign. This guide covers how progression works: leveling, skill points, class branches, weapon skills, and where to invest for an 8-hour act. Where official materials are silent, we follow the studio's confirmed series design (Expeditions: Conquistador, Viking, Rome) and mark the difference.

## How Leveling Works

The confirmed backbone: your party "evolves into true masters of the battlefield" (**official framing**), and the [Classes Guide](/guides/classes-guide) details the four creation classes. The expected loop, based on the series' DNA:

| Stage | What happens |
|-------|--------------|
| **Earn XP** | Combat, quests, stealth resolutions, and diplomatic successes all generate experience — the pillars feed progression equally |
| **Level up** | Level-ups award skill points to spend in your class's branches |
| **Branch out** | Classes historically offer **three specialization branches** each, chosen as you level |
| **Weapon mastery** | Weapon skills grow with use — the more you fight with a katana, the better you are with one |

Level-ups apply to **companions as well as your Captain** — progression is a party-wide project, not a solo one.

## Skill Points: The Allocation Priorities

The [Best Builds Guide](/guides/best-builds-guide) has the philosophy; here is the Act One spending order:

1. **One pillar to 70% first.** Commit to your primary pillar before anything else. Jack-of-all-trades builds underperform in every Expeditions game.
2. **Weapon skills over utility.** Skill lines that boost your chosen weapons beat generic utility early — the game's "leverage weapon skills" framing is the designers telling you focus pays.
3. **Companion synergy at every level.** Your build is four characters, not one — level companions into the roles your lineup needs (see the [Party Composition Guide](/guides/party-composition-guide)).
4. **Survival secondaries.** A point in injury resistance or mobility protects every other point you've spent.
5. **Don't bank for late game.** Early Access is Act One only — build for the content that exists, not for a level-40 fantasy that ships at 1.0.

## Class Branches: What to Expect

The launch build's exact class trees are being documented on launch day (**TBD** details). The studio's confirmed pattern across Conquistador, Viking, and Rome — and the shape the three pillars imply — is three branches per class:

- **A stealth-leaning branch** — movement, silent takedowns, ambush bonuses, gadget proficiency.
- **A weapon-mastery branch** — raw damage, armor-breaking, weapon-specific unlocks.
- **A leader branch** — auras, dialogue bonuses, initiative control, support.

Plan your class pick (four available at creation) with this structure in mind: whatever branch names the launch build uses, the pillars will be behind them.

## Weapon Skills: Mastery Through Use

The game's "weapon skills" system rewards **focus**:

- Skills grow through use — a character who fights with a naginata gets better with a naginata (**expected**, series tradition).
- Carry **two weapon families minimum** for matchup flexibility ("leverage weapon skills to tackle diverse enemy types"), but invest skill points into one primary family.
- Your crafted gear should match your mastered families — a great weapon you have no skill in is a wasted slot (see the [Weapons & Armor Guide](/guides/weapons-armor-guide)).

## The Progression Traps Nobody Warns You About

- **Injuries are a progression tax.** An untreated injury is a compounding debuff on every roll and every skill — the [Injuries & Healing Guide](/guides/injuries-healing-guide) explains why healing is the cheapest skill point you'll ever spend.
- **Dead companions end branches.** A companion who dies takes their progression (and their story) with them — protect the party.
- **Respec may or may not exist.** The studio has historically been generous with respec options, but the launch build's availability is **TBD**. Don't spend recklessly assuming a fix is guaranteed — check the options menu early.
- **Difficulty changes progression pressure.** High resource difficulty means fewer supplies and more injuries, which taxes the same skill points you're investing — tune consciously (see the [Difficulty Guide](/guides/difficulty-guide)).

## Progression in Co-op

Both twin captains level their own characters through the shared campaign. The efficient split: one twin invests in dialogue and utility, the other in combat and weapon skills — the shared dialogue checks (both characters' skills count) reward exactly this division of labor.

## Progression Checklist

- [ ] Pick your primary pillar and commit before spreading points
- [ ] Focus weapon skills on one family (carry a second for matchups)
- [ ] Level companions into the lineup's missing roles
- [ ] Check respec availability in the launch build on day one
- [ ] Tend injuries between missions — a debuffed build is a broken build
- [ ] Spend for Act One, not for a 1.0 fantasy

## Progression Mistakes

1. **Spreading points across all three pillars.** Mediocre at everything beats competent at nothing.
2. **Banking skill points "for later."** Later doesn't exist in Early Access — Act One is the whole game until the updates land.
3. **Ignoring companion progression.** Eight companions, four active slots — benched companions level slower, and under-leveled bench riders fail when you finally rotate them in.
4. **Skipping dialogue-based XP.** Diplomacy and stealth resolutions level you just like combat — the cheapest XP in the game comes from the checklist in the [Three Pillars Guide](/guides/three-pillars-guide).
