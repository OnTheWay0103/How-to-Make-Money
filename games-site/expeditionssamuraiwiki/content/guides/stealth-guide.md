# Expeditions: Samurai — Stealth Guide

> Early Access Build (Act One) / Updated: August 1, 2026

Stealth is the biggest new system in Expeditions: Samurai — a full third pillar alongside diplomacy and violence, and the first time the series has had one. Press coverage has credited **veterans of the Hitman series** with work on the stealth AI — a detail we're marking **TBD** until official materials confirm it, though it tells you how much depth to expect. This guide breaks down everything officially confirmed about the system and how to make it your party's deadliest weapon.

## The Stealth Loop: Setup Beats Everything

Series creator Jonas Wæver's description is the whole system in one quote: *"You can sneak around, split up your party, set up your positions for a really effective surprise attack and then when you make that initial devastating sneak attack to get into combat, everything is set up for your perfect victory."*

The loop has four stages:

1. **Sneak** — Move your party in real time, staying out of enemy sightlines.
2. **Split** — Separate your characters to surround the objective.
3. **Position** — Put each fighter where they can strike or control the field.
4. **Strike** — Open combat with a devastating surprise attack.

That last step is the point: **the pre-combat setup phase is where battles are won in Expeditions: Samurai.**

## Core Stealth Abilities

Officially confirmed tools of the trade:

- **Firecrackers** — Officially confirmed: throw one to lure guards away from patrol routes, slip past sentries, or set up silent takedowns.
- **Smoke bombs** — Conceal movement and break enemy lines of sight for escapes and repositioning (**TBD**: previews have described smoke-type tools, but official materials have only confirmed firecrackers by name — the full gadget roster arrives with the launch build).
- **Silent takedowns** — Remove sentries one by one to thin enemy lines before full-scale battle.
- **Dynamic combat initiation** — You control exactly how (and whether) combat begins — including avoiding combat entirely in many situations.
- **Party splitting** — The system is built around splitting the party; solo characters are viable at stealth tasks.

## Stealth Tactics That Work

### The Double Ambush

- Split your party into two groups, flanking a patrol route.
- Use a firecracker to split the patrol — the distracted half walks past your first group.
- Takedown the separated guards silently, then spring the ambush on the remainder.

### The Feint

- Draw enemies toward a firecracker noise with one decoy character.
- While their backs are turned, your real party slips into position behind them.
- Open with the surprise attack from behind — positioning bonuses and flanking advantage apply.

### The Ghost Run

- Don't fight at all. Sneak past every encounter, complete the objective, leave.
- Perfect for supply-strained runs or scouting a level before committing to combat.

### The Thinning

- Takedown every isolated sentry before ever revealing yourself.
- By the time the main force realizes anything is wrong, they're half-strength.

## Stealth in Co-op: The Shared Toolbox

Co-op turns stealth into a coordination game, with tools the official materials describe explicitly:

- **The coordinated distraction** — "Throw a distracting firecracker at the right moment to let a partner sneak past a sentry."
- **The double stealth takedown** — "A flawlessly timed double stealth takedown" where both players strike simultaneously.
- **Simultaneous positioning** — Both captains position their halves of the party at the same time, halving setup time for ambushes.

Communication is everything: the firecracker timing window is tight, and a mistimed takedown turns your perfect ambush into a brawl.

## Stealth vs. the Other Pillars

Stealth is not a "win button" — it's a multiplier. The best runs in Expeditions: Samurai **mix pillars**:

- **Stealth + Combat:** Thin the enemy, then fight the remainder with numbers advantage.
- **Stealth + Diplomacy:** Sneak into position to acquire leverage (a hostage, a document, a safe), then negotiate from strength.
- **Stealth alone:** The pure ghost run — highest risk, highest reward, and officially supported ("players may avoid combat entirely in many situations").

## Stealth Checklist for Every Mission

- [ ] Scout the level's sightlines before engaging anything
- [ ] Identify isolated sentries for takedowns
- [ ] Carry at least one firecracker per stealth character (plus a smoke-type escape tool if the launch build has one)
- [ ] Agree on the opener before triggering combat (especially in co-op)
- [ ] Have a fallback plan — where do you retreat if the ambush fails?

## Common Stealth Mistakes

1. **Splitting the party too thin.** Positioning matters, but a lone character caught out can die before you can save them.
2. **Wasting takedown opportunities.** Every sentry you remove is a free advantage — don't skip them.
3. **Triggering combat from a bad position.** The surprise attack opener is everything; a rushed opener wastes your entire setup.
4. **Forgetting smoke.** Smoke bombs are your escape tool when the ambush goes wrong — carry them always.
