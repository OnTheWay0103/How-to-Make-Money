# Expeditions: Samurai — Combat Mechanics Deep Dive

> Early Access Build (Act One) / Updated: August 7, 2026

The [Combat Guide](/guides/combat-guide) covers how to win fights. This guide covers *what is actually happening on the battlefield* — the mechanics under the hood: initiative and the simultaneous turn system, the hex-grid battlefield, cover, flanking, attacks of opportunity, morale, environmental combat, and stance play. Details confirmed by official materials are marked **confirmed**; details reported from playable previews are marked **preview**; anything expected but unverified is marked **TBD**.

## Initiative & Turn Order: The Simultaneous System

The official description: **movement, attacks, and interruptions can all play out simultaneously** — the series' evolution of turn-based combat into something faster and more positional (**confirmed**).

What that means in practice, per first-hand preview reports:

- **You move your units, then the enemy reacts.** One preview describes the flow as: players move all their units before enemies act — a "move-then-resolve" rhythm that turns positioning into the core skill (**preview**).
- **Interrupts break sequencing.** Actions can be interrupted mid-execution, so your action *order* is a decision: bait the enemy's interrupt with a fast attack, then land the slow, powerful one (**confirmed**).
- **Setup beats speed.** Because the first exchange resolves around positioning, the party that enters combat already placed (see the [Stealth Guide](/guides/stealth-guide)) starts with a free round of advantage (**confirmed** framing).

**The initiative takeaway:** don't think in "my turn / their turn." Think in *phases* — setup, opening exchange, cleanup. Every ability should be chosen for the phase it lands in.

## The Battlefield: Hex Grid, Cover & Positioning

The launch build's battlefield is a **hex grid** with real positional rules (**preview**):

- **Cover** — blocking line of sight and reducing incoming damage. Full cover hides you; half cover merely protects you. In a game where the setup phase decides fights, cover is a resource you place before the fight starts.
- **Flanking** — attacking from the side or rear avoids (and punishes) the enemy's facing. The ambush opener from stealth is flanking at its most extreme: every enemy caught facing the wrong way is a free hit.
- **Attacks of opportunity** — leaving an enemy's threatened zone invites a punish strike (**preview**). This is why reach weapons (naginata) shine: they threaten more hexes and make the battlefield shrink for the enemy.
- **Elevation** — the series has always rewarded high ground; expect ranged characters to pay for every vertical advantage (**TBD** details at launch).

**The positioning takeaway:** every fight is a geometry problem. Hold chokepoints with reach weapons, keep squishy characters in cover, and always know your escape route before the first exchange.

## Morale: The Hidden Second Health Bar

Combat has a **morale system** (**preview**) — enemies (and presumably your crew) can break under pressure. What feeds it:

- **Surprise attacks** — an ambush opener is a morale event: a patrol that loses two members before acting starts the fight broken.
- **Focus-fired kills** — dropping enemies quickly compounds panic; the [Combat Guide](/guides/combat-guide) already preaches killing interrupters first, and morale is why that works.
- **Environment plays** — see below.

**The morale takeaway:** fights snowball. The first kill isn't just damage — it's the start of a cascade. Prioritize finishing weakened enemies over spreading chip damage.

## Environmental Combat: The World Is a Weapon

The previews' favorite detail: the environment participates in fights (**preview**):

- **Knocking enemies through walls and off ledges** — some abilities interact with the battlefield itself for instant removal.
- **Setting fires with lanterns** — oil, lanterns, and thatch make fire a crowd-control tool.
- **Destructible cover** — the cover you stand behind may be the cover you can lose.

**The environment takeaway:** scout for hazards during the stealth phase, not during the fight. A lantern behind a clustered enemy squad is a free fireball you set up before combat starts.

## Stances & Play Postures

A formal stance system (toggleable offensive/defensive modes) is **TBD** — the launch build's exact ability list hasn't been fully documented yet. What *is* confirmed is the stance-shaped reality of the system: because actions resolve simultaneously and can be interrupted, every character must adopt a **play posture**:

| Posture | What you do | When it wins |
|---------|-------------|--------------|
| **Aggressive** | Commit high-value actions early, fish for interrupts | When your opener is the ambush and you have numbers |
| **Reactive** | Fast attacks to bait enemy interrupts, punish with follow-ups | Against enemies with strong interrupt abilities |
| **Guard** | Hold a defensive action, punish attackers | Protecting a squishy character or a chokepoint |
| **Fluid** | Switch postures between phases | The general-purpose choice for most of Act One |

If the launch build does include formal stances, expect them to map onto this framework — we'll update this section the moment the full ability list is documented.

## How the Pillars Feed the Mechanics

The deep mechanics don't exist in a vacuum — they're why the other pillars work:

- **Stealth sets the initiative.** The ambush opener places your units, chooses the enemy's facing, and starts the morale cascade — all before the first exchange (**confirmed**).
- **Diplomacy sets the matchups.** Negotiating away a squad means the remaining fight starts with a smaller, weaker enemy force (**confirmed** framing).
- **Gadgets set the geometry.** Firecrackers move patrols; smoke (reported in previews, official list **TBD**) resets sightlines mid-fight.

## The Deep-Dive Checklist

- [ ] Identify the phase your next action lands in (setup / opening / cleanup)
- [ ] Place your party in cover *before* triggering combat
- [ ] Hold chokepoints with reach weapons — count threatened hexes
- [ ] Target the first kill for the morale cascade, not just the damage
- [ ] Scout for environmental hazards during the stealth phase
- [ ] Adopt a posture per phase — aggressive opener, reactive mid, guard on the squishies
- [ ] Know your retreat route before the first exchange

## Deep Mechanics Mistakes

1. **Ignoring facing.** Enemies fought from the front fight at full strength — flanking is free damage.
2. **Standing in the open.** In a cover-based battlefield, an exposed unit is a damage magnet and a morale gift.
3. **Wasting environment.** A lantern, a wall, a ledge — the map's hazards are abilities you never have to slot.
4. **Committing to one posture.** A party that's all-aggressive dies to interrupts; a party that's all-guard never kills anything.
