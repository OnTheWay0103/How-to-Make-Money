# Expeditions: Samurai — The Three Pillars Guide (Stealth, Diplomacy & Steel)

> Early Access Build (Act One) / Updated: August 7, 2026

Every situation in Expeditions: Samurai can be approached three ways — **stealth, diplomacy, or steel**. The developers call these the game's pillars, and they are the organizing principle of everything: the party you bring, the builds you make, the missions you can resolve. The [Beginner Guide](/guides/beginner-guide) introduces the pillars; this guide is the system-level playbook — what each pillar actually gets you, when each one wins, and how to combine them into approaches that no single-pillar party can match.

## The Three Pillars

| Pillar | What it is | What it costs | What it wins |
|--------|-----------|---------------|--------------|
| **Stealth** | Real-time sneaking, party splitting, ambush setups, silent takedowns | Time and positioning discipline | A fight won before it starts — or no fight at all |
| **Diplomacy** | Dialogue, skill checks, negotiation, de-escalation | A talker in the active party | Resolution with zero casualties and zero supplies spent |
| **Steel** | Fast, simultaneous turn-based combat | Supplies, injuries, and ammunition | Certainty — dead enemies stay dead |

None of the pillars is a "wrong" path: official materials confirm each can carry you through most of the story. But each has a **terrain where it dominates** — and a terrain where it wastes you.

## When Each Pillar Wins

### Stealth wins when…

- **The map is full of patrols and locks.** Stealth grounds pay for an infiltrator twice over — see the [Stealth Guide](/guides/stealth-guide) for the full system.
- **You're outnumbered.** A thinned patrol is a fair fight; a thinned patrol plus an ambush opener is a rout.
- **You need something without anyone knowing.** Loot, documents, leverage — the ghost run exists, and it's officially supported ("players may avoid combat entirely in many situations").

### Diplomacy wins when…

- **The enemy can be reasoned with.** Not every fight has to be a fight — the [Diplomacy Guide](/guides/diplomacy-guide) covers reading which encounters negotiate.
- **Resources are low.** A negotiated resolution costs no lives and no supplies — in a starvation economy, words are the cheapest currency.
- **The stakes are political.** Faction reputation is a carried resource; how you resolve Act One's talks reshapes the map for later acts.

### Steel wins when…

- **The enemy cannot be reasoned with.** Bandits with nothing to lose, sworn oaths, orders from above.
- **The objective is annihilation.** Some missions end only when the other side stops existing.
- **You're already positioned.** If your party is placed for an ambush, the fastest resolution is the combat that follows it.

## The Pillar Decision Framework

When an encounter appears, run this checklist:

1. **Can I avoid it?** Sneak past — free resources saved. (Stealth)
2. **Can I talk it down?** Negotiate — zero cost resolution. (Diplomacy)
3. **Can I thin it first?** Takedowns + ambush opener, then fight the remainder. (Stealth → Steel)
4. **Can I fight it now?** Only after steps 1–3 are exhausted or inappropriate. (Steel)

Most new players default to step 4. The best players run the checklist in order — and the game rewards it at every difficulty level.

## Pillar Synergy: The Combinations That Dominate

Mixing pillars beats any single-pillar party:

| Combination | How it works | Why it's strong |
|-------------|--------------|-----------------|
| **Stealth + Steel** | Thin sentries, ambush opener, then clean up the remainder | Fights start at 60% enemy strength with a free first exchange |
| **Stealth + Diplomacy** | Sneak in, acquire leverage (hostage, document, position), then negotiate from strength | The strongest negotiation position is one the other side doesn't know you have |
| **Diplomacy + Steel** | Talk past weak encounters, fight only the ones worth fighting | Your supplies pay for the fights that matter |
| **All three** | Scout → leverage → ambush → negotiate the survivors | The "perfect victory" the developers describe |

## Companions & Pillars: The Enabling Roster

Your active four decide which pillars are available at all:

| Pillar | Enablers | Note |
|--------|----------|------|
| **Stealth** | Antje, a stealth-built Captain | Antje is the only confirmed locks specialist — benching her closes locked routes |
| **Diplomacy** | Étienne Durand, a socially built Captain | Without a talker in the active four, your best dialogue options don't appear |
| **Steel** | Johan, Piet Ruisch, any combat build | The most replaceable pillar — anyone can fight |
| **Flex** | The Captain | Your class choice should fill the pillar your lineup lacks |

The [Party Composition Guide](/guides/party-composition-guide) builds full lineups around this table.

## Pillars and Difficulty

Your difficulty settings change the pillars' value — the [Difficulty Guide](/guides/difficulty-guide) has the full breakdown:

- **High combat difficulty:** stealth stops being optional — ambushes, not brawls, win hard fights.
- **High resource difficulty:** diplomacy stops being a bonus and becomes survival — negotiating for supplies beats buying them.
- **Low everything:** pillars become flavor, not necessity — fine for story-first players.

## Pillars in Co-op

Co-op doubles the toolbox — and doubles the failure mode if both players pick the same pillar. The strongest setup: **one twin owns stealth or combat, the other owns the other, and shared dialogue checks (both captains' skills count) cover diplomacy together.** See the [Co-op Guide](/guides/coop-multiplayer-guide) for the full division of labor.

## Pillar Checklist

- [ ] Run the decision framework before every encounter: avoid → talk → thin → fight
- [ ] Keep Antje (stealth) and Étienne (diplomacy) available in the rotation
- [ ] Set up leverage *before* you open negotiations
- [ ] Ambush before you brawl — the opener is a pillar in itself
- [ ] In co-op, split the pillars instead of doubling them

## Pillar Mistakes

1. **Fighting first by default.** The checklist exists because step 4 is the most expensive option.
2. **Going all-in on one pillar.** Pure playthroughs work; mixed playthroughs dominate — the synergy combos above are worth more than any single build.
3. **Negotiating without a talker or sneaking without Antje.** Pillars you didn't bring are pillars you don't have.
4. **Treating pillars as exclusive.** The best missions use two or three in sequence — that's the game's intended flow, not a trick.
