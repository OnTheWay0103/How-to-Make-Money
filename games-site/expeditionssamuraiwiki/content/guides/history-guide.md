# Sengoku Japan 101 — The Historical Context of Expeditions: Samurai

> Early Access Build (Act One) / Updated: August 1, 2026

Expeditions: Samurai drops your privateer crew into Japan in **1600 AD** — the hinge year of the **Sengoku period** (the Warring States era), weeks before the **Battle of Sekigahara** decided the nation's future for the next 250 years. You don't need a history degree to enjoy the game, but knowing the era makes every faction choice, every dialogue, and every Hatamoto ambition land harder. This guide covers the real history the game builds on — and how the game uses, adapts, and rewrites it.

## The Sengoku Period: Warring States Japan

- **Spanned roughly 1467–1600**: from the Onin War that shattered central authority to the Battle of Sekigahara that reunified the country.
- **A century of civil war**: regional warlords (**daimyō**) fought endlessly for land and power as the shogunate's control collapsed.
- **The three unifiers**: **Oda Nobunaga** (the brutal innovator who began reunification), **Toyotomi Hideyoshi** (his successor, who completed most of it), and **Tokugawa Ieyasu** (the survivor who won it all at Sekigahara).
- **By 1600**, Hideyoshi was dead, and the country had split into two armed camps fighting over his succession — the setting of the game.

## The Battle of Sekigahara (October 21, 1600)

The single most important event in the game's timeframe:

- **The stakes:** the largest samurai battle of the era decided who ruled Japan — Ieyasu's **Eastern Army** vs. **Ishida Mitsunari's** Western Army.
- **The result:** Ieyasu won decisively in a single day, thanks to generals who betrayed the Western Army mid-battle.
- **The consequences:** Ieyasu's victory led to the **Tokugawa Shogunate** — over 250 years of peace under one dynasty.
- **In the game:** the story is set in the same year, building toward this confrontation. The game's central conflict — the factions you serve and betray — is this battle, fought with a Dutch privateer crew in the mix.

## The Hatamoto: Your Place in the New Order

Hatamoto — literally **"under the banners"** — were the direct vassals of the shogun, the highest-ranking samurai who could be granted their own fiefs. In the game:

- **Becoming Hatamoto is a major story goal** — it's the rank that unlocks **village management**, your "village of your own in Japan."
- Historically, Hatamoto were the backbone of the shogunate's military and administration — a foreigner (or a woman like the onna-musha companion Tora-Hime) earning that rank is the game's central historical fantasy.
- The title's historical reality makes the game's version meaningful: in 1600, being made a Hatamoto by Ieyasu meant a permanent stake in the Tokugawa victory — exactly the deal the game offers you.

## Samurai Culture Basics

- **The warrior class** of feudal Japan — a hereditary military caste whose honor code (later codified as *bushidō*) demanded loyalty, discipline, and skill with the blade.
- **Bushidō in 1600 was less codified than legend suggests** — the era's samurai were as much pragmatists and politicians as they were idealists. The game reflects this: loyalty and rivalry are systems, not flavor.
- **The onna-musha** — women trained in martial arts, who historically defended homes and castles during the wars. Companion Tora-Hime belongs to this tradition, "seeking her place in history" in a culture that denied women the formal warrior path.
- **Honor as currency** — reputation, face, and obligation shaped every negotiation of the era. The game's diplomacy pillar and faction reputation system are built on exactly this.
- **The sword** — the katana was already the samurai's symbol by 1600, but the era was also one of guns: matchlock firearms had transformed Japanese warfare decades earlier. The game's East-meets-West arsenal (katana and naginata alongside flintlock and cutlass) sits squarely in that reality.

## How the Game Uses (and Rewrites) History

- **Alternate history by design.** You arrive as the first European samurai — replacing William Adams's historical role and, the developers note, with real consequences for the story.
- **The era is the sandbox.** Factions, battles, and politics of 1600 are the game's backbone — "your choices can alter the political landscape of Japan and alter the fate of powerful warlords."
- **Historical texture, game mechanics.** Hatamoto service drives village management; Sengoku honor drives the loyalty/rivalry system; the Sekigahara conflict drives the faction choice — the history is the gameplay.
- **Not a textbook.** Expect adaptation, compression, and invention. The wiki will track known divergences from real history as players document them after launch.

## Where to Learn More

The game is a good excuse to read up on the era. Solid starting points (not affiliated with the wiki — plain history recommendations):

- **Stephen Turnbull's works** on Sengoku Japan — the standard English-language entry point, including the specific *Sekigahara 1600: The Final Struggle for Power*.
- **"The Samurai: A Military History"** by Stephen Turnbull — the broad arc of the warrior class.
- **William Adams** — the real first English samurai has been widely covered; his story makes the game's alternate-history premise click (the game itself is the counterfactual).
- **The onna-musha** — the historical reality of women in samurai warfare is increasingly well-documented in English-language scholarship; Tora-Hime's character draws on that tradition.
