# Expeditions: Samurai — Injuries, Healing & Companion Death Guide

> Early Access Build (Act One) / Updated: August 7, 2026

Expeditions games are famous for one thing above all: **careless leaders lose people**. Injuries in Expeditions: Samurai are not a health bar afterthought — they are a compounding debuff system, a resource sink, and the game's sharpest edge. A wounded companion fights worse, an untreated wound becomes permanent, and a dead companion is a story that ends. This guide covers how the injury system works, how to heal, and how to keep all eight companions alive through Act One.

## How Injuries Work

The confirmed framing, from official materials and the series' design DNA:

- **Damage becomes wounds.** When a character takes serious damage in combat, they come out of the fight injured — the wound persists after the battle ends.
- **Injuries compound.** An injured companion fights worse; an untreated injury degrades further. The community shorthand is "injury tax": every fight you enter wounded is a fight you start behind.
- **Untreated injuries become permanent.** Push a wounded companion through too many missions without tending them and the debuff can become a permanent stat penalty — a build ruined by neglect. This is the core of the [Progression Guide](/guides/progression-skills-guide)'s warning: healing is the cheapest skill investment in the game.
- **Wounds have severity.** Expect light / serious / critical tiers (exact labels **TBD** at launch), where higher tiers need more medicine, more time, or both.

## How to Heal Your Crew

The healing loop, in priority order:

1. **Tend injuries between every mission.** Rest and treatment at camp is the baseline — make it a ritual, not a reaction. A debuffed crew fails the first checks of the next mission.
2. **Spend medicine on serious wounds.** Medicine is a finite resource in Early Access — budget it for the wounds that would otherwise compound, and let light wounds heal with rest.
3. **Protect the talker and the infiltrator.** Injury penalties hit skill checks too — a wounded Étienne loses negotiations, a wounded Antje fumbles takedowns. Your pillar specialists are the most expensive people to have wounded.
4. **Don't fight injured if you can avoid it.** The world map is open and non-linear — if your crew is banged up, run a scavenge event or a diplomatic errand instead of a battle. The [World Map Guide](/guides/world-map-guide) explains why the map is your recovery valve.

## Injury Severity and the Difficulty Settings

The [Difficulty Guide](/guides/difficulty-guide) covers the two difficulty axes — and the injury system is where **resource difficulty** bites hardest:

- **High resource difficulty** means fewer supplies found, more consumed, and wounds compounding faster — injuries become the main threat, not enemy damage.
- **Low resource difficulty** lets you heal freely — injuries become a background tax rather than a run-ender.
- **Combat difficulty** changes how often you *get* wounded: on high combat settings, an unambushed fight (see the [Stealth Guide](/guides/stealth-guide)) is an injury machine.

## Can Companions Die? Yes — and It's Permanent

The harshest rule of the Expeditions series, carried into Samurai:

- **Companions can die permanently.** A dead companion is a lost story, a lost quest chain, and a lost build — not a reload screen.
- **Death is not limited to the enemy's turn.** Rushing an ambush, fighting injured, or leaving a squishy character exposed can cost a life in any fight.
- **The series culture is "play with your mistakes."** Even if the launch build has no ironman mode, many veterans self-impose single-save runs — decide your permadeath stance *before* a companion falls.

### The Companion Death Prevention Protocol

1. **Never fight fair.** Every ambush opener is an injury (and death) prevention measure — the [Combat Guide](/guides/combat-guide) and [Stealth Guide](/guides/stealth-guide) exist for this reason.
2. **Keep a frontline alive.** Someone must absorb the first exchange; a party with no tank bleeds out over a long fight.
3. **Retreat early, not late.** If a fight is going badly, break contact — firecrackers, smoke (reported in previews, official list **TBD**), and repositioning let you live to fight another day. Retreating with your party intact is a victory.
4. **Rotate the wounded out.** A benched companion heals; a fielded one takes damage. The active-four system is also your medical rotation.
5. **Save at chapter boundaries.** If you're attached to a specific outcome, keep saves before big fights and big conversations — the series is famous for consequences that only surface hours later.

## The Act One Healing Cadence

A practical rhythm for the 8-hour act:

| When | What you do |
|------|-------------|
| **After every mission** | Tend all injuries, rest the party |
| **Before every major fight** | Check everyone's wound state; postpone if critical wounds are untreated |
| **Mid-mission** | Use medicine on serious wounds only — light wounds can wait |
| **After a companion's personal quest** | Full heal if possible — loyalty quests get harder as they progress |

## Injuries FAQ

**Do injuries affect dialogue checks?** Yes — injury penalties apply to a character's abilities, and dialogue draws on abilities. A wounded talker is a worse talker.

**Does resting cost supplies?** Expected to cost food and/or time — exact costs **TBD** at launch. Budget for rest in your supply planning.

**Can I heal mid-combat?** Some abilities, gadgets, or items may heal on the battlefield — the launch build's combat healing options are **TBD**.

**Do companions heal when benched?** Expected yes — rotation is the free heal (series tradition). Details **TBD** at launch.

**Do injuries carry into 1.0?** Save data carries to the full release, so treat every wound as if it does — heal before you finish Act One, not after.

## Injury Checklist

- [ ] Tend wounds after every mission without exception
- [ ] Budget medicine for serious wounds; rest for light ones
- [ ] Keep pillar specialists (Étienne, Antje) healthy — their checks matter
- [ ] Avoid hard fights with a wounded party — the map offers alternatives
- [ ] Decide your permadeath stance before someone falls
- [ ] Save at chapter boundaries and before big conversations

## Injury Mistakes

1. **"I'll heal after the next mission."** That's how permanent debuffs happen — the next mission is the one that pushes the wound over.
2. **Selling medicine for gold.** Medicine is the resource that keeps your builds working; gold buys what medicine can't.
3. **Fighting with a wounded pillar specialist.** A debuffed Étienne loses the negotiation you needed to avoid the fight that wounds everyone else.
4. **Ignoring the morale of loss.** A dead companion isn't just a stat sheet — their quest chain and story close forever.
