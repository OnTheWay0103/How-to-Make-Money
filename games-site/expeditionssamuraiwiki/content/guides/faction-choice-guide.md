# Expeditions: Samurai — Faction Guide (Which Side to Choose in Act One)

> Early Access Build (Act One) / Updated: August 7, 2026

It's 1600 AD. Japan is split into two armed camps over who will rule the nation — and your shipwrecked privateer crew has just walked into the middle of it. The game's central political decision is **whose banners you serve**, and the official framing is blunt: your choices "can alter the political landscape of Japan and alter the fate of powerful warlords." This guide covers the two sides, what each one offers in Act One, when the choice lands, and how to decide. Exact quest and dialogue details are being mapped on launch day — anything unconfirmed is marked **TBD**.

## The Two Sides: The Sekigahara Conflict

The [History Guide](/guides/history-guide) has the full historical context; here is the short version of the political map:

| | **Tokugawa Ieyasu — Eastern Army** | **Ishida Mitsunari — Western Army** |
|---|---|---|
| **Who** | The most powerful daimyo in Japan, positioned as the heir to Hideyoshi's unification | Hideyoshi's loyalist administrator, leading the coalition against Ieyasu |
| **The cause** | A new order under Ieyasu — pragmatic, powerful, and historically the winner | Loyalty to the late Hideyoshi's legacy and the anti-Tokugawa coalition |
| **The prize** | The shogunate — Ieyasu would rule Japan for 250+ years | Control of the regency and the succession |
| **In-game upside (expected)** | The **Hatamoto path** — village management is officially service to Ieyasu | The independent / underdog route — more room to write your own politics |

The real Battle of Sekigahara (October 21, 1600) was won by Ieyasu in a single day — in part because key Western generals **betrayed Mitsunari mid-battle**. Expect the game's alternate history to leave the outcome genuinely open: you are the variable that wasn't there historically.

## What Choosing a Side Does in Act One

Faction allegiance is a **carried decision** — it shapes dialogue routes, available missions, and reputation from the moment you commit:

- **Dialogue and quest routes change.** Faction seats open their quests to friends and close them to enemies — expect faction-specific missions, allies, and possibly companions to appear depending on your choice.
- **Reputation is a resource.** What the warlords think of you in Act One is your political starting position in the later acts. Insult a faction now and you'll meet that grudge later.
- **The Hatamoto thread.** Village management (arriving during Early Access development, tied to the Hatamoto title) is officially framed as **service to Tokugawa Ieyasu** — the Eastern Army is the direct road to the village-rule fantasy of the full release.
- **Endings.** Act One's ending is expected to hinge on where your loyalty stands when the act's climax lands (exact shape **TBD** at launch).

## When Does the Choice Land?

The exact trigger is **TBD** — no walkthrough of Act One exists yet beyond the confirmed opening beat. The expected shape, from the [Act One Walkthrough](/guides/walkthrough-act-one):

1. **First contact (early)** — your crew meets the local powers; early encounters signal each faction's disposition toward foreigners.
2. **The alliance talks (mid-act)** — the defining commitment: which court you serve publicly.
3. **The Sekigahara road (late act)** — expect negotiation attempts before battles, and treachery in both directions — history's mid-battle betrayal is a promise, not a spoiler.

Save at the alliance talks. This is a "choices carry for 60 hours" decision.

## How to Choose: A Decision Framework

There is no objectively correct side — but there are correct choices for your playstyle:

**Choose the Eastern Army (Tokugawa) if you want:**
- The **Hatamoto fantasy** — village management in the full release is the payoff for serving Ieyasu well.
- The **historically winning side** — the Tokugawa path aligns with where Japan is actually heading.
- **Pragmatic power** — the strongest faction offers the strongest backing, and the game's systems (loyalty, reputation) reward success.

**Choose the Western Army (Mitsunari) if you want:**
- The **underdog story** — supporting the side history abandoned is the classic CRPG route to a unique narrative.
- **Narrative independence** — the coalition is a looser alliance, which may mean more room for the player to define their own politics.
- **The alternate history** — your arrival is the historical variable; the Western path is where the game's divergence most clearly shows.

**Stay independent (if the option exists — TBD):** the game's framing ("most of the story can be carried by any pillar") suggests neutrality is viable, but a crew of shipwrecked foreigners with no patron faces a harder Act One. Treat neutrality as the expert option.

## Reputation Consequences in Later Acts

Whatever you pick in Act One, treat it as the beginning of a relationship, not the end of a choice:

- **Faction reputation carries into 1.0** — your Early Access save is your full-release save.
- **The village system will remember.** When village management ships, who you served determines whose land you rule and who your village starts allied with (see the [Village Management Guide](/guides/village-management-guide)).
- **Companions have opinions.** Your faction choice feeds the friendship/rivalry/romance tracks — some companions will judge you for your master.

## Faction Mistakes

1. **Choosing for "the winning side" without considering the Hatamoto tie-in.** If you want village rule in 1.0, the Eastern Army is the direct road — don't learn this at the end of Act One.
2. **Burning bridges carelessly.** A dismissed faction is a faction you'll meet again in Acts Two through Four.
3. **Ignoring companion reactions.** Your allegiance is a loyalty-system event — talk to your crew after you commit.
4. **Assuming the history is fixed.** Your presence is the variable — the Western Army may win in your timeline. That's the game.
