# How to Use Camping in Expeditions: Samurai — Charges, Injuries & Home Bases

> Early Access build 0.6.1 / Updated: October 23, 2026

Most players treat camp as a rest button they press when a mission is over. In **Expeditions: Samurai**, camp is closer to a pre-mission reset: it is where your tactical items come back, where injuries stop compounding, and where the people you travel with actually talk to you. Skip it for a few missions and you will feel it in the first round of the next fight.

This guide covers what camping does, the difference between a field camp and your home base, and a routine that keeps the crew ready.

## What Camping Actually Does

The official design framing is important: camping is a place you visit **because you want the benefits**, not a survival meter you have to feed. The developers explicitly compare it to *Expeditions: Rome*, where the legion camp was somewhere you went to gain things rather than somewhere you went to avoid penalties.

In practice, camping has three confirmed jobs:

1. **Replenish tactical item charges.** Smoke bombs, kunai, and similar tactical tools run on charges, and camp is where those charges are restored.
2. **Treat injuries.** Injured companions fight below their best; camp is where you treat the wounds that are reducing their effectiveness.
3. **Check in with your companions.** Camp is a conversation hub. The developers describe "oodles of dialogue" and a design goal of letting you get to know your crew gradually as they open up over time.

That third point is easy to underestimate: camp conversations drive loyalty, personal quests, and romance, not just flavor.

## Camp vs Home Base: What's the Difference?

You do not have to camp in the wilderness to get these benefits. Each act gives you a **home base**, and the developers confirm that you can do everything at the home base that you can do while camping.

| Act | Home base | Status |
|-----|-----------|--------|
| **Act 1** | **House of Whispers, Nagasaki** | Available in the current Early Access build |
| **Act 2** | A new home base in **Osaka** | Announced for Act 2, not part of the current build |
| **Act 3 onward** | **Your own village** | Announced for the full release, after you become hatamoto |

For current players, the **House of Whispers is the practical camp**. You can also make camp during world-map travel events — the "Red Blood and Black Bile" event, for example, lets you set up camp, talk to Rania, and heal injured characters.

- **Field camp** — a stop during travel or a scripted event, useful when you are away from Nagasaki and need charges or treatment.
- **Home base** — a fixed place you return to between missions, with the same recovery and conversation options plus a stable point in the world.

The home base also lets the story send you letters. Characters know where you live, and enemies and allies can send threats or compliments there. Update 1 made one exchange more flexible [Official]: you can give Captain van Renesse's letters to Johan **in camp or in the House of Whispers** during Act 1, rather than waiting until Act 3.

## Refilling Tactical Item Charges

This is the most mechanical reason to camp, and the one that changes how you open fights.

Smoke bombs and kunai are not consumables you buy in bulk at every vendor. They run on charges, and camping restores those charges. That makes camp the correct stop **before** any mission where you plan to lean on stealth tools rather than steel.

A practical rule:

- **Plan the approach first.** Decide whether the next mission is stealth, diplomacy, or steel.
- **Refill before you leave, not after you arrive.** If the plan needs smoke or kunai, camp first. Empty charges force a louder approach than you intended.
- **Do not hoard charges.** They reset at camp. A smoke bomb you never used is a tool you carried for nothing.

The same logic applies to injuries. If you are about to enter a mission with a wounded companion, camp is where you fix that — not mid-mission, and not after the next fight.

## Treating Injuries at Camp

The [Injuries & Healing Guide](/guides/injuries-healing-guide) covers the wound system in detail. The camping-specific point is simpler: **camp is the routine treatment window**.

Injuries reduce a companion's effectiveness, and an untreated injury is how a temporary problem becomes a permanent one. Camp gives you a dedicated moment to treat wounds before the next deployment, which is why the developers list injury treatment alongside charge refills as a core camp function.

The current build is also more forgiving about recovery supplies. The 0.6.1 patch notes confirm that additional salves, bandages, and revive kits were added to Act 1, mostly for players who did not take the first-aid chest on the ship. That makes the camp stop less punishing if you arrive underprepared, but it does not make camp optional.

A sensible order at camp:

1. Treat the most serious wounds first.
2. Check whether anyone is still fighting below their best.
3. Refill tactical charges.
4. Talk to companions before you leave.

Step 2 matters because the game does not always shout at you about a debuffed companion. The [Party Composition Guide](/guides/party-composition-guide) explains why a wounded specialist is worse than a wounded filler character.

## Companion Conversations and Camp Letters

Camp is where the game slows down and lets you talk. The developers describe a deliberate effort to make sure you chat with your companions often and get to know them as they open up over time.

What this buys you:

- **Relationship progress.** Loyalty, personal quests, and romance depend on spending time with people, not just using them in fights.
- **Context for decisions.** Conversations teach you what your crew wants before a faction choice forces the issue.
- **Letters.** The home base is a mailbox as well as a bedroom, and at least one letter hand-in (Johan's) now works in camp during Act 1.

If you are chasing a companion's story, talk to everyone at camp before you leave. The [Companions Guide](/guides/companions-guide) tracks recruitment and loyalty; camp is where much of that loyalty is earned.

## Act 3 Followers and Camp Upgrades

The same Dev Diary explains where camping is heading. Once you reach Act 3 and become **hatamoto**, you can invite people to join your fief, and most followers also show up in your camp. Village and camp upgrades are **not** unlocked by grinding resources: you unlock them by completing quests and inviting characters. The developers describe this as a narrative alternative to a sprawling upgrade UI — you talk to the person who provides the service.

Act 3 is not in the current Early Access build, so treat this as a roadmap for the full release. The [Village Management Guide](/guides/village-management-guide) covers the confirmed village system.

## When to Camp: A Practical Rhythm

| Moment | What to do |
|--------|------------|
| **Before a mission** | Refill charges, treat wounds, confirm your planned approach |
| **After a mission** | Treat new injuries before they compound |
| **After a companion quest** | Talk to the companion, then check the rest of the crew |
| **Before a faction decision** | Camp and exhaust companion dialogue — context is cheaper than a bad choice |
| **When the map offers a camp event** | Take it if anyone is wounded or low on charges |

The rhythm is deliberately boring. Camp, treat, refill, talk, leave. The players who struggle are usually the ones who treat camp as a victory screen and click through it.

## What Camping Does Not Do

Camping is not a survival simulator in this game. You are not managing hunger, thirst, or tent durability, and the developers explicitly moved away from the punishing survival layer that defined *Conquistador*.

It also does not:

- **Repair equipment for free.** Crafting and gear maintenance are separate systems; see the [Crafting Guide](/guides/crafting-guide).
- **Advance companion quests by itself.** Camp gives you the conversation; you still have to do the quest.
- **Replace medicine in an emergency.** If a companion is critically wounded mid-mission, camp is too late.

## Co-op and Camp

*Expeditions: Samurai* supports two-player drop-in/drop-out co-op, but the developers have not detailed how camp conversations and services are shared between the two captains. Treat any specific claim about split dialogue or shared charge refills as **[Unconfirmed]**. The safe habit is for both players to be at camp when you trigger conversations, so neither misses story content.

## Camping FAQ

**Do I have to camp in the wilderness?** No. Each act has a home base that provides the same benefits. In Act 1, that is the House of Whispers in Nagasaki.

**Does camping cost resources?** The developers describe camping as a benefit you visit, not a survival tax. Any specific supply cost for treatment is not spelled out in the current patch notes, so do not plan around a fixed price.

**Can I refill charges without camping?** Camp and the home base are the confirmed refill points. Do not assume vendors or fast travel will do it.

**Should I camp before every mission?** Before every mission where you plan to use tactical items or where someone is wounded, yes. For a short diplomatic errand with a healthy crew, it is optional.

## Camping Checklist

- [ ] Check every companion's injury state before leaving
- [ ] Refill smoke bombs, kunai, and other tactical charges
- [ ] Talk to every companion with new dialogue
- [ ] Hand in letters that are waiting at the home base
- [ ] Confirm the next mission's approach before spending charges
- [ ] Treat camp as a reset, not a victory screen

Camp is the cheapest power you have. It costs you a few minutes and returns charges, health, and story context — three things that are much more expensive to recover in the field.
