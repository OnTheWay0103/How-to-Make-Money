# Expeditions: Samurai — 12 Mistakes to Avoid (Early Access Edition)

> Early Access Build (Act One) / Updated: August 7, 2026

Expeditions: Samurai is a game that punishes careless captains — and Early Access adds a few pitfalls of its own. This guide collects the 12 mistakes that cost players the most in Act One, organized by system, with the fix for each. The [Beginner Guide](/guides/beginner-guide) covers the basics; this is the failure-mode manual.

## Combat & Stealth Mistakes

### 1. Fighting fair — skipping the setup phase

The single biggest swing in any fight is the **surprise attack opener**. A party that scouted, split, and positioned before triggering combat starts the fight with a free round against a broken enemy formation. Fights that start on your terms end faster and cost less. Fix: never trigger combat without a setup pass — the [Stealth Guide](/guides/stealth-guide) is the playbook.

### 2. Ignoring flanking, cover, and facing

The launch build's battlefield is a hex grid with cover, flanking, and attacks of opportunity. Standing in the open and attacking the front is fighting at maximum difficulty. Fix: place everyone in cover before the fight, and use the ambush to choose the enemy's facing — see the [Combat Mechanics Deep Dive](/guides/combat-mechanics-deep-dive).

### 3. Leaving enemy interrupters alive

In the simultaneous combat system, enemies that can cancel your actions will cancel your key abilities every single turn. Fix: kill interrupters first, ranged second, tanks last.

### 4. Spending everything on damage

A party with no frontline and no escape tools bleeds out over a long fight — and gets wounded doing it. Fix: keep someone who can absorb the first exchange, and carry firecrackers (smoke tools reported in previews, official list **TBD**) for breaking contact. Retreating with your party intact is a victory.

## Resource & Injury Mistakes

### 5. Ignoring injuries "until later"

An untreated injury compounds into a permanent debuff — and a permanent debuff is a ruined build. The "I'll heal after the next mission" mentality is how permanent penalties happen. Fix: tend wounds after every mission without exception — the [Injuries & Healing Guide](/guides/injuries-healing-guide) has the full cadence.

### 6. Selling medicine, materials, or ammunition for gold

Medicine keeps your builds working; materials feed the forge when crafting ships; ammunition is a gun's whole reason to exist. Selling them for gold buys what they can't. Fix: steal and negotiate before buying (diplomacy earns discounts, stealth earns loot) — never sell consumables you'll need the next mission.

### 7. Running into battles with empty stores

Expeditions games are resource games wearing RPG clothes. A supply-strained crew fails the first checks of any mission — and can't heal after it. Fix: run world map events when stores are low; the map is your supply engine (see the [World Map Guide](/guides/world-map-guide)).

## Companion Mistakes

### 8. Benching companions with active quests

Personal quest chains are often time-sensitive to story chapters. A companion who never fights misses loyalty triggers, quest progress, and story. Fix: rotate the active four so every chain stays alive — the [Party Composition Guide](/guides/party-composition-guide) covers the rotation.

### 9. Letting companions die

A dead companion is a lost story, a lost quest chain, and a lost build — permanent. Fix: never fight fair, retreat early, rotate the wounded out, and save at chapter boundaries. Decide your permadeath stance *before* someone falls.

## Progression & Build Mistakes

### 10. Spreading skill points across all three pillars

Jack-of-all-trades builds underperform in every Expeditions game. Fix: commit to one pillar first (weapon skills over utility), then branch — the [Progression Guide](/guides/progression-skills-guide) has the spending order.

### 11. Banking skill points and resources "for later"

Early Access is Act One only. A build that comes online at level 40 is wasted until 1.0 — and village management and crafting aren't in the launch build at all. Fix: build for the content that exists, spend what you have, and enjoy the tuning patches.

## Early Access-Specific Mistakes

### 12. Expecting the full game on day one

Act One is **five main quests and three side quests** — roughly 6–8 hours. Village management, crafting, Act Two, and the rest arrive via the planned updates and the Q1 2027 full release. The launch build is also rough in places — previewers reported crashes, missing voice lines, and camera jank. Fix: **save often**, report bugs to the developers (they personally read the Steam forums and Discord), and treat every day-one "meta" claim as provisional. The [Early Access Guide](/guides/early-access-guide) lays out exactly what's in and what's coming.

## The Day-One Golden Rules

1. **Save often.** Early Access builds crash — a save before every major encounter is cheap insurance.
2. **Scout everything.** The setup phase is where fights are won.
3. **Heal everything.** Injuries are the game's slowest, most expensive mistake.
4. **Talk to everyone.** Companions, factions, and vendors — dialogue is where half the game lives.
5. **Have fun with the sandbox.** Your save carries to 1.0, so experiment freely — nothing you do in Act One is wasted.
