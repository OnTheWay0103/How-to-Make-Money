# Expeditions: Samurai — Combat Guide

> Early Access Build (Act One) / Updated: August 1, 2026

Combat in Expeditions: Samurai is the series' signature system evolved: **turn-based, but fast and freeing** — "movement, attacks, and interruptions can all play out simultaneously," and the developers describe skirmishes as "cerebral," driven by tactical understanding rather than luck. This guide covers what's officially confirmed about the system and the tactics that will carry you through Act One.

## How the Combat System Works

The official description is specific about what makes this combat different:

- **Simultaneous movement and attacks** — No more "sluggish, traditional turn-based battles." Actions play out at the same time, which means positioning and timing matter in ways they don't in classic turn-based systems.
- **Interruptions** — Actions can be interrupted mid-execution. A duelist with interrupt abilities can break an enemy's attack before it lands.
- **Cerebral, not luck-based** — The designers emphasize understanding and planning over dice rolls.
- **Dynamic combat initiation** — How you enter combat matters. A surprise attack from stealth gives you a devastating opener.

## The Golden Rule: Combat Starts Before Combat Starts

The single most important tactical principle in Expeditions: Samurai — from the same design quote that defines stealth — is that **your setup determines your victory**. Every fight worth fighting deserves:

1. **A scouting pass.** Know enemy positions, numbers, and patrol routes before you commit.
2. **A positioning phase.** Split the party and place fighters before triggering the fight.
3. **A surprise opener when possible.** Open with an ambush, a flintlock volley, or a takedown — every enemy you remove before the first turn is a free win.

Fights that start on your terms end faster and cost less than fights that start on theirs.

## Core Combat Tactics

### Focus Fire Priority Targets

Not every enemy needs the same attention. In fast simultaneous combat:

- **Kill the interrupters first.** Any enemy that can cancel your actions must die before they act.
- **Then the ranged.** Archers and flintlock users punish you from range — close or remove them.
- **Tanks last.** Armored enemies are a resource drain; isolate them after the dangerous targets fall.

### Use Weapon Reach and Positioning

- **Naginatas and longbows** control space — hold chokepoints and keep enemies off your squishy characters.
- **Elevation and flanking** matter. The system rewards positional play; set up flanks during the stealth phase.
- **The East-meets-West arsenal** is matchup-dependent: "leverage weapon skills to tackle diverse enemy types." Carry two weapon families and switch when one underperforms.

### Manage Interruptions Defensively

Because actions can be interrupted, your sequencing is a decision:

- Don't commit your highest-value action first if the enemy can interrupt it.
- Use fast attacks to bait interrupts, then land the slow, powerful action.
- Keep a character on "guard" duty in dangerous fights to punish enemy attackers.

### Know When to Disengage

Not every fight must be won. Expeditions: Samurai is a resource game — if a fight is going badly, firecracker distractions, cover, and repositioning let you break contact and live to fight another day (smoke-type escape tools are TBD until the launch build's gadget roster is revealed). Retreating with your party intact is a victory.

## Combat in Co-op

Two-player co-op adds simultaneous coordination to the battlefield:

- **Real-time combined abilities** — official materials describe coordinating combat maneuvers in real time, "combining abilities for devastating combos."
- **Split control** — each captain controls half the party, letting you set up double-sided pincers in a single turn.
- **Synchronized openers** — one player's firecracker pulls enemies into the other player's ambush.

## The Combat Checklist

- [ ] Scout before engaging
- [ ] Position the party before triggering the fight
- [ ] Identify interrupters and ranged enemies as kill priorities
- [ ] Sequence actions to bait and punish interruptions
- [ ] Carry two weapon families for matchup flexibility
- [ ] Know your retreat route before you need it

## Common Combat Mistakes

1. **Rushing the opener.** A rushed trigger wastes the entire stealth positioning phase.
2. **Ignoring interrupters.** They will cancel your key abilities every single turn.
3. **All-damage, no defense.** A party with no tank or no escape tools bleeds out over a long fight.
4. **Fighting fair.** If you're fighting fair, you skipped the setup phase — go back and ambush.
