# Expeditions: Samurai — Village Management Guide

> Early Access Build (Act One) / Updated: August 1, 2026

Every samurai story ends with a domain. Expeditions: Samurai's version: rise to the rank of **Hatamoto** — a samurai lord in service to Tokugawa Ieyasu — and rule **a village of your own** in Japan. This guide covers everything officially confirmed about village management, how it connects to the story, and what the Early Access roadmap means for the feature.

## What Is Confirmed

From official materials across the announcement and previews:

- **Village management is real and central.** The full release (1.0, early 2027) explicitly includes "control over a village of the player's own in Japan."
- **It's tied to the Hatamoto title.** Village management becomes available after you acquire the rank of Hatamoto — a story progression, not a menu feature.
- **It's NOT in the Early Access launch.** The developers confirmed village management "will be added during development" — i.e., during the Early Access period rather than on day one. It arrives via the planned content updates.
- **It's a power-base system.** The team describes it as helping "shape a local power base" — expect it to interact with the game's faction politics (Tokugawa vs. the Western Army heading into Sekigahara).

## Why Hatamoto Matters Historically

Hatamoto — literally "under the banners" — were the direct vassals of the shogun, the highest rank of samurai who could be granted their own fiefs. In the game's timeline (1600 AD, weeks before Sekigahara), becoming a Hatamoto to Tokugawa Ieyasu means:

- **A stake in the Sekigahara outcome.** Your village's fate is tied to the side you serve.
- **Legitimacy for a foreigner.** In an alternate history where you beat William Adams to Japan, the Hatamoto title is your path from shipwrecked privateer to Japanese lord.
- **A long-term goal.** Village rule is framed as the payoff of Act One's choices, expanding in the later acts.

## What Village Management Will Probably Include

The feature isn't playable yet, so this section is based on the official framing ("shape a local power base," "village to rule over") plus the series' design DNA:

- **Buildings and infrastructure** — expect the classic roster: barracks, workshops, storehouses, farms.
- **Recruitment and garrison** — soldiers and workers to defend and grow the village.
- **Economy** — food, tax, and trade systems feeding your expedition supplies.
- **Faction interactions** — villages sit inside the civil war; who you host, fortify, or tax has political consequences.
- **Companion integration** — villages are a natural home for companion story beats and personal quests.

Treat everything in this section as informed speculation until the feature ships — the wiki will be updated with the real mechanics the moment they land.

## How to Prepare in Act One

Even though the village isn't playable at launch, your Act One choices shape it:

1. **Earn the Hatamoto title.** Village rule is gated behind it. Serve Ieyasu's cause, win loyalty, and take the rank when it's offered.
2. **Bank resources.** Supplies, materials, and gold accumulated in Act One will fund your village's start — Expeditions games carry resources across acts.
3. **Keep companions loyal.** A village is only as strong as the people who run it with you. Loyalty quests now pay off later.
4. **Build your reputation.** Diplomacy and faction choices in Act One determine who your village starts allied with — or against.

## Village Management FAQ

**When does village management release?** During Early Access development — not at launch. Full details in 1.0 (early 2027).

**Is it required for the main story?** It's tied to the Hatamoto rank, which is a story goal, but the depth of village systems is expected to be optional — you can engage with it as much or as little as you like.

**Does it work in co-op?** Co-op is full-campaign, so expect shared village control — specifics will be confirmed when the feature ships.

**What if I side against Tokugawa?** The alternate-history framing allows for different faction outcomes; how village rule interacts with non-Tokugawa paths will be a major 1.0 question.
