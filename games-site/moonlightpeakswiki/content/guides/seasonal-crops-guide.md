# Moonlight Peaks — Seasonal Crop Guide: Best Crops & Seeds by Season

> Launch Version / Updated: August 1, 2026

Moonlight Peaks runs on a real calendar: four seasons (Spring, Summer, Autumn, Winter), each with its own seed catalog at **Luna's Seed Stall in Moonlit Pines** — and one hard rule that catches every Stardew veteran out: **crops wither when the season changes**. An out-of-season crop planted in the last week of Spring dies the night Summer begins, so every planting decision is a calendar decision.

There are **53 seeds** in the game in total, and Luna's stock rotates with the season — check back each new season for what just came in. This guide ranks the best crops per season, explains the multi-harvest and processing strategies, and flags the magic crops worth your mana. Prices and growth times below are community-compiled from launch-window guides; treat anything unmarked as player-verified rather than official.

## The Two Rules That Shape Everything

**Rule 1 — Everything grows on night ticks.** You're awake 6 p.m. to 6 a.m.; the day passes instantly in your coffin. A crop planted tonight is ready in a fixed number of *nights*, and it doesn't care whether you watered it before or after midnight — only that you watered it.

**Rule 2 — Crops die at the season change.** When the new season begins, any crop out of season withers. In practice this means: never plant a slow crop late in its season unless it regrows fast enough to pay for itself, and count the nights carefully in the last week of a season.

## Spring Crops (Year 1 Start)

Spring is your tutorial season, and it starts with **Orlock's Wine Scheme** — he hands you Blood Grape seeds and a shovel on Spring 1.

**All-season basics:** **Onion** (10 Coins seed, 40 Coins sell, 4 nights), **Blood Tomato** (20/15), and **Wily Wheat** (30/60) grow in any season — useful for cooking and filler income between seasonal rotations.

**Pro tip:** don't be fooled by the low grape sell price. Raw Blood Grapes go for 5 Coins — they exist to be processed into wine (360 Coins per bottle). In Spring, the play is: a couple of Carrots for cash, then pivoting your field to grapes and strawberries for the multi-harvest engine.

## Winter Crops

Winter is the thinnest season — most of the catalog goes dormant, and the plants that survive are the multi-harvest holdovers:

- **Blueberry** (Spring *and* Winter — 9 nights, regrows every 3)
- **Cranberry** (Autumn *and* Winter — multi-harvest)

**Pro tip:** don't leave the farm dead in Winter. A blueberry/cranberry rotation keeps the Keg and Jam Maker fed, and winter is the classic season for focusing on the mine, fishing, Nokturna, and the potion chain instead of field expansion.

## The Multi-Harvest Strategy

The single best long-term farming decision is building your field around **multi-harvest crops** — plants that keep producing after their initial growth:

| Crop | Initial | Regrow | Seasons |
|------|---------|--------|---------|
| Blood Grape | 5 nights | every 4 nights | Spring–Summer |
| White Grape | 5 nights | every 4 nights | Spring–Summer |
| Raspberry | 7 nights | every 2 nights | Spring–Summer |
| Strawberry | 7 nights | every 3 nights | Spring–Summer |
| Blueberry | 9 nights | every 3 nights | Spring + Winter |
| Cranberry | multi-harvest | — | Autumn–Winter |

You pay for the seed once and harvest all season. Grapes pair with the **Keg** (crafted from 10 Wood) to become the game's best early processing chain; raspberries and strawberries go straight to the stall or the Jam Maker.

## Processing Beats Selling Raw

The golden rule of Moonlight Peaks farming: **raw produce is the worst way to monetize a crop.**

| Product | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Raw Blood Grapes | 5 Coins |
| **Blood Grape Wine** (Keg) | **360 Coins** (390 for one-star) |
| Raw White Grapes | 10 Coins |
| **White Grape Wine** (Keg) | **460 Coins** (490 for one-star) |

Kegs cost only **10 Wood** — build several. One-star quality (from fertilizer and good care) adds roughly 8–10% to sell prices, so a well-kept farm compounds: better crops → better wine → more coins → faster upgrades.

## Magic Crops: The Mana Trade

Some seeds are **magic crops**, and they don't take water — they take the **Aquaflux Spell**, which costs **mana**. Your mana pool is small (reported max 3), so magic plots should stay small:

| Magic crop | Sell value |
|------------|------------|
| **Nightshade** | 180 Coins |
| **Skunktail** | 180 Coins |
| **Gobbler** | 550 Coins |

**Pro tip:** Gobbler at 550 Coins is the highest-value single crop documented so far — but you can't scale it until your mana pool grows. In the mid-game, run a magic patch of 3–6 tiles alongside your regular field, and spend the rest of your mana on shapeshifting and spells.

## The Potion Pipeline

A few crops exist entirely to feed the cauldron:

- **Suffrain** (Autumn, 7 days) → **Suffrain Powder** → Love Potion
- **Hold-Me-Close** — a rare magical plant that matures in **13 days** → Love Potion
- **Amour** and **Muse Nut** — foraged/grown ingredients in the same recipe

One Love Potion sells for **2200 Coins** — the same coins as ~7 Carrots or ~12 Eggplants, for a handful of ingredients. Once the cauldron is online (see the Potions Guide), a small dedicated plot of potion crops is worth more than any direct-sale field.

## Seasonal Crop FAQ

**What's the best crop in each season?**
Carrot (Spring), Skunktail (Summer), Pumpkin (Autumn) — the three best direct-profit crops, no processing required. For the long game, grapes and strawberries beat all three through the Keg.

**Do crops die when the season changes?**
Yes. Any out-of-season crop withers when the new season begins — replace your fields before the calendar flips.

**Where do I buy seeds?**
**Luna's Seed Stall in Moonlit Pines** — stock rotates by season, and the full game has 53 seeds in total.

**Why does my raw grape only sell for 5 Coins?**
That's by design — grapes are a processing crop. Put them in a Keg (10 Wood) and the wine sells for 360 Coins.

**What are magic crops?**
Seeds watered with the **Aquaflux Spell** instead of a watering can — Nightshade, Skunktail, and Gobbler — with sell values up to 550 Coins. They cost mana, so keep magic plots small.
