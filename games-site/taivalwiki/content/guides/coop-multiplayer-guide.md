# Co-op Multiplayer Guide: Tips for 2-4 Player Groups

> Early Access Version / Updated: August 1, 2026

Taival is built from the ground up for cooperative play. Developed by Bonobo Software as a game designed for friends to share on a couch or across the internet, it supports up to 4 players with a unique hybrid system that lets you mix local split-screen and online players in the same session. This guide covers co-op modes, the group voting system, party composition, Henki form coordination, and strategies for every group size.

**Accuracy note (August 2026):** the core features here — online co-op, local split-screen, mixed local + online sessions, Remote Play Together, per-player characters/companions/skill trees, and group dialogue voting — are confirmed by official materials. Smaller details (vote anonymity, tie-break rules, host-disconnect behavior, split-screen layouts) are community reports and unconfirmed (TBD).

## Setting Up Co-op

Taival offers several ways to play with others. Choose the setup that works best for your group.

### Online Co-op
The standard multiplayer mode via Steam. One player hosts the session, and up to 3 friends join. The host's world state determines quest progress, but all players keep their individual character progression, Henki forms, and inventory.

**How to start:** From the main menu, select Play > Online. Choose "Host Session" or "Join Session" via the Steam friends list or invite code.

**Host considerations:** The host needs a stable internet connection. If the host disconnects, the session ends. Guest players keep any items and XP they earned during the session.

### Local Split-Screen
Up to 4 players can play on the same PC using split-screen. Each player needs their own controller. The screen divides into sections — 2-player horizontal split, 3 and 4-player quadrant split.

**Performance note:** Split-screen renders the world multiple times, which is more demanding. The recommended RTX 3060 Ti handles 4-player split-screen comfortably. If you experience frame drops, lower the graphics settings or reduce the number of local players.

### Mixed Mode (Local + Online)
This is where Taival shines. You can combine local split-screen players with remote online players in a single session. For example:

- 2 players on one PC (couch co-op) + 2 players joining online = 4-player party
- 1 player hosting + 1 local split-screen friend + 2 online friends

This is the most flexible co-op system in the game and works seamlessly once configured.

### Remote Play Together
Steam's Remote Play Together is fully supported. The host streams their screen to friends, and each friend connects a virtual controller. Only the host needs to own Taival. This is a great option for groups where not everyone has purchased the game yet.

**Limitation:** Remote Play Together works best with a strong upstream connection from the host. Latency may affect performance for players far from the host's region.

## Group Voting System Explained

One of Taival's standout co-op features is the Group Quest Dialogue system. When the party encounters a story decision, every player gets a say:

1. A dialogue wheel appears on every player's screen
2. Each player moves their cursor to highlight their preferred option
3. The game tallies the votes after a brief deliberation period
4. The majority choice determines how the story branches

**Key details about the voting system:**

- Votes are anonymous by default (other players see your cursor, not your locked-in choice)
- The deliberation timer can be adjusted in the settings menu
- In the event of a tie, the host's vote breaks the deadlock
- Votes happen in real-time — you cannot skip or automate your vote

### Voting Strategies

**Discuss before voting.** Use voice chat to talk through each decision before the timer runs out. Some choices have permanent consequences for quest availability and rewards.

**Designate a tiebreaker.** Agree on who makes the final call before the session starts. This prevents awkward stalls when the party splits 2-2.

**Embrace different outcomes.** Different voting paths unlock different quest lines, rewards, and story branches. This gives the game high replay value — subsequent playthroughs can explore the choices your group passed over.

**Respect the roleplayers.** Some players enjoy making in-character decisions. If your group is leaning into the narrative, let the votes fall where they may rather than optimizing for rewards.

## Party Composition by Group Size

### 2-Player Party
With two players, each person needs to cover multiple roles. A balanced duo looks like this:

- **Player 1:** Hybrid Tank/DPS with a Tank Beast Henki form. Focus on engaging enemies and holding aggro. Carry a Swift Mount for travel.
- **Player 2:** Hybrid Support/DPS with a Base Healer or Stealth Predator Henki form. Handle healing, resource gathering, and flanking. Also carry a Swift Mount.

**Tip:** Both players should invest in the Swift Mount early. Having only one mount slows the party down and makes exploration tedious.

### 3-Player Party
Three players allows for a classic tank-dps-support composition with some flex room:

- **Tank:** Full aggro management with Tank Beast. Focus on defensive skills.
- **DPS:** Stealth Predator or Purple Clone Variant for maximum damage output.
- **Support/Flex:** Base Healer for healing and revival, plus Pack Mule or Golden Variant for utility.

**Tip:** The support player should handle most of the crafting and resource management while the other two focus on combat. This keeps the party well-supplied without sacrificing combat strength.

### 4-Player Party
The ideal composition for tackling the hardest content Taival offers:

| Player | Role | Henki Form | Primary Responsibility |
|--------|------|------------|----------------------|
| 1 | Tank | Tank Beast | Engage enemies, hold aggro, absorb damage |
| 2 | DPS | Stealth Predator | Burst damage, flanking, priority kills |
| 3 | Support | Base Healer | Healing, revival, status cleansing, buffs |
| 4 | Flex | Depends on content | Pack Mule (farming), Purple Clone (bosses), Golden (loot), or Glider (exploration) |

**Tip:** The flex player should have multiple forms ready and swap based on what the group is doing at any given moment. This is the most demanding role in terms of Henki management.

## Coordinating Henki Forms Across Players

Effective co-op play requires more than just good individual form choices — your forms need to complement each other.

### Combat Coordination
- Tank engages first with Tank Beast, using taunt to draw all enemy attention
- DPS waits 2-3 seconds for aggro to lock, then strikes from behind with Stealth Predator
- Support stays at medium range with Base Healer, topping off health and watching for status effects
- Flex adapts — bringing in Purple Clone for burst damage phases or switching to a defensive form if the Tank is overwhelmed

### Exploration Coordination
- Assign one player as Scout (Glider/Bird Form) to survey the area from above
- Assign one player as Runner (Swift Mount) to cover ground and check points of interest
- Assign one player as Gatherer (Pack Mule) to collect everything the party finds
- The remaining player acts as Navigator (any form), keeping the party on the quest path

### Form Synergy Table

| Party Composition | Strength | Weakness |
|------------------|----------|----------|
| All Tank Beasts | Nearly unkillable | Low damage, slow clear speed |
| All Stealth Predators | Insane burst damage | Fragile, no sustain |
| All Base Healers | Infinite sustain | No damage or speed |
| Balanced (Tank + DPS + Healer + Flex) | Handles all content | Requires coordination |
| Double Tank + Double DPS | Excellent for boss fights | Poor exploration speed |

## The Henki Dungeon in Co-op

Henki Dungeons are special solo challenges where you control your Henki companion directly while your character stays outside. In co-op, each party member can attempt their own Henki Dungeon simultaneously, or the group can watch a single player's attempt and offer advice.

**Co-op Henki Dungeon tips:**
- Party members can spectate your run and call out enemy patterns
- Everyone in the party receives rewards, not just the player who clears the dungeon
- Coordinate dungeon attempts so multiple people tackle different dungeons for maximum reward collection

## 4-Player Advanced Strategies

### World Boss Hunting
When hunting world bosses as a full party:
- Tank positions the boss facing away from the group
- DPS attacks from behind for bonus backstab damage
- Support watches the entire party's health and cleanses debuffs
- Flex marks adds (minor enemies) and keeps them off the Support

### Speed Farming Route
For efficient material runs:
- Everyone starts on Swift Mounts
- Each player takes a different quadrant of the biome
- Gatherers call out high-value resource nodes on voice chat
- Regroup at a central point, swap to Pack Mule or Golden Variant, and clear the area

### Quest Efficiency
- Split up for parallel side quests when objectives are in different areas
- Reunite for main story decisions and boss encounters
- Use the Group Voting system to decide which quests to tackle first

## Common Co-op Issues

| Issue | Solution |
|-------|----------|
| Split-screen performance drops | Lower texture quality and shadow resolution; reduce local player count |
| Desync in mixed mode | Ensure host has stable upload speed; restart the session if desync persists |
| Voting deadlock | Pre-designate a session leader as tiebreaker |
| Guest progress not saving | Guest characters keep inventory and XP across sessions, but world state is host-only |
| One player keeps falling behind | Assign that player to the support role; they can focus on gathering while others scout ahead |
