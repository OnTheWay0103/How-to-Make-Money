# Skills & Raids Loot & Farming Guide — Items, Rarities, and Extraction Value

**Updated: August 11, 2026**

Loot is the entire economy of Skills & Raids. Enemies drop valuable resources, some enemies drop their own skills, and everything you extract feeds the crafting system and quest progression. This guide covers what is confirmed about loot — rarity tiers, drop sources, and what's worth an inventory slot — and how to farm it effectively: running combat areas you can reliably clear, extracting the resources and skill drops, and converting them into gear, skills, and quest completions.

---

## 1. Confirmed Rarity Tiers

The Steam page confirms **five rarity tiers** shared by gear and skills:

| Tier | Color | Notes |
|------|-------|-------|
| Common | White | The baseline; most early drops |
| Uncommon | Green | The early-mid game workhorse |
| Rare | Blue | Strong mid-game tier |
| Epic | Purple | High tier — build around it |
| Legendary | Orange/Gold | The best tier |

**Unconfirmed:** exact drop rates per tier, whether specific enemies/areas bias toward certain rarities, and any stat multipliers between tiers. Beware of "community drop rate" tables online — none are verified.

---

## 2. What Actually Drops (Confirmed Sources)

From the Steam page and demo coverage:

| Drop Type | Source | What It's For |
|-----------|--------|---------------|
| **Valuable resources** | Enemies (all combat areas) | Crafting equipment and skills; completing quests |
| **Skills** | "Enemies may also drop their own skills" | Build pieces — combine into synergies |
| **Quest unlocks** | Completing quests | New equipment and skills |

The important distinction for looting: **resources are consumable inputs, skills are permanent additions to your collection.** A dropped skill is almost always worth more than raw materials — it expands every future build.

---

## 3. Gear: What We Know

- Gear exists with the five rarity tiers (confirmed).
- Gear is **crafted from extracted resources** (confirmed).
- Exact gear slots, weapon types, and stat systems are **unconfirmed** — the Steam page doesn't name weapon types, armor slots, or stats, and no verified source documents them yet. See the [Gear Guide](/guides/gear-upgrade-guide) for the full gear progression picture.

**Practical loot guidance for gear:**
1. Keep the highest-rarity gear you find that fits a hero's role (defense for Shelldon, damage for Clawrk, healing for Owlivia).
2. Effect utility beats rarity color — a Common item that fits the build beats a Rare that doesn't.
3. Craft between runs rather than hoarding raw materials — resources only become value once processed.

---

## 4. The Extraction Value Ladder

Inventory space is limited (exact capacity unconfirmed), and anything not in the safe pocket is lost on a failed run. Rank your loot by this ladder:

| Priority | What to Keep | Why |
|----------|--------------|-----|
| 1 | Skill drops | Permanent build pieces — the rarest value in the game |
| 2 | Quest-required resources | Quest completion is your progression |
| 3 | High-rarity gear | Direct power |
| 4 | Rare crafting materials | Best crafting inputs |
| 5 | Common materials | Farmable anywhere — first to drop if bags get full |

---

## 5. Bag and Pocket Management

- **Bank early.** Anything not in the safe pocket is lost on a failed run — so get high-value loot into the safe pocket as soon as you can.
- **Send items back when full.** The game offers a way to send items back when bags fill up (confirmed in demo coverage) — use it, don't carry risk.
- **Drop Common first.** When forced to choose, Common materials are the most replaceable.
- **Plan around quests.** If the quest needs a specific resource, that resource is your mission — grab it, secure it, extract.

## 6. Farming: The Loop and Routes

Farming in Skills & Raids means running combat areas you can reliably clear. There are no respawn timers, no contested spawns, and no other squads to fight over loot — the game is single-player, so farming is purely a matter of route efficiency and risk management.

### What You're Farming

| Farm Target | Source | Use |
|-------------|--------|-----|
| **Resources** | Enemy drops (confirmed) | Crafting gear and skills; quests |
| **Skills** | Enemy skill drops (confirmed) | Permanent build pieces — 30 skills in the full game |
| **Quest progress** | Completing quest objectives | Unlock equipment and skills |

### The Farming Loop

```
Pick a safe area → clear enemies → bank loot in the safe pocket
→ extract → craft / complete quests → repeat, slightly deeper
```

**The core principle:** farm what you can clear *reliably*. In an extraction game, a reliable extraction rate at modest value beats a risky one at high value — failed runs lose everything not in the safe pocket.

### Safe Farming Route Design

1. **Start with the area you know.** The demo's two biomes and the early quests establish the easy content — farm there until clears are consistent.
2. **Push one step at a time.** When an area becomes easy, try the next combat area as a *study run* — no valuable loot, learn the skill mechanic.
3. **Learn each area's unique skill mechanic.** Combat areas have unique skill mechanics (confirmed) — the first clear of a new area is where you learn its twist.
4. **Extract at the comfortable point.** When bags hold good value, extract — don't gamble on one more room.

### Skill Farming: The Highest-Value Farm

"Enemies may also drop their own skills" is the game's signature loop, and skills are the highest-value farm target:

- **Every enemy type is a potential source.** Beat new enemies → new skills.
- **Skill drops beat resource drops** in inventory priority — permanent value vs. consumable inputs.
- **Deeper areas presumably drop better skills** (inference — unconfirmed).
- **Collection is the goal:** 30 skills in the full game means a substantial collection to build from.

### Farming Checklist (Per Run)

1. Check active quests — build the run around them.
2. Equip a loadout suited to the area (see [Builds Guide](/guides/builds-guide)).
3. Bank early — safe pocket first, then push.
4. Grab every skill drop; prioritize skill drops over materials in the bags.
5. Send items back when bags get full (confirmed mechanic).
6. Extract the moment the quest target is done or bags are full.

### What Is NOT Confirmed (Farming)

- Respawn timers, boss spawns, or contested loot — no such systems are documented; the game is single-player.
- Drop rates per enemy or area.
- Whether specific enemies drop specific skills.
- Named bosses with farmable loot tables.

Treat farming guides that name specific bosses and drop rates as unreliable — they're describing a game that doesn't exist.

---

## 7. A Warning About Fake Loot Data

Because the game is new and small, some sites publish "complete loot tables" with specific enemy names, drop percentages, and item names (e.g., "Bandit's Axe from the Bandit Leader"). **None of these are verified.** The real enemy roster, item names, and drop tables are not publicly documented. Judge loot guides by whether they stick to confirmed facts; if a guide names specific enemies or items you can't match in-game, treat it as unreliable.

---

## 8. Loot Economy Tips

- **Convert resources into gear and skills between runs.** Raw materials have no use until extracted and processed — crafting is confirmed as the sink.
- **Revisit completed areas.** Combat areas have unique skill mechanics; farming known-safe areas for skill drops is the confirmed-safe way to grow your collection.
- **Don't spend all resources.** Keep a reserve for quests that demand resource collection.
- **Extract with a goal.** Decide before the run what value you're after, and extract the moment you have it.
