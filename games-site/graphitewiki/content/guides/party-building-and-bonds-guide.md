# How Party Building and Bonds Work in Graphite

Graphite is a tactical roguelike built around a party, not a solo hero. The official store page says it plainly: **"Build your party, navigate a dynamic timeline, and shape your fate through notebook scenarios that test your team."** The Scribe entrusts seven heroes to grow stronger and **gather allies**, and each run asks you to turn the pieces you find into a team that can survive the next act.

That leaves two questions the store page does not answer: how many heroes actually fight together, and what the bond system does. This guide covers what is confirmed, what is community-documented, and what is still unknown — without inventing a party-size number.

**Accuracy note (October 2026):** party building, recruiting allies, party-responsive events, seven heroes and the friendship/bond system are confirmed by official or consistent community sources. The maximum party size, recruitment thresholds, bond-point sources and bond-level rewards are **not** officially documented and are marked **[Unconfirmed]**.

---

## The Confirmed Party Structure

Graphite is a run-based party builder. The confirmed pieces:

- You start a run with a **starting hero** and recruit more heroes as the run progresses.
- The full game has **seven heroes**, each with a distinct personality, backstory and playstyle.
- The free **Act 1 demo** includes **four** playable heroes.
- Your starting hero **changes the story** — different heroes experience different events.
- Party composition influences the **tarot-inspired adventure deck**, so who you bring changes which events and choices you see.
- The campaign spans **four acts of fifteen in-game days each**.

What that means in practice: the roster is not a fixed squad you lock in at the start. It is a pool you draw from, and the run's story reacts to who is travelling with you.

A crucial non-confirmation: **the maximum party size is not stated anywhere official.** [Unconfirmed] Guides that claim "three heroes," "four slots" or "all seven fight together" are guessing. If you need to plan around a number, plan around the fact that the game is designed for party composition to matter, not around a cap you cannot verify.

## Recruiting Allies

Recruitment is confirmed as a core loop, but the mechanism is only partly documented.

Confirmed:

- The Scribe's mission explicitly includes **gathering allies**.
- Heroes are **recruited during runs** — the GamerScout overview refers to "your recruited heroes" and notes that relationships develop "at the campfire."
- The demo contains four heroes, so at least three additional heroes join in the full campaign.

Not confirmed:

- Whether recruitment is story-gated, friendship-gated, or both. [Unconfirmed]
- Whether every hero can join in a single run. [Unconfirmed]
- Whether recruited heroes carry between runs or reset with the run. [Unconfirmed]
- The exact acts in which each hero becomes available. [Unconfirmed]

The practical takeaway is to treat recruitment as content, not a checklist. If a hero becomes available, bring them into the party for at least a few fights and events — the game's story is written so that each hero has their own arc, and the only way to see those arcs is to travel with them.

## The Bond System

The bond system is the social layer that sits on top of party composition. The clearest community description comes from GamerScout: Graphite has a **bond system that deepens relationships between your recruited heroes** and **unlocks story content tied to their individual backstories**. Relationships develop **at the campfire**.

Confirmed by official material: friendship between characters exists — the demo listing confirms **friendship levels up to 1** in the demo build.

Not confirmed: how bond points are earned. [Unconfirmed] The reasonable candidates are fighting together, campfire scenes and event choices, but none of those is officially stated. Guides that give exact thresholds — "Friendship 15 unlocks the Monk" — are fabricating numbers.

What the system is clearly designed to do:

1. **Reward using the same heroes together.** A bond between two characters implies shared time, and the campfire is the documented place where those relationships develop.
2. **Gate backstory, not just power.** The documented payoff is story content tied to individual backstories, which fits Graphite's structure of seven heroes with seven arcs.
3. **Make party choice a narrative choice.** If bonds unlock scenes, then who you bring is also which stories you see.

The demo's cap of friendship level 1 is a demo limitation, not a statement about the full game's cap. [Unconfirmed for the full-game maximum]

## Why Party Composition Matters

Composition matters in three separate systems, which is why "bring the strongest hero" is the wrong approach.

### 1. Combat Roles

The confirmed roster gives two clear anchors:

| Hero | Role | Why they anchor a party |
|------|------|--------------------------|
| The Knight | Frontline / Break | Durable shield kit built around depleting Poise and cancelling enemy actions |
| The Mage | Damage / support | Offensive spells plus a defensive buff for allies |

The Knight holds the timeline while the Mage protects the team and deals damage. That pairing is the only fully confirmed composition, and it is a sensible default while the rest of the roster is undocumented.

### 2. Tarot Events

The adventure deck is **party-responsive**. GamerScout describes "branching illustrated events [that] shift based on your party composition and previous choices." This is the strongest argument for rotating heroes: the same event can offer different choices depending on who is with you. A party built only for combat will see a narrower slice of the game's content.

### 3. Story Arcs

Each of the seven heroes has a distinct arc connected to the notebook's themes. Starting hero changes the story, and bonds unlock backstory scenes. If you play the entire campaign with one fixed trio, you are choosing to skip most of the narrative.

## Practical Party Building

A sensible approach for a new run:

1. **Start with the Knight if you are learning the Break system.** The Knight is the most forgiving confirmed hero and teaches the core mechanic.
2. **Pair the Knight with the Mage when both are available.** Frontline plus ranged support is the confirmed synergy.
3. **Recruit early and often.** A hero who joins late misses the fights and campfire scenes that build bonds.
4. **Rotate one slot per act.** Keep a stable core and swap the flex slot to see different events and arcs.
5. **Check the campfire between fights.** It is the documented relationship space; skipping it skips the bond content.
6. **Build artifacts around the party, not one hero.** Enemy artifact scaling punishes a single glass cannon; a party with protection and control is more consistent.
7. **Do not chase a party-size number.** No cap is confirmed. Build the strongest team the run gives you.

## Bonds and the Run Structure

The campaign's four acts of fifteen days give the bond system room to breathe. A run is long enough that relationships can develop across multiple acts, and the act structure gives you natural checkpoints for rotating heroes.

What is not confirmed is whether bonds persist across runs. The game has roguelite meta-progression, and the demo's friendship system exists within a run, but whether relationship levels carry over is **[Unconfirmed]**. Until the developer documents it, assume bonds are a per-run investment and treat each new run as a fresh chance to see different campfire scenes.
