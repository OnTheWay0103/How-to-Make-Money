## Advanced Strategies Guide — Stacking, Scaling, and Deck Play

Graphite's signature design is **unlimited artifact stacking** — but power comes with consequences, because enemies acquire artifacts too. Advanced play is about managing that arms race: when to stack, when to hold, how to steer your build, and how to use the tarot deck as a strategic engine. This guide stays grounded in confirmed mechanics and marks the rest honestly.

## Artifact Stacking — The Confirmed Rules

| Rule | Status |
|------|--------|
| Unlimited stacking (no cap) | Confirmed |
| Duplicates amplify the effect | Confirmed |
| Hyper-specialized builds supported | Confirmed |
| Enemies acquire artifacts during encounters | Confirmed |
| Level up + spend points on skills | Confirmed |
| "Diminishing returns past 12 stacks" | Unconfirmed — no such number exists |
| Individual vs. group artifact rules | Unconfirmed |

The confirmed economics of stacking are simple to state and hard to master: **every artifact compounds, on both sides.** Your stack grows your power; the enemy's stack grows theirs. The strategic layer is deciding what to amplify and when to stop amplifying offense in favor of protection.

## Managing the Arms Race

Confirmed enemy behavior: opponents pick up artifacts during encounters, get stronger over time, and can eventually use items. Advanced implications:

### 1. Fight Duration Is a Cost

Every extra exchange in a fight is time for the enemy to collect more artifacts. The advanced version of "finish fights fast" is: **sequence fights around your stack's peak**. Enter fights with a stack that spikes hard early, and finish before the enemy's collection catches up.

### 2. Scaling Favors Committed Builds

Because the enemy scales alongside your power, a half-committed grab-bag is the worst position: you carry the scaling cost of a big stack without the compounding benefit of a focused one. Commitment is not just a build philosophy — it is the confirmed-optimal response to symmetric scaling.

### 3. Balance Offense and Protection

The arms race means offense alone is fragile. A stack that amplifies one attack skill is powerful — and exactly the kind of build a Break-heavy enemy can shut down. Balance concentrated damage with effects that protect your Poise and your heroes' survival.

## Build Commitment Strategy

The confirmed progression loop (level up, spend points on chosen skills, stack artifacts without limit) supports a clear strategy:

1. **Decide your identity by the end of Act 1.** The demo's first act is long enough to commit: pick a skill, pick its amplifying artifacts, and spend points accordingly.
2. **Hyper-specialize or balance deliberately.** Both are confirmed-valid. Decide per run: focused single-target power, or a balanced party-wide loadout.
3. **Don't hoard.** Points and artifacts spent are worth more than points and artifacts saved. The only exception is deliberate protection against the arms race.
4. **Reuse what works.** Hero stories accumulate across runs and the deck remembers decisions — treat your growing knowledge of stacks and events as meta-progression, because it is.

## Tarot Deck Play at a High Level

The confirmed deck facts: tarot-inspired, illustrated narrated events, branching choices, shaped by party, items, and prior decisions.

Advanced deck habits:

- **Build the party for the deck, not just the fights.** Party composition influences the deck — the confirmed design means your roster choices shape your event options. Bring heroes for the outcomes you want.
- **Use events as build fuel.** The deck is the confirmed source of items and choices. Event rewards should feed your stack's direction, not a grab-bag.
- **Remember across runs.** Prior decisions — including decisions from earlier runs — shape what the deck offers. Log mentally what you chose and what it cost.
- **Treat the demo's 15 events as a study set.** Replay events with different parties and different stacks to learn the deck's response patterns before the full game.

## Team Composition Strategy

Only two heroes are confirmed (The Knight and The Mage), so composition strategy is necessarily limited — and honest about it:

| Composition | Confirmed Elements | Notes |
|-------------|-------------------|-------|
| Knight + Mage | Break-focused frontline + spell damage + defensive support | The only fully confirmed pairing; appears in multiple previews |
| Anything else | Requires unconfirmed heroes | TBD until official reveals |

Within the confirmed pair: the Knight holds attention and spends Break pressure on enemy heavy actions while the Mage deals damage and boosts defenses. That division of labor is a solid foundation to build on as the roster unlocks.

## Advanced Checklist

1. Commit to a build direction by the end of Act 1.
2. Track the arms race — is the enemy's artifact collection outpacing your stack?
3. Finish fights efficiently; long fights feed the enemy.
4. Bring a party that shapes the deck toward your build's needs.
5. Balance concentrated offense with Poise protection.
6. Learn the deck's 15 demo events across multiple parties.
7. Stay skeptical of unsourced specifics — named artifacts, stack caps, event names.

## What's Unconfirmed (and Worth Ignoring)

- "Stack to 12, then stop" — no such cap is documented
- Named artifacts and named events — none are confirmed
- Region-based scaling checks and deck-seeding rules — unconfirmed
- Any boss specifics — unconfirmed (see the [Boss Strategy Guide](/guides/boss-guide))
