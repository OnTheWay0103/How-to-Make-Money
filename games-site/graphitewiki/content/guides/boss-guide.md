## Boss Overview — What We Actually Know

This guide needs an honest preface: **no Graphite boss names, patterns, or phase mechanics have been officially confirmed** at the time of writing. The full game launched July 27, 2026, and the free Act 1 demo remains the most accessible way to experience its combat. Any guide that names specific bosses (such as a "Hollow Knight," "Tidecaller," or "Clockwork Tyrant") and describes their phases is fabricated — no such content is sourced.

What is confirmed:

- Each act of the campaign builds toward a **major confrontation** — the demo's Act 1 concludes with one, and press coverage describes acts ending in climactic encounters
- **Death** is the overarching threat the story prepares for — the final confrontation of the campaign is against Death (or its arrival), though whether that takes the form of a boss fight is unconfirmed
- Bosses are subject to the game's universal combat rules: shared timeline, action durations, and the **Break system**

## The Break System Is the Boss Mechanic

Even without boss details, the confirmed combat design tells you how boss fights will work:

- Bosses run on the same **timeline** as everything else, with every action having a duration
- **Breaking** — depleting an enemy's Poise to zero — cancels their scheduled action and pushes their turn down the timeline
- The system is symmetric: bosses can Break your heroes the same way

Since bosses are described in coverage as powerful attacks worth interrupting (the Break system exists specifically to interrupt "a powerful attack, summon, or buff"), the core boss strategy is already visible: **read the timeline, find the heavy actions, and Break them.** The demo is the best place to practice this before later acts raise the stakes.

## General Boss Tactics

These tactics follow from confirmed mechanics and apply regardless of which boss you face:

1. **Read the wind-up.** Timeline combat means you can see heavy attacks coming — count the ticks until the enemy action resolves and decide whether to interrupt, defend, or reposition.
2. **Save Break pressure for key actions.** The confirmed purpose of Break is interrupting powerful attacks, summons, and buffs. A boss's most dangerous abilities are the ones worth spending your Break on.
3. **Protect your own Poise.** Because Break is symmetric, a boss can cancel your party's critical actions too. Don't queue a long-duration action if the boss is acting before you can land it.
4. **Stack artifacts with intent.** Unlimited stacking is confirmed, and enemies scale alongside your power — before a boss, commit to a loadout that matches the fight's demands rather than a grab-bag.
5. **Keep your timeline flexible.** Speed and delay effects exist in some form (their exact scope is unconfirmed), but the confirmed design philosophy is time management — actions with shorter durations keep you responsive.
