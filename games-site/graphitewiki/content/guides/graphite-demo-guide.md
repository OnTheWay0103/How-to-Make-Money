# Graphite Demo Guide — Act 1, What's Included, and First Run Tips

Graphite is a pen-and-ink roguelike tactical RPG from Colombian studio RipRed, published by indie.io, launching on Steam on July 27, 2026. Before the full release, the team released **Graphite: Act 1** — a free demo that covers the entire first act of the campaign. If you are trying the demo and wondering what you are actually looking at (and what carries over), this guide walks you through it.

## What Is the Graphite: Act 1 Demo?

Graphite: Act 1 is a free standalone demo available now on Steam (store app 3307810). Instead of a short vertical slice, it gives you the **complete first act** — roughly 30 minutes per run, per the Steam listing. The demo is not a separate game: it is the opening act of the full campaign, released early so players can try the core systems before the July 27 launch.

Two things are worth noting about this approach. First, demoing a full act rather than a tutorial slice is an unusual confidence signal from a debut studio — reviewers have repeatedly highlighted "originality" as the demo's most common word in community impressions. Second, because the demo is a roguelike, a single run is short by design: the goal of a run is to push as far as you can, learn the systems, and try again.

## What's Included in the Demo

Per the Steam store page, the demo contains:

- **4 playable heroes** (the full game has 7)
- **50 stackable artifacts**
- **15 hand-drawn adventure events**
- **Friendship levels up to 1** between characters

Of the four demo heroes, launch coverage has confirmed two by name: **The Knight**, who uses a shield to exploit the Break system and deals significant damage, and **The Mage**, who casts offensive spells and doubles as a support by boosting allies' defenses. The other demo heroes have not been officially named in public coverage at the time of writing — treat any other hero names you see on the internet as unconfirmed.

The artifact count is the number available in the demo pool, not a cap on how many you can hold: stacking has no upper limit (more on that below).

## The Setting and Story Setup

Graphite takes place inside a child's school notebook. The heroes are pencil-drawn characters who do not know they are figments of a child's imagination, and the world is made of paper and repurposed household junk — everyday objects stand in for monsters and treasure. A mysterious figure called **The Scribe** tasks the seven heroes with growing stronger and gathering allies before **Death** arrives.

The demo establishes this setup: who the Scribe is, why the notebook world exists, and why Death is coming. Reviews of the demo praise the dramatic irony — characters taking a world-threatening quest seriously while being doodles — as the emotional core of the writing. Details beyond the Act 1 setup (including the identity of the child behind the notebook) are revealed later in the campaign; the full act count has not been officially confirmed.

## How a Run Works

A demo run follows the roguelike loop that shapes the full game:

1. **Pick a starting hero** — your choice matters; choosing different heroes changes story events and progression to fit that character's themes.
2. **Fight through procedurally generated encounters** — enemies are generated randomly, get stronger over time, and can eventually use items themselves.
3. **Level up and build your character** — after each battle you gain levels and spend points to upgrade your chosen skills, steering your build as the run develops.
4. **Draw from the tarot adventure deck** — between fights, illustrated event cards present narrated, choose-your-own-adventure style choices.
5. **Reach the Act 1 conclusion** — the act ends in a major confrontation, then the run wraps up.

Because the demo is the first act, a run ends after the Act 1 finale. That makes the demo an excellent environment for experimenting: there is very little time invested per run, so you can test different heroes, builds, and event choices quickly.

## The Timeline Combat System (Quick Primer)

Combat in Graphite plays out on a **dynamic timeline** shared by every participant. Each action you take has a duration, and the timeline shows when every queued action will resolve. Multiple units act simultaneously, and when you act is as important as what you do. Coverage of the system compares it to classic active-time battle systems and to the tactical RPG **Star Renegades**.

In practical terms: you can see an enemy winding up a heavy attack several ticks out, and you can respond before it connects — or decide to spend that time doing something else. Reading the timeline is the single most important combat skill in the demo.

## The Break System — the One Mechanic to Learn

Break is Graphite's signature combat mechanic, and it is worth understanding before your first fight:

- Every combatant has **Poise** (an action gauge).
- **Breaking** depletes the target's Poise; when it hits zero, their scheduled action is **cancelled and their turn is pushed down the timeline** — this interrupts powerful attacks, summons, and buffs.
- The system is **fully symmetric**: enemies can Break your heroes the same way. There is no safety net.

This symmetry creates the game's central trade-off: push for offense and risk your own actions being cancelled, or play defensively and protect your Poise. The Knight is a natural teacher for this system — shield-based abilities that exploit Break are his specialty, and the demo is the best place to learn Break timing before the full game's later acts.

## Artifacts: Stack Without Limit

Artifacts are passive items, and there is **no cap on stacking** — you can equip unlimited artifacts, and stacking multiples of the same artifact increases its effect. Coverage notes that the game explicitly supports both balanced and hyper-specialized builds: stacking one skill with many duplicates of the same item is a valid strategy.

The catch is that **enemies acquire artifacts too**. Opponents pick up artifacts during encounters and scale alongside you, which reviewers describe as an arms race. The demo's 50-artifact pool is a good sampling of this system — try one hyper-specialized run and one balanced run to feel the difference.

## First-Run Tips for the Demo

1. **Start with the Knight.** He is the most forgiving hero in the demo: a shield, strong Break usage, and significant damage. Learn the timeline with a durable frontline before trying squishier heroes.
2. **Read the timeline before every action.** The whole combat system rewards pausing and checking what is queued, on both sides.
3. **Don't hoard artifacts — stack them.** Unequipped artifacts do nothing. Try duplicating one strong effect to feel how fast stacking compounds.
4. **Take event cards seriously.** The tarot deck's branching choices are influenced by your party, your items, and your previous decisions — the same event can play out differently on a second run.
5. **Let yourself lose.** Runs are ~30 minutes. The demo is a learning environment; every failed run teaches you a mechanic, an event outcome, or an enemy pattern you can exploit next time.
6. **Spend skill points early.** Levels come fast in Act 1, and committed builds outperform hoarded points.
7. **Watch the enemies' items.** If you notice opponents picking up artifacts mid-fight, expect their power to climb — that scaling is by design.

## What's Not in the Demo

The demo deliberately leaves out the rest of the campaign: heroes 5 through 7, later acts, and the endgame narrative are full-game content. Two things are also officially unconfirmed and should be treated as unknown rather than assumed:

- **Save carryover** — whether demo progress (unlocks, friendship, achievements) carries into the full game has not been confirmed.
- **Full-game systems** — meta-progression beyond what the demo shows (unlockables, persistent progression between runs) is confirmed to exist in the roguelite sense, but the details are only partially known.

## What to Expect at Full Release

The full game launches **July 27, 2026** on Steam for PC (Windows). The launch version includes all 7 heroes, the complete campaign, and the full artifact and event pools. The price has not been announced at the time of writing — check the Steam page for the current listing. Minimum requirements listed for the demo are modest (Windows 10, a 2.0 GHz processor, 2 GB RAM, 2 GB graphics, 1 GB storage); the full game's requirements have not been separately confirmed.
